# ～をかわきりに(して) {#をかわきりに}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～を皮切りに（して）

**Furigana form:** ～をかわきりに（して）

**Meaning:** "Starting with X (and continuing on from there)" — marks the first in a series of events, especially one that kicks off an expanding sequence (e.g., a tour, campaign, or series of occurrences).

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + を皮切りに（して）
- Example base: 東京公演 → 東京公演を皮切りに

**Typical context:** Formal, written register; very common in news reporting and formal announcements about tours, product launches, or expanding event series. Not used in casual speech.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **を手始めに** — "starting with X as a first step"; close in meaning, slightly more general-purpose and less tied specifically to an expanding multi-stage series.
- **をはじめとして** — "starting with X (as a representative example), and including others"; similar "starting point" framing but often lists representative members of a category rather than a sequential chain of events.
- **を契機に** — "triggered by X / taking X as an opportunity"; shares the "starting point" idea but focuses on X as a causal trigger for a subsequent change, rather than specifically the first event in a touring/sequential series.

**Opposite grammar structures:** ～を最後に ("ending with X / as the final occurrence") expresses the reverse — marking the conclusion of a series rather than its start.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼のツアーは東京公演を皮切りに、全国十都市を回る予定だ。 (His tour, starting with the Tokyo performance, is set to travel to ten cities nationwide.)
2. この新商品は日本での発売を皮切りに、海外展開も計画されている。 (Starting with its release in Japan, overseas expansion for this new product is also planned.)
3. 来月の記者会見を皮切りに、一連のキャンペーンが始まる。 (Starting with next month's press conference, a series of campaign events will begin.)
4. その事件を皮切りに、次々と同様の被害が報告された。 (Starting with that incident, similar damage was reported one after another.)
5. 彼女のデビュー作を皮切りに、話題作が立て続けに発表された。 (Starting with her debut work, a string of talked-about works followed in succession.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** を皮切りに always implies that what follows is a growing or continuing series — a single isolated event with no follow-up doesn't fit this pattern. 皮切り literally means "the first cut" (from cutting fabric/leather), evoking the image of making the first incision that opens up a larger process — learners can use that image to remember its "kicking off a sequence" meaning, distinct from simple "starting" words like 最初に.
