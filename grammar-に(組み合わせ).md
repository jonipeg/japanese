# Nに+N (combination) {#に-組み合わせ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** 名詞+に+名詞 (example: 朝食はパンにコーヒーです)

**Furigana form:** めいし+に+めいし

**Meaning:** "A combination of A and B" — uses に to link two nouns that naturally go together as a set or pairing, often describing a meal, outfit, or typical combination of items.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞A (noun A) + に + 名詞B (noun B)
- Example: 朝食はいつもパンにコーヒーです。 (Breakfast is always bread with coffee.)

**Typical context:** Casual-to-neutral spoken Japanese; commonly used when listing items that form a natural set, such as meals, outfits, or everyday combinations.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **と** — "and" — a more neutral, general conjunction for listing items, without the implication that the items form a natural complementary pairing.
- [**や**](grammar-や.md) — "and/or (non-exhaustive)" — used for listing a few representative examples from a larger group, rather than emphasizing a fixed combination.
- **プラス** — "plus" (borrowed from English) — a casual way to add one item to another, often used in spoken Japanese similarly to this に construction.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though 単品で ("as a single item, standalone") contrasts in spirit by indicating something is not part of a combination.

**Example sentences:**
1. 朝食はいつもパンにコーヒーです。
   (Breakfast is always bread with coffee.)
2. 今日のランチはカレーにサラダにした。
   (For today's lunch, I went with curry and salad.)
3. 彼女のファッションはいつもTシャツにジーンズだ。
   (Her fashion is always a T-shirt with jeans.)
4. お正月の料理といえば、おせちにお雑煮でしょう。
   (When it comes to New Year's food, it's osechi with ozoni, right?)
5. このラーメン屋では、ラーメンに餃子を頼む人が多い。
   (At this ramen shop, many people order ramen with gyoza.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This に is easy to confuse with the locative/time に; here it functions purely as a listing connector implying "plus, together with," used almost exclusively for naturally paired items (food combos, outfits) — it cannot replace と in general sentence contexts like 友達と映画を見た.
