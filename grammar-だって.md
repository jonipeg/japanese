# だって {#だって}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** だって（漢字なし／平仮名のみ）

**Furigana form:** だって

**Meaning:** "But, because..." — a very casual, often petulant or defensive conjunction used to give an excuse or justification, frequently in response to being questioned or criticized.

**Conjugation:** Sentence-initial conjunction, no conjugation needed — attaches at the sentence boundary, connecting the previous remark (usually someone else's question or criticism) to the speaker's excuse/justification that follows. It is essentially always sentence-initial in dialogue and is not used to link two clauses within a single sentence.

**Typical context:** Very casual, spoken register — used almost exclusively in conversation between close friends, family members, or in child-like/childish speech. It carries a whiny, pouty, or self-justifying tone, as if the speaker is defending themselves against an implicit accusation. It is essentially never appropriate in formal writing or polite/formal speech, and sounds notably immature or petulant if used with a superior or in a professional setting.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**だから**](grammar-だから.md) — casual, spoken; a neutral statement of cause, lacking だって's defensive/excuse-making, petulant tone.
- [**なぜなら**](grammar-なぜなら.md) — formal, written; the polar-opposite register counterpart, used for explicit, composed justification in essays rather than emotional excuse-making in conversation.
- [**というのは**](grammar-というのは.md) — written/spoken explanatory; a calmer, more neutral way to introduce a reason/elaboration, without だって's whiny or childish nuance.
- [**それで**](grammar-それで.md) — casual-neutral, spoken; narrates a reason/result neutrally, without the defensive "but it's not my fault" flavor of だって.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; its register-opposite counterpart is なぜなら, the formal, composed way of stating "the reason is..." instead of だって's casual excuse.

**Example sentences:**
1. A: なんでまた遅刻したの? B: だって、バスが全然来なかったんだもん。
   (A: Why were you late again? B: But the bus just wouldn't come!)
2. A: 野菜食べなさい。 B: だって、嫌いなんだもん。
   (A: Eat your vegetables. B: But I hate them!)
3. A: 宿題終わったの? B: だって、難しすぎて分からなかったんだよ。
   (A: Did you finish your homework? B: But it was too hard, I couldn't figure it out!)
4. A: どうして彼に謝らないの? B: だって、悪いのは向こうじゃん。
   (A: Why won't you apologize to him? B: But he's the one who's wrong!)
5. A: また新しい服買ったの? B: だって、安売りしてたんだもん。
   (A: You bought new clothes again? B: But it was on sale!)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The key mistake learners make is using だって in neutral or formal contexts where they simply mean "because," not realizing it carries a built-in whiny, defensive, excuse-making nuance — using it with a boss, teacher, or stranger can sound childish or even rude, as if making excuses rather than giving a reasonable explanation. In such cases, だから, それで, or ですから would be appropriate instead. Learners should also note that だって is almost always a reactive response to something just said (a question, criticism, or expectation), unlike だから/それで, which can introduce a reason proactively without responding to a prior prompt.
