# ～くらいなら {#くらいなら}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～くらいなら

**Furigana form:** ～くらいなら

**Meaning:** "I would rather ~ than ~" — expresses that the speaker finds a situation so undesirable that another (often also undesirable) alternative would be preferable. Strong subjective judgment, often with frustration or rhetorical emphasis.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形 (verb dictionary form) + くらいなら + [preferred alternative, often ～方がいい／～ほうがましだ／命令形]
- Example: え、これ要らないんですか。捨てるくらいなら、私にください。 (Oh, you don't need this? If you're going to throw it away, give it to me instead.)

**Typical context:** Spoken, casual-to-neutral register; frequently used in everyday conversation to express strong personal preference or complaint. Can sound emphatic or emotional.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **むしろ** — "rather, instead" — a standalone adverb expressing preference, often paired with くらいなら for extra emphasis, but can be used without the comparative clause structure.
- **よりは** — "rather than ~" — a more neutral comparative marker without the strong negative nuance toward the first option.
- [**ほうがましだ**](grammar-ほうがましだ.md) — "would be better off ~" — often appears in the second clause of くらいなら sentences to reinforce the "lesser evil" nuance.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though 喜んで～する ("gladly do ~") contrasts in spirit by expressing willing preference rather than reluctant comparison.

**Example sentences:**
1. え、これ要らないんですか。捨てるくらいなら、私にください。
   (Oh, you don't need this? If you're going to throw it away, give it to me instead.)
2. あんな人と結婚するくらいなら、一生独身でいたほうがましだ。
   (I'd rather stay single my whole life than marry someone like that.)
3. 満員電車に乗るくらいなら、1時間早く起きて歩いて行く。
   (Rather than ride a packed train, I'd get up an hour early and walk.)
4. 謝らずに我慢するくらいなら、最初から正直に話せばよかった。
   (Rather than endure it without apologizing, I should have just been honest from the start.)
5. まずい料理を我慢して食べるくらいなら、何も食べないほうがいい。
   (Rather than force myself to eat bad food, it's better to eat nothing at all.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes mistake くらいなら for a neutral comparison like より; in fact it strongly implies the first option is undesirable/distasteful to the speaker, and the second option (while often also non-ideal) is framed as the lesser evil, not a genuinely good choice.
