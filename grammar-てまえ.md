# ～てまえ {#てまえ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～手前

**Furigana form:** ～てまえ

**Meaning:** "Given the circumstances/in front of (others)" — expresses a face-saving obligation: the speaker must act (or refrain from acting) a certain way because of appearances, position, or a prior statement made in front of others.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞普通形 (verb plain form) + てまえ
- 名詞 (noun) + の + てまえ
- Example from source: 勤めている手前 (given that [he] works [there])

**Typical context:** Formal-to-neutral spoken and written Japanese. Carries a nuance of social obligation, embarrassment-avoidance, or "keeping up appearances" — often used when explaining why one can't do something that would otherwise seem natural, due to one's position, prior words, or social standing. Not rude; often slightly self-deprecating or apologetic in tone.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**とひきかえに**](grammar-とひきかえに.md) — means "in exchange for X"; a transactional trade-off, not a face-saving/appearance-based obligation. This was a confirmed confusion pair on a graded test: a student picked てまえ instead of とひきかえに.
- [**以上(は)**](grammar-うえは・以上(は).md) — "given that X (is the case), ..."; a logical-consequence construction based on a stated fact or commitment, similar in spirit but without the specific "in front of others / saving face" nuance.
- [**からには**](grammar-からには.md) — "now that X, ..."; similar logical-obligation nuance (having done X, one must do Y), but lacks the explicit "appearances/others are watching" flavor central to てまえ.
- **上で (うえで)** — "on the matter of X / in doing X"; unrelated procedural/sequential meaning, easily confused only by surface similarity in some sentence positions.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a conceptual contrast might be 人目を気にせず ("without worrying about others' eyes"), which describes acting without regard for appearances — the reverse attitude from てまえ's face-conscious obligation.

**Example sentences:**
1. 夫がH自動車に勤めている手前、T自動車の車を買うわけにはいかないでしょう。
   (Given that my husband works at H Motors, we obviously can't buy a car from T Motors.)
2. 部下の前であれだけ偉そうなことを言った手前、今さら謝るわけにはいかない。
   (Having said such grand things in front of my subordinates, I can't very well apologize now.)
3. 先生という立場上、生徒の手前、いい加減なことは言えない。
   (Given my position as a teacher, and with my students watching, I can't say anything careless.)
4. みんなに「絶対やる」と宣言した手前、今更できませんとは言えない。
   (Having declared to everyone "I'll definitely do it," I can't say "I can't do it" at this point.)
5. 客の手前、店員としてはあまり文句を言うわけにはいかない。
   (Given that customers are watching, as a store clerk I can't really complain.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** On a graded test, a student picked てまえ when the correct answer was とひきかえに — the key distinguishing factor is that とひきかえに is purely transactional ("in exchange for X," a trade), while てまえ is about social obligation/face-saving in front of others due to one's position or prior statement. Students should check whether the sentence involves "keeping up appearances because of who's watching or what was said" (→てまえ) versus "giving up/trading one thing for another" (→とひきかえに).
