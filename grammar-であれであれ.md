# ～であれ～であれ／かれ～かれ／であろうと(が) {#であれであれ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～であれ～であれ／かれ～かれ／であろうと（が）

**Furigana form:** ～であれ～であれ／かれ～かれ／であろうと（が）

**Meaning:** "Whatever A or B / no matter whether it's A or B" — presents two or more contrasting possibilities as equally irrelevant to a stated conclusion, emphasizing the conclusion holds regardless.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + であれ + 名詞 + であれ
- 形容詞語幹 + かれ + 形容詞語幹 + かれ (fixed set: 良かれ悪しかれ, "for better or worse")
- 名詞 + であろうと（が）
- Example base: 大人 → 大人であれ子供であれ

**Typical context:** Formal, written/literary register; common in formal writing and speeches to assert that a rule or conclusion applies universally, regardless of varying circumstances. Not casual.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **にしろ～にしろ／にせよ～にせよ** — "whether A or B"; nearly synonymous and commonly interchangeable, perhaps slightly more common in everyday formal speech than であれ～であれ.
- **(よ)うが～まいが** — "whether X happens or not"; a related structure for an affirmative/negative pair of the same verb, whereas であれ～であれ typically contrasts two distinct named alternatives (nouns).
- **であろうとなかろうと** — a fuller variant combining であろうと with its negative counterpart, meaning "whether it is or isn't X," closely related to であろうと(が) used alone.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a statement making the outcome strictly conditional on which alternative holds (次第で違う、"it differs depending on X") would contrast with this pattern's insistence on a uniform outcome regardless.

**Example sentences:**
1. 大人であれ子供であれ、ルールは守らなければならない。 (Whether adult or child, the rules must be followed.)
2. 良かれ悪しかれ、これが現実だ。 (For better or worse, this is reality.)
3. 社長であろうと新入社員であろうと、評価の基準は同じだ。 (Whether president or new employee, the evaluation standards are the same.)
4. 国内であれ海外であれ、出張には変わりない。 (Whether domestic or overseas, it's still a business trip either way.)
5. 賛成であろうと反対であろうと、決定には従うべきだ。 (Whether in favor or opposed, one should abide by the decision.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** であれ～であれ requires naming two (or more) contrasting alternatives explicitly — it is not used with a single item. The かれ～かれ variant is largely fossilized in the single fixed idiom 良かれ悪しかれ ("for better or worse") and is not freely productive with other adjectives, unlike であれ～であれ which can be applied to various noun pairs.
