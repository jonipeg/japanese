# さらに {#さらに}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** 更に

**Furigana form:** さらに

**Meaning:** "Furthermore, moreover" — adds an escalating point, stronger in degree than plain addition.

**Conjugation:** Sentence-initial conjunction, no conjugation needed — attaches at the sentence boundary, connecting the previous sentence to the next.

**Typical context:** Written/formal register, common in essays, news reports, academic writing, and formal speeches when the writer wants to intensify or escalate a point already made (e.g. a problem that gets worse, or a benefit that increases further). Usable in careful/formal spoken contexts too, but uncommon in casual daily conversation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**それに**](grammar-それに.md) — adds a supplementary point reinforcing an argument; weaker in degree than さらに's escalating emphasis.
- [**また**](grammar-また.md) — neutral listing of parallel items; さらに implies the additional point intensifies or builds on the first, while また is just parallel listing.
- [**および**](grammar-および.md) — very formal/written "and," used in official lists; purely additive, without さらに's escalating-degree nuance.
- [**あと**](grammar-あと.md) — casual spoken "also," afterthought in tone; opposite register from さらに's formal escalation.
- [**しかも**](grammar-しかも.md) — adds a surprising/notable additional fact, often making things even more impressive or severe; close in strength to さらに but しかも carries more of a "what's more, surprisingly" flavor, while さらに is more neutrally about increasing degree.
- [**そのうえ**](grammar-そのうえ.md) — similar to しかも, slightly more neutral/written-leaning; close synonym to さらに but さらに emphasizes escalating degree more explicitly (e.g. "even more so").

**Opposite grammar structures:** しかし/だが (contrastive "however" conjunctions) are the functional opposite, since they counter the preceding statement rather than intensify it.

**Example sentences:**
1. 台風で電車が止まった。さらに、強風で停電も起きた。
   (The typhoon stopped the trains. Furthermore, the strong wind also caused a power outage.)
2. この薬は効果が高い。さらに、副作用もほとんどない。
   (This medicine is highly effective. Furthermore, it has almost no side effects.)
3. 売上は去年の二倍になった。さらに、来年はそれを上回る見込みだ。
   (Sales doubled from last year. Furthermore, next year is expected to surpass even that.)
4. 彼は語学が堪能だ。さらに、プログラミングの技術も持っている。
   (He is fluent in languages. Furthermore, he also has programming skills.)
5. 問題はすでに深刻だった。さらに、事態は悪化の一途をたどった。
   (The problem was already serious. Furthermore, the situation continued to worsen.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes use さらに interchangeably with それに or また, but さらに specifically signals that the new point intensifies or escalates the degree of what was already true (more, worse, further), not just a neutral additional fact. Also distinguish from しかも: しかも adds a surprising extra fact ("what's more, surprisingly..."), while さらに is more about plain escalation in degree without necessarily implying surprise.
