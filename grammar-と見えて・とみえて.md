# ～と見えて／とみえて {#と見えて・とみえて}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**1. Kanji version:** ～と見えて

**2. Furigana version:** ～とみえて

**3. Meaning:** It seems/appears that X — an inference drawn from visible evidence or circumstances.

**4. Conjugation (connection form):** 動詞・い形・な形・名詞の普通形 + とみえて

**5. Usage context:** Written and spoken, neutral-to-formal register. Used to state an inference based on something the speaker has directly observed.

**6. Similar grammar structures:**
- らしい — a common near-synonym for inference, but らしい often draws on indirect/hearsay information, whereas とみえて is more tied to direct visual/circumstantial evidence.
- ようだ — a broader "looks like/seems" pattern covering many kinds of inference, less specifically tied to visible evidence than とみえて.
- こそすれ — a completely unrelated concessive/contrastive pattern meaning "although X is true, ..." Confirmed as a real student confusion (Q14, student wrongly picked こそすれ) — this is a pure memorization trap since the two patterns share no functional overlap.

**7. Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**8. Example sentences:**
1. 隣のうちは留守と見えて、先週からずっとカーテンが閉まったままだ。(The house next door seems to be empty — the curtains have been closed ever since last week.)
2. 彼は疲れているとみえて、会議中に何度もあくびをしていた。(He seemed tired — he kept yawning during the meeting.)
3. 道が濡れているところを見ると、さっきまで雨が降っていたとみえる。(Judging by the wet road, it seems it was raining until just a little while ago.)
4. その店は人気があるとみえて、いつも行列ができている。(That shop seems to be popular — there's always a line.)
5. 子供はお腹が空いていたとみえて、出された料理をあっという間に食べてしまった。(The child seemed to be hungry — he finished the food that was served in no time.)

**9. Common mistakes / key nuances:** Confirmed student confusion with こそすれ on Q14 — とみえて signals an inference based on observed evidence ("judging from what I see, it seems..."), while こそすれ is a concessive pattern meaning "although/granted that X, nonetheless..." They are unrelated in meaning, so this mistake reflects rote confusion rather than a genuine nuance overlap.
