# ～の末に {#の末に}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～の末に

**Furigana form:** ～のすえに

**Meaning:** "After (much deliberation/effort) / at the end of X, finally" — marks the conclusion of a long, often difficult or effortful process, emphasizing that the result came only after considerable time or struggle.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + の末に (例: 長い議論の末に、苦労の末に)
- 動詞た形 + 末に (例: 悩んだ末に、話し合った末に)
- Also: 〜末の + 名詞 (attributive, e.g. 苦労の末の成功)

**Typical context:** Formal, mostly written — common in narratives, biographies, news reports, and formal speech describing the culmination of a lengthy or arduous process.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**あげく(に)**](grammar-あげく(に).md) — similar "after much X, finally," but typically carries a negative or regrettable nuance about the outcome, unlike 末に which is neutral.
- **た結果** — "as a result of doing X," more neutral/factual, without emphasizing the length or difficulty of the process.
- **ついに** — "finally," simple adverb marking the end point without describing the preceding process in detail.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 長い話し合いの末に、両社は合意に達した。
   (After lengthy discussions, the two companies reached an agreement.)
2. 何度も悩んだ末に、転職を決意した。
   (After agonizing over it many times, I decided to change jobs.)
3. 激しい戦いの末に、彼らは勝利を手にした。
   (After a fierce battle, they achieved victory.)
4. 長年の努力の末に、彼はついに夢を実現させた。
   (After years of effort, he finally made his dream come true.)
5. さんざん迷った末に、この家を買うことに決めた。
   (After much hesitation, I decided to buy this house.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 末に is neutral regarding the outcome's desirability (can be positive or negative), whereas あげく almost always implies the final result was unfortunate, wasted effort, or ironic — don't use them interchangeably when the ending is clearly positive (末に is safer for success stories).
