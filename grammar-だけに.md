# ～だけに {#だけに}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～だけに

**Furigana form:** ～だけに (no kanji)

**Meaning:** "Precisely because X, all the more so" — indicates that a known fact or situation intensifies the following result, which can be either positive or negative.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + だけに
- な形容詞 + な + だけに
- い形容詞 (plain form) + だけに
- 動詞 (plain form, past or non-past) + だけに

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal, used in both written commentary and somewhat formal spoken Japanese to remark on an intensified, expected outcome.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**だけあって**](grammar-だけあって.md) — similar, but almost always positive/admiring (living up to a reputation); だけに is broader and can intensify negative outcomes too.
- [**にしては**](grammar-にしては.md) — "for being X, surprisingly..." — contrasts by introducing an outcome that defies expectation rather than intensifying it.

**Opposite grammar structures:** にもかかわらず ("despite X," an unexpected result, contrary to what だけに implies).

**Example sentences:**
1. 長年努力しただけに、合格の喜びはひとしおだった。
   (Precisely because he'd worked hard for years, the joy of passing was all the greater.)
2. 期待していただけに、失敗した時のショックは大きかった。
   (Precisely because I had high expectations, the shock of failure was huge.)
3. ベテラン選手だけに、プレッシャーのかかる場面でも落ち着いていた。
   (Being a veteran player, he stayed calm even under pressure.)
4. 有名なレストランだけに、予約を取るのが難しい。
   (Precisely because it's a famous restaurant, it's hard to get a reservation.)
5. 一生懸命準備しただけに、中止になって残念だ。
   (Precisely because we prepared so hard, it's disappointing that it was cancelled.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often confuse だけに with だけあって. だけあって is reserved for praising a result that matches reputation or effort (always positive), while だけに can intensify a negative outcome just as easily as a positive one.
