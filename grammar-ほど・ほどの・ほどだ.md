# ～ほど／～ほどの／～ほどだ {#ほど・ほどの・ほどだ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～程／～程の／～程だ

**Furigana form:** ～ほど／～ほどの／～ほどだ (程 read ほど)

**Meaning:** "To the extent that," "so much that" — expresses degree by giving a concrete example/comparison that illustrates just how intense a state or action is. Also used in the comparative pattern ～ば～ほど ("the more... the more...").

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞普通形／い形容詞／な形容詞 + ほど (degree): 疲れて死ぬほど眠い。(So tired I could die — extremely sleepy.)
- 名詞 + ほどの + 名詞: これほどの出来事は滅多にない。(An event to this extent is rare.)
- 普通形 + ほどだ (sentence-final): 痛くて涙が出るほどだ。(It hurts so much that tears come out.)
- Comparative: 動詞ば形 + 動詞辞書形 + ほど: 練習すればするほど上手になる。(The more you practice, the better you get.)

**Typical context:** Spoken and written, neutral register. Very common for vivid/exaggerated comparisons in everyday speech as well as descriptive writing.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **くらい/ぐらい** — near-synonym for degree comparison, slightly more casual than ほど.
- **というほど** — used for emphasizing/quoting the degree expression, e.g., "〜というほどではない."
- **にほど遠い** — "far from X," a related but distinct idiomatic phrase using ほど in a different sense.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though ～ほどでもない ("not quite to the extent of...") expresses a toned-down negation of the degree.

**Example sentences:**
1. 今日は歩けないほど足が疲れた。
   (Today my legs got so tired that I could barely walk.)
2. 彼女は誰もが振り返るほどの美人だ。
   (She's beautiful to the extent that everyone turns to look.)
3. このラーメンは涙が出るほど辛い。
   (This ramen is so spicy that it brings tears to your eyes.)
4. 時間が経てば経つほど、記憶は薄れていく。
   (The more time passes, the more memories fade.)
5. 彼の実力はプロと言ってもいいほどだ。
   (His skill is to the extent that you could call it professional.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ほど often works with hyperbolic examples (死ぬほど, 涙が出るほど) purely for emphasis, not meant literally — learners sometimes take these too literally. Also, in the ～ば～ほど comparative structure, both verbs must match the same verb (e.g., 高ければ高いほど), and mixing different verbs is a common error.
