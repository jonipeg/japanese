# ～っぷり／ぶり {#っぷり・ぶり}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～っぷり／～振り

**Furigana form:** ～っぷり／～ぶり

**Meaning:** Two closely related uses: (1) attached to a verb stem or noun, describes the "manner/way of doing X" (e.g., 食べっぷり, "the way one eats," often admiringly); (2) attached to a time-duration noun, ぶり means "for the first time in [duration]" (e.g., 三年ぶり, "for the first time in three years").

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ます形 (verb ます-stem) + っぷり／ぶり (manner-of-doing sense)
- 期間を表す名詞 (noun expressing a duration of time) + ぶり (time-elapsed sense)
- Inferred from common usage since no example sentence is given in the source: 飲みっぷり (the way one drinks), 話しぶり (manner of speaking), 三年ぶり (for the first time in three years)

**Typical context:** Casual-to-neutral spoken and written Japanese — very common in everyday conversation. The "manner of doing" sense (っぷり/ぶり) often carries an admiring or vivid descriptive nuance (e.g., praising someone's generous/impressive way of doing something), while the "time elapsed" sense (ぶり) is neutral and extremely common in daily speech ("it's been a while since..."). Not formal or literary; natural in casual speech.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **方 (かた)** — the plain, neutral "way of doing X" (食べ方, "how to eat/the way of eating"); more descriptive/instructional, while っぷり/ぶり often adds an emotional, admiring, or vivid coloring to the manner described.
- **ざま** — also means "manner/state of doing X," but almost always carries a negative, derogatory nuance (負けざま, "the humiliating way one lost"), unlike っぷり/ぶり which is often neutral-to-positive.
- **以来 (いらい)** — "ever since X"; close to the time-elapsed sense of ぶり, but 以来 emphasizes continuity since that point, whereas ぶり simply marks that an interval of time has passed before something recurs.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences (no example sentence given in source for this entry; all below are original):**
1. 彼の食べっぷりは見ていて気持ちがいい。
   (The way he eats is satisfying just to watch.)
2. 彼女の話しぶりから、かなり自信があることが伝わってきた。
   (From her manner of speaking, I could tell she was quite confident.)
3. 三年ぶりに故郷に帰った。
   (I returned to my hometown for the first time in three years.)
4. 彼の潔い引退っぷりに、みんなが感動した。
   (Everyone was moved by the clean, admirable way he retired.)
5. 久しぶりに会った友人は、全く変わっていなかった。
   (My friend, whom I met for the first time in a while, hadn't changed at all.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** No confirmed student wrong-answer or example sentence is recorded in the source for this pattern (scan4 only notes it as MC-confirmed). A key nuance for learners: っぷり and ぶり are largely interchangeable in the "manner of doing" sense (っぷり is slightly more casual/emphatic), but only ぶり (not っぷり) is used in the "time elapsed since X" sense — students should not substitute っぷり into a duration-based sentence like 三年ぶり.
