# ～たなら {#たなら}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～たなら

**Furigana form:** ～たなら

**Meaning:** "If (I/you) had done ~ (things would have been different)" — a counterfactual past conditional expressing regret or hypothetical reflection about a past action that didn't happen, implying a different (usually better) outcome would have resulted.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞た形 (verb past form) + なら
- Example: あの時すぐ手術を受けていたなら、助かったのに。 (If I had undergone surgery right away back then, I would have been saved.)

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal spoken and written Japanese; commonly used with ～のに at the end of the sentence to add a tone of regret or lament about an unrealized past outcome.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **ていれば** — "if (I) had been doing/had done ~" — a very similar counterfactual conditional, often interchangeable with たなら, though ば focuses slightly more on condition/consequence logic.
- **たら** — the general past-tense conditional, usable for both counterfactual and simple sequential past conditions, but less specifically tied to the "if only" regretful nuance that たなら often carries with のに.
- [**ものを**](grammar-ものを.md) — "if only ~ (but didn't)" — a sentence-final expression of regret often paired with conditionals like たなら to deepen the lamenting tone.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though 結果的に良かった ("it turned out well in the end") contrasts in spirit by expressing satisfaction rather than regret.

**Example sentences:**
1. あの時すぐ手術を受けていたなら、助かったのに。
   (If I had undergone surgery right away back then, I would have been saved.)
2. もっと早く彼に相談していたなら、こんな事態にはならなかっただろう。
   (If I had consulted him sooner, things probably wouldn't have turned out this way.)
3. あの会社に就職していたなら、今頃は海外で働いていたかもしれない。
   (If I had taken that job at that company, I might be working overseas by now.)
4. 若い頃にもっと勉強していたなら、違う人生を送れていたかもしれない。
   (If I had studied harder when I was young, I might have lived a different life.)
5. あの時彼女に本当の気持ちを伝えていたなら、今でも一緒にいられたのに。
   (If I had told her my true feelings back then, we might still be together now.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often forget that たなら requires the past-tense verb form (ていた/した) rather than the plain dictionary form, since it specifically expresses a counterfactual about something that already happened (or didn't happen) in the past — using the non-past form changes the meaning to a hypothetical future condition instead.
