# ～に～ない／～（よ）うにも～ない {#に・ない・(よ)うにも・ない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～に～ない／～（よ）うにも～ない

**Furigana form:** ～に～ない／～（よ）うにも～ない

**Meaning:** "Want to do X, but can't" — expresses that although the speaker wants to do something, some circumstance (psychological or physical) makes it impossible.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形 + に + 可能動詞の否定形
- 動詞意向形（う・よう形） + にも + 可能動詞の否定形
- 捨てるに捨てられず — "want to throw it away, but can't bring myself to"
- 連絡しようにも連絡できなかった — "even if I tried to contact [them], I couldn't"

**Typical context:** Spoken and written, neutral register — used in personal narration and explanation of frustrating, conflicted situations; common in anecdotes about being stuck between wanting to act and being unable to.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～に～ない** vs **～（よ）うにも～ない** — the textbook distinguishes these two sub-patterns: ～に～ない more often expresses a psychological obstacle (e.g., 泣くに泣けない, "can't bring myself to cry"), while ～（よ）うにも～ない more often expresses a physical/practical obstacle (e.g., 連絡しようにも連絡できない, "can't contact them even if I try," due to lacking the means).
- **～たくてもできない** — a simpler, more direct way to say "want to but can't," without the repeated-verb emphasis.
- [**～ようがない**](grammar-ようがない.md) — "there's no way to do X," emphasizing the complete absence of a method, rather than an internal conflict about wanting to do something.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a structure like ～ばできる ("if [condition], then [it] is possible") would instead describe a situation where the action is in fact achievable.

**Example sentences:**
1. 野菜をたくさんもらったが、食べきれない。捨てるに捨てられず困っている。
   (I was given so many vegetables I can't eat them all. I want to throw them out, but I just can't bring myself to — it's a real dilemma.)
2. 子供に大切な書類を汚されて、泣くに泣けない心境だ。
   (My child got an important document dirty, and I'm in a state where I want to cry but can't even do that.)
3. 彼女はあいさつもせずに会社を辞めた。何か言うに言えない事情があったのだろうか。
   (She quit the company without even saying goodbye. Perhaps there was some circumstance she couldn't bring herself to talk about.)
4. 連絡先がわからなくて、連絡しようにも連絡できなかった。
   (I didn't know how to reach them, so even though I wanted to get in touch, there was no way to do it.)
5. 当時、子供がおもちゃを欲しがったが、お金がなくて買ってやろうにも買ってやれなかった。
   (At the time, my child wanted a toy, but we had no money, so even though I wanted to buy it for him, I simply couldn't.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The same verb must appear on both sides of the pattern (in its base/volitional form, then in its potential negative form) — you cannot mix two different verbs. According to the textbook, ～に～ない tends to describe an internal/emotional obstacle, while ～（よ）うにも～ない tends to describe an external/physical obstacle (such as lacking contact information or resources), so pick the sub-pattern that matches the nature of the obstacle being described.
