# だけど {#だけど}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** だけど (no kanji; always written in kana)

**Furigana form:** だけど

**Meaning:** "But, though" — a very casual, colloquial contrastive conjunction.

**Conjugation:** Sentence-initial conjunction, no conjugation needed — attaches at the sentence boundary, connecting the previous sentence/clause to the next. Also frequently attaches directly after a plain-form clause or noun+だ (〜だけど) mid-sentence in casual speech.

**Typical context:** Very casual, colloquial, spoken register. Used constantly among friends, family, and in casual texting, but inappropriate in formal writing, business emails, or polite speech, where でも, けれども, or しかし would be expected instead.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**でも**](grammar-でも.md) — casual spoken "but," slightly more neutral/common than だけど but still informal.
- [**けれども**](grammar-けれども.md) — polite-neutral, spoken/written, considerably softer and more formal than だけど.
- [**しかし**](grammar-しかし.md) — standard written/formal "however"; far more formal than だけど.
- [**だが**](grammar-だが.md) — literary/written, terse; opposite register extreme from だけど despite both being short.
- [**それなのに**](grammar-それなのに.md) — adds an emotional "and yet, despite that" nuance; だけど is plainer and more neutral in tone, just casual.
- [**一方**](grammar-一方.md) — formal structural contrast between two parallel facts; unrelated register to だけど.

**Opposite grammar structures:** そして/また (casual additive conjunctions) oppose だけど structurally as continuation rather than contrast.

**Example sentences:**
1. 行きたいんだけど、お金がないんだよね。
   (I want to go, but I don't have any money.)
2. 彼に聞いたんだけど、全然分からなかった。
   (I asked him, but I didn't understand at all.)
3. もう遅い時間だけど、まだ電気がついてる。
   (It's already late, but the lights are still on.)
4. 美味しそうだけど、ちょっと高くない?
   (It looks delicious, but isn't it a bit expensive?)
5. 頑張ったんだけど、結局うまくいかなかった。
   (I tried hard, but it didn't work out in the end.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes use だけど in essays or polite conversation, where it sounds jarringly casual — けれども or しかし would be the appropriate register swap. Note also that だけど is frequently used not just for pure contrast but as a soft hedge or cushion before a request/opinion (e.g. "いいんだけど、〜" = "it's fine, but..."), a pragmatic softening function that formal equivalents like しかし don't share.
