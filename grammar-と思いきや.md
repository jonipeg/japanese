# ～と思いきや {#と思いきや}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～と思いきや

**Furigana form:** ～とおもいきや

**Meaning:** "Thought/expected X, but actually Y" — expresses a reversal of expectation: the speaker (or a general assumption) anticipated one outcome, but reality turned out differently.

**Conjugation:**
- [動・い形・な形・名] の普通形 + とおもいきや
- Example: 難しいと思いきや、案外簡単だった (thought it'd be hard, but it was surprisingly easy).

**Typical context:** Written/literary register, but also used in spoken storytelling when narrating a surprising turn of events. Politeness-neutral; feels slightly witty or knowing when used well — common in essays, news commentary, and anecdotes.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **かと思うと** — "no sooner had X than Y"; emphasizes quick succession of two events, not necessarily a reversal of expectation.
- **と思ったら** — casual equivalent expressing a similar "expected X, but then Y" surprise; more common in everyday spoken Japanese, while と思いきや sounds more literary/witty.
- [**にもかかわらず**](grammar-にかかわらず・にもかかわらず.md) — "despite X," expresses a contrast/concession but without the "I/we assumed X" framing that と思いきや has.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No single grammatical opposite, but conceptually contrasts with 案の定 ("as expected," confirming rather than reversing an expectation).

**Example sentences:**
1. 1級レベルの漢字テストだから難しいと思いきや、案外簡単でよかった。
   (I thought the kanji test would be hard since it's N1 level, but it turned out to be surprisingly easy.)
2. 彼は怒っていると思いきや、意外と冷静だった。
   (I thought he'd be angry, but he was surprisingly calm.)
3. その店は高級レストランかと思いきや、実はリーズナブルな値段だった。
   (I thought that restaurant would be upscale, but it was actually reasonably priced.)
4. 試合は簡単に勝てると思いきや、まさかの逆転負けを喫した。
   (We thought we'd win easily, but we suffered a shocking come-from-behind loss.)
5. 新入社員はおとなしいと思いきや、会議では積極的に発言していた。
   (I thought the new employee was quiet, but she spoke up actively in the meeting.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** A margin note from the source material gives the synonym gloss 思っていたが、しかし ("thought so, but..."), which is a useful paraphrase check: if a sentence can be restated as "I thought X, but actually Y," と思いきや is likely correct. Students should avoid confusing this with と思ったら, which is more casual and can simply mean "just as I thought X, then Y happened" without as strong a surprise/reversal nuance.
