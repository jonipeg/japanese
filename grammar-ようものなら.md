# ～ようものなら {#ようものなら}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ようものなら

**Furigana form:** ～ようものなら

**Meaning:** "If you dare do X, (something bad) will follow" — a bad-result conditional warning that a certain action, even attempted slightly, will trigger a severe or unwanted consequence.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞意向形（よう／おう） + ものなら + (negative/severe consequence clause)
- Example base: 遅刻する → 遅刻しようものなら

**Typical context:** Written and spoken formal-to-neutral register; often used to describe someone's strict or scary reaction, frequently with a slightly exaggerated or dramatic tone. Appears in both narrative writing and vivid conversational storytelling.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **（辞書形）ものなら** — IMPORTANT: this looks nearly identical but is a COMPLETELY DIFFERENT grammar point, used with potential-form verbs or desire expressions (たい/ほしい) to mean "if only one could... / if it were possible to..." (e.g., できるものなら叶えたい). Despite the shared ものなら shape, ようものなら (volitional form + ものなら) and dictionary/potential-form + ものなら are unrelated in meaning and must be treated as two separate grammar points.
- [**たら最後**](grammar-たら最後・たが最後.md) — "once X happens, that's the end/there's no going back"; similarly describes a severe chain of consequences from a single triggering action, but without the "even daring to try" nuance of ようものなら.
- **うものなら** (same construction, alternate romanization boundary) — identical pattern; no difference, just how it's parsed for different verb groups.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; conceptually 〜ても構わない ("it's fine even if X") expresses the reverse — no bad consequence follows.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女は気が短くて、デートに少しでも遅刻しようものなら、怒って大変なことになるんだ。 (She's short-tempered, so if you're even a little late to a date, she'll get angry and it becomes a huge problem.)
2. 父の前でたばこを吸おうものなら、ひどく叱られるだろう。 (If you dare smoke in front of my father, you'll get scolded badly.)
3. あの上司に意見しようものなら、すぐに左遷されかねない。 (If you dare voice an opinion to that boss, you could easily get demoted on the spot.)
4. 子どものころ、宿題を忘れようものなら、母にひどく怒られたものだ。 (As a child, if I ever dared forget my homework, my mother would get furious at me.)
5. このマンションで夜中に騒ごうものなら、すぐに苦情が来る。 (If you dare make noise in this apartment building at night, complaints will come immediately.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This is explicitly flagged in the source as a common point of confusion: ようものなら (volitional form + ものなら) means "if you dare do X, bad things follow," while the superficially similar dictionary/potential-form + ものなら (e.g., 行けるものなら行きたい) means "if only it were possible to X." These look nearly identical on the page but are grammatically and semantically unrelated — do not merge them into one flashcard, and always check whether the verb before ものなら is in volitional form (→ ようものなら, bad-consequence warning) or dictionary/potential form (→ ものなら, wistful "if only").
