# さすが {#さすが}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** 流石

**Furigana form:** さすが

**Meaning:** "As one would expect (of X) / that's X for you" — an expression of admiration acknowledging that someone or something has lived up to its reputation, skill, or expected high standard.

**Conjugation:**
- さすが + 名詞 (+は) (admiring a person/thing directly): さすが田中さんですね
- さすがに + 動詞/形容詞 (admiring a quality or behavior, or expressing "even so/still"): さすがに疲れた
- さすが(に)〜だけあって: さすがプロだけあって、仕上がりが違う
- Example: さすが彼は経験が豊富だ (As expected of him, he's very experienced)

**Typical context:** Used in both spoken and written Japanese, neutral-to-admiring register. Carries a tone of genuine praise or impressed acknowledgment, implying "this result/behavior matches what I'd expect given X's reputation/status/skill." Note the related but distinct usage さすがに, which can mean "even so" or "to be expected to a point, but..." when followed by a negative or a statement about reaching a limit (e.g. さすがに疲れた, "even I get tired [after all that]").

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**〜だけあって**](grammar-だけあって.md) — "precisely because of X," a similar structure used to explain that a praiseworthy result follows naturally from someone's background or qualification, often combined with さすが (さすが〜だけあって) for extra emphasis.
- **やはり/やっぱり** — "as expected / after all," can overlap when confirming an expectation was met, but lacks the admiring, complimentary tone specific to さすが — やはり is neutral confirmation, while さすが implies praise.
- **さすがに** — closely related variant that can shift toward meaning "even so / to be expected, but there's a limit," often in sentences expressing mild exasperation or a breaking point (e.g. さすがに腹が立った, "even I got angry at that point").
- **感心する** — "to be impressed," expresses the same admiring sentiment as a verb rather than an exclamatory adverb, usable in more descriptive/narrative contexts.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite grammatical structure exists, but がっかりする ("to be disappointed") expresses the reverse sentiment — a letdown rather than admiration that expectations were met.

**Example sentences:**
1. さすが田中さんだ、どんな質問にもすぐ答えられる。
   (That's Tanaka for you — he can answer any question immediately.)
2. さすがプロの料理人だけあって、味付けが絶妙だ。
   (As expected of a professional chef, the seasoning is exquisite.)
3. 三日間も徹夜して、さすがに疲れた。
   (After three straight nights without sleep, even I got tired.)
4. さすがは一流大学、学生のレベルが高い。
   (As you'd expect from a top university, the students' level is high.)
5. いくら仲がいいとはいえ、さすがにそれは失礼じゃない?
   (No matter how close you two are, isn't that still a bit rude, even so?)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes use さすが in situations with no genuine basis for admiration, which can sound sarcastic or odd — さすが always implies the subject has a known reputation, skill, or status that the current behavior/result is living up to. Another common confusion is between plain さすが (pure admiration: "as expected of X, impressive") and さすがに (which often signals a tipping point reached despite high tolerance, frequently followed by a negative or a complaint, e.g. "even I couldn't stand that"). Context and what follows the phrase help distinguish the two nuances.
