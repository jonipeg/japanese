# ～はさておき {#はさておき}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～はさておき

**Furigana form:** ～はさておき *(no kanji; conventionally written in kana)*

**Meaning:** "Setting X aside" / "leaving X for later" — used to table a topic temporarily in order to move on to another, usually more pressing, topic.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 (noun) + はさておき
- Functions as a discourse-connector, typically opening a new sentence or clause that shifts the conversation topic.

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal, usable in both spoken and written Japanese (business meetings, casual conversation among adults, essays). Not rude, but implies a deliberate, sometimes abrupt pivot away from a topic — can feel slightly brusque if used to cut off small talk.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**をなおざりにして**](grammar-をなおざりにして.md) — means "neglecting X"; implies ongoing carelessness toward X rather than a deliberate, temporary tabling of the topic for later discussion.
- [**ならまだしも**](grammar-ならまだしも.md) — means "if it were just X, that would be bearable, but..."; a contrastive-tolerance construction, not a topic-deferral one.
- [**こそすれ**](grammar-こそすれ.md) — means "if anything, X (never Y)," a concessive-emphatic construction; unrelated in function to topic-shifting.

**Opposite grammar structures:** A conceptual opposite would be a phrase that brings a topic to the fore rather than setting it aside, such as ～について言えば ("speaking of X") or ～はもちろん ("X is of course [included]"), which keep the topic in focus rather than deferring it. No single fixed "direct opposite" pattern exists.

**Example sentences:**
1. 挨拶はさておき、例の件ですが…
   (Setting greetings aside, regarding that matter...)
2. 冗談はさておき、今日の会議の本題に入りましょう。
   (Jokes aside, let's get into the main topic of today's meeting.)
3. 味の好みはさておき、この店の接客は本当に素晴らしい。
   (Setting taste preferences aside, this restaurant's customer service is truly wonderful.)
4. 予算の問題はさておき、まずはデザイン案を決めましょう。
   (Setting the budget issue aside for now, let's first decide on the design proposal.)
5. 結果はさておき、彼が全力を尽くしたことは間違いない。
   (Setting the result aside, there's no doubt he gave it his all.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Confused on graded tests with をなおざりにして and ならまだしも/こそすれ. The key nuance students must grasp: はさておき is a conversational pivot device — it doesn't criticize or evaluate X, it simply defers discussion of X to focus on something else right now. This differs from をなおざりにして, which implies X is being wrongfully ignored/neglected (a criticism), and from ならまだしも, which sets up a tolerance threshold comparison rather than a topic shift.
