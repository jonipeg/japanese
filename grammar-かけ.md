# ～かけ {#かけ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～かけ（～かけの/～かけだ/～かける）

**Furigana form:** ～かけ

**Meaning:** "In the middle of doing ~ / half-done / partway through" — indicates an action was started but left incomplete or interrupted partway through.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ます形 (verb stem) + かけ — 例: 食べかけ (half-eaten), 読みかけ (partway read)
- 動詞ます形 + かけの + 名詞 — 例: 食べかけのパン (half-eaten bread)
- 動詞ます形 + かけだ — 例: まだ読みかけだ (I'm still only partway through reading)
- 動詞ます形 + かける — 例: 言いかけてやめた (started to say it, then stopped)

**Typical context:** Both spoken and written, neutral register — very common in daily conversation to describe unfinished tasks or objects left mid-use.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **ている途中** — "in the middle of doing," more explicit/verbose way of saying the same thing.
- [**つつある**](grammar-つつある.md) — "in the process of (gradually changing)," more formal and used for ongoing trends/changes rather than a single interrupted task.
- **かかる** (related but distinct) — "about to happen / on the verge of," e.g. 死にかかる, close in form but shifts toward "nearly" rather than "half-done."

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though ～終わる ("finish doing ~") contrasts as the completed counterpart.

**Example sentences:**
1. テーブルの上に食べかけのケーキが置いてある。
   (There's a half-eaten cake on the table.)
2. この本はまだ読みかけで、最後まで読んでいない。
   (I'm still only partway through this book; I haven't read it to the end.)
3. 彼は何か言いかけて、急に黙ってしまった。
   (He started to say something, then suddenly fell silent.)
4. やりかけの仕事があるので、先に帰れない。
   (I have unfinished work, so I can't leave before it's done.)
5. 飲みかけのコーヒーがまだ机の上にある。
   (The half-drunk coffee is still on the desk.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** かけ strictly implies the action was interrupted/abandoned partway, not simply "in progress" — learners sometimes confuse it with ～ている (ongoing action), but 食べている means "currently eating" while 食べかけ means "left half-eaten, action currently paused."
