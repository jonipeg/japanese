# ～ったらない {#ったらない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ったらない

**Furigana form:** ～ったらない

**Meaning:** An extremely emphatic degree expression meaning "indescribably/to an extreme degree" — used to stress that a feeling, state, or quality is so intense it defies ordinary description, usually about something striking, extreme, or exasperating.

**Conjugation:**
- い形容詞語幹 + といったらない / ったらない: 嬉しいといったらない / 嬉しいったらない (indescribably happy)
- な形容詞語幹 + といったらない: 不便といったらない (indescribably inconvenient)
- 名詞 + といったらない: ばかさ加減といったらない (the sheer stupidity is beyond words)
- Closely related variant: **いったらない** — functionally the same construction, more often written with the full と言ったら(ない) spelled out (societal/dictionary citation form), while ったらない is the common contracted spoken form after an adjective stem.
- Example: この部屋の汚さといったらない (The filthiness of this room is beyond words)

**Typical context:** Spoken, colloquial, emotionally expressive register — often used when venting strong feelings (frustration, admiration, disgust, delight) about something that exceeds normal bounds. Carries a vivid, almost theatrical emphasis, common in casual conversation and sometimes in written narrative for dramatic effect, but rarely in formal/business writing.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**いったらない**](grammar-いったらない・ありゃしない.md) — the fuller, un-contracted variant of the same ～ったらない cluster, used interchangeably, more often seen in writing or when quoting the full と言ったら construction.
- [**てたまらない**](grammar-てたまらない・しようがない・しかたがない.md) — "unbearably X," similarly expresses an intense feeling, but focuses on a feeling the speaker is currently experiencing and can barely tolerate, rather than describing an extreme quality as indescribable from an outside/narrative perspective.
- [**極まりない**](grammar-極まる・極まりない.md) — "extremely X, in the extreme," a more formal/written way to express an extreme degree, often in slightly archaic or literary register, without the emotionally charged, colloquial flavor of ったらない.
- [**といったら**](grammar-というと・といえば・といったら.md) — the base pattern without ない, meaning "when it comes to X" or introducing a topic with emphasis, which can also independently convey strong feeling but is less fixed as a pure intensifier than ったらない.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite grammatical structure, though 〜程度だ ("to a moderate degree") would express the contrasting idea of something being unremarkable or within normal bounds, rather than indescribably extreme.

**Example sentences:**
1. 久しぶりに親友に会えて、嬉しいったらない。
   (Being able to see my best friend after so long — I'm indescribably happy.)
2. この渋滞の酷さといったらない、もう一時間も動いていない。
   (The severity of this traffic jam is beyond words — we haven't moved in an hour.)
3. 彼の態度の悪さったらない、客への態度とは思えない。
   (His attitude is unbelievably bad — you'd never guess he's supposed to be serving customers.)
4. あの景色の美しさといったらなかった。
   (The beauty of that scenery was simply indescribable.)
5. 試験に落ちたと分かったときの悔しさったらなかった。
   (The frustration I felt upon learning I'd failed the exam was beyond description.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes mistake ったらない for a simple negative form (because of the trailing ない) and think it negates the preceding adjective — in fact, the ない here does not negate anything; the whole ～ったらない chunk functions as an intensifier meaning "to an extreme, indescribable degree." Also, remember that ったらない and いったらない belong to the same expression cluster and are interchangeable; the difference is purely about how contracted/colloquial the form is, not about meaning.
