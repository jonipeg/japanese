# ～かれ…かれ {#かれ…かれ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～かれ…かれ

**Furigana form:** ～かれ…かれ

**Meaning:** "Whether very A or very B, the same thing is true" — pairs two antonymous adjective stems to say that the conclusion holds regardless of degree or direction (e.g. "more or less," "for better or worse," "sooner or later").

**Conjugation:**
- 形容詞語幹 + かれ + （対になる形容詞語幹）+ かれ　※例外: 悪い → 悪しかれ
- 多かれ少なかれ — "more or less"

**Typical context:** Set, somewhat formal expressions found in essays, speeches, and everyday conversation when generalizing about something regardless of degree — not freely productive beyond a handful of fixed pairs.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～にせよ～にせよ** — "whether A or B," a broader concessive pattern that can pair nouns and verbs, not just antonymous adjective stems.
- **～であれ～であれ** — similar concessive meaning ("whether X or Y"), more literary/formal in tone.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; asserting one specific condition rather than covering both extremes would use a plain conditional like ～ば or ～なら instead of the paired concessive form.

**Example sentences:**
1. 多かれ少なかれ、みんな悩みを抱えている。
   (Everyone has worries, to a greater or lesser degree.)
2. 良かれ悪しかれ、親の考えは保守的だ。
   (For better or worse, parents' thinking tends to be conservative.)
3. 早かれ遅かれ、真実は明らかになるだろう。
   (Sooner or later, the truth is bound to come out.)
4. 遠かれ近かれ、いつかは故郷に帰るつもりだ。
   (Sooner or later, I intend to return to my hometown.)
5. 多かれ少なかれ、誰でも他人の意見に影響される。
   (To a greater or lesser degree, everyone is influenced by other people's opinions.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern only works with a small closed set of antonymous adjective pairs (多い／少ない, 良い／悪い, 早い／遅い, 遠い／近い) — you cannot freely substitute any pair of adjectives into it. Also note the irregular stem 悪しかれ (not 悪くかれ) when 悪い is one of the pair, and that対立する意味の形容詞 (contrasting adjectives) must be used together, never the same adjective twice.
