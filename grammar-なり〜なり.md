# ～なり～なり {#なり~なり}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**1. Kanji version:** ～なり～なり (no kanji form; purely grammatical kana word)

**2. Furigana version:** ～なり～なり

**3. Meaning:** Either X or Y — whichever one chooses; a non-exhaustive, illustrative listing of options/alternatives.

**4. Conjugation (connection form):** 動詞辞書形 + なり、動詞辞書形 + なり (also usable with nouns: 名詞 + なり、名詞 + なり)

**5. Usage context:** Written and spoken, neutral-to-formal register. Commonly used when giving advice or suggestions about possible courses of action.

**6. Similar grammar structures:**
- つ〜つ — describes two actions happening alternately or simultaneously (e.g. 行きつ戻りつ "going back and forth"); a completely different meaning from offering a choice. Confirmed as a real student wrong answer (picked つ〜つ instead of なり〜なり).
- や〜や — lists several examples/instances more loosely, without the "pick one" nuance of なり〜なり; more casual.
- といい〜といい — cites two examples to support an evaluative claim ("whether you look at X or Y, both are great"), NOT a choice between actions. This is the single most confirmed recurring confusion for なり〜なり in the source data — one student mixed these up in both directions across two different tests.
- と〜まいと — means "whether or not X" (a different concessive structure), unrelated to offering parallel choices.

**7. Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**8. Example sentences:**
1. 分からないときは、先生に聞くなり、辞書を引くなりしてください。(When you don't understand something, either ask the teacher or look it up in a dictionary.)
2. 疲れたら、休憩するなり、早く帰るなりすればいい。(If you're tired, you can either take a break or go home early.)
3. 不満があるなら、直接話すなり、メールを送るなりして伝えたほうがいい。(If you have a complaint, you should either talk directly or send an email to communicate it.)
4. 暇な時間には、本を読むなり、映画を見るなりして過ごしている。(In my free time, I spend it either reading books or watching movies.)
5. 困ったことがあれば、友達に相談するなり、専門家に聞くなりしてください。(If you run into trouble, please either consult a friend or ask an expert.)

**9. Common mistakes / key nuances:** Confirmed recurring real-world confusion with といい〜といい — remember なり〜なり presents a CHOICE between two or more actions ("do either A or B, whichever"), while といい〜といい offers two supporting EXAMPLES for an evaluative statement about something as a whole, with no element of choice. Also distinguish from つ〜つ, which describes repeated alternating/simultaneous actions by a single subject rather than a list of options.
