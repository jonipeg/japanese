# ～向け {#向け}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～向け

**Furigana form:** ～むけ

**Meaning:** "Targeted at X," "intended for X (audience/market)" — indicates that something was deliberately designed, produced, or aimed at a specific group.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + 向け(の) + 名詞: 子供向けの番組 (a program intended for children)
- Can also attach directly to verbs of targeting: 海外向けに輸出する (export targeted at overseas markets)
- このアプリは初心者向けに作られた。(This app was made targeted at beginners.)

**Typical context:** Spoken and written, neutral-to-formal register. Very common in marketing, product descriptions, media classifications, and business contexts.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**向き**](grammar-向き.md) — "naturally suited for," describes inherent fit rather than deliberate targeting (contrast pair with 向け).
- **を対象とした** — "aimed at," more formal/explicit phrase with identical meaning.
- **用** — "for use in X," a more compact noun-attaching suffix (例: 子供用).

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct single opposite; general-purpose/unspecified audience would simply omit 向け (e.g., 一般向け "general audience" is itself a form of 向け, so there isn't a true grammatical opposite).

**Example sentences:**
1. この製品は海外向けに開発された。
   (This product was developed targeted at overseas markets.)
2. 女性向けのファッション雑誌が人気だ。
   (Fashion magazines targeted at women are popular.)
3. 上級者向けの教材は内容が難しい。
   (Teaching materials intended for advanced learners have difficult content.)
4. この映画は子供向けではなく、大人向けの内容だ。
   (This movie isn't intended for children — the content is intended for adults.)
5. 企業は若者向けの新しいサービスを始めた。
   (The company started a new service targeted at young people.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 向け emphasizes deliberate design/targeting by a producer or provider, while 向き emphasizes natural/inherent suitability regardless of intent. For example, 日本語学習者向けの教科書 (a textbook made FOR Japanese learners) uses 向け because it was designed with that purpose; don't substitute 向き here, as it would wrongly imply the textbook simply "happens to suit" learners rather than being purpose-built.
