# ～べくして～ {#べくして～}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～べくして～

**Furigana form:** ～べくして～

**Meaning:** "X happened because it was bound/destined to happen" — emphasizes, in hindsight, that an outcome was the natural, inevitable result of what preceded it, not mere chance.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形 + べくして + 同じ動詞のた形
- 勝つべくして勝った — "won because victory was the natural, inevitable result"

**Typical context:** Written, formal/literary register; used in commentary, essays, sports analysis, and reflections on outcomes (accidents, victories, historical events) where the writer is analyzing causes after the fact.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **当然の結果として** — "as a natural consequence," a looser paraphrase without the fixed repeated-verb structure.
- **起こるべくして起こる** — the most common fixed collocation of this pattern itself, worth memorizing as a set phrase.

**Opposite grammar structures:** 思いがけず～した ("X happened unexpectedly") contrasts directly with the inevitability this pattern asserts.

**Example sentences:**
1. 我々は勝つべくして勝った。
   (We won because victory was the inevitable result.)
2. 事故は起きるべくして起きた。
   (The accident happened because it was bound to happen.)
3. 彼の成功は成功するべくして成功したと言える。
   (His success can be said to be success that was destined to happen.)
4. このチームの優勝は、優勝するべくして優勝したものだ。
   (This team's championship win was a victory that was bound to happen.)
5. 長年の努力があったからこそ、彼は合格するべくして合格した。
   (Thanks to his years of effort, his passing the exam was an inevitable result.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The same verb must appear in both halves of the pattern (Xべくして、Xた) — mixing different verbs is ungrammatical. The pattern frames an outcome as foreseeable given its prior causes, so it's used in hindsight analysis rather than prediction about the future.
