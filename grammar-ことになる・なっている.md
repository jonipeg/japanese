# ～ことになる／なっている {#ことになる}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ことになる／ことになっている

**Furigana form:** ～ことになる／ことになっている (no special kanji reading)

**Meaning:** "It has been decided that X (by circumstance or others, not the speaker's own will)" (ことになる); "It is the rule/custom that X" (ことになっている) — describes decisions, arrangements, or established rules, often ones the speaker had little direct control over.

**Conjugation:**
- Verb (dictionary form / ない form) + ことになる
- Verb (dictionary form) + ことになっている (ongoing rule/custom)
- 来月、大阪に転勤することになった (It's been decided that I'll transfer to Osaka next month)
- ここでは靴を脱ぐことになっている (The rule here is that you take off your shoes)

**Typical context:** Common in both spoken and written Japanese for announcing decisions (often workplace/school related) in a modest, indirect way that avoids claiming personal agency. ことになっている is used for standing rules/conventions.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **ことにする** — "I decide to do X" (speaker's own active decision), contrasts with ことになる's passive/external nuance.
- **はずだ** — expresses expectation/supposition rather than a decided fact or rule.
- **ようになる** — describes a gradual change in state/ability, not a decision or rule.

**Opposite grammar structures:** **ことにする** is the active-decision counterpart (speaker decides) versus ことになる (decision made by circumstances/others).

**Example sentences:**
1. 来年、海外支社で働くことになりました。
   (It has been decided that I will work at the overseas branch next year.)
2. このクラスでは、毎週日本語で日記を書くことになっている。
   (In this class, the rule is that we write a diary in Japanese every week.)
3. 会議は来週の月曜日に延期することになった。
   (It has been decided that the meeting will be postponed to next Monday.)
4. この寮では、夜11時以降は外出禁止ということになっている。
   (In this dormitory, it is the rule that going out after 11 PM is prohibited.)
5. 彼と結婚することになったと聞いて驚いた。
   (I was surprised to hear that it had been decided she would marry him.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often confuse ことになる (externally decided) with ことにする (actively decided by oneself) — using the wrong one changes the nuance of agency. ことになっている specifically marks standing rules/customs, not one-time decisions.
