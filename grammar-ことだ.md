# ～ことだ {#ことだ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ことだ

**Furigana form:** ～ことだ (no special kanji reading)

**Meaning:** "The (best) thing to do is...; you should..." — used to give advice, strong recommendations, or admonitions based on the speaker's judgment of what's necessary or wise.

**Conjugation:**
- Verb (dictionary form) + ことだ → affirmative advice
- Verb (ない form) + ことだ → negative advice ("you shouldn't...")
- 合格したいなら、毎日勉強することだ (If you want to pass, you should study every day)

**Typical context:** Spoken, somewhat direct/blunt advice-giving, often from someone in an authoritative or experienced position (teacher, senior, parent) to someone younger/less experienced. Can sound preachy if misused with equals.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**べきだ**](grammar-べきだ.md) — "should," more normative/moralistic judgment rather than practical advice.
- **たほうがいい** — softer, more casual suggestion.
- [**ものだ**](grammar-ものだ.md) — expresses general truths/customs rather than direct personal advice.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though ～なくてもいい ("you don't have to...") contrasts by removing the obligation.

**Example sentences:**
1. 健康でいたいなら、毎日運動することだ。
   (If you want to stay healthy, you should exercise every day.)
2. そんなに心配なら、自分で確認することだ。
   (If you're so worried, you should check it yourself.)
3. 上司に何か言われても、気にしないことだ。
   (Even if your boss says something, you shouldn't let it bother you.)
4. 新しい言語を覚えたいなら、毎日少しずつ練習することだ。
   (If you want to learn a new language, you should practice a little every day.)
5. 失敗を恐れず、まず挑戦してみることだ。
   (Don't be afraid of failure — the thing to do is just try.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ことだ sounds like direct advice/command and can come across as condescending if used toward superiors or strangers; it's best used among close relationships or in general advice-giving contexts (e.g., self-help writing, teacher-to-student).
