# ～ても／でもさしつかえない {#てもさしつかえない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ても／でも差し支えない

**Furigana form:** ～ても／でもさしつかえない

**Meaning:** "It's fine/no problem even if X" — politely grants permission or reassures that an action causes no inconvenience or issue.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞て形 + も差し支えない
- 名詞／な形容詞 + でも差し支えない
- Example base: 捨てる → 捨てても差し支えない

**Typical context:** Formal, polite register; common in business writing, instructions, and formal permission-granting. More formal/polite than casual てもいい.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **てもいい** — plain "it's okay to do X"; the everyday, casual-to-neutral equivalent, while ても差し支えない is noticeably more formal and polite, often used in written instructions or professional contexts.
- **ても構わない** — "it's fine even if X"; similar politeness level and meaning, largely interchangeable, though 差し支えない leans slightly more toward formal/written register and 構わない slightly more toward spoken reassurance.
- **には及ばない** — "there's no need to X"; different nuance (declining an unnecessary action) rather than granting permission for an optional one.

**Opposite grammar structures:** てはいけない／てはならない ("must not do X") expresses the reverse — that the action is prohibited or problematic.

**Example sentences:**
1. その資料は捨てても差し支えありませんよ。 (It's fine to throw that document away.)
2. この席にお座りいただいても差し支えございません。 (It's perfectly fine for you to sit in this seat.)
3. 多少遅れても差し支えないので、ごゆっくりどうぞ。 (It's no problem even if you're a bit late, so please take your time.)
4. この件は明日回答しても差し支えないでしょうか。 (Would it be all right if I respond to this matter tomorrow?)
5. 簡単な内容でしたら、電話でお伝えいただいても差し支えありません。 (If it's a simple matter, it's fine for you to let me know by phone.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 差し支える literally means "to interfere/cause trouble," so 差し支えない literally reads as "causes no interference" — learners should recognize this as a polite permission-granting phrase, not a statement about the action's quality. It is frequently used in polite questions (～ても差し支えないでしょうか) to ask permission formally, a common business-Japanese pattern worth drilling alongside the plain statement form.
