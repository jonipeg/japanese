# ～を前提に {#を前提に}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～を前提に

**Furigana form:** ～を前提(ぜんてい)に

**Meaning:** "On the premise/assumption of X" — states that a subsequent plan, action, or proposal is based on X as its foundational condition or assumption.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 (noun) + を前提に
- Often extended as を前提として or を前提とした + noun, functioning adverbially to modify the following clause or noun phrase.

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal, common in both spoken and written Japanese — frequently used in business, legal, and relationship contexts (e.g., proposing marriage "on the premise of marriage," discussing contract terms). Not rude; a straightforward, businesslike register.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**にかかっては**](grammar-にかかっては.md) — means "when it comes to X('s hands/skill/power)"; the source material explicitly confirms this was tested as a confusion pair (Q12), where a student picked にかかっては instead of を前提に. The key difference: を前提に establishes the foundational assumption or basis for a plan, while にかかっては describes an outcome determined by someone's skill or influence — they answer fundamentally different questions ("what's the basis for this plan?" vs. "whose hands/skill determines this outcome?").
- [**にかまけて**](grammar-にかまけて.md) — means "being preoccupied with/too busy with X (to do Y)"; an unrelated meaning about distraction, not about establishing a premise.
- **ならいざ知らず** — means "that may be one thing, but..." (contrastive concession); unrelated structurally and semantically to premise-setting.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct single opposite, though ～にかかわらず ("regardless of X") or ～を度外視して ("disregarding X") conceptually invert the idea of X being a necessary foundational condition. No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 「弘美さん、結婚を前提に僕と付き合ってください！」「それはちょっと…」
   ("Hiromi, please date me with marriage as the premise!" "Well, that's a bit...")
2. この契約は双方の合意を前提に成立するものです。
   (This contract is established on the premise of mutual agreement from both parties.)
3. 彼は日本への永住を前提に、日本語の勉強を続けている。
   (He continues studying Japanese on the premise of permanently settling in Japan.)
4. 当社はお客様のご要望を前提に、商品開発を行っております。
   (Our company carries out product development on the premise of our customers' requests.)
5. このプロジェクトは来年度の予算確保を前提に進められている。
   (This project is proceeding on the premise that next year's budget will be secured.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The source material confirms a graded-test confusion pair (Q12): students mixed up を前提に with にかかっては. The essential nuance: を前提に always answers "what is the underlying assumption/basis for this plan or statement?", whereas にかかっては answers "whose hands, skill, or influence determines this outcome?" Also listed as potentially confused with にかまけて and ならいざ知らず, though these are less semantically close — students should simply verify whether the sentence is setting up a foundational condition for a plan (を前提に) versus describing distraction (にかまけて) or contrastive concession (ならいざ知らず).
