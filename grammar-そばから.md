# ～そばから {#そばから}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～そばから

**Furigana form:** ～そばから *(no kanji; written entirely in kana)*

**Meaning:** "No sooner does X happen than Y happens" — expresses that Y occurs immediately after X, often with a nuance that the effort put into X is immediately undone or repeated endlessly (commonly negative, cyclical, frustrating actions).

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形 (verb dictionary form) + そばから
- 動詞た形 (verb た-form) + そばから
- Both forms are attested; the dictionary form emphasizes habitual/repeated immediacy, the た form emphasizes a completed action immediately undone.

**Typical context:** Formal, written/literary N1-level grammar. Can appear in spoken complaints about repetitive, exasperating situations (e.g., a parent describing a child's behavior), but the construction itself feels bookish and is tested heavily on JLPT N1. No strong politeness/rudeness connotation — it's register-neutral-to-formal, used to vividly describe an endless frustrating cycle.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**がてら**](grammar-がてら.md) — means "while doing X, also do Y" (combining two purposes in one trip); unlike そばから, there's no "immediately undone" nuance — がてら is about convenience/efficiency, not futility.
- [**かたがた**](grammar-かたがた.md) — similar to がてら, "doing Y for the purpose of/along with X" (often formal greetings, like "お礼かたがた"); it's about combining a secondary purpose, not about rapid repeated negation.
- **かのごとく** — means "as if/as though," a simile marker; completely different meaning (comparison, not temporal immediacy).
- [**ながらに**](grammar-ながらに.md) — means "while in the state of X" (e.g., 生まれながらに "from birth"); describes a persisting state, not an immediate repeated undoing.
- [**こととて**](grammar-こととて.md) — means "since it's a matter of X" (an excuse/reason construction); no temporal immediacy sense at all.
- [**のみならず**](grammar-のみならず.md) — means "not only X but also Y"; purely additive, unrelated to the immediate-repetition nuance of そばから.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct grammatical opposite exists, but conceptually contrasts with patterns like ～てはじめて ("only after X do you realize Y," implying a delay rather than immediacy). No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. この子は片付けたそばから、おもちゃを広げてしまう。
   (As soon as this child tidies up, he spreads his toys out again.)
2. 最近は年のせいか、人の名前を聞いたそばから忘れてしまう。
   (Maybe because of my age lately, I forget people's names the moment I hear them.)
3. 教室を掃除したそばから、生徒たちがまた汚してしまう。
   (No sooner do I clean the classroom than the students mess it up again.)
4. 新しい法律ができるそばから、抜け道を探す人が現れる。
   (As soon as a new law is made, people appear looking for loopholes.)
5. 母は皿を洗うそばから、父がまた汚れた皿を置いていく。
   (The moment my mother washes a dish, my father puts down another dirty one.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** On graded JLPT N1 tests, そばから is confused with がてら, かたがた, かのごとく, ながらに, こととて, and のみならず — all of these can superficially look like "conjunction-ish" grammar patterns to unprepared students, but none of them carry the "immediate, repeated, often futile" nuance that defines そばから. A key tell: そばから sentences almost always describe a cyclical, exasperating pattern (tidying → mess, learning → forgetting), not a single one-off event. Students should check whether the sentence implies repetition/futility before choosing this pattern.
