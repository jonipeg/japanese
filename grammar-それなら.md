# それなら {#それなら}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** それなら

**Furigana form:** それなら

**Meaning:** "If that's the case, then; in that case" — a conditional/suppositional conjunction that takes the preceding statement as a premise and draws a practical conclusion or suggestion from it.

**Conjugation:** Sentence-initial conjunction, no conjugation needed — attaches at the sentence boundary, connecting the previous statement (treated as a given premise) to the conclusion, suggestion, or question that follows. It is almost always used sentence-initially as a reactive response to something just said, rather than linking clauses within a single sentence.

**Typical context:** Neutral, usable in both casual and polite spoken conversation as well as in writing — extremely common in dialogue when reacting practically to new information ("Oh, if that's the case, then let's..."). It is conversational and responsive by nature, slightly more common in speech than in formal written argumentation, but is not restricted to casual register; it works fine in polite conversation with more formal phrasing attached (それなら、〜いたしましょうか).

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**それでも**](grammar-それでも.md) — its commonly confused counterpart: それなら is 条件 (conditional — "given the preceding as a premise, here's what follows"), while それでも is 逆接 (concessive — "despite the preceding, X is still true"); both react to a preceding statement, but in opposite logical directions.
- **では/それでは** — transitional "well then," used to move to a new action/topic in general; それなら is more specifically conditional, directly tying its suggestion/conclusion to the content of the preceding statement as a premise, rather than simply transitioning to a next step.
- **だったら** — a near-synonym, casual spoken equivalent of それなら, used interchangeably in informal conversation ("if that's so, then...").
- **そういうことなら** — a more explicit, slightly more formal variant meaning "if that's the situation/matter," often used when summarizing a more complex preceding explanation before responding.

**Opposite grammar structures:** Its natural opposite-category counterpart is それでも, the concessive conjunction: それなら draws a conclusion that follows from accepting the preceding statement as a premise (condition), while それでも asserts something is true despite the preceding statement (concession).

**Example sentences:**
1. A: 明日は雨らしいよ。 B: それなら、予定を変更しよう。
   (A: It seems it'll rain tomorrow. B: If that's the case, let's change our plans.)
2. A: もう切符を買っちゃった。 B: それなら、一緒に行くしかないね。
   (A: I already bought the ticket. B: In that case, we have no choice but to go together.)
3. A: 彼はアレルギーがあるんです。 B: それなら、別のメニューを用意しましょう。
   (A: He has allergies. B: If that's so, let's prepare a different menu.)
4. A: 予算が足りないんです。 B: それなら、規模を縮小しましょうか。
   (A: We don't have enough budget. B: In that case, shall we scale down the project?)
5. A: 今日は体調が悪くて。 B: それなら、無理しないで休んでいいよ。
   (A: I'm not feeling well today. B: If that's the case, it's fine for you to rest without pushing yourself.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The most common mistake is confusing それなら with それでも, since both react directly to a preceding statement — but それなら treats the preceding statement as new information to act on practically ("given that, here's my suggestion/conclusion"), while それでも treats it as an obstacle that doesn't change the outcome ("despite that, nothing changes"). For example, 「疲れている。それなら休んだら?」("You're tired? Then why not rest?") is a practical suggestion based on the premise, whereas 「疲れている。それでも頑張る」("I'm tired. Even so, I'll keep going") pushes through despite it — mixing these up reverses the intended logical relationship entirely. Learners should also note だったら is a very close casual synonym, often used more frequently than それなら in relaxed daily speech.
