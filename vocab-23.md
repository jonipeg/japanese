## セール (セール)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a sale (discount sale, especially end-of-year or seasonal)

**Similar words:**
- [割引](vocab-36.md#割引-わりびき)（わりびき）— "discount"; refers to the price reduction itself, whereas セール refers to the sales event as a whole.
- バーゲン — another loanword for "bargain sale," largely interchangeable with セール though slightly more old-fashioned.

**Opposite words:**
- 定価（ていか）— regular/list price

**Example sentences:**
1. デパートで冬のセールが始まった。— The winter sale started at the department store.
2. セール品なので返品できません。— Since it's a sale item, it can't be returned.
3. セール期間中にたくさん買い物をした。— I did a lot of shopping during the sale period.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** セール refers to the sale event/period itself (タイムセール, 冬のセール), not an individual act of "selling" something — for "to sell," use 売る, not セールする (which isn't standard usage).

## 背負う (せおう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to carry on one's back; to be burdened/charged with (a responsibility, debt)

**Similar words:**
- 担う（になう）— "to bear, carry (a role/responsibility)"; more formal and abstract, often for duties or missions, whereas 背負う keeps a vivid physical "on the back" image even when used figuratively.
- [抱える](vocab-12.md#抱える-かかえる)（かかえる）— "to hold/carry in one's arms," used for burdens held in front (problems, debts) rather than the back.

**Opposite words:**
- 下ろす（おろす）— to put down, set down

**Example sentences:**
1. 子供をおんぶして背負って歩いた。— I walked while carrying my child piggyback on my back.
2. 彼は家族の生活を背負っている。— He bears the burden of supporting his family.
3. 多額の借金を背負うことになった。— I ended up shouldering a large debt.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 背負う vividly combines the literal (carrying a backpack/child on one's back) and figurative (shouldering responsibility or debt) senses — English "carry" doesn't capture the specifically "on the back" physicality that colors the figurative use too.

## 世界 (せかい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** the world

**Similar words:**
- 地球（ちきゅう）— "Earth," the physical planet, whereas 世界 is broader, including abstract senses ("the world of music," "the business world").
- [社会](vocab-20.md#社会-しゃかい)（しゃかい）— "society"; refers to human community/social structures, a narrower concept than the all-encompassing 世界.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 世界中を旅してみたい。— I want to travel all around the world.
2. これは世界的に有名な作品だ。— This is a world-famous work.
3. 彼はアニメの世界に夢中だ。— He's absorbed in the world of anime.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 世界 is used figuratively for specific "worlds" or realms (音楽の世界 = the world of music, ファッションの世界 = the fashion world), a usage broader than the literal planet sense of 地球 — don't default to 地球 when discussing an abstract "world of X."

## 倅 (せがれ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** one's (own) son (humble, old-fashioned term)

**Similar words:**
- 息子（むすこ）— the standard, neutral word for "son"; せがれ is humble/self-deprecating, traditionally used by a father referring to his own son when speaking to others.
- 愚息（ぐそく）— an even more formal/humble written term for "my foolish son," used in very formal correspondence.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. うちのせがれがお世話になっております。— My son has been in your care (humble expression).
2. せがれはまだ学生です。— My son is still a student.
3. 息子ではなく、年配の人は「せがれ」と言うことがある。— Older people sometimes say "segare" instead of "musuko."

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** せがれ is a humble, somewhat old-fashioned term used exclusively by a parent (traditionally the father) referring to their own son to others — never used to refer to someone else's son, and sounds dated/rustic if used by younger speakers today.

## 咳 (せき)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a cough

**Similar words:**
- [くしゃみ](vocab-16.md#くしゃみ) — "a sneeze," a different but related cold/allergy symptom often mentioned alongside 咳.
- 咳払い（せきばらい）— "a clearing of the throat" (intentional, to get attention or ease nervousness), distinct from an involuntary illness-related 咳.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 風邪で咳が止まらない。— I can't stop coughing because of a cold.
2. 咳が出るときはマスクをしましょう。— Let's wear a mask when coughing.
3. 夜中に咳き込んで眠れなかった。— I couldn't sleep because I kept coughing at night.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 咳 is a する-noun (咳をする = to cough), and the related verb 咳き込む means "to cough repeatedly/violently" — don't confuse plain 咳 with 咳払い, which is a deliberate, singular throat-clear rather than an illness symptom.

## 石炭 (せきたん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** coal

**Similar words:**
- [石油](#石油-せきゆ)（せきゆ）— "oil, petroleum"; another fossil fuel often mentioned alongside 石炭 in energy discussions.
- 木炭（もくたん）— "charcoal" (made from wood), distinct from mineral-origin 石炭.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 昔は石炭で電車を動かしていた。— In the old days, trains were powered by coal.
2. 石炭火力発電所が各地にある。— There are coal-fired power plants in various places.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 石炭 (mineral coal) and 木炭 (wood charcoal, used for grilling) are different materials despite both being called "charcoal/coal" loosely in English — don't confuse them, especially in barbecue/cooking contexts where 木炭 is the correct word.

## 責任 (せきにん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** responsibility, liability

**Similar words:**
- [義務](vocab-14.md#義務-ぎむ)（ぎむ）— "duty, obligation"; a formally required task/role, whereas 責任 is broader, including moral/consequential accountability for outcomes, not just a prescribed duty.
- [責務](#責務-せきむ)（せきむ）— near-synonym, slightly more formal/written, often used for official or professional obligations.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. この事故の責任は誰にあるのか。— Who is responsible for this accident?
2. 彼は責任感が強い人だ。— He's a person with a strong sense of responsibility.
3. 自分の行動に責任を持つべきだ。— One should take responsibility for one's own actions.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 責任 covers both "duty/role" and "blame/accountability for an outcome" — English sometimes separates these ("responsibility" vs "fault"), but Japanese 責任 bridges both, e.g. 責任を取る means "to take the blame/consequences," not just "to fulfill a duty."

## 責務 (せきむ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** duty, obligation, responsibility (formal)

**Similar words:**
- [責任](#責任-せきにん)（せきにん）— the everyday word for "responsibility"; 責務 is more formal/written, often used in official, professional, or legal contexts for a duty tied to one's position.
- [使命](vocab-02.md#使命-しめい)（しめい）— "mission, calling"; implies a larger, often noble purpose, whereas 責務 is a more concrete, position-based obligation.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 国民としての責務を果たす。— To fulfill one's duty as a citizen.
2. 医師には重い責務がある。— Doctors carry heavy responsibilities.
3. 社会的責務を自覚する。— To be aware of one's social obligations.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 責務 is more formal and written than 責任 — it's common in speeches, official documents, and professional codes of conduct, but sounds overly stiff in everyday casual conversation where 責任 is preferred.

#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** oil, petroleum

**Similar words:**
- ガソリン — "gasoline," a refined product derived from 石油, not the crude oil itself.
- 原油（げんゆ）— "crude oil," the unrefined raw form of 石油 as extracted from the ground.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 中東は石油の産出地として知られる。— The Middle East is known as a region that produces oil.
2. 石油価格が高騰している。— Oil prices are soaring.
3. 石油に頼らないエネルギー政策が求められる。— An energy policy that doesn't rely on oil is needed.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 石油 is the general term for petroleum/oil as a resource, while specific refined products use their own words (ガソリン = gasoline, 灯油 = kerosene) — don't use 石油 when you specifically mean the gasoline you put in a car.

## 世間 (せけん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** the world, society, the public (as in "what people think")

**Similar words:**
- [社会](vocab-20.md#社会-しゃかい)（しゃかい）— "society," a more formal/structural term (institutions, social systems), whereas 世間 is more about public opinion and everyday social perception ("what will people say").
- 人々（ひとびと）— "people" in general, a plainer term without the connotation of collective judgment/gossip that 世間 often carries.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 世間の目が気になる。— I worry about what people (society) think.
2. 世間では彼のことが話題になっている。— He's the talk of the town.
3. 世間知らずだと言われた。— I was told I'm naive about the world/society.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 世間 often carries a nuance of social perception, gossip, or "what others will think," distinct from the more neutral, institutional 社会 — the set phrase 世間体 (せけんてい, appearance/reputation in others' eyes) captures this social-pressure nuance well.

## 世辞 (せじ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** flattery, a compliment (often insincere)

**Similar words:**
- [お世辞](vocab-10.md#お世辞-おせじ)（おせじ）— the more commonly used polite form with お-prefix; bare 世辞 alone is less common in modern speech.
- 褒める（ほめる）— "to praise/compliment"; the neutral verb for genuine praise, without the insincere connotation that お世辞 often carries.

**Opposite words:**
- [皮肉](vocab-32.md#皮肉-ひにく)（ひにく）— sarcasm, irony

**Example sentences:**
1. それはお世辞でしょう。— That's just flattery, isn't it?
2. 彼はお世辞がうまい。— He's good at flattery/buttering people up.
3. お世辞抜きで、本当に素晴らしい作品だ。— Flattery aside, it's truly a wonderful work.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Almost always used with the honorific お- prefix as お世辞 in modern Japanese; bare 世辞 sounds slightly archaic. It implies flattery that may not be fully sincere, distinct from genuine 褒め言葉 (words of praise).

## 是正 (ぜせい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** correction, rectification, putting right (formal/official)

**Similar words:**
- [修正](vocab-20.md#修正-しゅうせい)（しゅうせい）— "revision, amendment"; used for correcting documents, plans, or drafts, whereas 是正 specifically implies correcting a wrong/unjust situation to make it right.
- [改善](vocab-11.md#改善-かいぜん)（かいぜん）— "improvement"; focuses on making something better overall, not necessarily correcting a specific wrong or imbalance like 是正.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 格差の是正が求められている。— Correcting the disparity is called for.
2. 不公平な制度を是正すべきだ。— The unfair system should be rectified.
3. 政府は是正措置を取った。— The government took corrective measures.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 是正 is formal/bureaucratic, almost always used for correcting systemic unfairness, imbalances, or errors in policy (格差の是正, 制度の是正) — it's too stiff/formal for correcting a small personal mistake, where 直す would be natural.

## 世帯 (せたい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a household, family unit (as a statistical/administrative concept)

**Similar words:**
- 家族（かぞく）— "family," the everyday word for the personal/emotional family unit, whereas 世帯 is an administrative/statistical term (as used in census data, residency registration).
- 所帯（しょたい）— a near-synonym, slightly more old-fashioned/colloquial, used in phrases like 所帯を持つ ("to start a household/get married").

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. この地域には約千世帯が住んでいる。— About a thousand households live in this area.
2. 一人暮らしの世帯が増えている。— Single-person households are increasing.
3. 世帯収入によって税額が変わる。— The tax amount varies depending on household income.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 世帯 is a formal, often statistical/administrative term (census, insurance, tax forms) counting a residential unit, not an emotional term like 家族 — "世帯主" (head of household) is an administrative role, distinct from the casual idea of "family."

## 世代 (せだい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** generation

**Similar words:**
- [年代](vocab-31.md#年代-ねんだい)（ねんだい）— "age bracket, era (by decade)"; refers to a time period or age range, whereas 世代 groups people by shared generational identity (Gen Z, baby boomers, etc.).
- 時代（じだい）— "era, period" in history, a broader temporal concept not tied to specific cohorts of people like 世代.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 若い世代はSNSをよく使う。— The younger generation uses social media a lot.
2. 世代間のギャップを感じる。— I feel a generation gap.
3. 次の世代に技術を伝えたい。— I want to pass on the skills to the next generation.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 世代 groups people by shared cultural/age cohort identity (世代間ギャップ = generation gap), distinct from 年代, which more often refers to a numeric age range or historical decade (1980年代 = the 1980s).

## 説 (せつ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** an opinion, a theory, a rumor, a hypothesis

**Similar words:**
- 意見（いけん）— "opinion"; a personal view, typically in discussion/debate, whereas 説 often implies a more structured theory or widely circulated claim.
- [噂](vocab-08.md#噂-うわさ)（うわさ）— "rumor, gossip"; more casual and often about unverified social information, overlapping with one sense of 説 (as in 〜という説がある, "there's a theory/rumor that...").

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. いろいろな説があるが、真相は不明だ。— There are various theories, but the truth is unknown.
2. 彼の説には根拠がある。— His theory has a basis/evidence.
3. それはただの噂説にすぎない。— That's nothing more than a rumor/speculation.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 説 is often used as a suffix attached to a claim (〜説 = "the theory/rumor that...", e.g. 陰謀説 = conspiracy theory) rather than standing alone as a common noun in casual speech — learners should note this compound-forming pattern.

## 切開 (せっかい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** incision, to make a surgical cut

**Similar words:**
- 手術（しゅじゅつ）— "surgery" in general, the broader medical procedure, whereas 切開 refers specifically to the act of cutting open.
- 開腹（かいふく）— "abdominal incision/laparotomy," a more specific type of 切開 limited to the abdomen.

**Opposite words:**
- 縫合（ほうごう）— suturing, stitching closed

**Example sentences:**
1. 緊急手術で切開が行われた。— An incision was made during the emergency surgery.
2. 帝王切開で赤ちゃんが生まれた。— The baby was born via cesarean section (lit. "emperor incision").
3. 傷口を切開して膿を出した。— They incised the wound to drain the pus.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 切開 is a precise medical term for a surgical cut — it appears in the common compound 帝王切開 (cesarean section), which learners should recognize as a set medical term rather than parsing word-by-word.

<br>

## 積極的 (せっきょくてき)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** positive, proactive, taking an active/assertive attitude

**Similar words:**
- 前向き（まえむき）— "forward-looking, positive (in attitude)"; similar nuance but more about optimistic mindset, whereas 積極的 emphasizes taking initiative/action.
- 能動的（のうどうてき）— "active" (grammatical/behavioral term, as opposed to passive); more technical, often contrasted with 受動的 in linguistics or psychology.

**Opposite words:**
- 消極的（しょうきょくてき）— passive, reserved, negative

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は何事にも積極的に取り組む。— He tackles everything proactively.

3. 積極的な姿勢が評価された。— His proactive attitude was praised.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 積極的 describes taking initiative/action (speaking up, volunteering, engaging), not just feeling optimistic — its direct antonym 消極的 (passive/reserved) is a key paired vocabulary item frequently tested together.

## 接近 (せっきん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** approach, to come close to, draw near

**Similar words:**
- 近づく（ちかづく）— the plain, everyday verb "to get closer/approach"; 接近する is more formal/technical, often used in weather reports or scientific contexts.
- [迫る](#迫る-せまる)（せまる）— "to press in on, draw near urgently"; implies more urgency/pressure than the neutral 接近する.

**Opposite words:**
- 遠ざかる（とおざかる）— to move away, recede

**Example sentences:**
1. 台風が日本列島に接近している。— A typhoon is approaching the Japanese archipelago.
2. 二つの星が接近して見えた。— The two stars appeared to draw close together.
3. 彼らの関係は急速に接近した。— Their relationship grew close rapidly.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 接近 is common in formal/news contexts (typhoon approach reports: 台風が接近中) and can also describe relationships growing closer figuratively — in casual daily speech about physically approaching something nearby, 近づく is more natural.

## 切実 (せつじつ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Similar words:**

- [緊急](vocab-15.md#緊急-きんきゅう)（きんきゅう）— "urgent, emergency"; more about time-sensitivity, whereas 切実 is more about the intensity of personal feeling/need.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 切実 emphasizes how deeply and personally felt a need or problem is (often paired with 願い = wish, or 問題 = issue) — it's less about objective severity (深刻) and more about the emotional weight/urgency felt by the person involved.

## 接触 (せっしょく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** touch, contact (physical or interpersonal/diplomatic)

**Similar words:**
- 触れる（ふれる）— the plain verb "to touch"; 接触する is more formal, often used for abstract/interpersonal contact (e.g. political contact, contact with a virus) as well as physical.
- [交流](vocab-19.md#交流-こうりゅう)（こうりゅう）— "exchange, interaction"; implies a more sustained, mutual relationship, whereas 接触 can be a single, possibly brief instance of contact.

- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 感染者と接触した可能性がある。— There's a possibility I had contact with an infected person.
2. 両国の外交官が接触した。— Diplomats from both countries made contact.
3. 車が壁に接触して傷がついた。— The car scraped against the wall, causing damage.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 接触 is commonly used in both literal physical-contact contexts (車の接触事故 = minor car collision) and abstract diplomatic/social contact (要人と接触する = to make contact with an important figure) — a dual range broader than the plain verb 触れる.

## 接する (せっする)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to come in contact with; to deal with/treat (people); to adjoin, border

**Similar words:**
- 触れる（ふれる）— "to touch"; more about brief, often light physical contact, whereas 接する can describe an ongoing adjoining relationship or how one treats people.
- 対応する（たいおうする）— "to handle, deal with" (a situation/customer); overlaps with the "treating/interacting with people" sense of 接する but is more procedural/businesslike.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼はいつも親切に人に接する。— He always treats people kindly.
2. この部屋は庭に接している。— This room adjoins the garden.
3. 外国の文化に接する機会が増えた。— Opportunities to come into contact with foreign cultures have increased.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 接する has a notably wide range — physical adjoining (土地が道路に接する = the land borders the road), interpersonal treatment (客に接する態度 = attitude toward customers), and exposure to ideas/culture — much broader than a single English verb like "touch" or "contact" suggests.

**Meaning:** an increase, to increase, to grow

**Similar words:**
- 増える（ふえる）— the everyday verb "to increase," used in casual speech; 増加 is the more formal noun/suru-verb form, common in statistics and reports.
- 上昇（じょうしょう）— a rise (often of numbers like prices, temperature), overlapping with 増加 but more specific to measurable values climbing.

**Opposite words:**
- [減少](vocab-02.md#減少-げんしょう)（げんしょう）— decrease

**Example sentences:**
1. 人口が増加している。— The population is increasing.
2. 売上が前年比で増加した。— Sales increased compared to the previous year.
3. 犯罪の増加が社会問題になっている。— The increase in crime has become a social problem.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 増加 and 増える convey the same core meaning, but 増加 is distinctly more formal/written (news, reports, graphs) — in casual spoken Japanese, 増える is far more natural.

## 総会 (そうかい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** general meeting, general assembly

**Similar words:**
- 会議（かいぎ）— a meeting in general, much broader and more casual; 総会 specifically refers to a formal, often annual gathering of all members of an organization (shareholders, union members).
- [集会](vocab-20.md#集会-しゅうかい)（しゅうかい）— a gathering/rally, which can be informal or political, lacking the formal "all-members, periodic" structure implied by 総会.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 株主総会が来月開催される。— The shareholders' general meeting will be held next month.
2. 町内会の総会に出席した。— I attended the neighborhood association's general meeting.
3. 総会で新しい規約が承認された。— The new bylaws were approved at the general assembly.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 総会 specifically implies an official, often legally-mandated periodic meeting of an organization's entire membership (most famously 株主総会, shareholders' meeting) — it's not used for a casual team meeting, where 会議 fits better.

## 創刊 (そうかん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** first publication/issue (of a magazine or periodical)

**Similar words:**
- 発刊（はっかん）— publication (of a book/periodical), can refer to any issue being published, while 創刊 specifically marks the very first issue/launch.
- 創業（そうぎょう）— founding a business, a parallel concept (first launch) but for companies rather than publications.

**Opposite words:**
- 廃刊（はいかん）— discontinuation of a publication

**Example sentences:**
1. この雑誌は1980年に創刊された。— This magazine was first published in 1980.
2. 創刊号はすぐに売り切れた。— The inaugural issue sold out immediately.
3. 創刊50周年を迎えた。— It celebrated its 50th anniversary since first publication.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 創刊 specifically marks the very first issue/launch of a periodical (創刊号 = "first issue") — using it for any regular/later issue would be incorrect; 発刊 or just 発行 covers ongoing publication.
