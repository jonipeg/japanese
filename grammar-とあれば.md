# ～とあれば {#とあれば}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～とあれば

**Furigana form:** ～とあれば

**Meaning:** "If it's a matter of ~" / "Given the special circumstance that ~" — presents a particular condition as noteworthy or special, and the following clause expresses what the speaker is willing to do or believes will be the case under that condition.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞／普通形 ＋ とあれば
- 子供のためとあれば、わたしはどんなことでも我慢します — "if it's for my child's sake, I'll put up with anything"

**Typical context:** Written and formal spoken register — often used to express strong resolve or willingness in the face of a special circumstance; carries a slightly dramatic, somewhat old-fashioned or literary flavor.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**～とあっては**](grammar-とあって・とあっては・あれば.md) — nearly identical in form and meaning, also presenting a special circumstance, but more commonly followed by a negative or reluctant conclusion rather than willing resolve.
- **～とすれば／としたら** — "if we assume ~," a more neutral hypothetical conditional without the "special/noteworthy condition" nuance that とあれば carries.
- **～なら** — a general-purpose conditional that can express a similar idea but without the formal, almost rhetorical weight of とあれば.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a plain conditional like ～なら表現 describing indifference regardless of the condition (e.g. ～であろうとなかろうと, "whether or not ~") would express the reverse — that the special condition makes no difference.

**Example sentences:**
1. 子供のためとあれば、わたしはどんなことでも我慢しますよ。
   (If it's for my child's sake, I'll put up with anything.)
2. 小さい島での一人暮らしとあれば、不自由なことも多いだろう。
   (Given that it's living alone on a small island, there must be a lot of inconveniences.)
3. 入院のためにお金が必要だとあれば、なんとかしてお金を用意しなければならない。
   (If money is needed for hospitalization, we'll have to find a way to come up with it.)
4. 彼の頼みとあれば、断るわけにはいかない。
   (If it's a request from him, I can't refuse.)
5. 昇進がかかっているとあれば、誰でも必死になるだろう。
   (If a promotion is on the line, anyone would give it their all.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The clause following とあれば usually expresses the speaker's own resolve, willingness, or judgment about how things would unfold under the stated special condition — it isn't simply a neutral factual conditional. The nuance of "this is a special/noteworthy circumstance" should be preserved in translation; using とあれば for an utterly ordinary, unremarkable condition sounds unnatural.
