# ～わ～わ {#わわ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～わ～わ *(particle repetition, no kanji)*

**Furigana form:** ～わ～わ

**Meaning:** "So much X and Y (one after another)!" — repeats the sentence-final particle わ after two (often the same) verbs to express surprise or exasperation at an overwhelming, excessive degree of something happening.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形／た形 + わ + （同じ動詞 or 関連動詞）+ わ
- Example base: 食う → 食うわ食うわ

**Typical context:** Spoken, casual register; conveys lively, often humorous exaggeration/exclamation about a surprising or over-the-top situation. Common in casual storytelling and anecdotes.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **わ** (single, sentence-final) — the standalone feminine/soft emphatic particle in standard use; the doubled わ～わ construction is a distinct idiomatic repetition for emphasis/surprise, not simply two instances of the normal sentence-ending わ.
- **といったら（ありはしない）** — "indescribably X" (e.g., 嬉しいといったらない); similarly expresses an overwhelming degree, but as an adjectival intensifier rather than a verb-repetition exclamation.
- **やら～やら** — "what with X and Y"; lists multiple simultaneous things happening, overlapping somewhat with わ～わ's "one thing after another" feel, though やら～やら is more neutral/listing while わ～わ is more exclamatory/surprised.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a flat, minimal description (少しだけ～した, "did only a little X") would contrast with わ～わ's emphasis on an overwhelming amount.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女をすし屋に連れて行ったら、食うわ食うわ、金がなくなっちゃったよ。 (I took her to a sushi restaurant, and she ate and ate so much that I ran out of money.)
2. 台風が来て、風は吹くわ雨は降るわで、大変だった。 (The typhoon came, and what with the wind blowing and the rain pouring, it was terrible.)
3. 子どもが泣くわ騒ぐわで、電車の中は大騒ぎだった。 (With the child crying and making a fuss, it was total chaos on the train.)
4. 久しぶりに実家に帰ったら、母は泣くわ喜ぶわで大変だった。 (When I went home for the first time in a while, my mother was crying and overjoyed all at once — quite a scene.)
5. セール初日は、客は押し寄せるわ商品はなくなるわで、店は大混乱だった。 (On the first day of the sale, with customers flooding in and products running out, the store was in total chaos.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** わ～わ requires two parallel verb/adjective phrases (often but not always the same verb repeated, or two related actions like 泣くわ喜ぶわ) each marked with わ, and the whole clause typically ends in で describing an overall chaotic/overwhelming result. Learners should note this わ is unrelated to the plain gentle sentence-final わ used for mild emphasis (e.g. 行くわ, said softly) — the doubled form specifically signals "to an overwhelming/surprising degree," often used humorously.
