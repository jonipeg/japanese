# ～あげく(に) {#あげく(に)}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～挙句(に)

**Furigana form:** ～あげく(に)

**Meaning:** "After much ~, (in the end, as a bad result)" — describes the negative or disappointing outcome that results after a long process of effort, deliberation, or trouble.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞た形 + あげく(に) — 例: 悩んだあげく (after much agonizing)
- 名詞の + あげく(に) — 例: 議論の挙句 (after much debate)

**Typical context:** Written and formal-to-neutral spoken — common in narratives, complaints, and recounting troublesome experiences; carries a storytelling, slightly literary tone.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **末に** — "in the end, after ~," very similar but slightly more neutral and can lead to either good or bad outcomes, whereas あげく almost always leads to a negative/regrettable result.
- **結果** — "as a result," a plain, neutral connector without the "long, troublesome process" nuance.
- **ついに** — "finally," emphasizes the long-awaited arrival of a result, not necessarily negative.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though おかげで ("thanks to ~," positive result) contrasts in tone.

**Example sentences:**
1. 長時間悩んだあげく、結局彼に別れを告げた。
   (After agonizing for a long time, I ended up telling him we were through.)
2. さんざん迷ったあげく、何も買わずに店を出た。
   (After much hesitation, I left the store without buying anything.)
3. 彼は会社を転々としたあげく、結局独立することにした。
   (After bouncing from company to company, he ended up deciding to go independent.)
4. 激しい口論のあげく、二人は口をきかなくなった。
   (After a fierce argument, the two stopped speaking to each other.)
5. 長い交渉のあげく、契約は破談になってしまった。
   (After long negotiations, the contract ended up falling through.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** あげく almost always signals a negative, often ironic or disappointing conclusion after wasted effort — unlike 末に, which can report a hard-won positive achievement, using あげく for a happy ending (e.g., あげく、成功した) sounds jarring and unnatural.
