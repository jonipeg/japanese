## 似る (にる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to resemble, to look alike

**Similar words:**
- 似ている（にている）— the common stative te-form used in almost all everyday contexts ("looks like/resembles"); 似る alone is the dictionary form, less often used standalone.
- [そっくり](vocab-24.md#そっくり) — "a spitting image, exactly alike," a stronger/more emphatic degree of resemblance than plain 似る.
- 類似する（るいじする）— "to be similar" in a more formal/analytical sense (patterns, cases), used in written/academic contexts.

**Opposite words:**
- 異なる（ことなる）— to differ, be different

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は父親によく似ている。— He closely resembles his father.
2. この二つの事件は似ている点が多い。— These two incidents have many similar points.
3. 姉妹なのに全然似ていない。— Even though they're sisters, they don't look alike at all.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Almost always used in the ている form (似ている) to describe an ongoing state of resemblance — using 似る in plain non-past form (似る) sounds unnatural in most everyday contexts; learners should default to 似ている.

## 庭 (にわ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** garden, yard

**Similar words:**
- [花壇](vocab-04.md#花壇-かだん)（かだん）— a flower bed, a specific feature within a garden rather than the whole yard.
- [公園](vocab-18.md#公園-こうえん)（こうえん）— a park, a larger public green space, distinct from a private home's 庭.
- 中庭（なかにわ）— a courtyard (an interior garden space within a building complex).

**Opposite words:**
- (not applicable — concrete noun)

**Example sentences:**
1. 庭に花を植えた。— I planted flowers in the garden.
2. 子供たちは庭で遊んでいる。— The kids are playing in the yard.
3. 日本庭園は美しい庭の代表例だ。— A Japanese garden is a prime example of a beautiful garden.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 庭 refers to a private garden/yard attached to a home, distinct from public green spaces (公園) — compound forms like 日本庭園 (Japanese garden) and 裏庭 (backyard) extend the basic word in common, useful ways.

## にわかに
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** suddenly, abruptly (often describing weather or a sudden change in situation)

**Similar words:**
- 急に（きゅうに）— "suddenly," more common/casual and general-purpose than the more literary にわかに.
- [突然](vocab-30.md#突然-とつぜん)（とつぜん）— "suddenly, abruptly," similarly formal, very close in meaning and often interchangeable.
- 俄雨（にわかあめ）— "a sudden shower," a direct compound derived from にわか, commonly used for sudden rain.

**Opposite words:**
- [徐々に](vocab-21.md#徐々に-じょじょに)（じょじょに）— gradually

**Example sentences:**
1. 空がにわかに曇り出した。— The sky suddenly began to cloud over.
2. にわかに信じがたい話だった。— It was a story hard to believe all of a sudden (on the face of it).
3. 天候がにわかに悪化した。— The weather suddenly worsened.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** にわかに is literary/written, common in weather reports and news narration — note the related adjective にわか (an amateur/dabbler, as in にわかファン, "a bandwagon fan") is a separate but related usage worth knowing for modern casual slang.

## 願う (ねがう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to wish, to request, to hope for, to implore

**Similar words:**
- [望む](#望む-のぞむ)（のぞむ）— "to wish/hope for," similar but slightly more about aspiration/expectation than active request.
- 祈る（いのる）— "to pray," implies a spiritual/religious act of wishing, often for something beyond one's control.
- 頼む（たのむ）— "to ask/request" (a favor), more direct and practical, less about deep hope.

**Opposite words:**
- [諦める](vocab-06.md#諦める-あきらめる)（あきらめる）— to give up, abandon hope

**Example sentences:**
1. ご多幸をお祈り申し上げます、皆様のご健康を願っております。— I pray for your happiness and wish for everyone's health.
2. 彼の無事を心から願っている。— I sincerely hope for his safety.
3. どうかお許しくださいますようお願い申し上げます。— I humbly implore you to please forgive me.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 願う is often used in formal/polite requests (お願いします derives from this verb) as well as deep heartfelt wishes/hopes — distinct from 頼む, which is more about a concrete practical request, while 願う carries more emotional/aspirational weight.

## 寝かす (ねかす)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to lay down, put to bed, let lie (also: to let something sit/ferment)

**Similar words:**
- 寝かせる（ねかせる）— the more standard causative form with essentially the same meaning; 寝かす is a slightly contracted, casual variant.
- [横たえる](vocab-01.md#横たえる-よこたえる)（よこたえる）— "to lay down" (a body), more formal/literary, often used for laying someone down carefully (e.g., an injured person).
- 眠らせる（ねむらせる）— "to put to sleep" (causative of 眠る), emphasizes inducing sleep specifically rather than just laying down.

**Opposite words:**
- 起こす（おこす）— to wake up, raise up

**Example sentences:**
1. 赤ちゃんを布団に寝かした。— I laid the baby down on the futon.
2. 生地をしばらく寝かしてから焼く。— Let the dough rest for a while before baking.
3. ワインを寝かせて熟成させる。— Age (let rest) the wine to mature it.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Beyond literally laying a person down to sleep, 寝かす/寝かせる has a specialized culinary/fermentation sense — letting dough, wine, or other ingredients "rest" to develop flavor — this figurative extension is commonly tested and often missed by learners who only know the literal sense.

## 根 (ね)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a root (of a plant); (figuratively) the root/origin/basic nature of something

**Similar words:**
- [原因](vocab-18.md#原因-げんいん)（げんいん）— "cause," more analytical/neutral than the more organic, foundational metaphor of 根.
- 根本（こんぽん）— "the root/foundation" (of a problem, issue), a common compound extending 根's figurative sense.
- 根性（こんじょう）— "guts, grit, inherent character," another figurative extension referring to one's fundamental temperament.

**Opposite words:**
- 葉（は）— leaf (literal botanical opposite/complement)

**Example sentences:**
1. この木は根が深く張っている。— This tree has deep roots.
2. 問題の根はもっと深いところにある。— The root of the problem lies much deeper.
3. 彼は根が正直な人間だ。— He is fundamentally (at his core) an honest person.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 根 extends figuratively to mean someone's fundamental nature/character (根が優しい, "kind at heart") or the underlying cause of a problem (根が深い, "deep-rooted") — this abstract usage is as common as the literal botanical meaning and essential for reading comprehension.

## 熱する (ねっする)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to heat (something); to become heated/enthusiastic, engrossed

**Similar words:**
- 温める（あたためる）— "to warm (up)," a gentler process than 熱する's more intense heating.
- 加熱する（かねつする）— "to heat" in a more technical/cooking context (heating food/materials to a specific temperature).
- 興奮する（こうふんする）— "to get excited/worked up," overlaps with 熱する's figurative "become heated/enthusiastic" sense.

**Opposite words:**
- 冷やす（ひやす）— to cool, chill

**Example sentences:**
1. フライパンを熱してから油を入れる。— Heat the frying pan before adding oil.
2. 議論が熱してきた。— The discussion has heated up.
3. 彼は研究に熱している。— He's deeply engrossed in his research.
**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Used both literally (熱する物理的に, heating a pan/metal) and figuratively (議論が熱する, a discussion heating up; 熱しやすく冷めやすい, "quick to get excited and quick to cool off," a set idiom describing fickle enthusiasm) — the idiom is a common N1 expression worth memorizing.

## 熱心 (ねっしん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** eager, enthusiastic, earnest (about something)

**Similar words:**
- 熱意（ねつい）— "enthusiasm/zeal" (noun), the abstract quality itself, whereas 熱心 (na-adjective) describes being in that eager state.
- [真剣](vocab-21.md#真剣-しんけん)（しんけん）— "serious, earnest," emphasizes seriousness/focus rather than passionate enthusiasm.
- 情熱的（じょうねつてき）— "passionate," a stronger, more emotionally charged term than the steadier 熱心.

**Opposite words:**
- 無関心（むかんしん）— indifference, apathy

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は熱心に勉強している。— He studies enthusiastically/earnestly.
2. 熱心な指導のおかげで上達した。— Thanks to the enthusiastic coaching, I improved.
3. 彼女は仕事に熱心に取り組んでいる。— She tackles her work with great dedication.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 熱心 describes sustained, dedicated enthusiasm/diligence toward a task or goal (熱心な学生, "a diligent/eager student") — more about consistent dedication than momentary excitement, which distinguishes it from 興奮する (to get excited, often briefly).

## 熱帯 (ねったい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** the tropics, the torrid zone

**Similar words:**
- 亜熱帯（あねったい）— the subtropics, a milder zone adjacent to the full tropics.
- [温帯](vocab-11.md#温帯-おんたい)（おんたい）— the temperate zone, the climate zone between tropical and polar.
- 熱帯雨林（ねったいうりん）— tropical rainforest, a common compound combining 熱帯 with rainforest.

**Opposite words:**
- [寒帯](vocab-13.md#寒帯-かんたい)（かんたい）— the frigid/polar zone

**Example sentences:**
1. 熱帯地域は一年中暑い。— Tropical regions are hot year-round.
2. 熱帯雨林には多様な生物が生息している。— Tropical rainforests are home to diverse wildlife.
3. 熱帯低気圧が接近している。— A tropical depression is approaching.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Commonly appears in weather/geography compounds: 熱帯夜 (a tropical night, when temps stay high overnight), 熱帯低気圧 (tropical depression/storm) — knowing these set compounds helps with news and weather report comprehension.

## 値上がり (ねあがり)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a price rise, increase in value

**Similar words:**
- 値上げ（ねあげ）— "a price increase" that is actively carried out by a seller/company (transitive-feeling); 値上がり describes prices rising more generally/passively (market-driven).
- 高騰（こうとう）— "a sharp/steep rise" (prices soaring), implies a more dramatic, often alarming increase.
- インフレ — inflation, the broader economic phenomenon that can cause 値上がり across the board.

**Opposite words:**
- 値下がり（ねさがり）— a price drop

**Example sentences:**
1. 最近、野菜の値上がりが続いている。— Vegetable prices have kept rising recently.
2. 株価の値上がりで利益を得た。— I profited from the rise in stock prices.
3. ガソリンの値上がりが家計を圧迫している。— The rise in gasoline prices is straining household budgets.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 値上がり (intransitive, prices rising on their own, e.g. market forces) contrasts with 値上げ (a company/seller actively raising prices) — mixing these up is a common error since they look and sound similar but differ in agency.

## 粘る (ねばる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to be sticky, glutinous; (figuratively) to persevere, hang in there

**Similar words:**
- べたべたする — to be sticky (texture), a more casual/onomatopoeic description without the figurative "persevere" sense.
- [頑張る](vocab-13.md#頑張る-がんばる)（がんばる）— "to try hard/persevere," the more common general word for effort; 粘る specifically implies tenacious persistence against resistance or a long struggle.
- [耐える](vocab-04.md#耐える-たえる)（たえる）— "to endure," emphasizes withstanding hardship rather than actively struggling toward a goal.

**Opposite words:**
- あきらめる — to give up

**Example sentences:**
1. 納豆は粘る食べ物の代表だ。— Natto is a representative sticky food.
2. 最後まで粘って交渉を続けた。— I tenaciously kept negotiating until the very end.
3. 彼は土壇場まで粘ったが、結局負けてしまった。— He held on until the last moment, but ultimately lost.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The figurative "persevere/hold out" sense (最後まで粘る, "hang in there until the end") is extremely common in sports and negotiation contexts — distinct from simply 頑張る, 粘る specifically emphasizes stubborn persistence under pressure, often near a deadline or losing position.

## 熱湯 (ねっとう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** boiling water

**Similar words:**
- お湯（おゆ）— hot water in general, not necessarily boiling; 熱湯 specifically means very hot/boiling water.
- 沸騰したお湯（ふっとうしたおゆ）— "water that has boiled," a descriptive phrase equivalent to 熱湯 but more explicit/verbose.

**Opposite words:**
- 冷水（れいすい）— cold water

**Example sentences:**
1. カップ麺に熱湯を注いだ。— I poured boiling water into the instant noodle cup.
2. 熱湯でやけどをしないように気をつけて。— Be careful not to get burned by the boiling water.
3. 器を熱湯で消毒した。— I sterilized the container with boiling water.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 熱湯 specifically implies water hot enough to scald/burn (used in warnings and instructions, e.g. instant food packaging) — distinct from the more general お湯, which just means "hot water" at any warm-to-hot temperature.

## 熱中する (ねっちゅうする)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to be devoted to, engrossed in, absorbed in (an activity/hobby)

**Similar words:**
- 夢中になる（むちゅうになる）— "to become absorbed/crazy about," very similar and often interchangeable, perhaps slightly more casual.
- 没頭する（ぼっとうする）— "to immerse oneself in" (work, study), more formal/serious, often implies deep focus on a task.
- 興味を持つ（きょうみをもつ）— "to take an interest in," much milder, doesn't imply the all-consuming absorption of 熱中する.

**Opposite words:**
- [飽きる](vocab-06.md#飽きる-あきる)（あきる）— to get bored/tired of

**Example sentences:**
1. 子供はゲームに熱中している。— The child is absorbed in the video game.
2. 彼女は最近、ガーデニングに熱中している。— She's been devoted to gardening lately.
3. 研究に熱中するあまり、食事を忘れてしまった。— I got so absorbed in my research that I forgot to eat.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Implies a strong, often all-consuming focus/passion for a hobby or activity — commonly paired with あまり (〜するあまり, "to such an extent that...") to show a side effect of the absorption, like forgetting to eat or sleep.

## 値引き (ねびき)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a discount, price reduction

**Similar words:**
- [割引](vocab-36.md#割引-わりびき)（わりびき）— "a discount," very close in meaning and often interchangeable, perhaps slightly more common in retail/commercial contexts (割引券, discount coupon).
- [セール](vocab-23.md#セール-セール) — a loanword for "sale," refers to a broader promotional event rather than a specific price cut.
- 値下げ（ねさげ）— "a price cut" (an active reduction by the seller), overlapping closely with 値引き.

**Opposite words:**
- 値上げ（ねあげ）— a price increase

**Example sentences:**
1. この商品は値引きされている。— This product has been discounted.
2. 店員に値引き交渉をしてみた。— I tried negotiating a discount with the store clerk.
3. 在庫処分のため大幅な値引きを行っている。— We're offering a major discount to clear out inventory.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 値引き and 割引 are largely interchangeable for "discount," though 割引 is more standard in formal retail signage (10%割引) while 値引き often implies a more negotiated/case-by-case reduction (値引き交渉, discount negotiation).

## 狙う (ねらう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to aim at (a target), to have one's eye on, to target

**Similar words:**
- 目指す（めざす）— "to aim for/head toward" (a goal), more about long-term direction than a precise targeted shot/opportunity.
- 狙い撃ちする（ねらいうちする）— "to snipe/target precisely," a more intense, deliberate form derived from 狙う.
- 企む（たくらむ）— "to scheme/plot," implies a more secretive or devious intention than the neutral 狙う.

**Opposite words:**
- (not applicable)

**Example sentences:**
1. 猟師は獲物を狙っている。— The hunter is aiming at his prey.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Extends naturally from literal aiming (a gun, an arrow) to figurative targeting of opportunities, positions, or even people as targets of crime (スキを狙う, "to target an opening/weakness") — a versatile verb useful in both competitive and cautionary contexts.

## 眠い (ねむい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** sleepy, drowsy

**Similar words:**
- 眠たい（ねむたい）— nearly identical in meaning, a slightly softer/more childlike variant of 眠い, both common in everyday speech.
- [だるい](vocab-26.md#だるい) — "sluggish, lethargic" (physically), related to tiredness but not specifically about wanting to sleep.
- [疲れる](vocab-28.md#疲れる-つかれる)（つかれる）— "to get tired," refers to fatigue/exhaustion generally, not specifically the urge to sleep.

**Opposite words:**
- 目が覚める（めがさめる）— to wake up, be wide awake

**Example sentences:**
1. 昨夜あまり寝られなくて眠い。— I didn't sleep much last night, so I'm sleepy.
2. 授業中に眠くなってしまった。— I got sleepy during class.
3. 眠い目をこすりながら勉強を続けた。— I kept studying while rubbing my sleepy eyes.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 眠い and 眠たい are essentially interchangeable in casual speech, with 眠たい sounding slightly cuter/softer — don't confuse with 眠る (to sleep, the verb) or the noun 眠り (sleep), which are related but grammatically distinct forms.

## 寝ぼける (ねぼける)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to be half asleep, to act in a daze from just waking up

**Similar words:**
- 寝起き（ねおき）— "just having woken up" (a state/noun), describes the moment right after waking rather than the dazed behavior itself.
- ぼんやりする — "to be absent-minded/spaced out," a broader daze not necessarily tied to just waking up.
- 寝言を言う（ねごとをいう）— "to talk in one's sleep," a related but distinct phenomenon (occurring during sleep, not right after waking).

**Opposite words:**
- しっかりする — to get a grip, be alert/composed

**Example sentences:**
1. 寝ぼけて電気をつけ忘れた。— I was half asleep and forgot to turn on the light.
2. 彼は寝ぼけた声で電話に出た。— He answered the phone in a groggy, half-asleep voice.
3. 寝ぼけて違う部屋に入ってしまった。— Still half asleep, I walked into the wrong room.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Describes confused, disoriented behavior or speech specifically caused by not being fully awake yet (fumbling, saying odd things, acting on autopilot) — distinct from simply 眠い (feeling sleepy/wanting to sleep), which doesn't imply confused behavior.

## 年賀状 (ねんがじょう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a New Year's card

**Similar words:**
- クリスマスカード — a Christmas card, the Western seasonal-greeting-card counterpart, less culturally central in Japan than 年賀状.
- 挨拶状（あいさつじょう）— a greeting card/letter in general, a broader category that includes 年賀状 as a specific type.
- 暑中見舞い（しょちゅうみまい）— a summer greeting card, the mid-year counterpart custom to 年賀状.

**Opposite words:**
- (not applicable — concrete noun)

**Example sentences:**
1. 毎年、親戚に年賀状を送っている。— I send New Year's cards to relatives every year.
2. 年賀状には干支のイラストがよく使われる。— New Year's cards often feature illustrations of the zodiac animal.
3. 今年から年賀状をやめてメールで挨拶することにした。— Starting this year, I decided to stop sending New Year's cards and greet people by email instead.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 年賀状 is a deeply rooted Japanese New Year's custom (cards are timed to arrive exactly on January 1st via special postal service) — distinct from Western Christmas cards in timing and cultural significance; recently there's a trend (年賀状じまい) of people formally stopping the practice.

## 年間 (ねんかん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a year, a period of one year; (as suffix) annual, yearly

**Similar words:**
- 一年（いちねん）— a more literal, neutral "one year"; 年間 is often used as a counter suffix (e.g. 三年間) or in set phrases like 年間行事.
- 年度（ねんど）— a fiscal/school year with defined start-end dates, rather than a generic span of 12 months.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable (a time-span noun has no true antonym)

**Example sentences:**
1. この会社の年間売上高は増加している。— This company's annual sales are increasing.
2. 彼女は年間を通じてジムに通っている。— She goes to the gym throughout the year.
3. 年間計画を立てましょう。— Let's make an annual plan.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 年間 is usually attached after a number as a counter (三年間 "for three years") or used in compounds (年間行事・年間計画); it isn't normally used alone to mean simply "a year" the way 一年 is.

## 年号 (ねんごう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** an era name (e.g. Reiwa, Heisei)

**Similar words:**
- 元号（げんごう）— essentially a synonym, and actually the more common/official term in modern usage; 年号 is slightly older-fashioned.
- [西暦](vocab-22.md#西暦-せいれき)（せいれき）— the Western (Gregorian) calendar year, as opposed to the Japanese era system.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 令和という年号は2019年に始まった。— The era name "Reiwa" began in 2019.
2. 書類には年号で日付を書いてください。— Please write the date using the era name on the document.
3. 年号が変わると新しい時代の始まりを感じる。— When the era name changes, you feel the start of a new age.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often confuse 年号 with 年号を使った西暦 calculations; remember 年号 refers specifically to the named era (令和, 平成, etc.), not a numeral year itself.

## 年中 (ねんじゅう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** all year round; always, constantly

**Similar words:**
- いつも — more casual, general "always"; 年中 specifically emphasizes a repeated behavior spanning the whole year.
- [しょっちゅう](vocab-04.md#しょっちゅう-しょっちゅう) — colloquial "all the time," often with a complaining nuance, similar register to casual 年中.

**Opposite words:**
- たまに — occasionally
- めったに〜ない — rarely

**Example sentences:**
1. このプールは年中無休で営業している。— This pool is open year-round without holidays.
2. 彼は年中文句を言っている。— He's always complaining.
3. 沖縄は年中温暖な気候だ。— Okinawa has a mild climate all year round.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 年中 can mean a literal "throughout the year" (年中無休) or a figurative "constantly/always" (年中文句を言う) — context determines which; it's not limited to the literal calendar-year meaning.

## 年代 (ねんだい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** an age, an era, a period; a decade (e.g. the 1980s)

**Similar words:**
- 時代（じだい）— broader "era/age," often historical or cultural (江戸時代); 年代 is more often used with specific decade numbers (1980年代).

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 年代 is frequently paired with a decade number (〜年代), unlike 時代 which pairs with named historical periods; also watch the homograph trap with 年代記 (ねんだいき), a different, more formal word.

- くすむ — to become dull, lose luster

**Example sentences:**
1. 夜空に星が光っている。— Stars are shining in the night sky.
2. 彼の才能はチームの中でも光っていた。— His talent stood out (shone) even within the team.
3. 濡れた道路が街灯に光っていた。— The wet road glistened under the streetlight.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 光る is frequently used figuratively to mean "to stand out" in a positive sense (才能が光る, "talent shines through") — don't limit your understanding to just literal light.

## 引き上げる (ひきあげる)
#vocabulary

## 引き下げる (ひきさげる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to lower, reduce, bring down (a rate, price, or level)

**Similar words:**
- 下げる（さげる）— the plain general verb "to lower," while 引き下げる often implies a formal, deliberate reduction (e.g. of interest rates or prices by an authority).
- 削減する（さくげんする）— "to cut/reduce," often used for budgets or costs, somewhat overlapping with 引き下げる but more about reducing quantity than rate/level.

3. 年齢制限を引き下げるべきだという意見もある。— There are also opinions that the age limit should be lowered.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 引き下げる is typically used for formal, often economic or policy-related reductions (金利・価格・基準の引き下げ) — for simply lowering an object physically, plain 下げる is more natural.
