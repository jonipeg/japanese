# ～ことは～が {#ことは～が}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ことは～が

**Furigana form:** ～ことは～が

**Meaning:** "It is true that X, but..." — a concessive construction that first acknowledges a fact (often by repeating the same verb/adjective) before pivoting to a contrasting point with が.

**Conjugation:**
- Verb/い形容詞/な形容詞の普通形 + ことは + (same or related verb/adjective) + が
- Example pattern: 行くことは行くが、あまり気が進まない (I will go, true, but I'm not very enthusiastic about it).

**Typical context:** Used in both spoken and written Japanese; register-neutral to mildly formal. Polite and natural in everyday conversation as a hedging/concessive device — softens a statement before introducing a caveat or complaint.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **には** — a related hedging structure ("as for doing X..."), often used similarly to acknowledge something reservedly before a contrasting clause, but less explicitly "repeats" the verb/adjective the way ことは～が does.
- [**ものの**](grammar-ものの.md) — "although X," a more literary concessive connector, typically used in writing rather than conversation.
- **とはいうものの** — "having said that..."; a more formal concessive phrase expressing a similar "yes, but" structure, often used to open a sentence rather than embedded mid-clause.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; contrasts with simple affirmations like ただ ("simply/only," without concession).

**Example sentences:**
1. 高いことは高いが、品質を考えれば妥当な値段だと思う。
   (It is expensive, true, but considering the quality, I think the price is reasonable.)
2. 行くことは行くが、あまり気が進まない。
   (I'll go, sure, but I'm not very enthusiastic about it.)
3. わかることはわかるが、納得はしていない。
   (I understand it, true, but I'm not convinced.)
4. おいしいことはおいしいが、量が少なすぎる。
   (It's tasty, sure, but the portion is way too small.)
5. 約束を守ることは守ったが、結果的に誰も喜ばなかった。
   (I did keep my promise, true, but in the end no one was happy about it.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** A listening-drill note in the source material glosses this exactly as "it is true that X, but..." — the key feature students must recognize is the repetition of the same verb/adjective before が, which signals a reluctant or qualified concession rather than a flat denial. Don't confuse this with simply saying Xが negatively; the repeated structure specifically softens the acknowledgment before the "but."
