# おかげさまで {#おかげさまで}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** お陰様で

**Furigana form:** おかげさまで

**Meaning:** "Thanks to [your help/everyone's support/circumstances]" — a humble, polite expression used to attribute one's good fortune or successful outcome to the help, concern, or goodwill of others, often used in response to well-wishes or inquiries about one's wellbeing.

**Conjugation:**
- Stands alone as a set phrase, often sentence-initial: おかげさまで + [positive outcome]
- Can attach to a specific cause: 先生のおかげさまで合格しました (more commonly just 先生のおかげで, with おかげさまで reserved for the humble standalone usage)
- Example: おかげさまで、無事に手術が終わりました (Thanks to everyone's support, the surgery was completed safely)

**Typical context:** Polite, humble spoken and written register — frequently used as a reflexive, almost automatic response to greetings like お元気ですか (How are you?) or ご家族は元気ですか (Is your family doing well?), where the respondent credits unspecified "someone's" goodwill (be it the listener's, a deity's, or just good fortune) rather than taking sole credit for their own success. Carries humility and gratitude, and is considered good manners in Japanese social interaction even when no specific person actually helped.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **〜のおかげで** — the more literal, specific version naming an exact cause ("thanks to X specifically"), whereas おかげさまで is often used as a stand-alone humble reflex without naming anyone.
- **おかげをもちまして** — a more formal/written variant used in speeches, greetings, and business correspondence, carrying the same meaning with heightened politeness.
- **ありがたいことに** — "fortunately" / "I'm grateful that...", expresses gratitude for a fortunate outcome but without the implication of someone else's direct help.
- [**せいで**](grammar-せいで.md) — the negative-outcome counterpart-shaped structure ("because of X," blaming X for a bad result), structurally parallel to のおかげで but opposite in connotation.

**Opposite grammar structures:** 〜のせいで ("because of X," used for blaming someone/something for a negative outcome) stands in direct contrast, as おかげさまで/おかげで always frames a positive, fortunate result.

**Example sentences:**
1. 「お元気ですか。」「はい、おかげさまで元気にしております。」
   ("How have you been?" "Yes, thanks to everyone, I've been doing well.")
2. おかげさまで、無事に新しい仕事が決まりました。
   (Thanks to everyone's support, I've successfully landed a new job.)
3. おかげさまで、娘も大学に無事合格することができました。
   (Thankfully, my daughter was able to pass her university entrance exam.)
4. 皆様のおかげさまで、今年も無事に会社を続けることができました。
   (Thanks to everyone, we were able to keep the company running safely again this year.)
5. おかげさまで手術は成功し、今はすっかり元気になりました。
   (Thankfully, the surgery was a success, and I've completely recovered now.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes attach a specific person or cause directly before おかげさまで (e.g., ✕先生のおかげさまで) — in natural Japanese, おかげさまで is typically used standalone, without naming the specific benefactor, because its politeness comes precisely from vaguely crediting unnamed good fortune or collective goodwill; if you want to name a specific cause explicitly, use 〜のおかげで instead (e.g., 先生のおかげで). Also remember おかげさまで is always used for positive outcomes — never use it when describing something negative, which would require せいで instead.
