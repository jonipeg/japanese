# ～もかまわず {#もかまわず}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～も構わず

**Furigana form:** ～もかまわず (構わず read かまわず)

**Meaning:** "Not caring about X," "regardless of X," "heedless of X" — describes an action done while deliberately ignoring or disregarding some consideration (appearance, danger, others' feelings, social norms, etc.), often implying recklessness or boldness.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + もかまわず: 人目もかまわず泣いた。(He/she cried, not caring about the eyes of others.)
- 動詞ている形 + のもかまわず: 雨が降っているのもかまわず出かけた。(He went out despite the rain, not caring about it.)

**Typical context:** Written and formal spoken Japanese. Often used to describe dramatic, emotional, or reckless behavior; carries a slightly literary flavor.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**にもかかわらず**](grammar-にかかわらず・にもかかわらず.md) — "despite/in spite of," expresses contrast with an expected outcome rather than willful disregard of a concern.
- **を気にせず** — more casual equivalent meaning "without worrying about."
- [**をよそに**](grammar-をよそに.md) — "ignoring/in disregard of," similar literary nuance, often used for ignoring surrounding circumstances or others' feelings.

**Opposite grammar structures:** **を気にして／を気にかけて** — "being mindful/concerned about X," the direct opposite of disregarding something.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は周りの目もかまわず、大声で泣き出した。
   (Not caring about the eyes around him, he burst out crying loudly.)
2. 彼女は服が汚れるのもかまわず、子供を助けに駆け寄った。
   (Not caring that her clothes would get dirty, she rushed to help the child.)
3. 雨に濡れるのもかまわず、彼は試合を続けた。
   (Not caring about getting soaked in the rain, he continued the match.)
4. 危険もかまわず、消防士は炎の中に飛び込んだ。
   (Not caring about the danger, the firefighter jumped into the flames.)
5. 周囲の反対もかまわず、彼女は自分の夢を追い続けた。
   (Not caring about the opposition around her, she kept pursuing her dream.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** もかまわず implies a conscious, almost defiant disregard for something that would normally stop or deter most people — it's stronger and more dramatic than simply にもかかわらず, which is more neutral "despite." Learners sometimes use もかまわず for mundane contrasts where にもかかわらず would be more appropriate.
