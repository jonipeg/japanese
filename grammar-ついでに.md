# ～ついでに {#ついでに}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ついでに

**Furigana form:** ～ついでに (no kanji)

**Meaning:** "While at it, taking the opportunity to also do Y" — performing a secondary, incidental action while doing a main action.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形／た形 (verb, dictionary or past form) + ついでに
- 名詞 + の + ついでに

**Typical context:** Casual-to-neutral spoken and written Japanese.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**ながら**](grammar-ながら.md) — "while doing simultaneously," but lacks the "secondary opportunistic" nuance of ついでに.
- [**かたがた**](grammar-かたがた.md) — formal written equivalent, "while doing X, also doing Y," used in very polite correspondence.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. スーパーに行くついでに、牛乳を買ってきてくれる?
   (Since you're going to the supermarket, could you buy some milk while you're at it?)
2. 散歩のついでに、郵便局に寄った。
   (While taking a walk, I stopped by the post office.)
3. 出張のついでに、昔の友人に会ってきた。
   (While on a business trip, I met up with an old friend.)
4. 部屋を掃除するついでに、いらない物を捨てた。
   (While cleaning my room, I threw away things I didn't need.)
5. 友達を駅まで送るついでに、本屋にも行った。
   (While giving my friend a ride to the station, I also went to the bookstore.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ついでに implies the secondary action is convenient or opportunistic, not the main purpose of the trip/action; learners often confuse this with ながら, which describes two actions of equal weight happening simultaneously.
