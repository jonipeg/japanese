# ～に相違ない {#に相違ない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～に相違ない

**Furigana form:** ～にそういない

**Meaning:** "There is no doubt that X / X must certainly be the case" — a formal expression of strong logical certainty, close in meaning to に違いない but more literary/formal.

**Conjugation:**
- 普通形 + に相違ない — 彼が犯人に相違ない (there's no doubt he's the culprit)
- 名詞/な形容詞 + に相違ない — 事実に相違ない (it must be true)

**Typical context:** Formal, written — legal documents, formal reports, newspaper editorials, detective/mystery writing. Rare in casual spoken Japanese.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**に違いない**](grammar-に違いない.md) — nearly identical meaning, far more common in everyday speech and writing; に相違ない is its stiffer, more formal counterpart.
- **はずだ** — "should be X," weaker certainty based on expectation/reasoning rather than firm conviction.
- [**に決まっている**](grammar-に決まっている.md) — expresses confident conviction but in a casual, sometimes blunt spoken tone, unlike the formal に相違ない.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though かもしれない ("might be") contrasts with its lower certainty.

**Example sentences:**
1. この筆跡は彼のものに相違ない。
   (This handwriting is undoubtedly his.)
2. 証拠から見て、彼が真犯人に相違ない。
   (Judging from the evidence, he is without doubt the true culprit.)
3. この決定は会社にとって大きな損失をもたらすに相違ない。
   (This decision will undoubtedly bring great losses to the company.)
4. 彼女の説明は事実に相違ないと確信している。
   (I am convinced that her explanation is without doubt the truth.)
5. この絵は本物の名画に相違ない。
   (This painting is undoubtedly a genuine masterpiece.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** に相違ない is notably more formal/literary than に違いない — using it in casual conversation sounds stilted or overly dramatic; reserve it for formal writing, legal/investigative contexts, or deliberately formal speech.
