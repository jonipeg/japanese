## うつぶせ 【うつぶせ】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Face down (lying prone), as opposed to face up.

**Similar words:** 伏せる（ふせる）— the verb form, "to lie/turn face down," more active in nuance; うつむく — to hang one's head/look down while standing or sitting (not lying down), often used for shyness or shame, which learners confuse with うつぶせ since both involve a downward-facing posture.

**Opposite words:** あおむけ（仰向け）— lying face up.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼はうつぶせになって昼寝をしていた。— He was taking a nap lying face down.
2. 医者はうつぶせの姿勢で寝るように言った。— The doctor told him to sleep in a face-down position.
3. 子供はうつぶせに倒れて泣き出した。— The child fell face down and started crying.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often mix up うつぶせ (a static posture, used with なる/寝る, describing the whole body lying down) with うつむく (an action of lowering just the head/face, usually while upright). うつぶせ cannot be used for someone standing and looking down.

## しょんぼり 【しょんぼり】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Dejected, looking crestfallen/downcast (usually a visible, somewhat pitiable appearance).

**Similar words:** がっかり — disappointed, focuses on the emotion/cause (failed expectation) rather than outward appearance; しょげる — to be discouraged, a verb describing the internal state that produces the しょんぼり look, often overlapping with it; 落ち込む（おちこむ）— to feel depressed/down, broader and can last longer or be more serious.

**Opposite words:** 元気（げんき）— lively, energetic (no single-word direct antonym, but 元気 is the natural contrast).

**Example sentences:**
1. テストの点が悪くて、彼はしょんぼりしていた。— His test score was bad, so he looked dejected.
2. 犬は叱られてしょんぼりと隅に座った。— The dog, having been scolded, sat dejectedly in the corner.
3. 彼女はしょんぼりした顔で帰ってきた。— She came home with a dejected look on her face.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** しょんぼり is an adverb/mimetic word describing appearance (often with する or している), while がっかり centers on the feeling of letdown itself; learners often use them interchangeably, but しょんぼり emphasizes how the person visibly looks, not just how they feel inside.

## しょげる 【しょげる】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To be discouraged, to lose heart/spirit after a setback or scolding.

**Similar words:** 落ち込む — to feel down/depressed, can apply to more serious or longer-lasting low moods; へこむ — colloquial for "to be deflated/demoralized," casual register close to しょげる; しょんぼりする — describes the outward dejected look that often accompanies しょげる.

**Opposite words:** 奮起する（ふんきする）— to rouse oneself to action; no single perfect antonym.

**Example sentences:**
1. 失敗してしょげてしまった。— I failed and lost my spirit.
2. そんなにしょげるなよ、次がある。— Don't be so discouraged, there's always next time.
3. 彼は叱られてすっかりしょげていた。— Having been scolded, he was completely dejected.
4. (extra context) 試合に負けてチーム全体がしょげていた。— The whole team was dispirited after losing the match.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** しょげる describes a temporary emotional slump typically triggered by a specific failure or reprimand; it shouldn't be used for chronic clinical depression (which calls for 落ち込む/うつ状態). Learners sometimes overextend it to serious psychological states.

## おびえる 【おびえる】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To be scared/frightened, to cower in fear (often a visible, trembling reaction).

**Similar words:** 恐れる（おそれる）— to fear, more formal/abstract, can refer to fearing an outcome or danger in general, not necessarily trembling; 怖がる（こわがる）— to be afraid, a common everyday verb describing the state of being scared, broader than おびえる which implies a nervous, flinching reaction; びくびくする — to be jittery/on edge, anticipatory fear.

**Opposite words:** 勇む（いさむ）— to be emboldened/spirited; no exact single antonym.

**Example sentences:**
1. 子猫は雷の音におびえていた。— The kitten was frightened by the sound of thunder.
2. 彼女は暗闇におびえて声も出せなかった。— She was so scared of the dark she couldn't even speak.
3. その犬は人におびえて隠れてしまう。— That dog is scared of people and hides.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** おびえる implies a visible physical/behavioral reaction to fear (cowering, trembling, flinching), whereas 恐れる is more about an internal or abstract apprehension (e.g., 失敗を恐れる "fear failure"). Learners often substitute 恐れる where おびえる's bodily nuance is needed, or vice versa.

## ふざける 【ふざける】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To fool around, joke around, mess about (sometimes with a negative "don't be ridiculous" nuance).

**Similar words:** 冗談を言う（じょうだんをいう）— to tell a joke, refers specifically to verbal joking, narrower than ふざける which includes physical horseplay; 戯れる（たわむれる）— to play/frolic, more literary and gentler in tone; いたずらする — to play a prank/mischief, focused on the mischievous act itself.

**Opposite words:** 真面目にする（まじめにする）— to act seriously; no single-word antonym.

**Example sentences:**
1. 授業中にふざけていて先生に怒られた。— He was fooling around during class and got scolded by the teacher.
2. ふざけるのもいい加減にしなさい。— Stop fooling around already (said in anger/exasperation).
3. 子供たちは公園でふざけ合って遊んでいた。— The kids were horsing around together at the park.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ふざけるな！is a common angry exclamation ("Don't be ridiculous!"/"Cut it out!") that doesn't literally mean "don't joke" — learners often under-translate its force, missing that it can express real anger or indignation, not just annoyance at playfulness.

## 提唱する 【ていしょうする】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To advocate, propose (a theory, policy, or idea), especially in academic or public discourse.

**Similar words:** 提案する（ていあんする）— to propose/suggest, more general and everyday (e.g., suggesting a plan at a meeting), while 提唱する is used for advocating a theory, movement, or principle with intellectual weight; 主張する（しゅちょうする）— to assert/insist on an opinion, emphasizes arguing for one's position rather than introducing a new idea.

**Opposite words:** 反対する（はんたいする）— to oppose; no exact single-word antonym.

**Example sentences:**
1. その学者は新しい理論を提唱した。— That scholar advocated a new theory.
2. 彼は環境保護の重要性を提唱している。— He is advocating the importance of environmental protection.
3. この運動は著名な思想家によって提唱された。— This movement was proposed/advocated by a famous thinker.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 提唱する carries more gravity than 提案する — it's used when someone originates and champions an idea (often one that others later adopt), not for casual suggestions like "let's go to lunch," where 提案する would be correct.

## 危惧する 【きぐする】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To fear, be concerned/apprehensive about a possible negative outcome.

**Similar words:** 心配する（しんぱいする）— to worry, much more common/everyday and can apply to small personal matters, whereas 危惧する is formal and usually concerns serious, larger-scale risks (e.g., environmental or social issues); 懸念する（けねんする）— to be concerned, very close in meaning and register to 危惧する, often interchangeable in formal writing.

**Opposite words:** 安心する（あんしんする）— to feel reassured/relieved.

**Example sentences:**
1. 専門家は環境破壊の進行を危惧している。— Experts are concerned about the progress of environmental destruction.
2. 彼の健康状態を危惧する声が上がっている。— Voices of concern are being raised about his health condition.
3. 少子化の影響が危惧されている。— The impact of the declining birthrate is a cause for concern.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 危惧する is formal/written register; learners should avoid using it in casual conversation where 心配する fits naturally (e.g., saying "危惧する" about being late for a date sounds oddly stiff).

## 業を煮やす 【ごうをにやす】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To lose patience, become exasperated/fed up (idiomatic expression).

**Similar words:** いらいらする — to feel irritated/on edge, a more general everyday word for irritation, while 業を煮やす implies patience has been tested over time and finally snaps; 痺れを切らす（しびれをきらす）— to run out of patience while waiting, very close in meaning, often used for waiting situations specifically; 堪忍袋の緒が切れる（かんにんぶくろのおがきれる）— the string of one's patience-bag snaps, an even more intense idiom for reaching one's limit.

**Opposite words:** 我慢する（がまんする）— to be patient/endure; no exact single-word antonym.

**Example sentences:**
1. 何度言っても直らないので、業を煮やして彼を叱った。— No matter how many times I told him, it didn't improve, so I lost patience and scolded him.
2. 上司は部下の遅刻続きに業を煮やしていた。— The boss was fed up with his subordinate's repeated lateness.
3. 交渉が進まず、業を煮やした両国はついに決裂した。— With negotiations stalled, both countries, having lost patience, finally broke off talks.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This is a set idiom (not used in other conjugations like 業が煮える); learners sometimes try to modify it like a regular verb phrase. It specifically implies patience built up and finally boiling over, distinguishing it from 痺れを切らす, which is more about waiting running out rather than frustration accumulating.

## 氾濫 【はんらん】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Flood, overflow (of a river); figuratively, a flood/glut of something (e.g., information).

**Similar words:** 洪水（こうずい）— flood, the more common everyday word for an actual flood disaster, whereas 氾濫 specifically describes a river overflowing its banks and is also used metaphorically (情報の氾濫 "flood of information"), a figurative use 洪水 shares less often; 溢れる（あふれる）— to overflow, the verb form describing liquid/things spilling over, more general.

**Opposite words:** 渇水（かっすい）— drought/water shortage.

**Example sentences:**
1. 大雨で川が氾濫し、周辺の家が浸水した。— Heavy rain caused the river to flood, and nearby houses were submerged.
2. インターネット上には偽情報が氾濫している。— False information is flooding the internet.
3. 台風の影響で各地の河川が氾濫する恐れがある。— Due to the typhoon, rivers in various areas are at risk of flooding.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 氾濫 specifically refers to a river/water body breaching its banks (or a metaphorical overflow), not generic flooding from any source (like a flooded bathroom from a leak, which would just be 水浸し/溢れる). Learners sometimes use it too broadly for any flooding scenario.

## 矛盾 【むじゅん】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Contradiction, inconsistency.

**Similar words:** 食い違い（くいちがい）— discrepancy/mismatch, softer and more neutral, often about differing accounts or details, whereas 矛盾 implies a logical contradiction where two things cannot both be true; 齟齬（そご）— a formal word for discrepancy/friction between things, closer in register to 矛盾 but less about logical impossibility and more about things not fitting together smoothly.

**Opposite words:** 一貫性（いっかんせい）— consistency.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼の話には矛盾がある。— There is a contradiction in his story.
2. その二つの主張は矛盾している。— Those two claims contradict each other.
3. 自分の発言の矛盾に気づいて黙り込んだ。— He noticed the contradiction in his own statement and fell silent.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 矛盾 comes from the classic Chinese story of the spear and shield (矛と盾) that could not logically coexist — it implies a strict logical incompatibility, stronger than 食い違い, which can simply mean two accounts don't quite match without being logically impossible.

## 所以 【ゆえん】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Reason, the reason why (something is so); often in the pattern 〜の所以である, "this is the reason that...".

**Similar words:** 理由（りゆう）— reason, the everyday, neutral word for "reason," whereas 所以 is literary/formal and typically used to explain *why something deserves its name or reputation* (e.g., 彼が天才と呼ばれる所以 "the reason he's called a genius"); 訳（わけ）— reason/circumstances, more conversational.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. これが彼が成功した所以である。— This is the reason he succeeded.
2. 彼が「鬼」と呼ばれる所以を知っていますか。— Do you know the reason he's called "the demon"?
3. 伝統が長く守られてきた所以がここにある。— Here lies the reason this tradition has been preserved for so long.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 所以 is almost always used in formal/written explanatory contexts, frequently to justify a nickname, reputation, or deeply rooted cause — using it in casual speech for mundane reasons ("the reason I was late") sounds unnaturally stiff; 理由 is correct there.

## 稔り（実り） 【みのり】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Fruit, harvest; figuratively, the fruitful result/reward of effort.

**Similar words:** 収穫（しゅうかく）— harvest, more concrete and agricultural, referring literally to gathering crops, whereas 実り is often used metaphorically for the fruits of labor (努力の実り "fruits of effort"); 成果（せいか）— result/achievement, used for outcomes of work in a more business/academic context.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite (though 不作（ふさく）"poor harvest" contrasts in the agricultural sense).

**Example sentences:**
1. 秋は実りの季節だ。— Autumn is the season of harvest.
2. 長年の努力が実りを迎えた。— Years of effort finally bore fruit.
3. この研究が実り多いものになることを願う。— I hope this research will be a fruitful one.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 実り is frequently used adjectivally in the phrase 実り多い ("fruitful," "rewarding") for abstract achievements, not just literal crops; learners sometimes limit it to agriculture and miss its common figurative use for a fruitful meeting, year, or relationship.

## 刺戟（刺激） 【しげき】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Stimulation, stimulus (physical, mental, or emotional).

**Similar words:** 興奮（こうふん）— excitement, describes the resulting emotional state rather than the external stimulus itself; 誘発（ゆうはつ）— to trigger/induce (a reaction), more causal/technical, often in medical or scientific contexts; 触発（しょくはつ）— to be inspired/triggered (often creatively) by something, narrower and more positive in connotation.

**Opposite words:** 鎮静（ちんせい）— sedation/calming (as a contrasting effect), though not a perfect lexical antonym.

**Example sentences:**
1. 新しい環境は彼に大きな刺激を与えた。— The new environment gave him a great deal of stimulation.
2. 辛い食べ物は胃に刺激が強い。— Spicy food is very stimulating (irritating) to the stomach.
3. その映画は観客に強い刺激を与えた。— That movie gave the audience a strong (emotional) stimulus.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 刺激 can describe both a positive, motivating stimulus (inspiration, excitement) and a negative/physical irritant (spicy food, harsh chemicals on skin); learners sometimes assume it's always positive like "inspiration," missing the neutral/negative physical sense.

## 愚民政策 【ぐみんせいさく】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Obscurantist policy, a policy of deliberately keeping the populace ignorant to make them easier to control.

**Similar words:** 愚民化政策（ぐみんかせいさく）— essentially the same idea phrased as "a policy of turning people into fools," emphasizing the active process; 情報統制（じょうほうとうせい）— information control/censorship, a narrower, more neutral/technical term for restricting information flow, which is often a tool used to carry out 愚民政策 but doesn't by itself imply the goal of dumbing down the population.

**Opposite words:** 啓蒙（けいもう）— enlightenment (educating and empowering the populace).

**Example sentences:**
1. 独裁政権はしばしば愚民政策をとる。— Dictatorial regimes often adopt policies of keeping the people ignorant.
2. 歴史の教科書では愚民政策の例が紹介されている。— History textbooks introduce examples of obscurantist policies.
3. 教育を制限することは一種の愚民政策だと批判された。— Restricting education was criticized as a kind of policy to keep people ignorant.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This is a politically loaded, critical/pejorative term — it's used to accuse a government of a specific, sinister agenda, not a neutral description of low education levels. Learners should be careful about register; it's strong, written/academic vocabulary, not for casual use.

## 滑稽 【こっけい】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Ridiculous, comical, absurdly funny.

**Similar words:** おかしい — funny/strange, a much more common everyday word that can mean either "funny" or "odd," broader and less literary than 滑稽; こっけい often carries a slightly mocking or pitying tone about someone's foolish behavior, while おかしい is more neutral; ばかばかしい — utterly foolish/absurd, stronger and closer to "ridiculous" in a dismissive sense.

**Opposite words:** 真面目（まじめ）— serious, earnest (as a contrasting tone, not a strict antonym).

**Example sentences:**
1. その格好はちょっと滑稽に見える。— That outfit looks a bit ridiculous.
2. 彼の言い訳は滑稽なほど苦しかった。— His excuse was comically weak.
3. 大の大人がそんなことで争うのは滑稽だ。— It's absurd for grown adults to fight over something like that.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 滑稽 often implies the speaker finds the situation laughably pathetic or undignified, with a hint of mockery or irony, rather than plain, friendly humor (which would be おかしい or 面白い); learners sometimes use it for simple jokes where a gentler word is more appropriate.

## 明朗 【めいろう】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Cheerful, bright (in personality); also "clear/transparent" (e.g., in accounting or conduct).

**Similar words:** 陽気（ようき）— cheerful/upbeat, describes a lively, sociable personality, overlapping closely with 明朗's "cheerful" sense; 快活（かいかつ）— lively and spirited, similarly close but slightly more about energetic liveliness; 明朗 uniquely also covers "clear and aboveboard" (明朗会計 "transparent accounting"), a sense 陽気/快活 don't share.

**Opposite words:** 陰気（いんき）— gloomy, dark in mood/disposition.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女は明朗な性格で誰からも好かれている。— She has a cheerful personality and is liked by everyone.
2. この店は明朗会計で安心して利用できる。— This shop has transparent pricing, so you can use it with peace of mind.
3. 明朗で前向きな社風を目指している。— We aim for a bright, positive corporate culture.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often only learn 明朗 as "cheerful" and miss its common business-context meaning "transparent/clear" (as in 明朗会計, "no hidden fees"), which has nothing to do with personality.

## 窮する 【きゅうする】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To be at a loss, to be in a difficult/cornered situation (financially, verbally, or practically).

**Similar words:** 困る（こまる）— to be troubled/have difficulty, the everyday, general word for being in trouble, while 窮する is more formal and implies being pushed into a corner with no easy way out; 行き詰まる（いきづまる）— to reach a deadlock/dead end, emphasizes being stuck with no progress possible, close in nuance when describing negotiations or plans.

**Opposite words:** 余裕がある（よゆうがある）— to have room/leeway (as a contrasting state).

**Example sentences:**
1. 質問に答えに窮して黙ってしまった。— He was at a loss for how to answer the question and fell silent.
2. 資金繰りに窮している中小企業が多い。— Many small businesses are struggling (at a loss) with cash flow.
3. 返す言葉に窮した彼は目をそらした。— Stuck for a response, he looked away.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 窮する is often paired with a specific cause (答えに窮する "at a loss for an answer," 生活に窮する "struggling to make a living") — learners sometimes use it as a free-standing adjective-like word rather than this verb + に construction pattern.

## 好悪 【こうお】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Likes and dislikes, preference (what one is fond of vs. averse to).

**Similar words:** 好き嫌い（すききらい）— likes and dislikes, the everyday equivalent used in casual conversation (e.g., about food), whereas 好悪 is formal/written and used in more abstract or analytical contexts (e.g., discussing personal bias in judgment); 嗜好（しこう）— taste/preference, focuses more on refined personal taste (e.g., in food, art) rather than a simple positive/negative reaction.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite (it already denotes a pair of opposites).

**Example sentences:**
1. 人の好悪の感情は判断に影響を与えることがある。— A person's feelings of like and dislike can influence their judgment.
2. 彼は好悪をはっきり顔に出すタイプだ。— He's the type who clearly shows his likes and dislikes on his face.
3. 好悪で人を評価するべきではない。— One should not judge people based on personal likes and dislikes.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 好悪 is formal/written vocabulary; in daily conversation, 好き嫌い is the natural choice (e.g., asking a child about food preferences) — using 好悪 there would sound oddly academic.

## 円盤 【えんばん】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Disk, disc (a flat, round object); also refers to the discus (in sports) or UFO (空飛ぶ円盤).

**Similar words:** ディスク — a loanword for "disk," commonly used for digital/data storage media (e.g., ハードディスク), more modern/technical than 円盤; 盤（ばん）— a general word for a flat board/disk-like object (e.g., 将棋盤 "shogi board," レコード盤 "record disk"), broader in application.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は体育の授業で円盤投げに挑戦した。— He tried the discus throw in P.E. class.
2. 空に奇妙な円盤状の物体が見えた。— A strange disk-shaped object was seen in the sky.
3. 古いレコードは円盤の形をしている。— Old records are shaped like a disk.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 円盤 is somewhat old-fashioned/literary for everyday "disk" in a tech context — modern Japanese usually says ディスク for CDs/hard drives, while 円盤 survives mainly in 円盤投げ (discus throw) and 空飛ぶ円盤 (flying saucer/UFO).

## 鬼役 【おにやく】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** The "it" role in tag/hide-and-seek (the person who chases or searches for others).

**Similar words:** 鬼（おに）— used alone, this is the actual common term for "it" in tag games (鬼役 explicitly spells out "the role of the oni"); 鬼ごっこ（おにごっこ）— the game of tag itself, referring to the whole game rather than the role.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite (the other players are simply 逃げる側 "the ones who run away").

**Example sentences:**
1. じゃんけんで負けた人が鬼役になる。— Whoever loses at rock-paper-scissors becomes "it."
2. 彼はいつも鬼役をやりたがる。— He always wants to be "it."
3. 鬼役は目を閉じて十数えなければならない。— The person who is "it" has to close their eyes and count to ten.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** In everyday speech, Japanese speakers usually just say 鬼 (not 鬼役) when talking about who is "it" in a game — 鬼役 more explicitly frames it as "the designated role," useful in explanatory/written contexts but slightly redundant in casual conversation.

## 明示的 【めいじてき】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Explicit, clearly and directly stated (as opposed to implied).

**Similar words:** 明確（めいかく）— clear/distinct, broader, describing clarity in general (of a plan, answer, etc.), whereas 明示的 specifically contrasts with "implicit" and is common in logic, linguistics, and programming contexts (明示的な指示 "explicit instructions"); はっきり（した）— clear, plain, a common everyday word for clarity in speech or manner.

**Opposite words:** 暗示的（あんじてき）— implicit, suggested rather than stated directly.

**Example sentences:**
1. 契約書には条件が明示的に記載されている。— The conditions are explicitly stated in the contract.
2. プログラムでは変数の型を明示的に指定する必要がある。— In the program, you need to explicitly specify the variable's type.
3. 彼は明示的な拒否の意思を示した。— He explicitly indicated his intention to refuse.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 明示的 is a fairly technical/formal word (common in legal, academic, and programming contexts) specifically paired with its antonym 暗示的; learners sometimes substitute the more casual 明確 or はっきり, which work for general clarity but don't carry the explicit/implicit contrast.

## 逆上する 【ぎゃくじょうする】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To lose composure, fly into a rage, become frantic/hysterical with anger or shock.

**Similar words:** 興奮する（こうふんする）— to become excited/agitated, more neutral and can be positive or negative, whereas 逆上する is almost always negative, implying a loss of rational control triggered by anger, humiliation, or shock; キレる — a colloquial slang term for "to snap/lose it," similar intensity but much more casual register.

3. そんなことで逆上するなんて大人げない。— It's childish to lose your temper over something like that.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 逆上する implies a sudden, often embarrassing loss of self-control (shouting, irrational behavior), stronger than simply "getting angry" (怒る); learners sometimes use it for mild irritation, but it should be reserved for a visible, dramatic outburst.

## 讃える 【たたえる】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To praise, extol, honor (someone's achievement or virtue), often in formal or ceremonial contexts.

**Similar words:** 褒める（ほめる）— to praise/compliment, the everyday word used for praising a child, colleague, etc., whereas 讃える is more formal/literary and often used for praising great achievements, heroes, or in speeches and writing; 賞賛する（しょうさんする）— to admire/applaud, similarly formal, close in meaning and often interchangeable with 讃える in written contexts.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 讃える is elevated, formal vocabulary suited to ceremonies, monuments, and writing — using it for everyday praise ("good job on the dishes") would sound overly grand; 褒める is the natural everyday choice.

## 接戦 【せっせん】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** A close match/contest, a tight race where the outcome is uncertain until the end.

**Similar words:** 白熱戦（はくねつせん）— a heated/intense match, emphasizes the excitement and fierce competitiveness rather than just closeness in score; デッドヒート — a dead heat/neck-and-neck race, a loanword often used for races (like in sports or elections) that are extremely close.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 接戦 describes the closeness of the competition/score, not necessarily its intensity or excitement (which would be 白熱した試合); a match can be 接戦 but dull, so learners shouldn't assume it always implies thrilling action.

#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Rounded up, curled up (into a ball shape), e.g., an animal curling up to sleep.

**Meaning:** Thick (of books, materials, or layers — notably thicker than merely 厚い).

#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Shabby, poor/wretched-looking in appearance (often implying poverty).

## ぼろぼろ 【ぼろぼろ】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Worn out, tattered, falling apart (describing the physical state of an object); also used for a person who is utterly exhausted/battered.

**Similar words:** みすぼらしい — shabby, focuses on the overall poor impression given (often of a person's appearance), while ぼろぼろ specifically describes an object's worn, crumbling condition (fabric fraying, pages falling apart); くたくた — exhausted/limp (of a person, from tiredness), close to ぼろぼろ's figurative "physically/emotionally wrecked" sense but without the connotation of being "beaten up."

## かさばる 【かさばる】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To be bulky, take up a lot of space (often said of luggage or belongings).

## 根回し 【ねまわし】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Groundwork, behind-the-scenes lobbying/consensus-building before a formal decision (common in Japanese business/organizational culture).

#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Swamp, marsh, bog (a body of still, shallow water with muddy/weedy ground); slang: an obsession/rabbit hole one can't escape from (沼にハマる).

**Similar words:** 湿地（しっち）— wetland, a broader, more formal/ecological term covering swamps, marshes, and bogs collectively; 池（いけ）— pond, refers to a clearer, often man-made or landscaped body of still water, without 沼's connotation of murkiness/wildness.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Besides the literal geographic meaning, 沼 has become popular slang for an addictive hobby/interest one gets sucked into and can't easily quit (沼にハマる, "falling into the swamp") — learners who only know the literal meaning may miss this very common modern colloquial usage, especially online.

## 馴らす 【ならす】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To tame, domesticate, acclimate/accustom (an animal or oneself to something).

**Similar words:** 慣らす（ならす）— to get accustomed/break in (same reading, different kanji), used for adapting oneself or equipment to conditions (体を慣らす "acclimate one's body"), while 馴らす specifically applies to taming animals or making them familiar with humans; しつける — to train/discipline (a pet or child), focused on teaching behavior/manners rather than simply making something tame/familiar.

**Opposite words:** 野生化する（やせいかする）— to go feral/return to the wild.

**Example sentences:**
1. 野良猫を少しずつ馴らしていった。— I gradually tamed the stray cat.
2. 調教師は馬を馴らすのに長い時間をかけた。— The trainer spent a long time taming the horse.
3. 新しい犬を家族に馴らすには根気が必要だ。— Taming a new dog to the family requires patience.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 馴らす (taming an animal) and 慣らす (acclimating/getting used to something) share the same reading ならす but differ in kanji and target — 馴らす applies to living creatures becoming tame/familiar with people, while 慣らす is broader, used for adapting bodies, machines, or oneself to new conditions; learners often conflate the two kanji.

## 尚更 【なおさら】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** All the more, even more so (used to intensify a statement given an additional reason).

**Similar words:** ますます — more and more, increasingly, describes a gradual intensification over time or degree, whereas 尚更 specifically emphasizes "given this additional fact/reason, X is even more true/necessary," often in response to new information; いっそう — all the more, very close in meaning to 尚更 and often interchangeable, though いっそう is slightly more formal/written.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 雨が降っているなら、尚更出かけたくない。— If it's raining, I want to go out even less.
2. 彼が反対しているなら、尚更やる価値がある。— If he's opposing it, that makes it all the more worth doing.
3. 体調が悪いなら、尚更休むべきだ。— If you're feeling unwell, you should rest all the more.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 尚更 is typically used in a conditional or reason-giving structure (〜なら尚更, 〜だから尚更) — it doesn't simply mean "more," but "even more so, given this particular circumstance"; learners sometimes use it as a standalone intensifier without the implied reason/condition it requires.

## 配慮 【はいりょ】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Consideration, thoughtful care/attention given to someone's needs or feelings.

**Similar words:** 気遣い（きづかい）— consideration/thoughtfulness, a more personal, warm everyday word for being considerate toward someone (e.g., noticing a friend is tired and offering help), whereas 配慮 is more formal/institutional, often used for policies or official consideration of needs (バリアフリーへの配慮 "consideration for accessibility"); 思いやり（おもいやり）— compassion/empathy, emphasizes the warm emotional motivation behind kind acts, broader than 配慮's more procedural "taking into account" sense.

## 控える 【ひかえる】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To hold back, refrain from, cut down on; also "to be near/at hand" (時間が差し迫る) and "to note/jot down" (控えておく).

**Similar words:** 我慢する（がまんする）— to endure/put up with, implies enduring discomfort or desire, whereas 控える is about voluntarily moderating or reducing an action (お酒を控える "cut down on alcohol") rather than enduring hardship; 慎む（つつしむ）— to be discreet/restrained, more formal and implies self-discipline out of propriety or respect, a more moralistic register than 控える.

## 摩擦 【まさつ】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Friction (both physical, as in physics, and figurative, as in interpersonal or diplomatic conflict).

**Similar words:** 衝突（しょうとつ）— collision/clash, implies a more direct, sudden confrontation or conflict (意見の衝突 "clash of opinions"), whereas 摩擦 suggests ongoing, grinding tension (貿易摩擦 "trade friction") rather than one sharp clash; 軋轢（あつれき）— friction/discord, a more literary/formal synonym for interpersonal friction, close to figurative 摩擦 but less used for physical friction.

3. 新しい方針を巡って社内に摩擦が生じた。— Friction arose within the company over the new policy.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 摩擦 is frequently used figuratively for ongoing interpersonal, diplomatic, or trade tension (not a single argument, but a persistent strained relationship) — learners sometimes use 衝突 interchangeably, but 衝突 implies a more acute, specific clash rather than 摩擦's grinding, continuous friction.

**Meaning:** To give (charity), perform/apply (a treatment, measure, or decoration), bestow.

## 偏見 【へんけん】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Prejudice, bias (an unfair, preconceived opinion, often negative).

**Similar words:** 先入観（せんにゅうかん）— preconception/preconceived idea, more neutral and doesn't necessarily imply unfairness or harm — it's simply an idea formed in advance, which can be true or false, whereas 偏見 specifically implies an unjust, often harmful bias against a person or group; 差別（さべつ）— discrimination, refers to the resulting unfair treatment/action based on prejudice, distinct from 偏見 which is the underlying biased belief/attitude itself.

**Opposite words:** 公平（こうへい）— fairness, impartiality.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は外国人に対する偏見を持っていた。— He held prejudice against foreigners.
2. 偏見のない目でものを見ることが大切だ。— It's important to look at things without prejudice.
3. メディアがステレオタイプを広め、偏見を助長することがある。— The media sometimes spreads stereotypes and fosters prejudice.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 偏見 (the biased attitude/belief) should be distinguished from 差別 (the resulting discriminatory action/treatment) — learners sometimes use them interchangeably, but one can hold 偏見 without necessarily acting on it through 差別, and conversely 差別 is the behavioral manifestation that 偏見 can lead to.

## 報酬 【ほうしゅう】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Reward, compensation, remuneration (payment for work, service, or achievement).

**Similar words:** 給料（きゅうりょう）— salary/wages, specifically regular employment pay, whereas 報酬 is broader and can refer to one-time payments, fees for services, rewards, or executive compensation, not just a regular salary; 謝礼（しゃれい）— a token of gratitude/honorarium, usually a smaller, more informal payment given as thanks (e.g., to a guest speaker), less substantial than 報酬 typically implies.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. この仕事の報酬は思ったより高かった。— The compensation for this job was higher than expected.
2. 彼は会社の役員として高額な報酬を得ている。— As a company executive, he receives a high salary/compensation.
3. ボランティアだが、交通費程度の報酬は出る。— It's volunteer work, but there is some compensation for transportation costs.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 報酬 is a broad term covering everything from executive compensation to small token payments and even non-monetary emotional "reward" (達成感という報酬 "the reward of a sense of accomplishment") — learners should note it's not limited to regular salaried income like 給料.

## 本音 【ほんね】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** True intention, one's genuine feelings/opinion (as opposed to a diplomatic public stance).

**Similar words:** 建前（たてまえ）— the direct contrasting pair word for "public stance/façade," the socially acceptable official position one presents, which may differ from one's 本音; 本心（ほんしん）— one's true heart/real feelings, very close to 本音 but 本心 emphasizes the inner emotional truth more broadly (not necessarily contrasted with a social façade), while 本音 specifically implies the words one would say if being fully honest, often in contrast to tatemae.

**Opposite words:** 建前（たてまえ）— public stance, official façade.

**Example sentences:**
1. 本音を言うと、この仕事はあまり好きではない。— To tell you my true feelings, I don't really like this job.
2. 彼は建前と本音をうまく使い分けている。— He skillfully switches between his public stance and his true feelings.
3. お互いに本音で話し合おう。— Let's talk to each other honestly (with our true feelings).

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 本音 is almost always discussed in relation to its counterpart 建前 — this pair is a key cultural concept in Japanese communication (the gap between public/polite stance and private true feelings); learners should understand both terms together, as using only one without awareness of the other misses an important cultural framework.

## 幹 【みき】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Trunk (of a tree); figuratively, the main/core part of something (as opposed to branches/details).

**Similar words:** 木の幹（きのみき）is the basic phrase; 枝（えだ）— branch, the contrasting part of a tree, often used figuratively alongside 幹 for "core vs. peripheral" distinctions (幹となる考え "the core/trunk idea" vs. 枝葉末節 "minor branch-and-leaf details"); 根幹（こんかん）— the root and trunk, i.e., the fundamental basis/core of a matter, a more abstract, formal compound emphasizing foundational importance.

**Opposite words:** 枝葉（えだは）— branches and leaves (peripheral, minor details), as a figurative contrast.

**Example sentences:**
1. その木の幹はとても太い。— That tree's trunk is very thick.
2. 幹に触れると樹皮のざらざらした感触がした。— Touching the trunk, I felt the rough texture of the bark.
3. この議論の幹となる部分をまず押さえよう。— Let's first grasp the core (trunk) part of this discussion.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Beyond the literal "tree trunk," 幹 is used figuratively for "the core/essential part" of an argument, plan, or organization, often contrasted with 枝葉（えだは）"branches and leaves" meaning minor/peripheral details — learners sometimes miss this common figurative pairing in discussions about prioritizing main points over details.

## 扶養 【ふよう】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Support (a dependent), providing for someone financially (a legal/tax term, e.g., 扶養家族 "dependent family members").

**Similar words:** 養う（やしなう）— to support/feed (a family), the more everyday verb for providing for dependents, whereas 扶養 is the formal, often legal/tax-related noun used in official documents (扶養控除 "dependent deduction," 扶養義務 "duty of support"); 養育（よういく）— raising/rearing (a child), focused specifically on child-rearing rather than general financial dependency support.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は両親を扶養する義務がある。— He has a duty to support his parents.
2. 配偶者を扶養家族として申告した。— He declared his spouse as a dependent (for tax purposes).
3. 扶養控除の条件が毎年見直されている。— The conditions for the dependent tax deduction are reviewed every year.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 扶養 is specifically formal/legal vocabulary tied to tax and family-law contexts (dependent status, deductions, legal duty of support) — in casual conversation about taking care of family, 養う is the natural, everyday verb; learners should avoid using 扶養する in casual speech about simply "supporting" someone emotionally.

## 放棄 【ほうき】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To give up, abandon, relinquish (a right, responsibility, or possession).

**Similar words:** 諦める（あきらめる）— to give up (on a goal or hope), an emotional/psychological giving up, whereas 放棄 is more formal and often legal/procedural, referring to formally relinquishing a right, claim, or duty (親権放棄 "giving up parental rights," 権利放棄 "waiver of rights"); 断念する（だんねんする）— to abandon/relinquish (a plan or hope), similarly formal, close to 諦める but with a more decisive, final connotation.

**Opposite words:** 保持（ほじ）— retention, maintaining possession/rights.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は相続権を放棄した。— He renounced his inheritance rights.
2. 試合の途中で選手が棄権(放棄)した。— The athlete withdrew partway through the match. (note: 棄権 is the specific sports term)
3. 責任を放棄するような行動は許されない。— Behavior that amounts to abandoning one's responsibility is not acceptable.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 放棄 specifically implies formally relinquishing a right, claim, duty, or possession (often with legal or formal weight), distinct from the more emotional "giving up on a dream" (諦める) — learners sometimes use 放棄 for simple personal disappointment/quitting, where 諦める is the natural, everyday choice.

## 補足 【ほそく】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Supplement, supplementary addition/explanation (to fill in missing information).

**Similar words:** 追加（ついか）— addition, the general word for adding something extra, whereas 補足 specifically implies adding information to complete or clarify something that was previously insufficient or unclear; 補完（ほかん）— complementing/completing, a more formal/technical word for filling in a gap to make something whole, often used in systems or processes (互いに補完し合う "complement each other").

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 先ほどの説明に補足させてください。— Allow me to add a supplement to my earlier explanation.

3. 彼の発言に補足する形で詳細を説明した。— I explained the details in a way that supplemented his remarks.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 補足 implies adding information specifically to clarify or complete an explanation that was previously incomplete, usually verbal or written explanation — it's narrower than 追加, which can refer to adding anything (ingredients, items, funds), not just explanatory content.

## へりくだる 【へりくだる】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To be humble, speak/act modestly by lowering oneself (often toward someone of higher status).

**Similar words:** 謙遜する（けんそんする）— to be modest/humble (especially verbally, downplaying one's own achievements), very close to へりくだる but 謙遜 is more specifically about downplaying praise or compliments, while へりくだる broadly describes an attitude/manner of speaking and behaving modestly toward others, especially superiors; 卑下する（ひげする）— to belittle oneself, implies an excessive, sometimes unhealthy degree of self-deprecation, stronger/more negative than へりくだる's respectful humility.

**Opposite words:** 高慢（こうまん）— arrogance, haughtiness; 威張る（いばる）— to swagger/act bossy.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼はいつも目上の人にへりくだった態度を取る。— He always takes a humble attitude toward his superiors.
2. あまりへりくだりすぎるのもかえって不自然だ。— Being overly humble can actually seem unnatural.
3. 彼女はへりくだった言葉遣いで客に対応した。— She responded to the customer using humble language.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** へりくだる describes humble behavior/speech as a respectful social practice (especially linguistic humility, overlapping with 謙譲語 usage), distinct from 卑下する, which implies putting oneself down in an unhealthy or excessive way — learners should note へりくだる is generally viewed positively/appropriately in Japanese social context, not as weakness.

## まごつく 【まごつく】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To be confused, flustered, at a loss over what to do (especially in an unfamiliar situation).

**Similar words:** 戸惑う（とまどう）— to be bewildered/perplexed, very close in meaning, perhaps slightly more common/neutral in written Japanese, while まごつく has a more colloquial, almost comedic flavor of visibly fumbling about; うろたえる — to panic/be flustered, implies a stronger degree of visible agitation or panic than the milder confusion of まごつく.

**Opposite words:** 落ち着く（おちつく）— to calm down, be composed.

**Example sentences:**
1. 初めての場所で道に迷い、まごついてしまった。— I got lost in an unfamiliar place and was flustered about what to do.
2. 急に質問されてまごついてしまった。— I was flustered when suddenly asked a question.
3. 新入社員は慣れない業務にまごついていた。— The new employee was fumbling around, unfamiliar with the work.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** まごつく tends to describe a visible, somewhat awkward fumbling/hesitating in unfamiliar situations (like not knowing which door to use, or how to respond) rather than deep anxiety or panic (うろたえる) — learners sometimes overstate its intensity; it's a milder, almost endearing kind of confusion.

## 免れる 【まぬがれる】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To escape, avoid, be spared from (a negative consequence, danger, or responsibility).

**Similar words:** 避ける（さける）— to avoid, a more general, proactive verb for steering clear of something (often by one's own choice/action), whereas 免れる implies narrowly escaping or being spared from something that was threatening to happen (危機を免れる "escape a crisis"), often with an element of luck or external circumstance rather than pure deliberate avoidance; 逃れる（のがれる）— to flee/escape (from), similar in meaning, often used interchangeably, though 逃れる can imply more active fleeing/evasion.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は辛うじて事故を免れた。— He narrowly escaped the accident.
2. この会社も倒産の危機を免れなかった。— This company, too, could not escape the risk of bankruptcy.
3. 彼は批判を免れるために言い訳を考えた。— He thought up an excuse to avoid criticism.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 免れる usually implies narrowly or fortunately escaping something negative that was already looming/threatening (a disaster, blame, punishment), rather than proactively avoiding something in general — learners sometimes use it like 避ける for everyday avoidance choices, but 免れる carries a stronger sense of "just barely escaping a bad fate."

## ほとり 【ほとり】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Beside, edge of, vicinity (typically of water — a river, lake, or pond); a somewhat literary/poetic word.

**Similar words:** そば — beside/near, a much more common, everyday word for proximity to anything, whereas ほとり is specifically and almost exclusively used for the edge/bank of water (川のほとり "by the riverside," 湖のほとり "by the lakeside"), giving it a more poetic, literary flavor; 岸（きし）— shore/bank, the concrete noun for the edge of water itself, while ほとり is more about the general vicinity/area near it.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は湖のほとりに小さな家を建てた。— He built a small house by the lakeside.
2. 川のほとりを散歩するのが日課だ。— Taking a walk by the riverside is my daily routine.
3. 寺は静かな池のほとりに佇んでいた。— The temple stood quietly by the edge of a calm pond.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ほとり is almost exclusively used for the edges of bodies of water and carries a literary, scenic, somewhat old-fashioned tone — learners should avoid using it generically for "beside" anything (like "beside the table"), where そば or 隣 is the natural, everyday choice.

## 憤慨 【ふんがい】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Indignation, resentful anger (especially at something perceived as unjust).

**Similar words:** 怒り（いかり）— anger, the general, neutral word for anger of any kind or intensity, whereas 憤慨 specifically implies anger triggered by a sense of injustice or moral outrage, often more restrained/dignified in expression than raw 怒り; 激怒（げきど）— fury/rage, implies a much more intense, often visibly explosive anger, stronger than the more composed indignation of 憤慨.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 不当な扱いに彼は憤慨した。— He was indignant at the unfair treatment.
2. 市民たちはその決定に憤慨の声を上げた。— The citizens raised voices of indignation at that decision.
3. 彼女は裏切りに激しく憤慨していた。— She was deeply indignant over the betrayal.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 憤慨 implies a morally-grounded anger at perceived injustice or unfairness, typically expressed with some composure or formality (often in writing or speeches) — it's less visceral/explosive than 激怒; learners sometimes treat it as a simple synonym for "very angry" without the underlying sense of moral indignation.

## 弁解 【べんかい】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Excuse, explanation/justification offered to defend oneself (often with a slightly negative "making excuses" connotation).

**Similar words:** 言い訳（いいわけ）— excuse, the more common, everyday word for making excuses, very close in meaning but slightly more casual and often more explicitly negative/dismissive (「言い訳するな」"don't make excuses"), whereas 弁解 is a bit more formal and can sound like a more "legitimate" attempt at explanation/defense, even if still often viewed skeptically; 釈明（しゃくめい）— clarification/explanation (especially in a public, formal setting, like a press conference), implies a more official defense of one's position or actions.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は遅刻の理由を弁解しようとした。— He tried to make an excuse for being late.
2. どんな弁解も聞きたくない。— I don't want to hear any excuses.
3. 社長は不祥事について弁解に追われた。— The company president was kept busy making excuses about the scandal.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 弁解 and 言い訳 are very close and often interchangeable, but 弁解 can sound slightly more formal/serious (used for scandals, misconduct) while 言い訳 is used in everyday, even trivial contexts (a child's excuse for not doing homework) — both usually carry a skeptical, "this is just an excuse" undertone rather than a neutral "explanation."

## 群がる 【むらがる】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To swarm, crowd, cluster together (often used for crowds of people, animals, or insects gathering densely).

**Similar words:** 集まる（あつまる）— to gather, the neutral, general verb for people/things coming together, whereas 群がる specifically implies a dense, swarming, somewhat chaotic or instinctive clustering (often with a slightly negative or animalistic connotation, like insects swarming or people crowding greedily); 殺到する（さっとうする）— to rush/flood in (toward something), emphasizes the rapid, overwhelming rush of a crowd toward a single point, often used for high demand situations.

**Opposite words:** 散らばる（ちらばる）— to scatter, disperse.

1. 甘い蜜にアリが群がっていた。— Ants were swarming around the sweet nectar.
2. ファンが芸能人の周りに群がった。— Fans swarmed around the celebrity.
3. 人々は無料のサンプルに群がった。— People crowded around the free samples.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 群がる often carries a slightly negative, instinctive, or undignified connotation (like insects around food, or people scrambling greedily for something free) — learners should be cautious using it for a dignified, orderly gathering (like an audience at a concert), where 集まる is more appropriate and neutral.

## 報いる 【むくいる】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To repay, reward, requite (an act of kindness, effort, or sometimes a wrong — as in revenge).

**Similar words:** 恩返しをする（おんがえしをする）— to repay a favor/kindness, a more everyday, warmer phrase specifically about returning gratitude for a kindness received, whereas 報いる is more formal/literary and can apply more broadly, including repaying effort with results (努力に報いる "reward one's efforts") or even repaying wrongdoing (仇を報いる "take revenge"); 応える（こたえる）— to respond to/live up to (expectations, trust), overlapping when used for "meeting" someone's hopes, though less about direct repayment.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼の努力はついに報われた。— His efforts were finally rewarded.
2. 恩人の親切に報いたいと思っている。— I want to repay my benefactor's kindness.
3. 期待に報いるため、全力を尽くした。— I gave my all in order to live up to (repay) the expectations placed on me.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 報われる (the passive form, "to be rewarded") is extremely common in everyday speech about effort paying off (努力が報われる), while the active 報いる (to actively repay/reward someone) is more formal/literary — learners often encounter the passive form far more frequently and should recognize both forms as related but used in different contexts.

## 恵まれる 【めぐまれる】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To be blessed, fortunate (with good circumstances, talent, resources, or environment).

**Similar words:** 幸運な（こううんな）— lucky/fortunate, more general, can apply to a single lucky event or outcome, whereas 恵まれる implies an ongoing, often structural blessing (being blessed with good health, talent, family, environment — resources one possesses rather than just one lucky occurrence); 裕福な（ゆうふくな）— wealthy, specifically about financial abundance, a narrower subset of what 恵まれる can describe.

**Opposite words:** 恵まれない（めぐまれない）— underprivileged, not blessed, disadvantaged.

**Example sentences:**

3. 恵まれない子供たちを支援する団体がある。— There are organizations that support underprivileged children.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 恵まれる is always passive in form (〜に恵まれる, "to be blessed with X") and describes possessing favorable, often innate or environmental conditions (talent, health, family, nature) — learners sometimes try to use it actively or for a single lucky event, but it's reserved for describing an ongoing state of good fortune/resources.

## 巡り 【めぐり】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Going around, touring, circulation (e.g., of blood, seasons, or visiting a series of places, as in 観光巡り "sightseeing tour").

**Similar words:** 循環（じゅんかん）— circulation, a more formal/technical term for cyclical circulation (血液循環 "blood circulation"), whereas 巡り is used more broadly, including casual touring of multiple locations (寺巡り "temple-hopping tour") as well as abstract cycles (季節の巡り "the cycling of the seasons"); ツアー — the loanword "tour," a modern, commercial term for organized sightseeing trips, narrower in scope than 巡り.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 京都の寺院巡りを楽しんだ。— I enjoyed touring around the temples of Kyoto.
2. 血の巡りが悪いと冷え性になりやすい。— Poor blood circulation can easily lead to chronic coldness.
3. 季節の巡りを感じる美しい庭園だ。— It's a beautiful garden where you can feel the cycling of the seasons.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 巡り appears in both concrete touring contexts (visiting a series of places, 神社巡り "shrine-hopping") and abstract cyclical contexts (circulation of blood, seasons cycling) — learners sometimes only learn one sense (e.g., just "touring") and are confused by phrases like 血の巡り, which has nothing to do with sightseeing.

## 妥協 【だきょう】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Compromise (reaching a middle ground by each side yielding something).

**Similar words:** 譲歩（じょうほう）— concession, specifically refers to one side yielding/giving ground, which can be a one-sided act, whereas 妥協 implies a mutual process where both sides adjust to reach an agreement; 和解（わかい）— reconciliation/settlement, focuses more on resolving a conflict or dispute and restoring good relations, which may or may not involve a 妥協 in terms/positions.

**Opposite words:** 対立（たいりつ）— confrontation, opposition.

**Example sentences:**
1. 両者は最終的に妥協点を見つけた。— The two parties finally found a point of compromise.
2. 彼は自分の意見を曲げず、妥協しなかった。— He didn't bend his opinion and refused to compromise.
3. ビジネスでは時に妥協も必要だ。— In business, compromise is sometimes necessary.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 妥協 implies a two-way process where both parties give something up to reach agreement, whereas 譲歩 can describe just one party's unilateral concession — learners sometimes use them interchangeably, but 妥協点 ("point of compromise") specifically implies mutual adjustment, not one-sided yielding.

## 修行 【しゅぎょう】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Training, ascetic practice, spiritual/disciplinary training (often religious, martial, or skill-based, implying rigorous, sustained effort).

**Similar words:** 練習（れんしゅう）— practice, the everyday, neutral word for practicing a skill (sports, music), without 修行's connotation of rigorous, often spiritual or character-building discipline; 訓練（くんれん）— training, more systematic/technical training (e.g., military or job training), practical and goal-oriented, lacking the ascetic/spiritual dimension that 修行 often carries (especially in Buddhist or martial-arts contexts).

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 修行 carries connotations of rigorous, character-building discipline often linked to spiritual, religious, or traditional craft contexts (martial arts, cooking apprenticeships, Buddhist practice) — learners sometimes use it interchangeably with plain 練習, but 修行 implies a deeper, more transformative, often arduous process than simple skill practice.

## 取り扱い 【とりあつかい】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Handling, treatment (of goods, items, or matters — both physical handling and procedural/administrative treatment).

## 模様 【もよう】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Pattern, design (on fabric, surfaces); also used to describe "the look of things"/situation, as in 〜模様 "it appears that...".

**Similar words:** パターン — the loanword "pattern," often used for repeating designs or patterns in a more technical/modern sense (e.g., behavioral patterns), whereas 模様 is the native word commonly used for visual/decorative patterns on objects (着物の模様 "kimono pattern"); デザイン — "design," broader, referring to overall aesthetic design rather than just a repeating visual pattern.

**Opposite words:** 無地（むじ）— plain, solid-colored (without pattern).

**Example sentences:**
1. この着物には美しい花の模様が描かれている。— This kimono has a beautiful floral pattern on it.
2. 空には不思議な模様の雲が広がっていた。— Strangely patterned clouds spread across the sky.
3. 明日は雨模様の天気になりそうだ。— It looks like tomorrow's weather will be rainy.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Besides describing visual patterns, 模様 has a secondary, common usage in news/weather reports as a suffix meaning "it appears that..." (雨模様 "looking like rain," 混雑模様 "appears to be crowded") — learners who only know the "pattern/design" meaning are often puzzled by this situational-description usage.

## 葬る 【ほうむる】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To bury (a person, i.e., hold a funeral/burial); figuratively, to bury/suppress (a scandal, plan, or fact, consigning it to oblivion).

**Similar words:** 埋める（うめる）— to bury (literally, covering something with earth), a more general, literal verb for burying objects or even bodies in a basic physical sense, whereas 葬る specifically implies a formal funeral/burial rite for a person, and extends figuratively to "burying" something metaphorically (事件を闇に葬る "bury an incident in darkness"); 埋葬する（まいそうする）— to inter/bury (a body), a more formal, technical synonym specifically for the burial of the deceased.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は故郷の墓地に葬られた。— He was buried in his hometown's cemetery.
2. その疑惑は結局、闇に葬られた。— That suspicion ended up being buried in the dark (hushed up/suppressed).
3. 政治家のスキャンダルは密かに葬られようとしていた。— The politician's scandal was being quietly buried/covered up.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 葬る has a strong, common figurative use meaning "to cover up/suppress" (often a scandal or inconvenient truth, especially in the set phrase 闇に葬る "bury in darkness") — learners who only know the literal "funeral burial" meaning often miss this frequent, somewhat dramatic figurative sense in news and political contexts.

## 避ける 【さける】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To avoid (a person, situation, topic, or danger).

**Similar words:** 逃げる（にげる）— to flee/run away, implies physically escaping from something (often danger or responsibility), more active/physical than 避ける, which can be as simple as choosing not to engage with a topic or person; 回避する（かいひする）— to avoid/evade, a more formal, often abstract synonym used in written/business contexts (リスクを回避する "avoid risk"), close in meaning to 避ける but more formal in register.

**Opposite words:** 直面する（ちょくめんする）— to face/confront.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女はその話題を避けているようだった。— She seemed to be avoiding that topic.
2. 混雑を避けるため、早めに出発した。— I left early to avoid the crowds.
3. 対立を避けるために妥協した。— I compromised in order to avoid confrontation.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 避ける is versatile across physical avoidance (avoiding crowds, obstacles), social avoidance (avoiding a person or topic), and abstract avoidance (avoiding risk, conflict) — its broad applicability sometimes leads learners to overuse it where a more specific verb (逃げる for fleeing, 回避する for formal risk avoidance) would be more precise or natural in context.

## ややこしい 【ややこしい】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Complicated, convoluted, confusingly intricate (often with a frustrated or exasperated nuance).

**Similar words:** 複雑な（ふくざつな）— complex, a more neutral, formal word for complexity (a complex system, complex emotions), whereas ややこしい is colloquial and often carries a note of exasperation or frustration at the confusing nature of something (often procedures, explanations, or relationships); 面倒な（めんどうな）— troublesome/bothersome, emphasizes the hassle/burden of dealing with something, which may or may not involve actual complexity.

**Opposite words:** 単純な（たんじゅんな）— simple.

**Example sentences:**
1. この手続きはとてもややこしい。— This procedure is very complicated.
2. 話がややこしくなってきたので整理しよう。— The story has gotten confusing, so let's organize it.
3. 人間関係がややこしくて疲れる。— Human relationships are complicated and tiring.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ややこしい is casual/colloquial and carries an emotional undertone of frustration or bewilderment at needless complexity, unlike the neutral, more formal 複雑な — learners should reserve ややこしい for conversational contexts (complaining about confusing paperwork, convoluted plots) rather than formal/academic writing, where 複雑 is more appropriate.

## 憂鬱 【ゆううつ】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Melancholy, depression, a gloomy/dispirited mood (can range from mild blues to describing clinical depression in context).

**Similar words:** 落ち込む（おちこむ）— to feel down/depressed, a verb describing the act of becoming low in spirits, often tied to a specific cause, whereas 憂鬱 is a noun/na-adjective describing a more pervasive, sometimes causeless gloomy mood or atmosphere (月曜日は憂鬱だ "Mondays are depressing"); ブルー — the loanword "blue" (as in "feeling blue"), a casual, lighter way to express feeling down, often used by younger speakers.

**Opposite words:** 陽気（ようき）— cheerful, high-spirited.

**Example sentences:**
1. 月曜日の朝はいつも憂鬱な気分になる。— I always feel melancholy on Monday mornings.
2. 彼は将来について憂鬱な気持ちを抱えていた。— He harbored a gloomy feeling about the future.
3. 雨続きの天気が憂鬱さを増している。— The continuous rainy weather is adding to the gloominess.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 憂鬱 can describe both a mild, everyday low mood (dreading Monday) and a more serious, sustained depressive state depending on context — learners should gauge severity from context rather than assuming it always signals clinical depression; conversely, it shouldn't be used too lightly when discussing an actual diagnosed condition, where うつ病 (clinical depression) is the precise term.

## 勇敢 【ゆうかん】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Brave, courageous (describing bold, fearless action or character).

**Similar words:** 勇気がある（ゆうきがある）— to have courage, a more everyday phrase describing someone's courageous quality in general, whereas 勇敢 is a more formal adjective often used to describe a specific brave act or a person's demonstrated bravery in a dramatic situation (勇敢に戦う "fight bravely"); 大胆な（だいたんな）— bold/daring, emphasizes boldness/audacity in taking risks, which may or may not involve the noble, often selfless connotation that 勇敢 carries.

**Opposite words:** 臆病な（おくびょうな）— cowardly, timid.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は勇敢に敵に立ち向かった。— He bravely stood up to the enemy.
2. 勇敢な消防士たちが多くの命を救った。— The brave firefighters saved many lives.
3. その少女は勇敢にも真実を話した。— That girl bravely told the truth.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 勇敢 typically carries a noble, admirable connotation (often associated with selfless or heroic acts, like firefighters, soldiers, or standing up for what's right) — learners sometimes use it for simple boldness/risk-taking without that heroic dimension, where 大胆 would be more fitting.

## 専ら 【もっぱら】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Solely, exclusively, mainly (devoting oneself to one thing in particular; also "the word on the street is...").

**Similar words:** ひたすら — single-mindedly, devotedly, emphasizes wholehearted focus/effort on one activity (ひたすら勉強する "study single-mindedly"), with more emotional intensity than 専ら, which is more neutral/factual about exclusivity; もっぱら〜らしい/という噂だ is also an idiomatic pattern meaning "rumor has it that..." — a distinct usage from the "exclusively" sense, which learners often miss; 主に（おもに）— mainly, a more neutral, common word for "mainly/primarily," without 専ら's stronger nuance of near-total exclusivity.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は専ら仕事に打ち込んでいる。— He devotes himself solely to his work.
2. 休日は専ら読書をして過ごしている。— I spend my holidays solely reading.
3. 二人は離婚するというのが専らの噂だ。— The rumor going around is that the two of them are getting divorced.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Besides meaning "exclusively/mainly," 専ら has a distinct idiomatic use meaning "it's rumored that..." (専らの噂 "the prevailing rumor") — learners who only learn the "exclusively" sense are often confused encountering this gossip-related usage, which has a different flavor entirely.

## 見積り 【みつもり】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Estimate, quotation (a calculated approximation of cost, time, or quantity, especially in business).

**Similar words:** 予算（よさん）— budget, refers to the planned/allocated amount of money for a project, distinct from 見積り, which is a calculated prediction/quote of what something will cost before the work/purchase happens; 概算（がいさん）— rough estimate/approximation, implies a less precise, "ballpark" calculation, whereas 見積り (especially 見積書 "written quotation") often implies a more formal, itemized figure provided by a business.

**Opposite words:** 実績（じっせき）— actual results/figures (as a contrasting concept to a prior estimate).

**Example sentences:**
1. 工事費用の見積りを業者に依頼した。— I asked the contractor for an estimate of the construction cost.
2. 見積りよりも実際の費用が高くついた。— The actual cost ended up higher than the estimate.
3. 複数の会社から見積りを取って比較した。— I got quotes from multiple companies and compared them.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 見積り specifically refers to a pre-calculated prediction of cost/time provided before work begins (especially in business, as a formal quotation document 見積書) — learners sometimes confuse it with 予算 (the budget, i.e., the amount one plans to spend), which is a different concept (one's own spending plan vs. a vendor's price quote).

## 源 【みなもと】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Source, origin, fountainhead (of a river, or figuratively of an idea, problem, or tradition).

**Similar words:** 起源（きげん）— origin, a more formal/abstract word for the historical beginning of something (起源は古代にさかのぼる "the origin dates back to ancient times"), whereas 源 is often used more concretely (the source of a river) as well as figuratively for the root cause/wellspring of something (力の源 "source of strength"); 根源（こんげん）— root cause/fundamental source, emphasizes the deepest, most fundamental origin of a problem or phenomenon, often more formal/serious than 源.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. この川の源は山奥にある。— The source of this river is deep in the mountains.
2. 愛情は彼の力の源だった。— Love was the source of his strength.
3. 問題の源を突き止める必要がある。— It's necessary to identify the source of the problem.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 源 is commonly used both literally (a river's headwaters) and figuratively (the "source" of strength, inspiration, or income, 収入の源 "source of income") — learners sometimes only learn the literal geographic meaning and miss its very frequent figurative extension to abstract "wellsprings" of emotion, energy, or resources.

## 未練 【みれん】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Lingering attachment/regret, reluctance to let go (especially of a past relationship, decision, or lost opportunity).

**Similar words:** 後悔（こうかい）— regret, focuses on regretting a past action/decision itself, whereas 未練 is specifically about an ongoing emotional attachment that makes it hard to move on (often romantic, like lingering feelings for an ex), not necessarily regret over having done something wrong; 執着（しゅうちゃく）— attachment/obsession, broader and can apply to objects, ideas, or outcomes, implying a stronger, sometimes unhealthy fixation, whereas 未練 specifically implies lingering feelings about something already lost/ended.

**Opposite words:** 諦め（あきらめ）— resignation, acceptance of letting go.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女にまだ未練があるみたいだ。— It seems he still has lingering feelings for her (his ex).
2. 未練を断ち切って新しい一歩を踏み出した。— He cut off his lingering attachment and took a new step forward.
3. 過去の仕事に未練はない。— I have no lingering regret about my past job.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 未練 specifically describes an emotional difficulty letting go of something already over (a past relationship, a decision already made, an opportunity already lost) — it's distinct from 後悔 (regretting that you made a choice at all); learners often conflate the two, but one can have 未練 without 後悔 (e.g., missing an ex without regretting the breakup) and vice versa.

## 目盛り 【めもり】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Scale marking, graduation (the marked increments on a measuring instrument, like a ruler, thermometer, or gauge).

**Similar words:** 単位（たんい）— unit (of measurement), refers to the unit itself (meters, degrees) rather than the physical markings on an instrument, whereas 目盛り specifically refers to the visible tick marks/gradations; スケール — the loanword "scale," can refer to a measuring scale/ruler itself or metaphorically to the scale/size of something, broader than the specific "markings" sense of 目盛り.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. この温度計の目盛りは細かくて読みやすい。— This thermometer's scale markings are fine and easy to read.
2. 定規の目盛りをよく見て長さを測った。— I carefully looked at the ruler's markings and measured the length.
3. グラフの目盛りの間隔を調整した。— I adjusted the interval of the graph's scale markings.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 目盛り specifically refers to the physical graduation marks on an instrument (not the unit of measurement itself, nor the overall size/scale of something) — learners sometimes confuse it with スケール in its "overall scale/size" sense, but 目盛り is narrowly about the marked increments used for reading a measurement.

## 盲点 【もうてん】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Blind spot (both the literal physiological blind spot in vision, and figuratively, an overlooked weakness or gap in reasoning/planning).

**Similar words:** 弱点（じゃくてん）— weak point/weakness, a more general term for any vulnerability or shortcoming, whereas 盲点 specifically implies something that is overlooked or unnoticed precisely because it's outside one's normal field of awareness (like a true blind spot), not just any weakness one might already know about; 落とし穴（おとしあな）— pitfall/trap, emphasizes a hidden danger that one might fall into unexpectedly, close to 盲点 in implying something unseen, but more about a hazard than simply an area of unawareness.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼の計画には大きな盲点があった。— There was a major blind spot in his plan.
2. バックミラーだけでは運転の盲点をカバーできない。— The rearview mirror alone can't cover the blind spots while driving.
3. 専門家でも意外な盲点を見落とすことがある。— Even experts sometimes overlook surprising blind spots.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 盲点 specifically implies something overlooked precisely *because* it falls outside normal awareness/attention (an inherent gap in perspective), not simply any known weakness — learners sometimes use it interchangeably with 弱点, but a 盲点 is, by definition, something one didn't realize was a problem until it was pointed out.

## 要因 【よういん】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Primary factor, (contributing) cause (of an event, outcome, or phenomenon, especially in analytical/academic contexts).

**Similar words:** 原因（げんいん）— cause, the more general, everyday word for the cause of something, often implying a direct, singular cause-effect relationship, whereas 要因 is more academic/analytical and typically refers to one of several contributing factors in a complex situation (success/failure often has multiple 要因 rather than one single 原因); 理由（りゆう）— reason, focuses on the justification/explanation for an action or decision, a different angle from 要因's focus on causal factors behind an outcome.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. この成功の要因はいくつか考えられる。— Several factors can be considered behind this success.
2. 少子化の要因は複雑に絡み合っている。— The factors behind the declining birthrate are intricately intertwined.
3. 事故の要因を徹底的に調査した。— The factors behind the accident were thoroughly investigated.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 要因 implies one of potentially multiple contributing factors in a complex causal picture (commonly used in the plural sense, 複数の要因), whereas 原因 often suggests a more direct, singular cause — learners analyzing complex phenomena (social, economic, scientific) should favor 要因 over 原因 when multiple interacting causes are being discussed.

## 余暇 【よか】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Leisure hours, free time, spare time (time not occupied by work or obligations).

**Similar words:** 暇（ひま）— free time, spare time, a casual, everyday word for having nothing to do/being free, whereas 余暇 is more formal/written, often used in sociological or lifestyle discussions (余暇の過ごし方 "how one spends one's leisure time"); 自由時間（じゆうじかん）— free time, a more literal, neutral compound often used in scheduling contexts (school/work), close to 余暇 but slightly more concrete/practical in tone.

**Opposite words:** 労働時間（ろうどうじかん）— working hours.

**Example sentences:**
1. 余暇を利用して資格の勉強をしている。— I'm using my leisure time to study for a certification.
2. 現代人は余暇の過ごし方に悩むことが多い。— Modern people often struggle with how to spend their leisure time.
3. 余暇活動として登山を始めた。— I took up mountain climbing as a leisure activity.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 余暇 is a somewhat formal/written term often used in discussions about lifestyle, work-life balance, or leisure industries — in casual conversation about having free time right now, 暇 is the natural, everyday word; learners sometimes use 余暇 in casual speech where it sounds slightly stiff.

## 滅亡 【めつぼう】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Extinction, ruin, downfall (typically of a nation, civilization, species, or dynasty — a dramatic, often historical collapse).

**Similar words:** 絶滅（ぜつめつ）— extinction, specifically used for the extinction of species (絶滅危惧種 "endangered species"), narrower than 滅亡, which applies to nations, empires, and civilizations rather than biological species; 崩壊（ほうかい）— collapse, can apply more broadly to systems, buildings, or structures collapsing, and doesn't necessarily imply complete disappearance/extinction the way 滅亡 does.

**Opposite words:** 繁栄（はんえい）— prosperity, flourishing.

**Example sentences:**
1. ローマ帝国はやがて滅亡した。— The Roman Empire eventually fell (met its ruin).
2. 恐竜は隕石の衝突によって滅亡したと言われている。— Dinosaurs are said to have gone extinct due to a meteor impact.
3. その王朝は内乱によって滅亡への道をたどった。— That dynasty followed a path toward ruin due to internal strife.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 滅亡 is specifically used for the complete downfall/disappearance of a nation, empire, dynasty, or (in a broader, more dramatic sense) a species — it's not used for smaller-scale failures (a company going bankrupt would use 倒産, not 滅亡); learners should reserve it for large-scale, historically dramatic collapse.

## 誘惑 【ゆうわく】
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Temptation (an enticement to do something, often something one knows one shouldn't).

**Similar words:** 魅力（みりょく）— charm/attractiveness, describes the appealing quality itself without necessarily implying a moral conflict, whereas 誘惑 specifically implies being drawn toward something one should perhaps resist (a temptation with a hint of moral or practical risk, like 誘惑に負ける "give in to temptation"); 甘い言葉（あまいことば）— sweet words, refers specifically to enticing/flattering verbal persuasion used to tempt someone, a more specific method of 誘惑 rather than the concept itself.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. ダイエット中なのに甘いものの誘惑に負けてしまった。— Even though I'm on a diet, I gave in to the temptation of sweets.
2. 彼は怪しい儲け話の誘惑に乗ってしまった。— He fell for the temptation of a suspicious money-making scheme.
3. 誘惑に打ち勝って勉強を続けた。— I overcame temptation and kept studying.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 誘惑 almost always implies a pull toward something one should resist for one's own good (diet, discipline, morality, safety) — distinct from 魅力, which is neutral "attractiveness/charm" without that moral tension; learners sometimes use 魅力 when describing resisting something, where 誘惑 (with 〜に負ける/打ち勝つ, "give in to/overcome temptation") is the natural collocation.
