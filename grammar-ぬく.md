# ～ぬく {#ぬく}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～抜く

**Furigana form:** ～ぬく

**Meaning:** "To do X all the way through / to the very end" — attached to a verb stem, expresses completing a difficult or demanding action thoroughly, persevering until the end despite hardship.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ます形 (stem) + 抜く (例: 生き抜く、走り抜く、やり抜く、考え抜く)
- Conjugates as a regular Godan verb: 抜く／抜いた／抜いて／抜かない, etc.

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal, used in both speech and writing; common in motivational contexts, biographies, sports narration, and stories of perseverance.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **〜通す (とおす)** — similar "do X all the way through," often used for maintaining a stance or attitude consistently (言い張り通す), slightly different nuance of consistency vs. endurance.
- **〜切る (きる)** — "do X completely," emphasizes completion/exhaustion of the action itself (疲れ切る, 使い切る) rather than enduring hardship.
- **最後まで〜する** — plain paraphrase "do X until the end," less idiomatic but clarifies the meaning.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though 〜かける ("to start doing X but leave it incomplete") contrasts in spirit.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は戦争の時代を生き抜いた。
   (He survived through the era of war, enduring to the end.)
2. マラソンの最後まで彼女は走り抜いた。
   (She ran all the way to the end of the marathon.)
3. どんなに辛くても、最後までやり抜くと決めた。
   (No matter how hard it is, I've decided to see it through to the end.)
4. その問題について、一晩中考え抜いた。
   (I thought the problem through thoroughly all night long.)
5. 彼は困難な時期を耐え抜いて、今の成功を手に入れた。
   (He endured through the difficult period and achieved his current success.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 抜く specifically conveys perseverance through difficulty or hardship — it is not simply "to finish" (that's closer to 切る). Learners sometimes misuse 抜く for trivial, easy completions where 切る or 終える would be more natural; 抜く implies the action was arduous and required endurance.
