# ～ことはない {#ことはない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ことはない

**Furigana form:** ～ことはない (no special kanji reading)

**Meaning:** "There's no need to do X / you don't have to do X" — reassures the listener that an action is unnecessary, often used to comfort or ease worry.

**Conjugation:**
- Verb (dictionary form) + ことはない
- 心配することはない (There's no need to worry)

**Typical context:** Spoken, reassuring/comforting tone; used between friends, by seniors to juniors, or in advice-giving contexts.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **なくてもいい** — "it's fine not to do X," more neutral, less reassuring in tone.
- **必要はない** — "there is no necessity," more formal/direct statement of lack of necessity.
- **には及ばない** — formal equivalent ("need not go so far as to..."), used in written/polite contexts.

**Opposite grammar structures:** **なければならない / なくてはいけない** — "must do X," expresses obligation, the direct opposite of ことはない's "no need."

**Example sentences:**
1. そんなに謝ることはないよ、気にしていないから。
   (There's no need to apologize so much — I'm not bothered by it.)
2. 一度失敗したくらいで、落ち込むことはない。
   (There's no need to feel down just because you failed once.)
3. わざわざ来ることはなかったのに。
   (You didn't have to go out of your way to come.)
4. 彼の意見に従うことはない、自分の考えを大事にしなさい。
   (There's no need to follow his opinion — value your own thinking.)
5. 今から急いで行くことはない、まだ時間があるから。
   (There's no need to rush there now — there's still time.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ことはない reassures that something is unnecessary, often with an implied emotional comfort (don't worry/don't bother) — it is NOT the same as ことがない ("have never done X" / "there is no such thing as X"), which learners sometimes confuse due to the similar sound.
