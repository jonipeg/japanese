# ～にこしたことはない {#にこしたことはない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～に越したことはない

**Furigana form:** ～にこしたことはない

**Meaning:** "It's best to X / nothing beats X" — recommends a particular choice as clearly the best or safest option, though not strictly mandatory.

**Conjugation:**
- 普通形（動詞／い形容詞／な形容詞＋である／名詞＋である）+ に越したことはない
- Example: 用心する → 用心するに越したことはない

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal; usable in both speech and writing, common in advice-giving contexts. Polite and mild — presents a recommendation rather than an obligation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **ほうがいい** — plain "it's better to X"; more casual and direct, while に越したことはない carries a slightly more formal, idiomatic "nothing could possibly beat this option" nuance.
- **にかぎる** — "nothing beats X / X is best" for a specific preference (often about comfort/pleasure, e.g., 疲れた時は寝るに限る); very close in meaning, but にかぎる leans toward personal preference/recommendation, while に越したことはない leans toward prudent, safety-oriented advice.
- [**べきだ**](grammar-べきだ.md) — "should do X"; expresses a stronger normative obligation, whereas に越したことはない is softer, merely noting that an option is ideal, not obligatory.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; conceptually ～なくてもいい ("it's fine even without X") downplays the necessity that に越したことはない emphasizes as ideal.

**Example sentences:**
1. 体は丈夫であるに越したことはありません。 (There's nothing better than having a strong, healthy body.)
2. 旅行には保険に入っておくに越したことはない。 (It's best to get insurance before traveling.)
3. お金は多いに越したことはないが、幸せとは限らない。 (More money is certainly better, but it doesn't always mean happiness.)
4. 面接には早めに着いておくに越したことはない。 (It's best to arrive early for an interview.)
5. 災害に備えて、水と食料を蓄えておくに越したことはない。 (It's best to stock up on water and food in preparation for a disaster.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** に越したことはない expresses "this is ideal/best," not "this is mandatory" — it is often followed by a softening clause like が／けれども to acknowledge that the ideal option isn't always achievable (as in example 3). Learners sometimes mistakenly treat it as equivalent to a strong obligation like べきだ or なければならない, but its tone is advisory and mild, acknowledging that other options, while less ideal, remain acceptable.
