# だから {#だから}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** だから（漢字なし／平仮名のみ）

**Furigana form:** だから

**Meaning:** "So, therefore, that's why" — a casual conjunction stating that the preceding statement is the reason/cause for what follows.

**Conjugation:** Sentence-initial conjunction, no conjugation needed — attaches at the sentence boundary, connecting the previous sentence to the next. It can also appear mid-sentence after a clause ending in plain/だ form (e.g. 雨だから、行かない) to directly link a reason-clause to its result within a single sentence.

**Typical context:** Casual, spoken register — used constantly in everyday conversation between friends, family, and casual acquaintances. In writing, it appears in casual text messages, blogs, diary entries, or quoted dialogue, but it reads as too blunt/informal for essays, business writing, or academic papers, where したがって or そのため would replace it.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**それで**](grammar-それで.md) — casual-neutral, spoken; narrates a reason/result more as "and so, that's how it happened," slightly softer and more narrative than だから's direct causal assertion.
- [**そのため**](grammar-そのため.md) — neutral-formal, written; a step up in formality from だから, common in explanatory writing.
- [**したがって**](grammar-したがって.md) — formal, written/business/academic; the stiff, logical "therefore" used in reports and formal argumentation.
- [**それゆえ**](grammar-それゆえ.md) — very formal, literary/written; archaic-feeling, found in essays, legal/philosophical text.
- [**だって**](grammar-だって.md) — very casual, spoken; unlike だから's neutral statement of cause, だって carries a petulant, excuse-making, child-like tone, often pushing back against something just said.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite in the sense of negating causation, but its contrastive counterpart would be a concessive conjunction like それでも ("even so") or でも ("but"), which denies that the expected consequence follows.

**Example sentences:**
1. 今日は雨だから、傘を持って行った方がいいよ。
   (It's raining today, so you'd better bring an umbrella.)
2. もう遅いから、そろそろ帰ろう。
   (It's already late, so let's head home soon.)
3. 彼は忙しいから、今日は会えないと思う。
   (He's busy, so I don't think we can meet today.)
4. お金がないから、旅行には行けない。
   (I don't have money, so I can't go on the trip.)
5. 疲れたから、今日は早く寝る。
   (I'm tired, so I'm going to sleep early today.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The most common mistake is using だから in formal written contexts (business emails, reports, essays) where したがって or そのため is expected — だから sounds jarringly casual and even a bit childish in such settings, similar to writing "so" instead of "therefore" in an English business memo. Learners should also avoid starting a reply to a superior's question with だから, since in spoken Japanese だから can sound impatient or argumentative ("that's exactly why...") if used defensively; それで or ですから softens this.
