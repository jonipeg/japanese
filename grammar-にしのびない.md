# ～にしのびない {#にしのびない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～に忍びない

**Furigana form:** ～にしのびない

**Meaning:** "Can't bring myself to X / can't bear to X" — expresses emotional reluctance to do something, usually because of attachment or sympathy, even though it may be rational or necessary.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形 + に忍びない
- Example base: 捨てる → 捨てるに忍びない

**Typical context:** Formal, written/literary register; conveys a refined, somewhat emotional reluctance. Common in narrative or reflective writing describing sentimental hesitation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**にたえない**](grammar-にたえる・にたえない.md) — "can't bear X" (also used for strong emotion, e.g., 見るに堪えない "unbearable to watch"); very close in meaning, though にたえない can also mean "cannot withstand/endure" in a more physical sense, while に忍びない is specifically about emotional reluctance rooted in attachment or compassion.
- **たくない** — plain "don't want to do X"; a simple desire negation, lacking に忍びない's specific "I know I should, but my feelings won't let me" nuance.
- **にしのびず** — the literary -zu negative form of 忍びない, used in formal written connector position rather than sentence-final.

**Opposite grammar structures:** 平気で～する ("do X without any hesitation/feeling") expresses the reverse — acting without emotional reluctance.

**Example sentences:**
1. これは汚れているけれど思い出の物だから、捨てるに忍びないなあ。 (It's dirty, but it's something full of memories, so I can't bring myself to throw it away.)
2. 彼の悲しそうな顔を見るに忍びなかった。 (I couldn't bear to look at his sad face.)
3. 長年連れ添った愛犬を手放すには忍びない。 (I can't bring myself to give up the dog I've been with for so many years.)
4. この古い家を取り壊すのは忍びない。 (I can't bear to have this old house torn down.)
5. 彼女に真実を告げるのは忍びなかったが、仕方なかった。 (I couldn't bear to tell her the truth, but there was no choice.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** に忍びない is reserved for situations involving genuine emotional attachment or compassion (old belongings, pets, a person's feelings) — it sounds odd applied to mundane, emotionally neutral tasks. Learners should also note 忍ぶ (しのぶ) normally means "to endure/bear patiently," so に忍びない literally means "cannot bear [doing this]" — distinguishing it from unrelated homophone-adjacent words like 偲ぶ (しのぶ, "to reminisce fondly about someone"), which shares a reading but different kanji and meaning.
