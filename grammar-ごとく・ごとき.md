# ～ごとく／～ごとき {#ごとく・ごとき}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～ごとく／～ごとき

**Furigana form:** ～ごとく／～ごとき

**Meaning:** Classical/literary equivalent of ～ように ("as if, like," adverbial) and ～ような ("like a ~," attributive) — expresses similarity or manner in a formal, bookish register.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形／た形 + (が)ごとく(に) — adverbial, modifies a verb
- 動詞／名詞 + の + ごとき + 名詞 — attributive, modifies a noun
- 下記のごとく — "as follows (below)"; 裏切るごとき行為 — "an act like betrayal"

**Typical context:** Written, classical/literary register; found in formal documents (下記のごとく in business/legal writing), literature, and speeches. Sounds stiff or archaic in everyday conversation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～ように／～ような** — the modern, neutral-register equivalent used in everyday speech and writing; ごとく／ごとき are simply their more formal, literary counterparts.
- **～がごとし** — the classical sentence-final form ("as if it were"), from which ごとく (continuative) and ごとき (attributive) derive.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; the plain modern forms ～ように／～ような serve as the neutral-register counterpart rather than an antonym.

**Example sentences:**
1. 下記のごとく、規則を定める。
   (The rules are established as follows below.)
2. 裏切るごとき行為は許されない。
   (An act of betrayal like that cannot be forgiven.)
3. 彼は何事もなかったかのごとく、平然と仕事を続けた。
   (He calmly continued working as if nothing had happened.)
4. 彼女はまるで女王であるかのごとき態度で振る舞った。
   (She behaved with an attitude as if she were a queen.)
5. 前述のごとく、今回の計画には多くの課題が残されている。
   (As stated above, many issues remain in this plan.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ごとく is the adverbial/continuative form that modifies verbs (parallel to ～ように), while ごとき is the attributive form that modifies nouns (parallel to ～ような) — keep the two roles straight. Because this is a classical, bookish pattern, overusing it in casual conversation sounds unnaturally stiff or pretentious.
