# ～（か）と思うと／～（か）と思ったら {#（か）と思うと・思ったら}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～（か）と思うと／～（か）と思ったら

**Furigana form:** ～（か）とおもうと／～（か）とおもったら

**Meaning:** "No sooner had X happened than Y" / "Just as X happened, Y happened" — describes two events occurring in rapid, often surprising succession, as observed from outside.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞た形 + （か）と思うと／（か）と思ったら
- 泣いたかと思うと笑っている — "was crying, and the next moment is already laughing"

**Typical context:** Written and spoken, neutral narrative register — common in storytelling, news descriptions of sudden events, and everyday anecdotes about surprising situations.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **か～ないかのうちに** — also expresses "as soon as," but with even stronger emphasis on near-simultaneity; more formal/literary.
- **とたんに** — "the instant that," stronger immediacy, frequently paired with a surprising or negative result.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a phrase like ～てからしばらくして ("some time after X") would instead describe a delayed, non-immediate sequence.

**Example sentences:**
1. 空が暗くなったと思うと、突然雨が降り出した。
   (No sooner had the sky gone dark than it suddenly started raining.)
2. 子供は泣いたかと思うと、もう笑っている。
   (The child was crying one moment, and the next he's already laughing.)
3. 彼女は部屋に入ったと思ったら、すぐに出て行った。
   (She'd no sooner entered the room than she left again.)
4. 電話が鳴ったかと思うと、切れてしまった。
   (The phone rang, and then it immediately cut off.)
5. 試合が始まったと思ったら、もう点が入った。
   (The match had just started when a point was already scored.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern describes external, often unexpected events — it isn't natural for describing one's own deliberate, sequential actions ("I thought X, so I did Y" is a different meaning of 思ったら). Keep the subject in the third person for the most natural usage.
