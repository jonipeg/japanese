# ～ならいざしらず {#ならいざしらず}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ならいざ知らず

**Furigana form:** ～ならいざ知(し)らず

**Meaning:** "That may be one thing, but..." — sets up a contrast where the first-mentioned case (often a past or hypothetical situation) is conceded as possibly true/acceptable, but the actual present/real case is different and treated more seriously or critically.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 (noun) + はいざ知らず／ならいざ知らず
- Both はいざ知らず and ならいざ知らず forms are attested; は is slightly more common in the "conceding X, but Y" sense, while なら explicitly frames X as a hypothetical condition being conceded.

**Typical context:** Formal, written/literary register; often appears in persuasive argumentation or scolding (e.g., "if you were a child, fine, but as an adult this is different"). Not rude per se, but the implied contrast often carries a critical edge toward the real, more serious case being discussed.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **をぜんていに (を前提に)** — means "on the premise of X"; a basis/condition meaning unrelated to the concessive contrast of いざ知らず.
- [**としたことが**](grammar-としたことが.md) — means "X, of all people" (surprise at an uncharacteristic single mistake); differs because いざ知らず sets up a two-part contrast (X conceded, Y is different/worse), while としたことが is about surprise at one event, without the explicit "but the real situation is Y" contrast.
- [**のなんの**](grammar-のなんの.md) — an intensifying expression (〜のなんの "incredibly X," e.g., 痛いのなんの "incredibly painful"); an entirely different emphatic-exclamation function, not a contrastive concession.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though はいうにおよばず ("needless to say X, [and Y also applies]") moves in an opposite direction — it affirms the first case as obviously included rather than conceding it as a separate, lesser case to be contrasted against the real point. No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 昔はいざ知らず、彼は今やCD売上№1の大スターなんだから、簡単には会えませんよ。
   (In the past, maybe, but he's now the top-selling CD superstar, so you can't easily meet him anymore.)
2. 学生ならいざ知らず、社会人がこんな簡単なミスをするなんて信じられない。
   (If you were a student, maybe, but it's unbelievable that a working adult would make such a simple mistake.)
3. 初心者ならいざ知らず、ベテランの彼がこんな失敗をするとは思わなかった。
   (If he were a beginner, maybe, but I never thought a veteran like him would make such a mistake.)
4. 田舎に住んでいるならいざ知らず、都会でそんなのんびりした生活は難しい。
   (If you lived in the countryside, maybe, but living such a leisurely life in the city is difficult.)
5. 子供ならいざ知らず、大人がそんな言い訳をするのは恥ずかしい。
   (If it were a child, maybe, but it's embarrassing for an adult to make such an excuse.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Confused on graded tests with をぜんていに, としたことが, and のなんの. The essential nuance for students: ならいざ知らず always requires a two-part structure — a conceded, lesser/hypothetical case (X) and a contrasted real, more serious case (Y) that follows, usually introduced without an explicit conjunction but understood from context. This is structurally and semantically distinct from としたことが, which involves no such two-part concession/contrast — it's simply surprise/reproach at one actual event.
