# ～あっての {#あっての}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～あっての

**Furigana form:** ～あっての

**Meaning:** Expresses that something can only exist or succeed precisely because the preceding thing exists — "it's only thanks to X that Y is possible," emphasizing X as indispensable.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + あっての + 名詞
- 読者あっての雑誌 — "a magazine that exists only because of its readers"

**Typical context:** Written and spoken formal-to-neutral Japanese — common in speeches, business contexts, and reflective statements about what truly matters or makes something possible.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～してこそ** — also emphasizes that true value or success comes specifically from X, but applies to actions/verbs rather than the existence of a noun.
- [**～ばこそ**](grammar-ばこそ.md) — formal pattern stressing "precisely because of X," overlapping in the sense of emphasizing an essential cause, though grammatically attaching to the conditional ば-form.
- **～なくしては** — "without X, ... would not be possible," a near-opposite phrasing of the same underlying idea, usually followed by a negative potential (〜なくしては…ない).

**Opposite grammar structures:** ～なくしては～ない ("without X, Y would be impossible") expresses the same core relationship but from the negative angle, explicitly stating what would fail in X's absence rather than affirming X's indispensability.

**Example sentences:**
1. 結婚は、相手あってのことだから、相手がいなければどうしようもない。
   (Marriage only exists because you have a partner — without one, there's nothing to be done.)
2. 海あっての漁業なのだから、海を汚してはいけない。
   (The fishing industry exists only because of the sea, so we mustn't pollute it.)
3. 読者あっての雑誌なので、読者が読みたいと思うものを提供したい。
   (A magazine exists only because of its readers, so we want to provide what readers actually want to read.)
4. 社員あっての会社だということを、経営者は忘れてはならない。
   (Management must never forget that a company exists only because of its employees.)
5. 健康あっての仕事だから、無理をしすぎないでほしい。
   (Work is only possible because you're healthy, so please don't push yourself too hard.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern is used to emphasize the significance or benefit of the thing marked with あっての, and it attaches to words the speaker considers absolutely indispensable. Don't use it for a merely helpful or incidental factor — it specifically conveys "without this, the other thing simply could not exist," so it carries a stronger, more essential nuance than a casual "thanks to."
