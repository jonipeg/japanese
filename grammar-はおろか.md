# ～はおろか～さえ／すら／も {#はおろか}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～は疎か～さえ／すら／も

**Furigana form:** ～はおろか～さえ／すら／も

**Meaning:** "Not to mention X, not even Y" — states that a lesser or more basic case (Y) is also not fulfilled, after dismissing a more obviously expected greater case (X) as not even worth mentioning.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + は疎か + 名詞 + さえ／すら／も + ～ない
- Example base: 旅行 → 旅行はおろか、外出さえできない

**Typical context:** Formal-to-neutral, usable in both speech and writing; conveys emphatic frustration or hardship, pointing out that even a minimal expectation fails to be met, let alone a grander one.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**どころか**](grammar-どころか.md) — "far from X, actually Y"; similarly dismisses an expected case in favor of a contrasting reality, but どころか often flips to an opposite/surprising outcome, while はおろか emphasizes a hierarchy of "not even the lesser case, let alone the greater one," both being negative.
- **もちろん** — "of course X (and Y too)"; used for affirmative scaling (of course the big thing, and naturally the small thing too), the positive-scaling counterpart to はおろか's negative-scaling function.
- **は言うまでもなく** — "needless to say X (and also Y)"; shares the "don't even need to mention X" framing, but is typically neutral/affirmative rather than はおろか's negative emphasis.

**Opposite grammar structures:** ～はもちろん～も ("X is of course true, and Y also") expresses the reverse — an affirmative scaling instead of a negative one.

**Example sentences:**
1. 今月は旅行はおろか、外出する余裕さえない。 (This month, forget about traveling — I don't even have the time to go out.)
2. 彼は英語はおろか、母国語の読み書きすら怪しい。 (Forget English — even his reading and writing in his native language is shaky.)
3. 忙しくて、休憩はおろか食事をとる時間もない。 (I'm so busy that, forget about a break, I don't even have time to eat.)
4. この機械は修理はおろか、起動すらしない。 (Forget repairing this machine — it won't even turn on.)
5. 彼女は返事はおろか、メッセージを読んですらいないようだ。 (Forget a reply — it seems she hasn't even read the message.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** はおろか always requires a following clause with an emphatic negative marker (さえ／すら／も + ない) naming a lesser case that still isn't fulfilled — omitting that follow-up breaks the pattern. Learners should keep the hierarchy clear: the item after はおろか is the bigger/more obvious expectation being waved off, and what follows さえ／すら is the smaller, more basic case that still fails — reversing this order is a common mistake.
