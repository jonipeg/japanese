# ～はいうにおよばず {#はいうにおよばず}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～は言うに及ばず

**Furigana form:** ～は言(い)うに及(およ)ばず

**Meaning:** "Needless to say X, (Y also applies)" / "not to mention X" — states that X is so obviously included/true that it doesn't even need mentioning, before adding further information.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 (noun) + は言うに及ばず
- Occasionally verb phrases nominalized before attaching, but the standard pattern is Noun + はいうにおよばず.

**Typical context:** Formal, written register — common in advertising copy, formal descriptions, and essays listing a hierarchy of obviously-true-plus-additional points. Not rude; slightly elevated/polished tone, frequently seen in promotional or expository writing (e.g., restaurant reviews, product descriptions).

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **はいざしらず** — means "that may be one thing, but..." (used to set up a contrast where the first case is uncertain/different from the second); unlike はいうにおよばず, it does NOT mean "needless to say" — it signals the opposite, that the first case is debatable while the second is the real point of contrast.
- [**こととて**](grammar-こととて.md) — means "since it's a matter of X" (reason/excuse); unrelated in function.
- [**にかまけて**](grammar-にかまけて.md) — means "being preoccupied with/too busy with X (to do Y)"; a completely different meaning about distraction, not emphasis-via-obviousness.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct grammatical opposite, though ～はおろか ("let alone X," used with negatives to say even the lesser case fails) moves in a conceptually opposite direction — はおろか denies even the baseline case, while はいうにおよばず affirms the baseline case as too obvious to need stating. No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. あのレストランは料理の味は言うに及ばず、ケーキの種類も多く、女性に人気がある。
   (That restaurant is needless to say delicious, and also has a wide variety of cakes, making it popular with women.)
2. 彼の実力は国内では言うに及ばず、海外でも高く評価されている。
   (His skill is, needless to say, recognized domestically, and is also highly rated abroad.)
3. この製品は性能の良さは言うに及ばず、デザインの美しさでも評判だ。
   (This product's high performance goes without saying, and it's also renowned for its beautiful design.)
4. 彼女の料理の腕前は言うに及ばず、もてなしの心遣いにも定評がある。
   (Her cooking skill needs no mention, and her thoughtful hospitality is also well regarded.)
5. 新入社員の熱意は言うに及ばず、先輩社員たちの指導力も素晴らしい。
   (The new employees' enthusiasm goes without saying, and the senior employees' mentoring ability is also wonderful.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Confused on graded tests with はいざ知らず and こととて, and にかまけて — the critical distinction to drill into students is that はいうにおよばず affirms the first item as obviously true before adding more (additive, "of course X, and also Y"), while はいざしらず sets up a contrast where the first case is uncertain or set aside ("X may be one thing, but Y is different"). Mixing these two up inverts the logical relationship between the clauses.
