# ～というか～というか {#というかというか}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～と言うか～と言うか

**Furigana form:** ～というか～というか

**Meaning:** "You could call it X, or maybe Y" — hedges between two (or more) possible descriptions when the speaker isn't sure which single word best captures something.

**Conjugation:**
- 形容詞／名詞／動詞普通形 + というか + 形容詞／名詞／動詞普通形 + というか、+ まとめの表現
- Example base: 人がよすぎる／単純 → 人がよすぎるというか、単純というか

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-casual spoken register; conveys a thoughtful, searching tone when trying to pin down an ambiguous or multifaceted impression of someone/something. Common in conversation and casual writing.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **とでも言おうか** — "or perhaps one might call it X"; a more literary, single-instance hedge expressing similar tentative word-choice uncertainty, but not paired/repeated like というか～というか.
- **なんというか** — "how should I put it..."; a filler hedge used before struggling to find the right word, often preceding or accompanying というか～というか in natural speech.
- **とか～とか** — plain "things like X and Y"; a simple listing construction without というか's "searching for the right description" nuance — とか lists examples, というか hedges between characterizations.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a confident, single clear description (まさに～だ, "it's precisely X") would contrast with というか～というか's hedging uncertainty.

**Example sentences:**
1. 母は人がよすぎるというか、単純というか、他人が言うことをすぐ信じてしまう。 (My mother is, how should I put it, too good-natured, or maybe just simple — she believes whatever other people tell her right away.)
2. 彼の料理は独特というか、個性的というか、とにかく忘れられない味だった。 (His cooking was, you could say, unique, or maybe just quirky — in any case it was an unforgettable taste.)
3. あの映画の結末は悲しいというか、切ないというか、複雑な気持ちになった。 (That movie's ending was, I'd say, sad, or maybe bittersweet — it left me with complicated feelings.)
4. 彼女の態度は冷たいというか、マイペースというか、よくわからない。 (Her attitude is, I'm not sure, cold, or maybe just in her own world — I can't quite tell.)
5. この街は静かというか、少し寂れているというか、独特の雰囲気がある。 (This town is, you could say, quiet, or maybe a bit rundown — it has a unique atmosphere.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** というか～というか typically pairs two (occasionally more) related-but-not-identical characterizations, and is usually followed by a summarizing remark (とにかく～だ, よくわからない) acknowledging the ambiguity remains unresolved — it isn't meant to definitively settle on one answer. Learners should avoid pairing two contradictory/unrelated descriptors, since the construction works best when the two options are plausible, overlapping ways of viewing the same ambiguous impression.
