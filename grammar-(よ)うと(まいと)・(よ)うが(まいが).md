# ～(よ)うと～まいと／～(よ)うが～まいが {#(よ)うと・まいと・(よ)うが・まいが}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～(よ)うと～まいと／～(よ)うが～まいが

**Furigana form:** ～(よ)うと～まいと／～(よ)うが～まいが

**Meaning:** "Whether X or not" — expresses that it doesn't matter whether something happens/is done or not; the outcome or the speaker's stance is unaffected either way.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞意向形（う・よう形） + と／が ＋ 動詞辞書形 + まい + と／が
- 動詞Ⅱ・Ⅲグループ → 動詞辞書形／ます幹 + まい（する → するまい・すまい）
- 理解しようとするまいと — "whether [they] try to understand or not"
- 降ろうが降るまいが — "whether it rains or not"

**Typical context:** Mostly spoken but also found in writing; used when expressing resolve or an opinion that is unaffected by circumstances — often slightly formal or rhetorical in tone, common in speeches, narration, and firm personal declarations.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～ても～なくても** — a simpler, more neutral way to say "whether or not," without the repeated-verb construction; usable in plainer, everyday speech.
- **～かどうかにかかわらず** — "regardless of whether," more formal/written, often used with nouns or clauses rather than requiring the volitional + まい pairing.
- **～であれ～であれ** — lists alternatives that don't change the outcome, but typically pairs two nouns/states rather than a verb and its negation.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a structure like ～次第で ("depending on") would instead stress that the outcome *does* change based on the condition.

**Example sentences:**
1. 田中先生は、学生たちが理解しようとするまいと、どんどん難しい話を続けた。
   (Whether the students understood or not, Professor Tanaka kept pushing on with increasingly difficult material.)
2. 雨が降ろうが降るまいが、サッカーの練習に休みはない。
   (Whether it rains or not, there's no day off from soccer practice.)
3. 合格の見込みがあろうがあるまいが、今はただ頑張るだけだ。
   (Whether there's a chance of passing or not, all I can do now is try my best.)
4. 周りが賛成しようとするまいと、彼は自分の計画を実行するつもりだ。
   (Whether the people around him agree or not, he intends to carry out his plan.)
5. 景気が良くなろうがなるまいが、我々は計画通りに進めるしかない。
   (Whether the economy improves or not, we have no choice but to proceed as planned.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The same verb must be repeated in both the affirmative volitional form and the negative まい form — you cannot substitute a different verb for the second half. The clause that follows typically expresses the speaker's own judgment, determination, or an unaffected fact, not a request or suggestion directed at someone else.
