# ～もしないで {#もしないで}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～もしないで

**Furigana form:** ～もしないで

**Meaning:** "Without even doing X" — emphasizes that someone failed to do even the most basic/expected action, often carrying a critical or reproachful nuance.

**Conjugation:**
- Verb-ます形 (stem) + もしないで
- Example: 挨拶もしないで帰ってしまった (He left without even saying hello).

**Typical context:** Used in both spoken and written Japanese; register-neutral to mildly critical in tone. Common when expressing disapproval or surprise at someone's lack of basic effort or courtesy.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **もせずに** — the more formal/written equivalent, using せず (the formal negative of する) instead of しないで; interchangeable in meaning but もせずに sounds more literary.
- **すらしないで** — "doesn't even do X," adds the particle すら for extra emphasis, slightly more formal/dramatic than もしないで.
- **ないで** — the plain "without doing X," lacking the emphatic "not even" nuance that も adds.

**Opposite grammar structures:** ～ばかりで ("only/nothing but doing X") is a loose opposite in emphasis, describing excessive doing rather than a lack of even minimal doing.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は挨拶もしないで、部屋を出て行った。
   (He left the room without even saying hello.)
2. 子どもは宿題もしないで、ゲームばかりしている。
   (The child plays games all the time without even doing homework.)
3. 彼女は満足な説明もしないで、会議を終わらせた。
   (She ended the meeting without even giving a satisfactory explanation.)
4. 彼は確認もしないで、重要な書類にサインしてしまった。
   (He signed the important document without even checking it.)
5. そんなに疲れているなら、無理もしないで休んだ方がいい。
   (If you're that tired, you should rest without overexerting yourself — note: here も retains its basic emphatic sense rather than the "without even" idiom, illustrating the particle's flexibility.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The source notes a confirmed student wrong-answer on this item, though the exact distractor chosen isn't specified in the digest — a general caution is that students should be careful not to confuse もしないで with simple ないで, since the inclusion of も specifically adds the "not even" emphatic nuance implying a baseline expectation was not met.
