# ～にかぎったことではない {#にかぎったことではない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～に限ったことではない

**Furigana form:** ～にかぎったことではない

**Meaning:** "It's not limited to X / this isn't unique to X" — points out that a phenomenon described applies more broadly, not just to the specific case just mentioned.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + に限ったことではない
- Example base: この国 → この国に限ったことではない

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal, usable in both speech and writing; common in commentary and discussion to broaden a point beyond the immediate example, often used to push back on a narrow framing of an issue.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**に限らず**](grammar-に限らず.md) — "not limited to X (and also Y)"; nearly synonymous and often interchangeable, though に限らず more directly sets up a following "and also" clause (X に限らずYも), while に限ったことではない more often stands as a complete rebuttal statement on its own.
- **だけではない** — "it's not only X"; a simpler, more general way to say something isn't exclusive to one case, without the specific "this is a recognized broader pattern" nuance of に限ったことではない.
- **も同然だ** — unrelated in meaning ("is practically the same as X"); included only as a surface-similar pattern some learners conflate due to the ことではない/も同然だ sentence-final shape, but semantically distinct.

**Opposite grammar structures:** ～だけの話だ／～に特有のことだ ("this is specific/unique to X") expresses the reverse — that the issue is indeed confined to one case.

**Example sentences:**
1. 若者のマナーが悪くなりつつあるのはこの国に限ったことではない。 (The decline in young people's manners isn't limited to this country.)
2. 長時間労働の問題は、この業界に限ったことではない。 (The problem of long working hours isn't limited to this industry.)
3. 物価の上昇は日本に限ったことではなく、世界的な傾向だ。 (Rising prices aren't limited to Japan — it's a global trend.)
4. こうしたトラブルは今回に限ったことではない。 (Troubles like this aren't limited to this one time.)
5. 少子化は地方に限ったことではなく、都市部でも深刻だ。 (The declining birthrate isn't limited to rural areas — it's serious in urban areas too.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern is typically used to correct or expand a narrow assumption implied by context — it almost always responds to (explicit or implicit) framing that suggests an issue is unique to one group, place, or time. Learners should pair it naturally with a following clause broadening the scope (e.g., "...and this is true elsewhere too"), rather than leaving it as an isolated, unexplained denial.
