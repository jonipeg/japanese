## いつでも
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** any time, always, at all times (whenever)

**Similar words:**
- いつも — "always, usually," describes habitual/regular occurrence; いつでも emphasizes availability/flexibility at any moment the listener chooses.
- [常に](vocab-29.md#常に-つねに)（つねに）— "constantly, always," more formal/written, emphasizing an unchanging state rather than open availability.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. いつでも遊びに来てください。— Please come visit any time.
2. 彼はいつでも冷静だ。— He's always calm (in any situation).
3. ご質問があればいつでもどうぞ。— If you have any questions, feel free to ask any time.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** いつでも emphasizes "whenever you like/at any time you choose" (flexibility/openness), distinct from いつも which describes a habitual/recurring state — confusing these can make an invitation sound like a statement of habit instead.

## いつの間にか (いつのまにか)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** before one realizes it, without noticing, all too soon

**Similar words:**
- 知らないうちに（しらないうちに）— "without knowing it," very close synonym, both emphasize an unnoticed passage of time/change.
- 気がつくと（きがつくと）— "when I noticed," similar structure describing a realization after an unnoticed process.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. いつの間にか日が暮れていた。— Before I knew it, the sun had set.
2. 子供たちはいつの間にか大きくなっていた。— The children had grown up before we knew it.
3. いつの間にか財布がなくなっていた。— My wallet was gone without my noticing.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This set phrase always describes an unnoticed gradual change discovered only in hindsight — it's not used for sudden or deliberately noticed events, only things that crept up gradually and were only realized afterward.

## 一杯 (いっぱい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a cupful (of a drink); full, a lot of

**Similar words:**
- たくさん — "a lot, many," a more general quantity word; 一杯 specifically ties to "a cup/glassful" or figuratively "to the brim, fully," often with a stronger sense of being at capacity.
- 満杯（まんぱい）— "full to capacity," more formal/technical, often used for containers, vehicles, venues.

**Opposite words:**
- [空](vocab-14.md#空-から)（から）— empty

**Example sentences:**
1. コーヒーを一杯いただけますか。— Could I have a cup of coffee?
2. お腹がいっぱいで、もう食べられない。— My stomach is full, I can't eat any more.
3. 会場は人でいっぱいだった。— The venue was packed full of people.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 一杯 has both a literal counter meaning ("one cupful," 一杯のコーヒー) and a figurative adverbial meaning ("full, to the max," お腹いっぱい) — written in kanji for the counter sense, often in kana (いっぱい) for the figurative "full/a lot" sense, though usage varies.

## 一般(に) (いっぱんに)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** general, generally, average, ordinary; in general

**Similar words:**
- 普通（ふつう）— "normal/usual," closer to everyday ordinariness; 一般 is more about broad applicability/commonality across a category (一般的な意見, a commonly-held opinion).
- だいたい — "roughly, mostly," a looser approximation, less formal than 一般に.

**Opposite words:**
- [特殊](vocab-04.md#特殊-とくしゅ)（とくしゅ）— special, particular, exceptional

**Example sentences:**
1. 一般に、日本人は勤勉だと言われる。— Generally speaking, Japanese people are said to be hardworking.
2. この商品は一般消費者向けに作られた。— This product was made for the general consumer.
3. 一般的な方法で解決できる問題だ。— This is a problem that can be solved by a general/common method.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 一般的 (na-adjective, "general/common") and 一般に (adverb, "generally speaking") are both built from the same base noun 一般 but function grammatically differently — learners should be careful to use the correct form depending on whether modifying a noun or a whole clause.

## 一変 (いっぺん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a complete change, a total transformation (often sudden)

**Similar words:**
- 変化（へんか）— general "change," neutral and gradual-or-sudden; 一変 specifically emphasizes a sudden, total, dramatic transformation.
- 激変（げきへん）— "drastic/radical change," very close synonym, perhaps emphasizing intensity/severity slightly more than 一変's totality.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼の態度は一変した。— His attitude completely changed.
2. 事故のニュースで状況が一変した。— The situation completely changed with the news of the accident.
3. 町の様子は震災後、一変してしまった。— The appearance of the town completely transformed after the earthquake.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 一変 implies totality and often suddenness of change (not gradual) — don't confuse with 一遍（いっぺん, "once, one time"), an unrelated homophone that is a counter/adverb, not a noun about change.

## 一方 (いっぽう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** one side/party (of two); meanwhile, on the other hand

**Similar words:**
- 他方（たほう）— "the other side," the paired counterpart term, used together with 一方 to contrast two sides (一方は〜、他方は〜).
- 一方で（いっぽうで）— the connective form specifically used to contrast two clauses, similar to "while/whereas" in English.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は厳しい一方で、とても優しい人でもある。— While he's strict, he's also a very kind person.
2. 景気は回復する一方、失業率は上がり続けている。— While the economy is recovering, the unemployment rate keeps rising.
3. 争いの一方の当事者が和解を申し出た。— One of the parties in the dispute proposed a reconciliation.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 一方 has two distinct grammatical roles: as a noun meaning "one side/party" (争いの一方), and as a conjunction/connective meaning "meanwhile/while" (〜一方で) contrasting two facts — the connective usage is extremely common in formal/written Japanese (news articles, essays).

## いつまでも
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** forever, indefinitely, for as long as possible

**Similar words:**
- 永遠に（えいえんに）— "eternally, forever," more formal/poetic, often used in more dramatic or solemn contexts.
- ずっと — "continuously, for a long time," more casual and doesn't necessarily imply "forever" in the absolute sense that いつまでも often does.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. いつまでも仲良くいようね。— Let's stay friends forever, okay?
2. 彼女のことはいつまでも忘れない。— I'll never forget her, no matter how much time passes.
3. いつまでも若くいられるわけではない。— You can't stay young forever.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** いつまでも often carries an emotional, heartfelt nuance (used in promises, song lyrics, sentimental statements) compared to the more neutral/factual 永遠に — it frequently appears with negative verbs too (いつまでも〜ない, "never, no matter how long") to emphasize persistence of a negative state not changing.

## 飢える (うえる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to starve, be famished, be hungry (also figuratively: to crave/be starved for something)

**Similar words:**
- お腹が空く（おなかがすく）— the everyday casual phrase for "to get hungry," a normal daily occurrence; 飢える implies a much more severe, prolonged lack of food (starvation) or, figuratively, a deep craving.
- 餓死する（がしする）— "to die of starvation/hunger," the extreme endpoint of 飢える.

**Opposite words:**
- 満たされる（みたされる）— to be satisfied, fulfilled

**Example sentences:**
1. 戦時中、多くの人々が飢えに苦しんだ。— During the war, many people suffered from starvation.
2. 愛情に飢えている子供たちがいる。— There are children starved for affection.
3. 難民キャンプでは飢えた人々が支援を待っていた。— In the refugee camp, starving people were waiting for aid.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 飢える is much stronger than everyday "hungry" (お腹が空く) — it implies serious, prolonged deprivation, either literal (famine/starvation) or figurative (〜に飢える, "to be starved/craving for ~," e.g., 愛情に飢える, "starved for love").

## 魚 (うお)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a fish

**Similar words:**
- [魚](#魚-うお)（さかな）— the much more common reading for "fish," used in everyday speech and especially for fish as food; うお is more literary/old-fashioned or used in compound words (魚市場/うおいちば, fish market) and idioms (魚心あれば水心, "mutual favor").
- 鮮魚（せんぎょ）— "fresh fish," a more formal/commercial term seen in supermarkets.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 築地には有名なうお市場がある。— There's a famous fish market in Tsukiji.
2. うお座生まれの人は優しいと言われる。— People born under Pisces (lit. "fish sign") are said to be gentle.
3. 川にはたくさんのうおが泳いでいる。— Many fish are swimming in the river.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Both うお and さかな are read for 魚, but さかな dominates in everyday conversation (especially as food), while うお appears mostly in set compounds (魚市場, 魚座/うおざ, Pisces) and traditional/regional speech — learners should default to さかな unless in one of these fixed expressions.

## うがい
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** gargling, rinsing out one's mouth/throat; to gargle

**Similar words:**
- 手洗い（てあらい）— "handwashing," commonly paired with うがい in health advice (うがい・手洗い) as a basic hygiene routine in Japan.
- すすぐ — "to rinse" more generally (dishes, mouth, laundry), a broader verb not specific to the throat-gargling action of うがい.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 帰宅したら、うがいと手洗いをしましょう。— When you get home, let's gargle and wash your hands.
2. 風邪予防にはうがいが効果的だ。— Gargling is effective for preventing colds.
3. 塩水でうがいをすると喉の痛みが和らぐ。— Gargling with salt water eases a sore throat.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** うがい is a culturally specific Japanese hygiene practice (tilting the head back and gargling water/mouthwash in the throat) commonly taught from childhood as cold prevention — the fixed phrase うがいをする (to gargle) is the standard collocation.

## 浮かぶ (うかぶ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to float, surface, appear, come to mind (flash into thought)

**Similar words:**
- 浮く（うく）— similar "to float," but 浮く can also mean "to be saved/left over (money)" or "to be out of place socially," a broader range of meanings than 浮かぶ.
- 思いつく（おもいつく）— "to come up with/think of (an idea)," closer to the figurative "come to mind" sense of 浮かぶ when used with ideas.

**Opposite words:**
- [沈む](vocab-20.md#沈む-しずむ)（しずむ）— to sink

**Example sentences:**
1. 空に浮かぶ雲を眺めた。— I gazed at the clouds floating in the sky.
2. いいアイデアが浮かんだ。— A good idea came to mind.
3. 彼女の顔に笑みが浮かんだ。— A smile appeared on her face.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 浮かぶ is intransitive (something floats/appears by itself), paired with transitive 浮かべる (to float/visualize something deliberately) — learners often mix up which one takes を vs. が, since both relate to the same root concept.

## 浮かべる (うかべる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to float (something), launch, visualize, show (an expression)

**Similar words:**
- [浮かぶ](#浮かぶ-うかぶ)（うかぶ）— the intransitive counterpart (something floats/appears on its own), while 浮かべる is transitive (someone floats/shows something deliberately or involuntarily on their face).
- 想像する（そうぞうする）— "to imagine," overlaps with 浮かべる's figurative sense of visualizing something in one's mind (頭に思い浮かべる).

**Opposite words:**
- [沈める](vocab-20.md#沈める-しずめる)（しずめる）— to sink (something)

**Example sentences:**
1. 船を池に浮かべて遊んだ。— We floated a boat on the pond and played.
2. 彼は涙を目に浮かべた。— He had tears welling up in his eyes.
3. 故郷の風景を思い浮かべた。— I visualized the scenery of my hometown in my mind.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Often used for facial expressions (笑みを浮かべる, 涙を浮かべる) describing an emotion visibly surfacing on someone's face — this idiomatic, somewhat literary usage is common in narrative writing but less so in casual speech.

## 受かる (うかる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to succeed/pass (an examination)

**Similar words:**
- 合格する（ごうかくする）— the more formal/common verb for "to pass (an exam)," used interchangeably with 受かる but slightly more neutral/official in tone.
- 通る（とおる）— "to pass/go through," can also mean passing an exam in casual speech (試験に通る), a broader verb with many other meanings.

**Opposite words:**
- 落ちる（おちる）— to fail (an exam), to fall

**Example sentences:**
1. 第一志望の大学に受かった。— I got into (passed the exam for) my first-choice university.
2. 試験に受かるために毎日勉強している。— I study every day in order to pass the exam.
3. 彼は資格試験に受かった。— He passed the certification exam.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 受かる and 落ちる form a common antonym pair specifically for exam results — note the pattern is 〜に受かる (to pass ~), using the particle に, which some learners mistakenly swap for を.

## 受け入れ (うけいれ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** acceptance, reception (of people, applications, ideas)

**Similar words:**
- 承認（しょうにん）— "approval," more formal and often implies an official decision/authorization process, narrower than the broader 受け入れ.
- 容認（ようにん）— "tolerance/acceptance," closer to tolerating something rather than actively welcoming/receiving it.

**Opposite words:**
- [拒否](vocab-02.md#拒否-きょひ)（きょひ）— refusal, rejection

**Example sentences:**
1. 留学生の受け入れ体制を整えた。— We prepared a system for accepting international students.
2. 新しい意見の受け入れには時間がかかる。— Accepting new opinions takes time.
3. 病院は患者の受け入れを再開した。— The hospital resumed accepting patients.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 受け入れ is the noun form of 受け入れる, commonly used in institutional/administrative contexts (受け入れ態勢, acceptance system/readiness) — it describes the act/process/capacity of accepting people or things into a system, not a personal emotional acceptance.

## 受け入れる (うけいれる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to receive, accept, grant, admit (people, conditions, ideas)

**Similar words:**
- 認める（みとめる）— "to acknowledge/recognize," can overlap when accepting a fact or idea, but 受け入れる specifically emphasizes welcoming/taking something in, not just recognizing its validity.
- [取り入れる](vocab-30.md#取り入れる-とりいれる)（とりいれる）— "to adopt/incorporate," closer to actively integrating something (a method, idea) into one's own system, distinct from accepting people/conditions.

**Opposite words:**
- 拒む（こばむ）— to refuse, reject

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は批判を素直に受け入れた。— He accepted the criticism honestly.
2. 大学は留学生を多く受け入れている。— The university accepts many international students.
3. 現実を受け入れるのは簡単ではない。— Accepting reality isn't easy.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 受け入れる can apply to concrete things (people, applications) and abstract things (opinions, reality, criticism) alike — a versatile verb whose breadth sometimes leads learners to under-use it in favor of narrower synonyms.

## 承る (うけたまわる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to hear, to receive, to comply with (humble form of 聞く/受ける/引き受ける)

**Similar words:**
- お聞きする（おききする）— a more common humble form of "to hear/ask," less formal than 承る.
- かしこまりました — "certainly, understood" (humble acknowledgment), often used alongside or instead of 承りました in service contexts.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. ご注文を承りました。— I have received your order (humbly).
2. その件については私が承っております。— I am the one handling that matter.
3. ご意見を承りたく存じます。— I would like to humbly hear your opinion.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 承る is a kenjougo (humble language) verb used by service staff/businesses toward customers — it substitutes for 聞く, 受ける, or 引き受ける in formal/polite business Japanese (ご注文を承りました is a textbook customer-service phrase), and should not be used to describe someone else's actions toward you.

## 受け継ぐ (うけつぐ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to take over, inherit, carry on (a tradition, role, trait)

**Similar words:**
- 相続する（そうぞくする）— "to inherit," specifically legal/financial inheritance (property, assets); 受け継ぐ is broader and covers traditions, skills, roles, traits.
- 引き継ぐ（ひきつぐ）— "to take over/hand over (a task/duty)," very close synonym, often interchangeable, though 引き継ぐ leans slightly more toward practical handover of work/responsibilities.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は父の技術を受け継いだ。— He inherited his father's skills.
2. 伝統は次の世代に受け継がれていく。— The tradition will be passed down to the next generation.
3. 彼女は祖母の性格を受け継いでいるようだ。— She seems to have inherited her grandmother's personality.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 受け継ぐ covers a broad range of "inheritance" beyond money/property — traditions, titles, genetic traits, and skills — distinguishing it from the narrower, strictly legal/financial 相続する.

## 受け付ける (うけつける)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to accept, receive (applications, requests); to listen to/tolerate

**Similar words:**
- 受理する（じゅりする）— "to officially accept/receive (a document/application)," more formal/bureaucratic than 受け付ける.
- 聞き入れる（ききいれる）— "to listen to and accept (a request/plea)," closer to the figurative sense of 受け付ける when used about opinions/requests.

**Opposite words:**
- 拒絶する（きょぜつする）— to reject, refuse

**Example sentences:**
1. 申し込みは明日から受け付けます。— Applications will be accepted starting tomorrow.
2. 彼はどんな意見も受け付けない。— He won't listen to/accept any opinions.
3. 胃が受け付けなくて何も食べられない。— My stomach won't accept anything; I can't eat at all.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 受け付ける has a figurative physical sense too — 体が受け付けない ("the body won't accept/tolerate [food/medicine]") — this extension to physical tolerance is a nuance beginners may not expect from the administrative "accept applications" meaning.

## 受け止める (うけとめる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to catch, stop, take (a physical or emotional impact); to come to terms with

**Similar words:**
- [受け入れる](#受け入れる-うけいれる)（うけいれる）— "to accept," implies agreeing with/welcoming something; 受け止める is about absorbing/processing the impact of something (which may or may not be agreed with).
- 捉える（とらえる）— "to grasp/perceive," closer to interpreting the meaning of something, less about emotional impact absorption.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は批判を真剣に受け止めた。— He took the criticism seriously.
2. ボールをしっかり受け止めた。— He firmly caught the ball.
3. この結果をどう受け止めればいいのか分からない。— I don't know how to come to terms with this result.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 受け止める specifically implies absorbing or processing an impact/shock (literal catch, or emotional processing) without necessarily agreeing with or embracing it — distinct from 受け入れる, which implies actual acceptance/agreement.

## 受け取り (うけとり)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** accepting, receiving; a receipt

**Similar words:**
- 領収書（りょうしゅうしょ）— "a receipt" in the formal, itemized financial sense (for tax/expense purposes), more specific than the broader 受け取り.
- レシート — "a receipt" in the everyday casual sense (from a store register), overlapping with but less formal than 領収書.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 荷物の受け取りにはサインが必要です。— A signature is required to receive the package.
2. 受け取りの確認メールが届いた。— I received a confirmation email of receipt.
3. 受け取りは本人でなければなりません。— The recipient must be the person themselves.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 受け取り can mean both the act of receiving something (荷物の受け取り) and, less commonly, a receipt document — in the "receipt" sense, 領収書 or レシート are far more standard; 受け取り alone usually emphasizes the act/process of receiving.

## 受け取る (うけとる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to receive, get, accept; to interpret (a remark) in a certain way

**Similar words:**
- もらう — the basic, casual verb "to receive/get," much more general-purpose; 受け取る is slightly more formal and can additionally mean "to interpret" something said.
- 解釈する（かいしゃくする）— "to interpret," overlaps with 受け取る's figurative sense of understanding a statement in a particular way, though 解釈する is more analytical/formal.

**Opposite words:**
- 渡す（わたす）— to hand over, give

**Example sentences:**
1. 荷物を受け取った。— I received the package.
2. 彼の言葉を冗談だと受け取った。— I took his words as a joke.
3. 給料を受け取るのは月末だ。— Payday (receiving one's salary) is at the end of the month.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 受け取る has a figurative meaning of "interpreting" a statement a certain way (〜と受け取る, "to take it as ~") in addition to physically receiving something — this dual usage (physical receipt vs. interpretation) is a key nuance beyond the basic もらう.

## 受け身 (うけみ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** passive voice (grammar); a passive/defensive stance or attitude

**Similar words:**
- 消極的（しょうきょくてき）— "passive/unassertive" as a personality trait, overlapping with the figurative attitude sense of 受け身, but 受け身 is also the specific grammar term for passive voice.
- 能動的（のうどうてき）— "active/proactive," the direct antonym describing an assertive attitude.

**Opposite words:**
- 能動（のうどう）— active (voice/attitude)

**Example sentences:**
1. 日本語の受け身の文法は難しい。— Japanese passive voice grammar is difficult.
2. 彼はいつも受け身の姿勢で仕事をしている。— He always works with a passive attitude.
3. 受け身になるのではなく、自分から行動しよう。— Let's not be passive, but take action ourselves.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 受け身 has both a grammatical meaning (passive voice, as in 受け身形) and a figurative personality/attitude meaning ("passive, reactive rather than proactive") — context (grammar lesson vs. describing someone's character) clarifies which is meant.

## 受け持ち (うけもち)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** being in charge (of something); one's area of responsibility

**Similar words:**
- [担当](vocab-26.md#担当-たんとう)（たんとう）— "being in charge of/responsible for," a very close synonym, often used interchangeably in business/work contexts; 受け持ち leans slightly more toward teaching/classroom contexts (受け持ちのクラス).
- 役割（やくわり）— "role," broader and more abstract, describing one's function rather than a specific assigned duty/area.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女は3年2組の受け持ちの先生だ。— She is the homeroom teacher in charge of class 3-2.
2. この地域はどなたの受け持ちですか。— Who is in charge of this area?
3. 受け持ちの患者が増えて忙しい。— I'm busy because the number of patients I'm in charge of has increased.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 受け持ち is especially common in educational contexts (受け持ちの先生/クラス, the teacher/class one is in charge of) — in general business contexts, 担当 is the more standard/frequent term for "being in charge of."

## 受け持つ (うけもつ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

- [経営](vocab-17.md#経営する-けいえい)（けいえい）— management specifically of a business for profit, while 運営 is broader and applies to non-profit organizations, events, and websites too.
- [管理](vocab-14.md#管理-かんり)（かんり）— management/control in a more administrative, day-to-day sense (e.g. managing data, facilities), narrower in scope than the organizational-level 運営.

**Opposite words:**
None notable.

**Example sentences:**
1. このウェブサイトはボランティアが運営している。— This website is run by volunteers.
2. イベントの運営スタッフを募集している。— They're recruiting operational staff for the event.
3. 学校の運営方針について話し合った。— We discussed the school's management policy.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 運営 is broader than 経営 — it applies to running non-profit groups, events, websites, and public institutions, not just profit-driven businesses, where 経営 would be the more precise term.

## 運河 (うんが)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a canal

**Similar words:**
- 川（かわ）— a river, a natural waterway, as opposed to 運河 which is artificially constructed.
- 水路（すいろ）— a waterway/channel in general, a broader term that includes both natural and artificial water routes.

**Opposite words:**
None notable.

**Example sentences:**
1. パナマ運河は重要な貿易ルートだ。— The Panama Canal is an important trade route.
2. この街には美しい運河が流れている。— A beautiful canal runs through this town.
3. 運河沿いにカフェが立ち並んでいる。— Cafés line the canal.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 運河 specifically denotes a human-made waterway (for transport or irrigation) — don't confuse it with 川, a natural river; famous examples like パナマ運河 and スエズ運河 are good anchors for remembering the word.

## うんざり
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** fed up, sick and tired (of something)

**Similar words:**
- あきあきする（飽き飽きする）— to be bored/fed up from repetition, closely overlapping, often used interchangeably with うんざり.
- 嫌気がさす（いやけがさす）— to become disgusted/sick of something, a slightly more formal phrase for the same feeling.

**Opposite words:**
None notable.

**Example sentences:**
1. 毎日同じ話を聞かされてうんざりする。— I'm fed up with being told the same story every day.
2. 長い会議にうんざりした。— I got sick and tired of the long meeting.
3. 彼の言い訳にはもううんざりだ。— I'm already fed up with his excuses.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** うんざり is always used with する (うんざりする/うんざりした) and implies exhaustion from repeated or prolonged exposure to something annoying — it's not used for a single brief annoyance, which would call for むかつく or 腹が立つ instead.

## 運送 (うんそう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** transportation, shipping, carriage (of goods)

**Similar words:**
- 配送（はいそう）— delivery, specifically the final step of getting goods to the recipient, narrower than the broader 運送.
- [輸送](vocab-35.md#輸送-ゆそう)（ゆそう）— transport, a near-synonym often used for larger-scale or longer-distance transportation (freight, cargo) in more technical/formal contexts.

**Opposite words:**
None notable.

**Example sentences:**
1. 運送会社に荷物を預けた。— I entrusted my package to a shipping company.
2. 運送業は人手不足に悩んでいる。— The transportation industry is struggling with labor shortages.
3. トラックで荷物を運送する。— Goods are transported by truck.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 運送, 配送, and 輸送 are often confused — 運送 generally refers to the business/industry of carrying goods (運送会社 "shipping company"), 配送 to delivering to a final destination, and 輸送 to transport on a larger or longer-distance scale.
