# ～向き {#向き}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～向き

**Furigana form:** ～むき

**Meaning:** "Suited for X," "geared toward X" — indicates that something is inherently well-matched or appropriate in nature/characteristics for a certain group or purpose (describes an objective fit, not a deliberate design choice).

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + 向き: 初心者向きの本 (a book suited for beginners)
- Can function as な-adjective-like modifier (向きの/向きな) or stand alone as 向きだ.
- この服は冬向きだ。(These clothes are suited for winter.)

**Typical context:** Spoken and written, neutral register. Common in product descriptions, recommendations, and general evaluative comments.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**向け**](grammar-向け.md) — "targeted at/intended for," implies deliberate design for an audience (contrast: 向き is about natural suitability, not intentional design).
- **に適した** — "suitable for," more formal and explicit synonym.
- **にぴったり** — casual equivalent meaning "perfect for."

**Opposite grammar structures:** **～向きではない** — "not suited for," the direct negation.

**Example sentences:**
1. この部屋は南向きで、日当たりがいい。
   (This room faces south and gets good sunlight.)
2. この仕事は几帳面な人向きだ。
   (This job is suited for meticulous people.)
3. 初心者向きのコースから始めたほうがいい。
   (It's better to start with a course suited for beginners.)
4. 辛い料理は子供向きではない。
   (Spicy food is not suited for children.)
5. この軽いノートパソコンは出張向きだ。
   (This lightweight laptop is well-suited for business trips.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 向き describes an inherent/natural fit (this is naturally suited for X), while 向け describes an intentional target audience (this was made for X). Learners frequently swap the two: 初心者向きの本 (naturally fits beginners' level) vs. 初心者向けの本 (a book specifically published/designed for beginners) — both are often interchangeable in casual use, but the nuance distinction is tested on N2.
