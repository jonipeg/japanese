# ～を皮切りに（して）・～を皮切りとして {#を皮切りに（して）・を皮切りとして}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～を皮切りに（して）・～を皮切りとして

**Furigana form:** ～をかわきりに（して）・～をかわきりとして

**Meaning:** "Starting with X, one thing after another follows" — describes X as the opening event of a planned, connected series, after which similar events occur one after another and develop further.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + を皮切りに（して）／を皮切りとして
- 東京公演を皮切りに全国ツアーが始まった — "the nationwide tour began, starting with the Tokyo concert"

**Typical context:** Formal, written register — common in news reports, speeches, and business announcements about product launches, concert tours, or planned expansions.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～をきっかけに** — "triggered by X," broader and more neutral/casual; can describe a single non-repeating change, not necessarily a planned series.
- **～から始まって** — plainer phrasing for "starting from X," without the formal, literary flavor.
- **～に端を発して** — "originating from X," focuses on the cause of a chain of events (often incidents or problems) rather than a planned, developing series.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; ～を最後に ("ending with X") marks the final event of a series rather than the first.

**Example sentences:**
1. この作家は自分の父親のことを書いた小説を皮切りに、次々に話題作を発表している。
   (Starting with the novel he wrote about his own father, this author has gone on to publish one noteworthy work after another.)
2. わたしたちのバンドは来月3日の東京公演を皮切りにして、全国ツアーを予定しています。
   (Our band is planning a nationwide tour, kicking off with the Tokyo concert on the 3rd of next month.)
3. K銀行とM銀行の合併を皮切りとして、ここ数年企業の合併・統合が相次いで行われている。
   (Starting with the merger of K Bank and M Bank, there has been a string of corporate mergers and consolidations over the past few years.)
4. その新薬の認可を皮切りに、同様の治療薬が次々と承認されていった。
   (Starting with the approval of that new drug, similar treatments were approved one after another.)
5. 彼女のデビュー作を皮切りに、ヒット作が続々と生まれた。
   (Beginning with her debut work, hit after hit followed.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** What follows must describe a clear, connected series of similar events that develop from the first one — this isn't for randomly linking "X happened, then Y happened." It's also best avoided for natural phenomena or unfortunate/negative chains of events, where expressions like ～をきっかけに or ～に端を発して sound more natural.
