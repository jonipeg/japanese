# ～ものと思われる {#ものと思われる}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～ものと思われる

**Furigana form:** ～ものとおもわれる

**Meaning:** "It is thought/presumed that X" — an impersonal, objective-sounding expression of inference or speculation that distances the statement from the speaker's personal opinion.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞普通形 + ものと思われる
- 北上するものと思われます — "it is expected to head north"

**Typical context:** Formal written register, especially news reports, weather forecasts, official announcements, and academic writing — conveys an impersonal, authoritative-sounding inference rather than a personal opinion; too stiff for casual conversation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～と考えられる** — "it is considered that," similarly impersonal and common in academic/formal writing.
- **～と見られる** — "it is seen/estimated that," frequently used in news reporting for forecasts and estimates.

**Opposite grammar structures:** ～に違いない ("must be," a strong, personally-asserted certainty) contrasts with the hedged, impersonal tone of ものと思われる.

**Example sentences:**
1. 連休はかなりの渋滞になるものと思われる。
   (It is expected that there will be considerable traffic congestion during the holiday period.)
2. 台風はこのまま北上するものと思われます。
   (The typhoon is expected to continue moving northward.)
3. 今後、物価はさらに上昇するものと思われる。
   (Prices are expected to rise further in the future.)
4. 事故の原因は運転手の不注意によるものと思われる。
   (The cause of the accident is believed to be the driver's carelessness.)
5. この地域の人口は今後も減少し続けるものと思われる。
   (The population in this area is expected to continue declining going forward.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The passive-like ～と思われる depersonalizes the claim, making it sound like a widely-held, objective inference rather than the speaker's own opinion. This makes it appropriate for news reports and formal writing, but too distant and stiff for ordinary conversation, where plain ～と思う is far more natural.
