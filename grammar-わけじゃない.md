# ～わけじゃない {#わけじゃない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～訳じゃない

**Furigana form:** ～わけじゃない

**Meaning:** "It's not that... / that doesn't mean that..." — a partial denial or softening expression, used to clarify that a conclusion someone might draw from the preceding context is not entirely accurate, without denying it outright.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞普通形 + わけじゃない
- い形容詞普通形 + わけじゃない
- な形容詞 + な + わけじゃない
- 名詞 + の/という + わけじゃない
- The more formal/written equivalent is **わけではない**, used identically in meaning but in polite speech, writing, and formal contexts: 名詞/な形容詞 + な + わけではない, 動詞・い形容詞普通形 + わけではない
- Example: 嫌いなわけじゃないけど、あまり食べたくない (It's not that I dislike it, but I don't really want to eat it)

**Typical context:** Common in everyday spoken Japanese (わけじゃない) as well as more formal/written contexts (わけではない). Used to soften a previous negative-sounding statement or to preempt a misunderstanding, often paired with けど/が for contrast. Carries a conciliatory, clarifying nuance rather than a flat denial.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**わけではない**](grammar-わけではない.md) — the more formal/written counterpart of わけじゃない, identical in meaning and function, preferred in essays, business writing, and polite speech.
- [**わけ（が）ない**](grammar-わけがない.md) — a much stronger, complete denial of possibility, the opposite force from the partial softening of わけじゃない.
- [**とは限らない**](grammar-とは限らない.md) — "it's not necessarily the case that...", similarly softens an overgeneralization, but focuses more on exceptions to a general rule than on correcting a listener's inference.
- **ないわけではない** — a double-negative construction meaning "it's not that [I] don't...", i.e. a cautious, hedged affirmation (e.g., 行きたくないわけではない = "it's not that I don't want to go," implying some willingness).

**Opposite grammar structures:** 〜わけだ ("so that means... / no wonder...", affirming that a conclusion logically follows) stands in contrast, as it confirms rather than softens an inference.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼のことが嫌いなわけじゃないけど、一緒に仕事はしたくない。
   (It's not that I dislike him, but I don't want to work with him.)
2. 毎日忙しいわけじゃないけど、最近少し疲れている。
   (It's not that I'm busy every day, but I've been a bit tired lately.)
3. 勉強が嫌いなわけではないんですが、時間がなかなか取れません。
   (It's not that I dislike studying, but I just can't seem to find the time.)
4. お金がないわけじゃないが、無駄遣いはしたくない。
   (It's not that I don't have money, but I don't want to waste it.)
5. 全員が反対しているわけではない。賛成している人も少しいる。
   (It's not that everyone is opposed; there are a few people who agree as well.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners frequently confuse わけじゃない with わけ（が）ない because both contain わけ + a negative. The key distinction: わけ（が）ない completely denies that something is possible ("there's no way"), while わけじゃない/わけではない only partially denies an inference ("that doesn't mean..."), leaving room for nuance. Also remember わけじゃない and わけではない are interchangeable in meaning — the choice between them is purely about register (casual vs. formal/written), not grammar or nuance.
