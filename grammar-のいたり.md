# ～のいたり {#のいたり}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～の至り

**Furigana form:** ～のいたり

**Meaning:** "The utmost extreme of X" — a humble, often self-referential expression that a feeling (honor, shame, gratitude) has reached its absolute peak. Frequently appears in fixed ceremonial phrases like 光栄の至り ("the height of honor") and 汗顔の至り ("the height of embarrassment").

**Conjugation:**
- N(感情を表す名詞) + の至り

**Typical context:** Very formal, ceremonial, humble-speech register — used in formal greetings, award acceptance speeches, and letters of thanks. Not used in casual conversation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**のきわみ**](grammar-のきわみ.md) — also means "the utmost extreme," but is more objective/descriptive and can apply to physical states (疲労の極み), whereas のいたり is reserved for humble, self-referential ceremonial emotion expressions.
- [**かぎりだ**](grammar-かぎりだ.md) — a more general personal feeling exclamation (うらやましい限りだ) usable outside ceremonial contexts, unlike のいたり's narrow, humble, formal usage.
- [**てやまない**](grammar-てやまない.md) — expresses a continuous wish via a verb, rather than のいたり's single-moment exclamatory peak feeling via a noun.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 私の作品が認めていただけるとは、感激の至りです。
   (That my work has been recognized is the height of my gratitude/emotion.)
2. このような賞をいただき、光栄の至りに存じます。
   (Receiving an award like this is truly the height of honor for me.)
3. 人前であのような失態を演じてしまい、汗顔の至りです。
   (Making such a blunder in public is the height of embarrassment for me.)
4. 若輩者の私がこの場に立たせていただくのは、恐縮の至りです。
   (For someone as inexperienced as me to stand here is the height of humbling gratitude.)
5. 先生から直接ご指導いただけるとは、幸甚の至りに存じます。
   (To receive guidance directly from you, sensei, is the height of good fortune.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** のいたり is a prime member of the N1 "extreme degree" confusable cluster and is constantly mixed up with のきわみ because both translate as "the height of X." The key test: のいたり is almost always humble and self-referential, appearing in fixed ceremonial collocations (光栄の至り, 汗顔の至り), whereas のきわみ is broader, more objective, and can describe third-party or even physical/negative states (疲労の極み, 失礼の極み) that would sound unnatural with のいたり.
