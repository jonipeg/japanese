## 水面 (すいめん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** the surface of the water

**Similar words:**
- 水面下（すいめんか）— "beneath the surface," a related compound often used figuratively for hidden/behind-the-scenes activity.
- [表面](vocab-32.md#表面-ひょうめん)（ひょうめん）— "surface" in general, not limited to water — 水面 is specifically the water's surface.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 水面に月が映っている。— The moon is reflected on the surface of the water.
2. 魚が水面近くを泳いでいた。— The fish were swimming near the surface of the water.
3. 水面下で交渉が進められていた。— Negotiations were proceeding beneath the surface (behind the scenes).

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The figurative phrase 水面下で (beneath the surface, i.e., secretly/behind the scenes) is extremely common in news and business Japanese — don't take it only literally; it often describes covert activity or unseen progress.

## 推理 (すいり)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** inference, reasoning, deduction

**Similar words:**
- [推測](#推測-すいそく)（すいそく）— "guess/conjecture," implies less certainty and more speculation than 推理, which suggests a logical deductive process (as in detective reasoning).
- [論理](vocab-36.md#論理-ろんり)（ろんり）— "logic," the broader system/method, while 推理 is the act of applying reasoning to reach a conclusion.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は推理小説を読むのが好きだ。— He likes reading mystery/detective novels.
2. 名探偵は巧みな推理で犯人を突き止めた。— The great detective identified the culprit through skillful deduction.
3. 証拠をもとに推理を重ねた。— I built up my reasoning based on the evidence.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Strongly associated with 推理小説 (mystery/detective novels) — implies a structured, logical deduction process (like Sherlock Holmes), distinct from 推測 which is a looser, less rigorous guess.

## 水田 (すいでん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** paddy field, rice paddy

**Similar words:**
- 田んぼ（たんぼ）— the everyday, casual word for "rice paddy/field"; 水田 is a more formal/technical term emphasizing the flooded, irrigated nature of the field.
- [畑](vocab-31.md#畑-はたけ)（はたけ）— "field" for dry-land crops (vegetables, etc.), distinct from 水田's flooded rice cultivation.

**Opposite words:**
- [畑](vocab-31.md#畑-はたけ)（はたけ）— dry field (non-irrigated)

**Example sentences:**
1. 水田に稲が青々と茂っている。— The rice plants are lushly green in the paddy field.
2. この地域は昔から水田農業が盛んだ。— This region has long had thriving paddy agriculture.
3. 水田に水を引く作業を手伝った。— I helped with the work of channeling water into the rice paddy.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 水田 emphasizes the irrigated/flooded nature of the land (水 = water), more formal than the everyday 田んぼ — both refer to rice paddies, but 水田 appears more in agricultural/academic writing.

## 随筆 (ずいひつ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** an essay (personal, reflective prose)

**Similar words:**
- エッセイ — loanword "essay," used interchangeably with 随筆 in modern contexts, perhaps slightly more casual/Western in connotation.
- 評論（ひょうろん）— "critique/commentary," more analytical and argumentative, distinct from 随筆's personal, reflective, often loosely structured style.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女は随筆家として知られている。— She is known as an essayist.
2. この随筆集には日常の気づきが綴られている。— This essay collection records everyday observations.
3. 枕草子は日本の随筆文学の代表作だ。— The Pillow Book is a representative work of Japanese essay literature.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** A classical literary term with deep roots in Japanese literature (e.g., 枕草子, 徒然草) — distinct from the more casual modern loanword エッセイ, though both are now used interchangeably in contemporary contexts.

## 水分 (すいぶん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** water content, moisture

**Similar words:**
- 湿気（しっけ）— "humidity/dampness," refers to moisture in the air or environment, while 水分 refers to water content within a substance (food, body, soil).
- [液体](vocab-09.md#液体-えきたい)（えきたい）— "liquid" in general, a broader category than the specific water-content sense of 水分.

**Opposite words:**
- [乾燥](vocab-13.md#乾燥-かんそう)（かんそう）— dryness

**Example sentences:**
1. このフルーツは水分が多い。— This fruit has a lot of water content.
2. 夏は水分補給をこまめにしましょう。— In summer, let's stay hydrated frequently.
3. 肌の水分が不足すると乾燥しやすくなる。— When skin lacks moisture, it tends to become dry.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Extremely common in health/skincare contexts (水分補給 = hydration, 肌の水分 = skin moisture) — distinct from 湿気, which is about ambient air humidity rather than moisture contained within an object or body.

## 推測 (すいそく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a guess, supposition, to suppose/infer

**Similar words:**
- [推理](#推理-すいり)（すいり）— implies a more rigorous, logical deduction process; 推測 is a softer, more tentative guess based on limited information.
- 憶測（おくそく）— "speculation," often implies guessing without solid evidence, sometimes with a slightly negative or gossipy connotation, more so than neutral 推測.

**Opposite words:**
- [断定](vocab-26.md#断定-だんてい)（だんてい）— definitive conclusion, assertion

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼の行動の理由は推測するしかない。— We can only guess at the reason for his actions.
2. これはあくまで私の推測に過ぎない。— This is merely my own supposition.
3. 専門家は原因をいくつか推測している。— Experts are speculating on several possible causes.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Implies a reasonable but uncertain guess — weaker in certainty than 推理 (logical deduction) and more neutral than 憶測 (speculation, sometimes gossip-tinged); choose based on how rigorous/evidence-based the guessing process is.

## 吸う (すう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to inhale, smoke (a cigarette), suck in

**Similar words:**
- 吸い込む（すいこむ）— "to suck/draw in," emphasizes a stronger, more forceful inhaling action than plain 吸う.
- 喫煙する（きつえんする）— formal verb specifically for "to smoke," a more official/written equivalent to タバコを吸う.

**Opposite words:**
- 吐く（はく）— to exhale, breathe out

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼はタバコを吸う習慣がある。— He has a habit of smoking cigarettes.
2. 深呼吸をして新鮮な空気を吸った。— I took a deep breath and inhaled the fresh air.
3. ストローでジュースを吸った。— I sucked the juice up through a straw.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Used broadly for inhaling anything (air, smoke, liquid through a straw) — タバコを吸う is the standard way to say "to smoke," not 喫煙する in casual conversation, which sounds more clinical/formal.

## 信頼性 (しんらいせい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** reliability, trustworthiness

**Similar words:**
- 信用度（しんようど）— "degree of trust/credit," often used in financial or credit-rating contexts, a narrower measure than the general 信頼性.
- 安定性（あんていせい）— "stability," related but focused on consistency rather than trustworthiness specifically.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. このデータの信頼性には疑問がある。— There are doubts about the reliability of this data.
2. 製品の信頼性を高めるための試験を行った。— We conducted tests to improve the product's reliability.
3. 信頼性の高い情報源を選ぶことが大切だ。— It's important to choose highly reliable information sources.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The 性 suffix turns 信頼 (trust, a feeling toward a person) into an abstract quality applicable to things (data, systems, products) — don't use 信頼性 to describe trusting a person directly; use 信頼 or 信頼できる instead.

## 親友 (しんゆう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a close friend, best friend

**Similar words:**
- 友達（ともだち）— the general, neutral word for "friend"; 親友 specifically denotes a close, deep friendship, a subset of 友達.
- 仲良し（なかよし）— "good friends/close pals," a more casual, often childlike term for close friendship.

**Opposite words:**
- 敵（てき）— enemy

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女は私の親友だ。— She is my close friend.
2. 親友と呼べる人は多くない。— Not many people can be called a close friend.
3. 中学時代からの親友と今も連絡を取っている。— I'm still in touch with my close friend from middle school.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Carries a stronger, more exclusive connotation than 友達 — calling someone 親友 implies genuine depth of trust and history, so it shouldn't be used lightly for casual acquaintances the way "friend" sometimes is in English.

## 信用 (しんよう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** trust, confidence, credit (often based on track record)

**Similar words:**
- [信頼](vocab-21.md#信頼-しんらい)（しんらい）— broader, more emotional trust in someone's character; 信用 often implies trust earned through track record or evidence (credit, reputation).
- クレジット — loanword specifically for financial "credit," a narrower technical use overlapping with 信用 in banking contexts (信用金庫, credit union).

**Opposite words:**
- 不信（ふしん）— distrust

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は長年の実績で信用を得た。— He earned trust through years of achievement.
2. 一度嘘をつくと信用を失う。— Once you tell a lie, you lose trust.
3. この会社は信用できる取引先だ。— This company is a trustworthy business partner.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 信用 often implies trust built on evidence/history (credit, reputation), appearing in finance terms like 信用金庫 (credit union) and 信用取引 (margin trading) — 信頼 is the better choice for describing trust in someone's character or emotional reliability.

## 心理学 (しんりがく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** psychology (the academic discipline)

**Similar words:**
- 精神医学（せいしんいがく）— "psychiatry," the medical/clinical discipline treating mental illness, distinct from 心理学 which is the broader academic study of mind and behavior.
- [心理](vocab-21.md#心理-しんり)（しんり）— the mental state/psychology of a specific person or situation, not the academic field itself.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 大学で心理学を専攻している。— I'm majoring in psychology at university.
2. 心理学の観点から人間の行動を分析する。— Analyze human behavior from a psychological perspective.
3. 彼は臨床心理学の資格を取得した。— He obtained a clinical psychology qualification.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Don't confuse with 心理 (the mental state itself) — 心理学 specifically denotes the academic/scientific discipline, parallel to other academic fields ending in 学 (経済学, 社会学, etc.).

## 深夜 (しんや)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** late night, dead of night

**Similar words:**
- [夜中](vocab-33.md#夜中-よなか)（よなか）— "the middle of the night," a more casual/colloquial equivalent to 深夜.
- 真夜中（まよなか）— "midnight/dead of night," emphasizes the deepest point of night, close synonym to both.

**Opposite words:**
- 真昼（まひる）— broad daylight, midday

**Example sentences:**
1. 深夜までずっと仕事をしていた。— I was working until late at night.
2. 深夜番組をよく見る。— I often watch late-night TV programs.
3. 深夜に地震があり、目が覚めた。— There was an earthquake late at night, and I woke up.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 深夜 is the more formal/written term (深夜番組, 深夜営業 = late-night business hours) while 夜中 is more conversational — both describe roughly the same time period (after midnight into the early morning).

## ストレス (すとれす)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** stress (mental/emotional)

**Similar words:**
- [緊張](vocab-15.md#緊張-きんちょう)（きんちょう）— "tension/nervousness," a more specific feeling often tied to a particular moment, while ストレス is broader and often chronic.
- プレッシャー — loanword "pressure," often from external expectations, a frequent cause contributing to ストレス.

**Opposite words:**
- リラックス — relaxation

**Example sentences:**
1. 仕事のストレスで疲れ切っている。— I'm exhausted from work stress.
2. 適度な運動はストレス解消に効果的だ。— Moderate exercise is effective for relieving stress.
3. ストレスが溜まると体調を崩しやすい。— When stress builds up, it's easy to fall ill.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** A loanword used much like in English, especially in the fixed phrase ストレス解消 (stress relief) and ストレスが溜まる (stress builds up) — treated grammatically as a noun, paired with する only indirectly (ストレスを感じる, not ストレスする).

## 素直 (すなお)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** obedient, gentle, honest/sober (in temperament)

**Similar words:**
- 従順（じゅうじゅん）— "obedient/docile," emphasizes compliance with authority, sometimes with a passive or submissive nuance; 素直 is warmer, implying genuine openness and honesty, not just compliance.
- 正直（しょうじき）— "honest," focused specifically on truthfulness, while 素直 is broader, covering a generally receptive, unaffected personality.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女は素直な性格で周りから好かれている。— She has an honest, unaffected personality and is well-liked by those around her.

3. 子供は素直に親の言うことを聞いた。— The child obediently listened to what the parent said.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Carries a positive connotation of being genuinely open, honest, and receptive to advice/correction without being fake or defensive — distinct from 従順, which can sound more passively obedient or even servile.

## すなわち (すなわち)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** namely, that is to say, in other words

**Similar words:**
- つまり — the everyday, conversational equivalent of "in other words/in short"; すなわち is more formal/written, common in essays and legal/academic text.
- 言い換えれば（いいかえれば）— "to put it another way," a more explicit phrase for rephrasing, similar function to すなわち.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は私の恩師、すなわち中学時代の担任だ。— He is my mentor, namely, my homeroom teacher from middle school.
2. この地域の最大都市、すなわち県庁所在地はここだ。— This is the largest city in the region, that is, the prefectural capital.
3. 収入が支出を上回る、すなわち黒字の状態だ。— Income exceeds expenses, in other words, it's in the black.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** A formal written-register conjunction used to restate or clarify a preceding noun/clause with precision — in casual speech, つまり is far more natural; using すなわち in everyday conversation can sound stiff or overly bookish.

## 頭脳 (ずのう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** the brain, one's brains/intellect

**Similar words:**
- 脳（のう）— the plain anatomical word for "brain"; 頭脳 often carries a figurative sense of "intellect/brainpower," not just the physical organ.
- [知能](vocab-27.md#知能-ちのう)（ちのう）— "intelligence," a measurable cognitive capacity (知能指数 = IQ), more abstract than 頭脳's broader reference to mental faculty.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Often used figuratively for "intellect/brainpower" rather than the literal organ (頭脳明晰 = sharp-minded, 頭脳プレー = brainy/strategic play) — for the purely anatomical brain, 脳 alone is more common in medical contexts.

## 素早い (すばやい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** quick, swift, nimble, agile

**Similar words:**
- 速い（はやい）— general "fast," describes speed of movement or action broadly; 素早い emphasizes nimbleness and quick reflexes/reaction, often in a skillful or alert way.
- 敏捷（びんしょう）— "agile/nimble," a more formal/written synonym often used to describe physical agility in sports or martial arts contexts.

**Opposite words:**
- 鈍い（にぶい）— slow, dull

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は素早い動きで敵をかわした。— He dodged the enemy with swift movements.
2. 猫は素早く物陰に隠れた。— The cat quickly hid behind an object.
3. 素早い判断が勝敗を分けた。— Quick judgment decided the outcome.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Implies agility/nimbleness and quick reaction, not just raw speed — a car can be 速い (fast) without being 素早い, which is reserved more for living things or quick-witted responses/movements.

## 素晴らしい (すばらしい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** wonderful, splendid, magnificent

**Similar words:**
- 見事（みごと）— "superb/masterly," often praises skill or execution specifically; 素晴らしい is more general, applicable to any impressive or delightful thing.
- 最高（さいこう）— "the best/greatest," more casual and superlative in tone, commonly used in spoken Japanese.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** A versatile, strongly positive adjective usable for people, scenery, ideas, and performances alike — slightly more formal/written than 最高 or すごい, which are more common in casual spoken praise.

## すくむ (すくむ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to cower, cringe, be too scared to move, freeze up

**Similar words:**
- 怖がる（こわがる）— general "to be scared/afraid," describes the emotion itself; すくむ specifically describes the physical freezing/paralysis that fear can cause.
- ひるむ — "to flinch/falter," close in meaning, often describes a momentary loss of nerve rather than full physical immobilization.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 大きな犬を見て足がすくんだ。— My legs froze up when I saw the big dog.
2. 恐怖で体がすくんで動けなかった。— My body froze with fear and I couldn't move.
3. 彼の鋭い視線にすくんでしまった。— I cowered under his sharp gaze.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Almost always used with 足 or 体 as the subject (足がすくむ, 体がすくむ) — describes an involuntary physical freeze response to fear, not a conscious decision to hide or retreat.

## 少なくとも (すくなくとも)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** at least

**Similar words:**
- [せめて](vocab-23.md#せめて-せめて) — "at least," but used to express a minimal wish/hope (せめて一度は行きたい = "I want to go at least once"), slightly different nuance from 少なくとも's factual/numerical "at minimum."
- 最低限（さいていげん）— "the bare minimum," more emphatic and often used for strict minimum requirements.

**Opposite words:**
- 多くとも（おおくとも）— at most (less common, more formal)

**Example sentences:**
1. この仕事には少なくとも三年の経験が必要だ。— This job requires at least three years of experience.
2. 少なくとも彼は嘘をついていない。— At least he isn't lying.
3. 少なくとも週に一回は運動するようにしている。— I try to exercise at least once a week.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 少なくとも can modify both numbers (少なくとも三回 = "at least three times") and whole statements (少なくとも〜ことは確かだ = "at least it's certain that...") — don't confuse with せめて, which expresses a hopeful minimal wish rather than a factual minimum.

## 救う (すくう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to save, rescue, help

**Similar words:**
- [助ける](vocab-25.md#助ける-たすける)（たすける）— more general, everyday word for "to help/save," usable in a wider range of situations (helping with tasks, rescuing from danger); 救う carries a stronger sense of saving from serious peril or distress.
- 救助する（きゅうじょする）— formal verb for "to rescue," often used in official/emergency contexts (disaster rescue operations).

**Opposite words:**
- 見捨てる（みすてる）— to abandon, forsake

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼の一言が私を救ってくれた。— His one remark saved me.
2. 溺れている人を救うために海に飛び込んだ。— I jumped into the sea to save the drowning person.
3. そのボランティア活動が多くの命を救った。— That volunteer activity saved many lives.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Carries more emotional/dramatic weight than 助ける — often used for saving someone from serious danger, despair, or death (命を救う, 魂を救う), while 助ける is the everyday, more neutral word for "to help."

## 優れる (すぐれる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to excel, surpass, be superior

**Similar words:**
- 勝る（まさる）— "to surpass/exceed," more directly comparative (A is better than B); 優れる emphasizes inherent excellence/high quality, not necessarily in direct comparison.
- 卓越する（たくえつする）— "to be outstanding/preeminent," a more formal, emphatic synonym for exceptional excellence.

3. 優れたリーダーシップが求められている。— Outstanding leadership is being called for.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Almost always used in the te-form + いる (優れている) to describe an ongoing state of excellence, rarely in plain non-past form alone — learners sometimes forget the ~ている is required for the natural "is excellent/excels" meaning.

## すっかり (すっかり)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** completely, entirely, totally

**Similar words:**
- 完全に（かんぜんに）— "completely/perfectly," more formal and precise; すっかり is more conversational and often conveys a sense of surprise at the completeness of a change.
