# ～には当たらない {#にはあたらない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～には当たらない

**Furigana form:** ～にはあたらない

**Meaning:** "It doesn't amount to/warrant X" — used to downplay a reaction (surprise, praise, blame, criticism) as unwarranted or excessive given the circumstances.

**Conjugation:**
- V辞書形/N + には当たらない (commonly with 驚く, 批判する, 称賛する, 感心する, etc.)

**Typical context:** Formal written/spoken register, common in commentary, critique, and formal argumentation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**にかたくない**](grammar-にかたくない.md) — "not hard to imagine/empathize with" — a confirmed confusion partner on N1 tests: にかたくない says something is easy to picture or understand emotionally, while にはあたらない says a REACTION (surprise, praise) is unwarranted/excessive — both share the "〜に + negative predicate" shape, which is exactly why they're so often mixed up despite meaning very different things.
- [**までもなく**](grammar-までもなく.md) — a similar "unnecessary" nuance, but applied to an ACTION being skippable because it's obviously unneeded, rather than にはあたらない's focus on a REACTION being inappropriate.
- [**ようがない**](grammar-ようがない.md) — "there's no way to do X" due to lack of means, a practical impossibility unrelated to にはあたらない's evaluative judgment about an appropriate reaction.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女が試験に合格したからといって驚くには当たらない。
   (Just because she passed the exam doesn't mean it warrants being surprised — she always studied hard.)
2. 彼の成功は努力の結果であり、特に称賛するには当たらない。
   (His success is the result of hard work, so it doesn't particularly warrant special praise — it was expected.)
3. 些細なミスなのだから、そこまで批判するには当たらない。
   (It's such a minor mistake that it doesn't warrant this much criticism.)
4. 新人が最初は苦労するのは当然で、責めるには当たらない。
   (It's natural for a newcomer to struggle at first, so it doesn't warrant being blamed.)
5. 多少の遅れは、問題視するには当たらないだろう。
   (A slight delay probably doesn't warrant being treated as a problem.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** にはあたらない is explicitly flagged as a confirmed confusion partner of にかたくない in N1 drills, since both patterns use the "〜に + negative predicate" shape. The key test: にはあたらない evaluates whether a REACTION (surprise, praise, blame) is appropriate given the facts, while にかたくない evaluates whether something is EASY TO IMAGINE/UNDERSTAND emotionally (想像にかたくない, "not hard to imagine") — one is about appropriateness of response, the other about ease of empathetic understanding.
