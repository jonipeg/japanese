# ～てはかなわない {#てはかなわない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～てはかなわない（敵わない）

**Furigana form:** ～てはかなわない

**Meaning:** "I can't stand/bear it if X (keeps happening)" — complains that a recurring or continuing action/state is intolerable.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞て形 + は + かなわない (Vて + はかなわない)
- い形容詞くて + はかなわない
- な形容詞/名詞で + はかなわない
- Example base: 暑い → 暑くてはかなわない

**Typical context:** Spoken, complaint register; casual-to-neutral and somewhat old-fashioned/colloquial — often used by middle-aged or older speakers grumbling about a situation. Rarely appears in formal writing.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**てたまらない**](grammar-てたまらない・しようがない・しかたがない.md) — "unbearably X"; more common and neutral in register, usable in both speech and writing, and expresses the speaker's own uncontrollable feeling rather than a complaint about an external repeated nuisance.
- **てしょうがない／てしかたがない** — "can't help feeling X"; focuses on an emotion/sensation the speaker can't suppress, milder than the outright exasperation of てはかなわない.
- **にはかなわない** — a different construction using the same verb 敵う/叶う meaning "cannot match/compete with ~"; do not confuse with てはかなわない, which always attaches to a te-form clause and means "can't bear."

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; conceptually ても平気だ ("I'm fine even if X") expresses the reverse attitude.

**Example sentences:**
1. こう毎日暑くてはかなわないなあ。 (I can't stand it being this hot every single day.)
2. 隣の部屋から毎晩うるさい音楽が聞こえてきてはかなわない。 (I can't bear the loud music coming from next door every night.)
3. こんなに残業ばかりさせられてはかなわないよ。 (I can't stand being made to work overtime like this all the time.)
4. 毎回同じ文句を言われてはかなわない。 (I can't bear being nagged with the same complaint every time.)
5. この渋滞で毎朝遅刻しそうになってはかなわない。 (I can't stand nearly being late every morning because of this traffic jam.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often confuse てはかなわない with にはかなわない — they share the verb 敵う/叶う but are unrelated constructions. てはかなわない always follows a te-form verb/adjective and means "I can't bear [repeated state/action]," while にはかなわない follows a noun and means "cannot match/compete with [someone/something]." Also note the register: てはかなわない sounds like a grumbling aside, not neutral written Japanese.
