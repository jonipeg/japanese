# そこをなんとか {#そこをなんとか}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** そこを何とか

**Furigana form:** そこをなんとか

**Meaning:** "Please, somehow... / I know, but please..." — a polite yet insistent request used when pushing back against someone's refusal or stated difficulty, asking them to make an exception or find a way despite the obstacle they've just mentioned.

**Conjugation:**
- そこを何とか + お願いします／していただけませんか (fixed request pattern, typically following the other person's refusal or statement of difficulty)
- Often used as a stand-alone plea: そこを何とか、お願いします！
- Example: 「それは規則なのでできません」「そこを何とかお願いします」 ("That's against the rules, so I can't." / "Please, somehow, I'm begging you.")

**Typical context:** Spoken, polite-but-persistent register — used in negotiation-like situations (asking a shopkeeper for a discount, pleading with an official to bend a rule, asking a boss for an exception). The phrase acknowledges the listener's stated obstacle ("そこ" = that point/issue they just raised) while still pressing for a favor, striking a balance between politeness and persistence. It can sound either earnestly pleading or, in some contexts, slightly cheeky/wheedling depending on tone.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **何とかお願いします** — a more general plea ("please, somehow"), without explicitly referencing "that point" the listener just raised; そこを何とか specifically follows up on a stated refusal or difficulty.
- **どうにかなりませんか** — "isn't there some way this could work out?", a softer, more indirect way of requesting an exception, phrased as a question rather than a direct plea.
- **お願いですから** — "I'm begging you, so...", a more emotionally charged plea, often used in more desperate or personal appeals rather than the slightly more transactional/negotiating tone of そこを何とか.
- **何卒よろしくお願いいたします** — an extremely formal, written request-closing phrase, lacking the insistent, in-the-moment pushback nuance of そこを何とか, which is specifically used in live spoken negotiation.

**Opposite grammar structures:** あっさり諦める ("to give up easily/readily") or 仕方ないと引き下がる ("to back down, accepting it can't be helped") — both represent accepting a refusal without pushing back, the opposite of persistently pleading with そこを何とか.

**Example sentences:**
1. 「今日はもう予約でいっぱいです」「そこを何とかお願いできませんか」
   ("We're fully booked today." "Please, is there any way you could somehow fit me in?")
2. 規則なので無理だとは分かっていますが、そこを何とかお願いします。
   (I understand it's against the rules and difficult, but please, somehow, I'm asking you.)
3. 「値引きはできません」「そこを何とか、お願いしますよ」
   ("We can't offer a discount." "Come on, please, somehow work something out.")
4. 締め切りは過ぎていますが、そこを何とかご検討いただけないでしょうか。
   (I know the deadline has passed, but could you please somehow still consider it?)
5. そこを何とか、今回だけは特別にお願いできませんか。
   (Please, just this once, couldn't you somehow make a special exception?)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes use そこを何とか as an opening request out of context, without the listener having first stated a refusal or difficulty — but the phrase only makes sense as a rebuttal/follow-up to something already declined ("そこ" refers back to the specific obstacle just mentioned). Using it as a first request, with no prior refusal to push back against, sounds confusing or presumptuous. Also, because it directly contests someone's "no," it should be used carefully with superiors — pair it with appropriately humble/polite verb forms to avoid sounding pushy rather than earnestly pleading.
