# ～も～ば、～も {#も~ば、~も}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～も～ば、～も

**Furigana form:** ～も～ば、～も (no special kanji reading)

**Meaning:** "Both X and Y" — lists two parallel qualities, examples, or facts about the same subject, often to emphasize versatility or balance (can include both positive-positive, negative-negative, or a mix of related points).

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞Aも + 動詞/形容詞Aのば形、名詞Bも + 動詞/形容詞B
- い形容詞 → ければ; な形容詞/名詞 → であれば
- 彼は勉強もできれば、スポーツもできる。(He can both study well and do sports well.)
- 時間もなければ、お金もない。(I have neither time nor money — negative-negative pairing.)

**Typical context:** Spoken and written, neutral register. Common in descriptive speech when highlighting multiple traits or circumstances of one subject.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **も～し、～も～し** — similar listing structure using し, slightly more casual/conversational.
- **だけでなく～も** — "not only X but also Y," similar additive meaning but without the parallel ば-structure.
- **たり～たりする** — lists example actions/states, but implies a non-exhaustive list of varied actions rather than paired qualities.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a restrictive structure like しか～ない ("only X, nothing else") contrasts with the inclusive "both X and Y" meaning.

**Example sentences:**
1. この店は値段も手頃なら、味もいい。
   (This restaurant's prices are reasonable, and the taste is good too.)
2. 彼女は歌も上手ければ、ダンスも上手だ。
   (She's good at singing, and she's good at dancing too.)
3. 田中さんは優しければ、仕事も丁寧だ。
   (Mr. Tanaka is kind, and his work is also meticulous.)
4. その部屋は広ければ、眺めもいい。
   (That room is spacious, and the view is also good.)
5. 彼は時間もなければ、やる気もない。
   (He has neither the time nor the motivation.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern requires the first element to be in the conditional ば-form, which learners sometimes forget, instead just stringing も clauses together without conditioning the first verb/adjective correctly. Also note both halves should be related/parallel in tone (both positive, or both negative) — mixing a strongly positive trait with a strongly negative one in the same も～ば～も sentence can sound awkward.
