# ～を余儀なくされる／～を余儀なくさせる {#を余儀なくされる・させる}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～を余儀なくされる／～を余儀なくさせる

**Furigana form:** ～をよぎなくされる／～をよぎなくさせる

**Meaning:** を余儀なくされる = "be forced to do X" (the subject has no choice, due to circumstances beyond their control, but to accept an undesired situation). を余儀なくさせる = the causative counterpart, "(something/someone) forces X to happen" — the subject is the cause compelling the change, not the one affected.

**Conjugation:**
- N + を余儀なくされる (passive — subject is forced into the situation)
- N + を余儀なくさせる (causative — subject forces the situation on someone/something else)

**Typical context:** Formal, written register — common in news reports, business/legal writing, describing being forced into an undesired change by disasters, accidents, or economic conditions.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**ずにはおかない**](grammar-ずにはおかない.md) — expresses that an outcome is inevitable due to natural force or strong determination/emotion, whereas 余儀なくされる expresses being forced into an undesired state by external circumstance beyond one's control.
- **ざるをえない** — "have no choice but to do X," a near-synonym, but ざるをえない frames the compulsion as the subject's own reluctant judgment/decision, while 余儀なくされる frames it as an objective, often officially-stated change forced by circumstance (more common in formal reports).
- [**しまつだ**](grammar-しまつだ.md) — describes an accumulated bad result from a chain of one's own actions/mistakes, whereas 余儀なくされる describes being forced by an external, often blameless circumstance (disaster, policy, accident).

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 台風の影響で電車が止まってしまい、旅行の中止を余儀なくされた。
   (Because of the typhoon, the trains stopped, forcing us to cancel our trip.)
2. 工場の火災は、周辺住民に避難を余儀なくさせた。
   (The factory fire forced the surrounding residents to evacuate.)
3. 業績の悪化により、会社はリストラを余儀なくされた。
   (Due to worsening business performance, the company was forced into restructuring.)
4. 大雪が交通機関のマヒを余儀なくさせた。
   (The heavy snow forced transportation systems into paralysis.)
5. 彼は怪我のため、引退を余儀なくされた。
   (Due to his injury, he was forced into retirement.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern pairs a passive form (される, "I was forced") with a causative form (させる, "it forced [someone]"), and students in the N1 extreme-degree/compulsion cluster frequently mix up which one takes the agent and which takes the affected party. The closest confusion partner is ざるをえない: 余儀なくされる should be chosen when the forcing comes from an objective external circumstance (disaster, policy) described in a formal/news register, while ざるをえない is used for a more personal "I have no choice but to..." judgment.
