## 運賃 (うんちん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a fare, a freight charge

**Similar words:**
- [料金](vocab-33.md#料金-りょうきん)（りょうきん）— a fee/charge in general, a broader term that covers all kinds of service charges, not limited to transportation.
- [送料](vocab-24.md#送料-そうりょう)（そうりょう）— shipping cost specifically for sending packages/mail, a narrower subset overlapping with 運賃 in a freight context.

**Opposite words:**
None notable.

**Example sentences:**
1. 電車の運賃が来月から値上がりする。— Train fares will go up starting next month.
2. バスの運賃は距離によって変わる。— Bus fares vary depending on the distance.
3. 国際便の運賃は高騰している。— Freight charges for international shipping have soared.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 運賃 specifically refers to a charge for transportation (trains, buses, planes, freight) — it shouldn't be used as a generic word for "fee" in unrelated contexts, where 料金 is the correct, broader term.

## 運転手 (うんてんしゅ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a driver

**Similar words:**
- ドライバー — a casual loanword equivalent for "driver," commonly used for taxi/delivery drivers in everyday speech.
- 運転者（うんてんしゃ）— a more formal/legal term for "the person operating a vehicle," often used in traffic laws and insurance documents.

**Opposite words:**
None notable.

**Example sentences:**
1. タクシーの運転手に行き先を伝えた。— I told the taxi driver my destination.
2. バスの運転手が丁寧に案内してくれた。— The bus driver kindly gave directions.
3. 運転手として長年働いている。— I've worked as a driver for many years.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 運転手 is the everyday word for a professional driver (taxi, bus, truck) — for the more legal/technical "operator of a vehicle" (e.g., in an accident report), 運転者 is the term used instead.

## うんと
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a lot, much, greatly (casual intensifier)

**Similar words:**
- たくさん — the everyday, neutral word for "a lot," usable in both formal and casual speech, whereas うんと is distinctly colloquial.
- ずっと — "by far/much more," used for comparisons (ずっといい "much better"), sometimes overlapping with うんと when intensifying a comparison.

**Opposite words:**
- 少し（すこし）— a little

**Example sentences:**
1. うんと勉強して試験に合格した。— I studied a lot and passed the exam.
2. もっとうんと食べてね。— Eat a lot more, okay?
3. 子供の頃はうんと背が低かった。— I was much shorter as a kid.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** うんと is a casual, spoken-register intensifier — it would sound out of place in formal writing or polite business speech, where たくさん or 大いに would be more appropriate.

## 云々 (うんぬん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** and so on, etc.; to comment on, to say this and that

**Similar words:**
- など — the everyday, neutral equivalent of "etc./and so on," used constantly in casual and formal speech alike.
- とやかく言う（とやかくいう）— to criticize/comment on something in a nagging way, overlapping with the "to comment on" sense of 云々.

**Opposite words:**
None notable.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼の過去について云々するつもりはない。— I don't intend to comment on his past.
2. 「結果が悪いから云々」と言い訳するのはやめよう。— Let's stop making excuses like "because the results were bad, etc."
3. 他人の生き方を云々する権利はない。— No one has the right to comment on/criticize others' way of living.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 云々 is a fairly formal, somewhat old-fashioned written word — as a verb (云々する) it often carries a slightly critical nuance of "making unnecessary comments/excuses" about something, rather than neutrally meaning "etc." as in casual speech.

## 運搬 (うんぱん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** transportation, conveyance, carrying (of goods/materials)

**Similar words:**
- [運送](vocab-08.md#運送-うんそう)（うんそう）— overlaps closely, but 運送 leans toward the commercial shipping industry, while 運搬 is more neutral/technical, often describing the physical act of carrying/moving materials (e.g., at a construction site).
- 搬送（はんそう）— to carry/transport, often used specifically for carrying patients (ambulances) or goods within a facility.

**Opposite words:**
None notable.

**Example sentences:**
1. 建材をトラックで運搬する。— Building materials are transported by truck.
2. 重い荷物の運搬には台車を使う。— We use a cart to carry heavy loads.
3. 工場内での運搬作業を効率化した。— We made the in-factory transport work more efficient.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 運搬 emphasizes the physical act of carrying/moving objects (often within a limited area like a factory or site), whereas 運送 is more about the commercial shipping/logistics industry as a whole — the two are easy to conflate but used in different registers.

## 運命 (うんめい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** fate, destiny

**Similar words:**
- [宿命](vocab-02.md#宿命-しゅくめい)（しゅくめい）— destiny/fate, but with a stronger sense of being unavoidable and predetermined from birth, more fatalistic than 運命.
- 運（うん）— luck, a related but narrower concept focused on fortunate/unfortunate chance rather than one's overall life trajectory.

**Opposite words:**
None notable.

**Example sentences:**
1. 二人は運命的な出会いをした。— The two of them had a fateful encounter.
2. 自分の運命を受け入れることにした。— I decided to accept my fate.
3. これも運命だと思って前に進もう。— Let's think of this as fate too and move forward.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 運命的（うんめいてき）"fateful/destined" is a very common adjectival form, especially in romantic contexts (運命の人 "destined person/soulmate") — learners who only know the bare noun 運命 might miss this frequent derived usage.

## 運輸 (うんゆ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** transportation, transport (as an industry/sector)

**Similar words:**
- [運送](vocab-08.md#運送-うんそう)（うんそう）— overlaps closely but tends to refer to the shipping business for goods, while 運輸 is a broader, more official term covering the entire transport sector (including passengers, used in government ministry names like 国土交通省 formerly 運輸省).
- 交通（こうつう）— traffic/transportation in general, a broader everyday term covering roads, trains, and travel infrastructure.

**Opposite words:**
None notable.

**Example sentences:**
1. 運輸業界は深刻な人手不足に直面している。— The transportation industry is facing a serious labor shortage.
2. かつて運輸省という省庁が存在した。— There used to be a government ministry called the Ministry of Transport.
3. 運輸関連の株価が上昇した。— Transportation-related stock prices rose.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 運輸 is a formal, industry/government-level term (used in company and ministry names) rather than everyday conversation — for daily use about getting around, 交通 is far more natural.

## 運用 (うんよう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** operation, utilization, application (of a system, rule, or funds)

**Similar words:**
- [運営](vocab-08.md#運営-うんえい)（うんえい）— management/administration of an organization or event, while 運用 is more about operating or applying a system, rule, or financial asset.
- [活用](vocab-13.md#活用-かつよう)（かつよう）— to make effective use of something, overlapping when 運用 means "utilizing" resources or funds effectively.

**Opposite words:**
None notable.

**Example sentences:**
1. 資産を効率的に運用する。— To manage/invest assets efficiently.
2. 新しいルールの運用を開始した。— We began operating under the new rule.
3. このシステムの運用にはコストがかかる。— Operating this system incurs costs.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 運用 is especially common in finance (資産運用 "asset management/investment") and IT/systems (システムの運用 "system operations") — don't confuse it with 運営, which is about managing an organization or event rather than operating a system or applying rules/funds.

## 柄 (え)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a handle, a grip

**Similar words:**
- 取っ手（とって）— a handle/knob, often used for doors, drawers, or pots, slightly more general than 柄, which often refers to the handle of a tool or weapon (e.g., a knife, brush, umbrella).
- ハンドル — a loanword for "handle," commonly used for car steering wheels or bicycle handlebars.

**Opposite words:**
None notable.

**Example sentences:**
1. 包丁の柄が割れてしまった。— The handle of the kitchen knife cracked.
2. 傘の柄をしっかり握った。— I gripped the umbrella's handle firmly.
3. 筆の柄は竹でできている。— The handle of the writing brush is made of bamboo.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The kanji 柄 has two completely different readings and meanings: え "handle" and がら "pattern/design" or "one's character" (人柄 ひとがら "personality") — context is essential to distinguish them, and this is a common source of misreading.

## エアメール
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** airmail

**Similar words:**
- 航空便（こうくうびん）— the more formal/official Japanese term for airmail, used on postal forms and at post office counters, while エアメール is the casual loanword equivalent.
- 船便（ふなびん）— surface/sea mail, the slower, cheaper alternative method of shipping often contrasted with airmail.

**Opposite words:**
- 船便（ふなびん）— surface/sea mail

**Example sentences:**
1. 海外の友人にエアメールで手紙を送った。— I sent a letter to my friend overseas by airmail.
2. エアメールは船便より早いが高い。— Airmail is faster than sea mail but more expensive.
3. 小包をエアメールで発送してもらった。— I had the parcel shipped by airmail.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** At Japanese post offices, the official term used on forms is 航空便, while エアメール is the everyday spoken loanword — both are understood, but recognizing 航空便 is important for navigating actual postal paperwork.

## ~営 (えい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** ~management/operation (suffix)

**Similar words:**
- ~経営（けいえい）— the fuller word meaning "management (of a business)," from which ~営 is often the abbreviated suffix form in compounds (直営 "direct management/operation").
- ~運営（うんえい）— "operation/administration," a related compound sharing the same 営 character.

**Opposite words:**
None notable.

**Example sentences:**
1. このレストランは直営店だ。— This restaurant is a directly-managed (company-owned) store.
2. 公営住宅に住んでいる。— I live in public (government-operated) housing.
3. 民営化によってサービスが向上した。— Privatization improved the service.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ~営 rarely stands alone — it's a bound suffix appearing in compounds like 直営（ちょくえい, directly managed）, 公営（こうえい, publicly operated）, 民営（みんえい, privately operated）— learning it as a suffix pattern is more useful than trying to use it as an independent word.

## 永遠 (えいえん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** eternity, permanence, forever

**Similar words:**
- [永久](#永久-えいきゅう)（えいきゅう）— very close in meaning ("permanence/eternity"), often interchangeable, though 永遠 leans slightly more poetic/emotional (永遠の愛 "eternal love"), while 永久 is more often used in formal/legal/scientific contexts (永久歯 "permanent teeth").
- とこしえ — a more archaic, literary word for "eternity," found in classical or poetic writing.

**Opposite words:**
- 一時的（いちじてき）— temporary

**Example sentences:**
1. 二人は永遠の愛を誓った。— The two of them vowed eternal love.
2. この美しい景色を永遠に忘れないだろう。— I will never forget this beautiful scenery.
3. 永遠に続くものなど何もない。— Nothing lasts forever.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 永遠 is the go-to word for emotional/poetic "eternity" (永遠の愛, 永遠に) while 永久 fits more technical or legal permanence (永久保存, 永久歯) — swapping them is understandable but sounds slightly unnatural to native speakers.

## 永久 (えいきゅう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** permanence, eternity, perpetuity

**Similar words:**
- [永遠](#永遠-えいえん)（えいえん）— nearly synonymous, but more poetic/emotional, while 永久 is favored in formal, scientific, or legal registers (永久凍土 "permafrost," 永久歯 "permanent teeth").
- 恒久的（こうきゅうてき）— "permanent/lasting," a formal adjective often used in policy or peace contexts (恒久平和 "lasting peace").

**Opposite words:**
- 一時的（いちじてき）— temporary

**Example sentences:**
1. この契約は永久に有効だ。— This contract is permanently valid.
2. 永久歯が生えてきた。— My permanent teeth have come in.
3. 平和が永久に続くことを願う。— I hope peace continues forever.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 永久 tends to appear in fixed, formal compounds (永久歯, 永久保存版, 永久凍土) rather than freely in emotional speech — for romantic or poetic "forever," 永遠 is the more natural choice.

## 影響 (えいきょう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** influence, effect, impact

**Similar words:**
- 効果（こうか）— effect/efficacy, more neutral and often positive (a medicine's 効果), whereas 影響 is neutral-to-negative and broader, covering any kind of impact.
- インパクト — a loanword for "impact," often used for a strong, dramatic effect, more casual than 影響.

**Opposite words:**
None notable.

**Example sentences:**
1. その出来事は彼の人生に大きな影響を与えた。— That event had a big influence on his life.
2. 気候変動が農業に影響を及ぼしている。— Climate change is affecting agriculture.
3. 親の教育方針が子供に影響する。— A parent's educational approach influences the child.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 影響 pairs with specific verbs — 影響を与える (to give/have an influence) and 影響を受ける (to be influenced/affected) — mixing up these two directions (giving vs. receiving influence) is a very common learner error.

## 営業 (えいぎょう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** business operations, sales (as a job function)

**Similar words:**
- 販売（はんばい）— sales (the act of selling products), narrower than 営業, which covers the whole business-operating/sales-rep function including client relations.
- 商売（しょうばい）— trade/business/commerce in a more general, sometimes old-fashioned or colloquial sense.

**Opposite words:**
None notable.

**Example sentences:**
1. このお店は年中無休で営業している。— This shop operates (is open) year-round without holidays.
2. 彼は営業部で働いている。— He works in the sales department.
3. 新規顧客を開拓するのが営業の仕事だ。— Developing new clients is the job of sales.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 営業 has two common senses — "to be open for business" (営業中 "open now") and "the sales department/job" (営業部, 営業マン) — learners sometimes only learn one sense and are confused encountering the other.

## 英字 (えいじ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** Roman letters, English script/characters

**Similar words:**
- アルファベット — the loanword for "the alphabet" in general, broader than 英字, which specifically implies English/Roman letters as used in Japanese contexts (英字新聞 "English-language newspaper").
- [ローマ字](vocab-36.md#ローマ字-ローマじ)（ろーまじ）— romanized Japanese (Japanese written in Roman letters), a related but distinct concept from 英字, which usually refers to actual English text/letters.

**Opposite words:**
- [漢字](vocab-13.md#漢字-かんじ)（かんじ）— kanji characters

**Example sentences:**
1. パスワードには英字と数字を含めてください。— Please include Roman letters and numbers in your password.
2. 彼は毎朝英字新聞を読んでいる。— He reads an English-language newspaper every morning.
3. 名前を英字で記入してください。— Please fill in your name in Roman letters.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 英字 specifically denotes the Roman/English alphabet characters themselves (often contrasted with 漢字 or 数字 on forms) — it's distinct from ローマ字, which is about writing Japanese words using Roman letters, not English text.

## 映写 (えいしゃ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** projection (of a film), screening

**Similar words:**
- 上映（じょうえい）— screening/showing a film (to an audience), the more common everyday word; 映写 is more technical, referring to the mechanical act of projecting the image.
- 投影（とうえい）— projection in a broader or more scientific/figurative sense (e.g., projecting an image or an idea), not limited to film.

**Opposite words:**
None notable.

**Example sentences:**
1. 映写技師が映画のフィルムを操作した。— The projectionist operated the film reel.
2. 映写機の調子が悪く、上映が中断した。— The projector malfunctioned and the screening was interrupted.
3. 古い映写室には独特の雰囲気がある。— The old projection room has a unique atmosphere.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 映写 is a technical term tied to the physical act/equipment of projecting film (映写機 "projector," 映写技師 "projectionist") — for the everyday sense of "a movie is showing/screening," 上映 is the natural word to use.

## 衛生 (えいせい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** hygiene, sanitation

**Similar words:**
- [清潔](vocab-22.md#清潔-せいけつ)（せいけつ）— cleanliness, a more general state of being clean, while 衛生 specifically concerns health-related cleanliness practices/standards (food safety, sanitation systems).
- [健康](vocab-18.md#健康-けんこう)（けんこう）— health, a broader concept of overall well-being, of which 衛生 (hygienic practices) is one contributing factor.

**Opposite words:**
- [不衛生](vocab-32.md#不衛生-ふえいせい)（ふえいせい）— unhygienic, unsanitary

3. この厨房は衛生管理が徹底している。— This kitchen thoroughly manages hygiene.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 衛生 is a formal, systems-level term (used in 食品衛生, 公衆衛生, 衛生管理) referring to sanitation standards and practices — for simply describing a tidy room, the more everyday 清潔 fits better.

## 映像 (えいぞう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** an image, a picture, footage (video/visual media)

**Similar words:**
- 画像（がぞう）— an image, often a still picture (especially digital/photo), while 映像 implies moving images/footage (video).
- 動画（どうが）— a video (clip), a more casual/common term for moving footage, especially online content.

None notable.

1. 事故の瞬間を捉えた映像が公開された。— Footage capturing the moment of the accident was released.

3. ニュースでその映像が繰り返し流れた。— That footage was played repeatedly on the news.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 映像 generally implies moving footage/visual media (news clips, film cinematography), while 画像 refers to still images — mixing these up is common since both can loosely translate to "image" in English.

#vocabulary

**Meaning:** English text, an English sentence/composition

**Similar words:**
- 英語（えいご）— the English language in general, broader than 英文, which specifically refers to written English text or composition.
- 英訳（えいやく）— an English translation, a specific product of translating something into English.

## 英雄 (えいゆう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a hero

**Similar words:**
- ヒーロー — the loanword equivalent, common in casual speech and pop culture (movies, comics), while 英雄 is more formal/historical, often describing real historical or national figures.
- 勇者（ゆうしゃ）— a brave warrior/hero, common in fantasy and folklore contexts (particularly games/stories), distinct from the historical/real-world connotation of 英雄.

**Opposite words:**
- 悪役（あくやく）— a villain

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は国民的英雄として称えられている。— He is celebrated as a national hero.
2. その戦いで多くの英雄が生まれた。— Many heroes emerged from that battle.
3. 歴史上の英雄について調べている。— I'm researching historical heroes.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 英雄 carries a weighty, formal connotation suited to historical or national figures — using it casually about, say, a friend who helped you move apartments would sound exaggeratedly grand; ヒーロー is the lighter, more casual alternative for everyday "hero" talk.

## 栄養 (えいよう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** nutrition

**Similar words:**
- 栄養素（えいようそ）— a nutrient, the specific component (vitamins, protein) that makes up 栄養 as a whole concept.
- 食事（しょくじ）— a meal, the broader act of eating, which provides 栄養 but isn't synonymous with it.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 栄養 is the abstract concept of "nutrition," while specific nutrients are 栄養素 — saying ビタミンという栄養 ("vitamin, a nutrition") instead of 栄養素 is a common error for learners still sorting out the noun hierarchy.

## 英和 (えいわ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** English-Japanese (as in a dictionary)

**Similar words:**

## ええと
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** um, let me see..., er... (filler word while thinking)

**Similar words:**
- あの（う）— another common filler used to get someone's attention or hesitate before speaking, slightly different in that it's often used when initiating a request.
- えっと — a near-identical casual variant of ええと, used interchangeably in spoken Japanese.

**Opposite words:**
None notable.

**Example sentences:**
1. ええと、今何時でしたっけ。— Um, what time is it again?
2. ええと、名前は何だったかな。— Let me see, what was the name again...

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ええと is a verbal filler used purely while thinking/hesitating — it carries no grammatical meaning and should not be incorporated into written formal Japanese; overusing it can sound natural in casual speech but unprofessional in presentations.

- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 大方の予想通り、彼が勝った。— As most people expected, he won.
2. 仕事は大方終わった。— The work is mostly finished.
3. 大方、彼は遅刻するだろう。— He'll probably be late, I'd guess.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 大方 has two distinct uses: a noun meaning "the general public/majority" (大方の意見, public opinion) and an adverb meaning "mostly" or "probably" (often with だろう) — learners should distinguish these from the simpler, more casual 大体.
