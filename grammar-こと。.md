# ～こと。 {#こと。}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～事。

**Furigana form:** ～こと。

**Meaning:** A sentence-final こと used as a formal/written command, instruction, or rule — equivalent to "Do X." / "Must X." / "Do not X." in notices, regulations, instructions, and official announcements.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形 + こと。 (must do X)
- 動詞ない形 + こと。 (must not do X)
- Example: 廊下を走らないこと。 (Do not run in the hallway.)
- Example: 使用後は電源を切ること。 (Turn off the power after use.)

**Typical context:** Exclusively written, formal register — found in school rules, signs, manuals, exam instructions, workplace regulations, and official notices. Impersonal and directive in tone; it is not used in ordinary conversation and would sound oddly curt or bureaucratic if spoken aloud. This usage is grammatically distinct from the nominalizer こと (which turns a verb/clause into a noun phrase, e.g. 泳ぐことが好きです), even though the surface form is identical — here こと at the end of a sentence (followed by a period) functions as an imperative/instructional marker, not as a noun.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **なさい** — a direct spoken/written command form ("do X"), softer and more personal, typically used by teachers/parents to children or in classroom instructions rather than formal written rules.
- **べし／べきである** — a formal written obligation form ("should/must do X"), common in legal or academic writing, slightly more archaic/formal than sentence-final こと。
- [**ように**](grammar-ように(example).md) — used for indirect requests or wishes ("please make sure to..."), softer and less rule-like than the blunt command of こと。
- **のこと** (nominalizer こと) — the surface-identical but grammatically different usage where こと turns a preceding clause into a noun; distinguished by context (mid-sentence noun phrase vs. sentence-final command with a period).

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite grammatical structure; the contrasting idea of "permission" would instead be expressed with 〜してもよい or 〜して構わない, rather than another こと。-type structure.

**Example sentences:**
1. 携帯電話の電源は必ず切ること。
   (Be sure to turn off your cell phone.)
2. 図書館内では静かにすること。
   (Be quiet inside the library.)
3. 他人の作品を無断でコピーしないこと。
   (Do not copy others' work without permission.)
4. 試験中は辞書を使用しないこと。
   (Do not use a dictionary during the exam.)
5. 提出物は必ず期限内に出すこと。
   (Submissions must be turned in by the deadline without fail.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often mistake this sentence-final こと。 for the ordinary nominalizer こと (as in 〜することが大切だ), especially since the two look identical. The key distinguishing clue is position and punctuation: if こと comes at the very end of a sentence, immediately followed by a period, with no particle or predicate after it, it functions as a formal instruction/command, not a noun. Another common mistake is using this structure in casual speech — it is strictly a written/notice-board register and sounds unnaturally stiff or even confusing if spoken in conversation.
