# ～のきらいがある {#のきらいがある}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～の嫌いがある

**Furigana form:** ～のきらいがある

**Meaning:** "Tends to have the (negative) tendency of X" — describes a mild, chronic character flaw or recurring undesirable tendency, usually stated as a gentle criticism.

**Conjugation:**
- Nの + きらいがある
- V辞書形 + きらいがある

**Typical context:** Formal written register, also usable in formal spoken critique (academic commentary, personality assessments). Mildly critical, understated tone — not harsh or casual.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**がち**](grammar-がち.md) — "tends to do X," a much more casual and general-purpose tendency marker usable for neutral or negative tendencies in everyday speech, lacking きらいがある's formal critical nuance.
- [**きわまる**](grammar-きわまる・きわまりない.md) — describes an extreme quality present right now, not a recurring behavioral tendency over time.
- [**しまつだ**](grammar-しまつだ.md) — describes the final bad result of an accumulated chain of actions/events, a consequence rather than きらいがある's ongoing character tendency.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite (could loosely contrast with 傾向がない, "has no such tendency," but this is not a fixed grammatical pattern).

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女はやや自分勝手で人の意見を無視するきらいがありますね。
   (She's a bit selfish and tends to ignore other people's opinions.)
2. 彼は何でも一人で抱え込むきらいがある。
   (He has a tendency to take everything on by himself.)
3. この会社は新しいアイデアを軽視するきらいがある。
   (This company tends to undervalue new ideas.)
4. 彼の説明は専門用語に頼りすぎるきらいがある。
   (His explanations tend to rely too much on technical jargon.)
5. 若い研究者は結論を急ぐきらいがある。
   (Young researchers tend to rush to conclusions.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** As part of the N1 extreme-degree/tendency confusable cluster, きらいがある is most often confused with the much more casual がち — students mistake them as interchangeable, but きらいがある carries a formal, understated critical judgment about character or systemic behavior, while がち is a lighter, more general-purpose "often happens to" marker usable even for neutral occurrences (曇りがち, "tends to be cloudy"). If the sentence is a formal critique of a persistent flaw, きらいがある is correct; if it's a casual description of frequency, がち fits better.
