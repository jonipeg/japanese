# ～ようがない {#ようがない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ようがない

**Furigana form:** ～ようがない

**Meaning:** "There's no way to do X" / "it's impossible to do X because the means/method to do so don't exist."

**Conjugation:**
- V-ます形(stem) + ようがない

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal; comfortably usable in both everyday spoken complaints and formal writing — one of the more conversational members of this cluster.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**にたえない**](grammar-にたえる・にたえない.md) — describes an emotional/aesthetic inability to bear enduring something, a reaction, distinct from ようがない's practical impossibility of carrying out an action due to lacking means.
- [**ないものでもない**](grammar-ないものでもない.md) — the conceptual opposite, allowing for some possibility, in contrast to ようがない's flat denial of any possibility.
- [**かぎりだ**](grammar-かぎりだ.md) — a subjective personal feeling exclamation, unrelated to ようがない's statement about practical impossibility.

**Opposite grammar structures:** [**ないものでもない**](grammar-ないものでもない.md) — "it's not impossible that X," allowing for some possibility, directly contrasting with ようがない's total impossibility.

**Example sentences:**
1. こんなに古いと、部品がないので、直しようがありませんね。
   (Since it's this old, there are no parts, so there's no way to fix it.)
2. 連絡先を知らないので、彼に伝えようがない。
   (I don't know his contact info, so there's no way to tell him.)
3. 証拠が何もないのだから、疑いようがない。
   (Since there's no evidence at all, there's no way to doubt it.)
4. こんなに複雑な問題は、簡単に説明しようがない。
   (A problem this complex has no easy way to be explained.)
5. 本人が黙っている以上、真相は確かめようがない。
   (As long as the person involved stays silent, there's no way to confirm the truth.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ようがない is a frequently tested member of the N1 impossibility/extreme-degree cluster and is often confused with にたえない. The key distinction: ようがない is about a PRACTICAL impossibility — there is literally no method or means available (直しようがない, "no way to fix it because there are no parts") — while にたえない is about an EMOTIONAL/AESTHETIC refusal to continue enduring something you are physically capable of doing (listening, watching) but find unbearable.
