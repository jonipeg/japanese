# ～べく／べし {#べくべし}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～べく／べし

**Furigana form:** ～べく／べし

**Meaning:** "In order to X" (べく, purpose) / "should/must do X, worthy of X" (べし, classical obligation/recommendation) — a classical auxiliary with two related but distinct uses: べく expresses purpose ("in order to"), while べし (and its modern descendant べきだ) expresses obligation or strong recommendation.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形 + べく (purpose clause, する→すべく)
- 動詞辞書形 + べし (classical sentence-final predicate of obligation/recommendation)
- Example base: 成功する → 成功すべく努力する；為すべし

**Typical context:** Formal, written/literary and classical register; べく appears in formal written purpose clauses (reports, formal writing), while べし is largely classical/archaic, surviving mainly in proverbs, mottos, and fixed phrases, with べきだ as its living modern equivalent.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **ために** — "in order to X"; the modern, everyday equivalent of purpose-clause べく, usable in any register, while べく is restricted to formal written contexts.
- [**べきだ**](grammar-べきだ.md) — "should do X"; the modern living descendant of classical べし, used productively in everyday formal speech and writing, whereas べし itself is largely archaic/classical.
- [**べからず**](grammar-べからず・べからざる.md) — "must not do X" (a related べく-stem prohibition, e.g., 立入るべからず, "No Entry"); shares the same classical べし root but expresses prohibition rather than purpose or recommendation.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct single opposite; べからず (prohibition) functions as a kind of negative counterpart to べし's affirmative obligation.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は試験に合格すべく、毎日猛勉強している。 (In order to pass the exam, he studies intensely every day.)
2. 会社を立て直すべく、新しい経営陣が任命された。 (In order to rebuild the company, a new management team was appointed.)
3. 若者よ、大志を抱くべし。(クラーク博士の言葉) (Young people, you should hold great ambitions. — Dr. Clark's famous words)
4. 彼女は優勝すべく、厳しい練習を続けた。 (In order to win the championship, she kept up rigorous training.)
5. 人はパンのみにて生くるものに非ず、されど為すべきことを為すべし。 (Man does not live by bread alone, but should do what must be done.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** べく and べし are often confused because they share the same classical root (べし, the conclusive/predicate form, versus べく, its conjunctive/purpose-clause form) — べく is used mid-sentence before a following verb describing effort toward a goal, while べし stands as a classical sentence-ending predicate (now mostly replaced by べきだ in modern speech, except in quotations, mottos, and set historical phrases). Learners should recognize べし primarily for reading classical or deliberately archaic text, not for active modern production.
