# ～なり {#なり}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**1. Kanji version:** ～なり (no kanji form; purely grammatical kana word)

**2. Furigana version:** ～なり

**3. Meaning:** The instant X happens, Y happens immediately afterward (a sudden, immediate next action).

**4. Conjugation (connection form):** 動詞辞書形 + なり

**5. Usage context:** Literary/written register — common in novels, newspaper narrative, and formal writing describing a sequence of events. Not used in casual conversation.

**6. Similar grammar structures:**
- が早いか — very close synonym ("no sooner had X than Y"), slightly more literary/written in tone.
- とたんに — a more common, slightly more casual synonym meaning "the moment X happened."
- なり〜なり — a DIFFERENT pattern using the same word repeated twice to mean "either X or Y" (listing alternatives); easily confused with single なり due to identical surface form but functions completely differently.

**7. Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; て以来／てから (after some duration has passed) loosely contrast with なり's instantaneity.

**8. Example sentences:**
1. 校長先生はスピーチをしようとマイクを持つなり、倒れてしまった。(The principal collapsed the instant he picked up the microphone to give his speech.)
2. 彼は部屋に入るなり、大声で叫び始めた。(The moment he entered the room, he started shouting loudly.)
3. 娘は学校から帰るなり、自分の部屋に駆け込んだ。(The instant my daughter got home from school, she ran straight to her room.)
4. その知らせを聞くなり、彼女の顔色が変わった。(The moment she heard the news, her expression changed.)
5. 犯人は警官の姿を見るなり、逃げ出した。(The instant the criminal saw the police officer, he ran away.)

**9. Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern appears twice independently confirmed in the source tests, showing it is heavily drilled. The biggest pitfall is confusing it with なり〜なり — single なり requires one verb followed by a separate clause describing a sudden subsequent event, while なり〜なり requires the word なり to appear twice, listing two alternative options.
