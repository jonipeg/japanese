## 看板 (かんばん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a signboard, a sign

**Similar words:**
- サイン — loanword "sign," often for autographs or smaller signs/signals, not store signboards.
- 標識（ひょうしき）— "a (road/traffic) sign," specifically for official signage like traffic signs, distinct from a shop's 看板.

**Example sentences:**
1. 店の看板を新しくした。— We updated the shop's signboard.
2. 彼はこの店の看板メニューだ。— This is the shop's flagship (signature) menu item.
3. 看板に偽りあり、という評判だ。— The reputation is "false advertising" (lit. "there is falsehood in the sign").

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 看板 extends figuratively to mean "flagship/signature" (看板商品, 看板メニュー — a business's star product), beyond the literal physical sign — learners who only know the literal meaning often miss this common figurative business usage.

## 看病 (かんびょう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** nursing, tending to a sick person (usually a family member)

**Similar words:**
- [看護](vocab-13.md#看護-かんご)（かんご）— professional medical nursing (by a nurse, 看護師), more clinical than the personal/family care implied by 看病.
- [介護](vocab-12.md#介護-かいご)（かいご）— caregiving for the elderly/disabled in daily life, often long-term, distinct from short-term sickness care.

**Example sentences:**
1. 母の看病で学校を休んだ。— I missed school to take care of my sick mother.
2. 夜通し父の看病をした。— I tended to my father all through the night.
3. 入院中の祖母を看病している。— I'm nursing my hospitalized grandmother.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 看病 is specifically about caring for someone who is sick (temporary illness), usually a family member, done by a non-professional — don't confuse it with 看護, the professional medical term performed by nurses.

## 完璧 (かんぺき)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** perfection, perfect

**Similar words:**
- 完全（かんぜん）— "complete/whole," can mean "perfect" too but is more neutral/technical; 完璧 has a stronger connotation of flawless excellence.
- パーフェクト — loanword "perfect," casual and often used playfully, similar register to 完璧.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼のプレゼンは完璧だった。— His presentation was perfect.
2. 完璧を求めすぎるとストレスになる。— Demanding too much perfection becomes stressful.
3. 完璧な計画など存在しない。— There's no such thing as a perfect plan.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 完璧主義（かんぺきしゅぎ, perfectionism) is a common related compound — 完璧 itself is a na-adjective/noun, so it needs な before a noun (完璧な人) and だ/です as a predicate, not direct verb conjugation.

## 勘弁 (かんべん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** pardon, forgiveness, to let off/spare

**Similar words:**
- [許す](vocab-35.md#許す-ゆるす)（ゆるす）— plain verb "to forgive/allow," broader and more neutral than the somewhat exasperated tone 勘弁 often carries.
- 容赦（ようしゃ）— "mercy/leniency," more formal, often in negative constructions (容赦なく, mercilessly).

**Example sentences:**
1. もう勘弁してください。— Please, enough already (spare me this).
2. 今回だけは勘弁してやろう。— I'll let it slide just this once.
3. そんな冗談は勘弁してほしい。— I wish you'd spare me jokes like that.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 勘弁して(ください) is often used colloquially with a tone of exasperation or pleading ("come on, give me a break!") rather than a neutral formal request for forgiveness — the tone is more emotional/informal than 許してください.

## 感無量 (かんむりょう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** being full of emotion, deeply moved beyond words

**Similar words:**
- 感慨無量（かんがいむりょう）— a four-character idiom meaning essentially the same thing, often used interchangeably and slightly more formal/literary.
- 胸がいっぱい（むねがいっぱい）— casual phrase "my heart/chest is full," a more everyday way to express the same overwhelmed emotional feeling.

**Example sentences:**
1. 優勝した瞬間、感無量だった。— At the moment of winning the championship, I was overwhelmed with emotion.
2. 卒業式で感無量の表情を浮かべた。— He wore a deeply moved expression at the graduation ceremony.
3. 長年の夢が叶い、感無量です。— My long-held dream has come true, and I am overcome with emotion.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 感無量 is reserved for significant, often once-in-a-lifetime emotional moments (graduations, championships, reunions) — using it for small everyday happiness would sound overblown and out of place.

## 関与 (かんよ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** participation, involvement

**Similar words:**
- 参加（さんか）— "participation" in an event, more neutral and often positive, while 関与 frequently implies involvement in something questionable or under scrutiny (crimes, scandals).
- [介入](vocab-11.md#介入-かいにゅう)（かいにゅう）— "intervention," implies actively stepping in to influence something, often externally and with more force than 関与.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼はその事件への関与を否定した。— He denied his involvement in the incident.
2. 政府の関与が疑われている。— Government involvement is suspected.
3. 不正取引への関与が発覚した。— Involvement in fraudulent transactions came to light.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 関与 frequently appears in news contexts about scandals, crimes, or corruption (事件への関与, 不正への関与) — it carries a more neutral-to-negative connotation than 参加, which learners should keep in mind when choosing between the two.

## 粥 (かゆ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** rice porridge, congee

**Similar words:**
- おかゆ — the polite/common everyday form with お-prefix, used in daily conversation far more than the bare 粥.
- 雑炊（ぞうすい）— a similar rice porridge but typically made by simmering cooked rice with broth and other ingredients, often as a hot-pot finishing dish, distinct from plain 粥.

**Example sentences:**
1. 風邪の時はお粥を食べるといい。— It's good to eat rice porridge when you have a cold.
2. 朝食に中華粥を作った。— I made Chinese-style congee for breakfast.
3. 病み上がりなので、消化に良いお粥を用意した。— Since I just recovered from being sick, I prepared easy-to-digest rice porridge.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** お粥 is strongly associated with being sick or recovering (easy to digest, comfort food) in Japanese culture — mentioning お粥 often implies someone is unwell, similar to how "chicken soup" implies illness in English.

## 痒い (かゆい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** itchy

**Similar words:**
- 痛い（いたい）— "painful," a different physical sensation, sometimes confused by learners due to similar adjective structure (〜い adjectives for bodily sensations).
- むずむずする — onomatopoeic expression for a tingling/itchy restless sensation, softer and more descriptive than the direct adjective 痒い.

**Example sentences:**
1. 蚊に刺されて腕が痒い。— My arm is itchy from a mosquito bite.
2. 背中が痒くて掻いてしまう。— My back is itchy, so I end up scratching it.
3. この服の素材は肌がかゆくなる。— The material of this clothing makes my skin itch.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** There's a common saying 痒い所に手が届く ("to reach the itchy spot," meaning thoughtful/thorough attention to detail) — recognizing this idiom helps learners understand praise for meticulous service or products.

## 通う (かよう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to commute, to go regularly (to work/school/a place)

**Similar words:**
- 行く（いく）— plain "to go," a single trip; 通う implies regular, repeated trips over time (commuting to the same place).
- 往復する（おうふくする）— "to go back and forth," emphasizes the round-trip aspect rather than the habitual/regular nature of 通う.

**Example sentences:**
1. 毎日電車で会社に通っている。— I commute to work by train every day.
2. 子供はピアノ教室に通っている。— The child regularly attends piano lessons.
3. 大学に通うために一人暮らしを始めた。— I started living alone in order to commute to university.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 通う implies habitual, repeated attendance over an extended period (school, work, a gym) — using it for a single one-time visit is incorrect; that would just be 行く.

## 火曜日 (かようび)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** Tuesday

**Similar words:**
- 月曜日（げつようび）— Monday, the preceding day of the week.
- [水曜日](vocab-22.md#水曜日-すいようび)（すいようび）— Wednesday, the following day of the week.

**Example sentences:**
1. 会議は火曜日に開かれる。— The meeting will be held on Tuesday.
2. 火曜日は祝日だったので休みだった。— Tuesday was a holiday, so I had the day off.
3. 毎週火曜日にジムに行っている。— I go to the gym every Tuesday.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The days of the week in Japanese are each tied to an element/celestial body (火 = fire, linked to Mars 火星) — recognizing this pattern (日月火水木金土) makes it much easier to memorize and recall all seven day names together.

## 空 (から)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** empty

**Similar words:**
- [空っぽ](#空っぽ-からっぽ)（からっぽ）— emphatic/casual version of "empty," often used in spoken Japanese for a more vivid, total emptiness.
- 虚しい（むなしい）— "empty/futile" in an emotional/existential sense, not physical emptiness like 空.

**Opposite words:**
- いっぱい — full

**Example sentences:**
1. 財布が空になってしまった。— My wallet has become empty.
2. 空の缶をリサイクルに出した。— I put the empty can out for recycling.
3. 冷蔵庫が空だったので買い物に行った。— The fridge was empty, so I went shopping.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 空（から, empty）and 空（そら, sky）are the same kanji with completely different readings and meanings — context is essential, since there's no way to distinguish them from the kanji alone.

## 柄 (がら)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** pattern, design; build, physique; character/nature

**Similar words:**
- 模様（もよう）— "pattern/design," more specifically visual/decorative, overlapping with 柄 in that sense but without the "build/character" extended meanings.
- [体格](vocab-25.md#体格-たいかく)（たいかく）— "physique/build," a more specific/technical term for body size compared to the broader, more casual 柄 (as in 柄が大きい, "to be big-built").

**Example sentences:**
1. この服の柄が気に入った。— I like the pattern of this clothing.
2. 彼は柄が大きいのでスーツのサイズが合わない。— He's big-built, so suit sizes don't fit him.
3. 人柄がいいので皆に好かれている。— He's well-liked by everyone because of his good character.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 柄 has three quite different senses (visual pattern, physical build, and personal character as in 人柄/家柄) — learners should pay attention to compound words to know which sense is meant, as they don't obviously connect to each other.

## カラー
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** color; a collar (clothing)

**Similar words:**
- 色（いろ）— the native Japanese word for "color," used far more often in everyday speech than the loanword カラー.
- [襟](vocab-09.md#襟-えり)（えり）— the native Japanese word for "collar," more common in everyday clothing descriptions than the loanword カラー in this sense.

**Example sentences:**
1. このテレビはカラー画面だ。— This TV has a color screen.
2. シャツのカラーにアイロンをかけた。— I ironed the shirt's collar.
3. チームカラーは青です。— The team color is blue.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** カラー covers two unrelated meanings borrowed from English "color" and "collar" — context (clothing vs. visual color) is needed to tell them apart, and learners should not assume one meaning covers both uses.

## 辛い (からい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** spicy, hot (taste)

**Similar words:**
- [辛い](#辛い-からい)（つらい）— a homograph (same kanji, different reading) meaning "painful/difficult emotionally" — completely different meaning from the spicy-taste 辛い（からい）.
- スパイシー — loanword "spicy," used more for describing complex spice flavor profiles (as in cuisine reviews) than simple heat.

**Opposite words:**
- 甘い（あまい）— sweet

**Example sentences:**
1. このカレーはとても辛い。— This curry is very spicy.
2. 辛い物が苦手な人もいる。— Some people don't do well with spicy food.
3. もう少し辛くしてもらえますか。— Could you make it a bit spicier?

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 辛い is a classic homograph: からい (spicy) vs. つらい (painful/hard) — both are written identically in kanji, so learners must infer the reading from context, which can cause real confusion in written text without furigana.

## ガラス
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** glass

**Similar words:**
- 硝子（しょうし/がらす）— the kanji form of ガラス, rarely used in modern writing (ガラス in katakana is standard).
- [グラス](vocab-16.md#グラス-グラス) — a different loanword specifically meaning "a drinking glass," distinct from ガラス, the material.

**Example sentences:**
1. 窓のガラスが割れてしまった。— The window glass broke.
2. ガラス製のコップを使っている。— I use a glass-made cup.
3. ガラス越しに景色を眺めた。— I looked at the scenery through the glass.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ガラス (the material "glass") and グラス (a "drinking glass") are easily confused loanwords with nearly identical pronunciation — mixing them up is a common beginner-to-intermediate error.

## 空っぽ (からっぽ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** empty, vacant (emphatic)

**Similar words:**
- [空](#空-から)（から）— the plainer, less emphatic word for "empty"; 空っぽ is more colloquial and vivid, often used in spoken Japanese.
- がらんと — onomatopoeic adverb describing a large space feeling empty/hollow (an empty room echoing), more about atmosphere than the simple state of being empty.

**Example sentences:**
1. 冷蔵庫が空っぽだ。— The fridge is completely empty.
2. 頭が空っぽで何も考えられない。— My mind is completely blank; I can't think of anything.
3. 財布が空っぽになるまで買い物をした。— I shopped until my wallet was completely empty.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 空っぽ is frequently used figuratively for mental/emotional emptiness (頭が空っぽ, "mind is blank"; 心が空っぽ, "heart feels empty") — not just literal physical containers, which learners should keep in mind.

## 体付き (からだつき)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** figure, build, physique

**Similar words:**
- [体格](vocab-25.md#体格-たいかく)（たいかく）— "physique/build," a more formal/technical term, often in medical or athletic contexts.
- スタイル — loanword "style/figure," often used specifically about an attractive or fashionable body shape, more casual/complimentary in tone.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼はがっしりした体付きをしている。— He has a sturdy build.
2. スポーツ選手らしい体付きだ。— He has the kind of build typical of an athlete.
3. 年齢とともに体付きが変わってきた。— My physique has changed with age.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 体付き describes someone's overall physical build/shape in a fairly neutral, descriptive way — it's less clinical than 体格 and less casually complimentary than スタイル, sitting in between in register.

## 絡む (からむ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to get entangled with, to be involved/connected with, to pick a fight (when drunk)

**Similar words:**
- 関わる（かかわる）— "to be involved with," more neutral and broader, without the physical "tangled" imagery of 絡む.
- まとわりつく — "to cling to/hover around," similar physical entanglement imagery but typically describing a person or animal clinging rather than abstract complications.

**Example sentences:**
1. 糸が絡んでほどけない。— The thread is tangled and won't come undone.
2. この事件には政治的な問題が絡んでいる。— Political issues are entangled in this incident.
3. 酔っ払いが通行人に絡んできた。— A drunk person started harassing a passerby.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Beyond literal "getting tangled" (threads, vines), 絡む commonly describes abstract complications (issues intertwined with a problem) and also the specific sense of a drunk person being aggressively clingy/confrontational — a fairly distinct slang-adjacent usage worth knowing.

## 借り (かり)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a debt, a loan, a favor owed

**Similar words:**
- 借金（しゃっきん）— specifically "a monetary debt/loan," more concrete and financial than the broader 借り, which can include favors.
- [恩](vocab-11.md#恩-おん)（おん）— "a debt of gratitude/favor," more about moral obligation than literal money, overlapping with the figurative sense of 借り.

**Opposite words:**
- [貸し](vocab-13.md#貸し-かし)（かし）— a loan given, a favor done for someone

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼には借りがある。— I owe him one (a favor).
2. 借りを返すまで安心できない。— I can't relax until I repay the debt.
3. 友人からお金の借りがある。— I have a monetary debt to a friend.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 借り and 貸し are a paired opposite set (borrowing vs. lending) often used figuratively for favors, not just money — 借りがある (I owe someone) and 貸しがある (someone owes me) are common idiomatic expressions worth memorizing together.

## 仮に (かりに)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** temporarily; hypothetically, supposing that

**Similar words:**
- [もし](vocab-34.md#もし-もし) — the common conditional "if," used constantly in casual speech; 仮に is more formal/deliberate, often introducing a hypothetical scenario for discussion.
- たとえ〜ても — "even if ~," a related but distinct conditional construction emphasizing "even under that hypothetical condition."

**Example sentences:**
1. 仮にその話が本当だとしたら、大変なことになる。— Supposing that story is true, it would become a serious matter.
2. この名前は仮につけたものです。— This name was given temporarily (provisionally).
3. 仮に失敗しても、また挑戦すればいい。— Even supposing you fail, you can just try again.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 仮に often pairs with 〜としたら/〜とすれば/〜なら later in the sentence to form a hypothetical conditional — learners should recognize this pattern as more formal/written than simply starting with もし.

## 借りる (かりる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to borrow (something) from someone

**Similar words:**
- [貸す](vocab-13.md#貸す-かす)（かす）— the opposite verb "to lend," frequently confused with 借りる since both involve the same transaction from different perspectives.
- レンタルする — loanword-based "to rent," typically used for formal/commercial rentals (cars, DVDs) rather than borrowing from a person.

**Opposite words:**
- [貸す](vocab-13.md#貸す-かす)（かす）— to lend

**Example sentences:**
1. 友達に本を借りた。— I borrowed a book from a friend.
2. 図書館でCDを借りることができる。— You can borrow CDs from the library.
3. お金を借りるときは計画的に。— Be mindful when borrowing money.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 借りる (to borrow) and 貸す (to lend) are a classic confusable pair for learners — the particle から/に marks who you borrow FROM (友達から借りる), which is the opposite direction from 貸す (友達に貸す, to lend TO a friend).

## かるた
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** karuta, Japanese traditional playing cards (often poem cards)

**Similar words:**
- トランプ — loanword for "playing cards" (Western-style), distinct from the traditional Japanese かるた.
- 百人一首（ひゃくにんいっしゅ）— a specific famous karuta set based on classical poems, a well-known subtype of かるた.

**Example sentences:**
1. お正月にはかるたで遊ぶ家庭が多い。— Many households play karuta during New Year's.
2. 百人一首のかるた大会に参加した。— I participated in a Hyakunin Isshu karuta tournament.
3. 子供向けのひらがなかるたを買った。— I bought a children's hiragana karuta set.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** かるた is a loanword originally from Portuguese ("carta," card) despite being deeply associated with traditional Japanese culture (New Year's games, 百人一首) — it's written in hiragana or katakana, not kanji.

## カルテ
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a patient's medical chart/case record

**Similar words:**
- 診断書（しんだんしょ）— "a medical certificate/diagnosis document," an official written diagnosis, distinct from the ongoing working record that is カルテ.
- 病歴（びょうれき）— "medical history," the information content, whereas カルテ is the physical/digital document itself.

**Example sentences:**
1. 医師が患者のカルテに記入した。— The doctor wrote in the patient's chart.
2. カルテを見れば治療の経過が分かる。— You can see the course of treatment by looking at the chart.
3. 電子カルテの導入が進んでいる。— The introduction of electronic medical records is progressing.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** カルテ comes from German "Karte" (card), not English — it's used almost exclusively in medical contexts for a patient's chart, and learners shouldn't confuse it with English "cart" despite the similar sound.

## 彼 (かれ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** he; boyfriend

**Similar words:**
- 彼氏（かれし）— unambiguously "boyfriend," used when 彼 alone might be mistaken for the plain pronoun "he."
- [彼女](vocab-13.md#彼女-かのじょ)（かのじょ）— the female counterpart, meaning "she" or "girlfriend."

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼はとても親切な人だ。— He is a very kind person.
2. 彼女には彼がいる。— She has a boyfriend.
3. 彼と初めて会ったのは大学だった。— The first time I met him was at university.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Like 彼女, context determines whether 彼 means "he" or "boyfriend" — 彼がいる specifically implies "has a boyfriend," so learners must parse the surrounding grammar, not just the word alone.

## ガレージ
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a garage

**Similar words:**
- [車庫](vocab-20.md#車庫-しゃこ)（しゃこ）— the native Japanese word for "garage," slightly more formal/written, used interchangeably with ガレージ.
- [駐車場](vocab-27.md#駐車場-ちゅうしゃじょう)（ちゅうしゃじょう）— "parking lot," a broader term for any parking space, not necessarily an enclosed garage like ガレージ.

**Example sentences:**
1. 車をガレージに入れた。— I put the car in the garage.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ガレージセール (garage sale) is a borrowed cultural concept from the US and is understood in Japan, though the practice itself is much less common there than in English-speaking countries — learners should know it's recognized but culturally less prevalent.

## 枯れる (かれる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to wither, to die (of plants)

**Similar words:**
- 萎れる（しおれる）— "to wilt," describing a temporary droopy state that may still be reversible, unlike 枯れる, which implies the plant has actually died.
- [腐る](vocab-16.md#腐る-くさる)（くさる）— "to rot," a different decay process involving decomposition rather than simply drying up and dying.

**Example sentences:**
1. 水をやらなかったので花が枯れてしまった。— I didn't water it, so the flower withered and died.
2. 秋になると木の葉が枯れて落ちる。— In autumn, the tree leaves wither and fall.
3. 枯れた枝を剪定した。— I pruned the withered branches.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 枯れる can also be used figuratively and positively to describe a mature, seasoned, understated quality in a person's skill or voice (枯れた味わい, 枯れた演技) — a nuance quite different from the negative "dying" image, which surprises many learners.

## 過労 (かろう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** fatigue from overwork, exhaustion from excessive labor

**Similar words:**
- [疲労](vocab-32.md#疲労-ひろう)（ひろう）— general fatigue/tiredness, not necessarily caused by overwork specifically.
- 働き過ぎ（はたらきすぎ）— more casual phrase for "working too much," describing the behavior rather than the resulting condition.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 過労 refers specifically to the physical/mental toll from working too much, often appearing in compounds like 過労死 (death from overwork) — it's not a generic word for "tired," so don't substitute it for 疲れ in casual conversation.

**Similar words:**
- [質問](vocab-21.md#質問-しつもん)（しつもん）— "a question" one asks someone else directly, whereas 疑問 refers to an internal doubt or uncertainty one holds, which may or may not be voiced as a question.
- 不審（ふしん）— "suspicion," implies distrust or wariness about something's legitimacy, stronger and more negative than the neutral uncertainty of 疑問.

**Opposite words:**
- [確信](vocab-01.md#確信-かくしん)（かくしん）— conviction, certainty

**Example sentences:**
1. この計画には疑問が残る。— Doubts remain about this plan.
2. 彼の説明に疑問を感じた。— I felt doubtful about his explanation.
3. 疑問に思ったことは質問してください。— Please ask if you have any doubts/questions.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 疑問 refers to the inner feeling of uncertainty/doubt (疑問を持つ, 疑問に思う), while 質問 is the actual act of asking a question to someone — learners sometimes use them interchangeably, but they describe different things (internal doubt vs. external inquiry).

## 客 (きゃく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a guest, customer, visitor

**Similar words:**
- お客様（おきゃくさま）— the polite/honorific form used when addressing or referring to customers directly, common in service industries.
- 顧客（こきゃく）— "client, customer," a more formal/business term often used for regular or corporate clients rather than a one-time visitor.

**Opposite words:**
- 主人（しゅじん）— host, master (of the house)

**Example sentences:**
1. 今日はたくさんの客が来店した。— Many customers came to the store today.
2. 彼は大事な客を家に招いた。— He invited an important guest to his home.
3. 観光客で街が賑わっている。— The town is bustling with tourists.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 客 alone is plain/neutral, but when addressing customers directly in a service context, お客様 is required for politeness — using bare 客 to a customer's face would sound rude.

## 規約 (きやく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** an agreement, bylaws, terms of service

**Similar words:**
- [契約](vocab-02.md#契約-けいやく)（けいやく）— "a contract," typically a binding agreement between specific parties, whereas 規約 often refers to a standardized set of rules governing membership or use (利用規約, 会則).
- [規定](#規定-きてい)（きてい）— "a regulation/provision," can overlap but 規約 specifically emphasizes mutually agreed-upon rules (like terms of service) rather than unilaterally imposed regulations.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. サービスを利用する前に利用規約を確認してください。— Please check the terms of service before using the service.
2. クラブの規約に違反する行為は禁止だ。— Acts that violate the club's bylaws are prohibited.
3. 規約の改定が行われた。— The bylaws were revised.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 規約 commonly appears in 利用規約 (terms of use/service), ubiquitous on websites and apps — it implies a set of rules users/members agree to abide by, distinct from a one-on-one contractual 契約.

## 逆 (ぎゃく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** contrary, reverse, opposite

**Similar words:**
- 反対（はんたい）— "opposite," overlaps closely and is often interchangeable, though 反対 can also mean "to oppose" (反対する) as a verb, which 逆 cannot do on its own.
- [あべこべ](vocab-03.md#あべこべ) — "mixed up, backwards," a more casual, colloquial way of describing things reversed or switched around, often playful in tone.

**Opposite words:**
- 正（せい）— correct, right (as a contrasting concept)

**Example sentences:**
1. 靴を逆に履いてしまった。— I put my shoes on the wrong feet (backwards).
2. 話が逆になっているようだ。— It seems the story is backwards/reversed.
3. 逆の立場から考えてみよう。— Let's try thinking from the opposite standpoint.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 逆に (conversely, on the contrary) is a very common connective adverb in spoken and written Japanese for pivoting an argument — distinct from 反対に, which can carry a similar meaning but is slightly more formal/explicit about opposition.
