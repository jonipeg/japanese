# ～んばかり {#んばかり}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～んばかり

**Furigana form:** ～んばかり

**Meaning:** "As if about to do X" / "almost to the point of doing X" — describes an action or state so intense it looks like it's just on the verge of happening, though it doesn't quite occur.

**Conjugation:**
- V-ない形(remove ない) + んばかり(に/の) (e.g., 今にも泣かんばかりに, "as if about to cry at any moment")

**Typical context:** Formal, written/literary register with vivid descriptive force — common in novels and descriptive narration, not casual speech.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**きわまりない**](grammar-きわまる・きわまりない.md) — directly intensifies a quality as extreme in itself (失礼極まりない), whereas んばかり describes something as looking like it is ON THE VERGE of happening, a visual/metaphorical simile rather than a direct intensifier.
- [**とばかりに**](grammar-とばかりに.md) — a closely related idiom meaning "as if to say X," narrower in application (usually implying an unspoken attitude or implicit message), while んばかり applies more broadly to any action/state appearing imminent.
- **かのごとく** — a general simile marker meaning "as if/as though," broader in scope than んばかり's specific "on the verge of an action" nuance.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 台風で木の枝が今にも折れんばかりだ。
   (The tree branch looks like it's about to snap at any moment because of the typhoon.)
2. 彼女は今にも泣かんばかりの顔で訴えた。
   (She pleaded with a face that looked like she was about to cry at any moment.)
3. 観客は割れんばかりの拍手を送った。
   (The audience gave applause so thunderous it seemed like it would split the walls.)
4. 彼は飛び上がらんばかりに喜んだ。
   (He was so delighted he looked like he was about to jump up.)
5. 店主はあふれんばかりの商品を並べていた。
   (The shop owner had goods displayed so abundantly they looked about to overflow.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** んばかり is a visually evocative member of the N1 extreme-degree cluster and is sometimes confused with きわまりない because both can describe something at an extreme. The key distinction: んばかり is strictly a SIMILE — the described event does NOT actually happen, it only LOOKS like it's about to (泣かんばかり = looks like about to cry, but doesn't necessarily cry), whereas きわまりない directly asserts that a quality IS at its extreme in actual fact, with no "as if" hedge.
