# ～ごとし／ごとく／ごときN {#ごとし}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～如し／如く／如きN

**Furigana form:** ～ごとし／ごとく／ごときN

**Meaning:** "As if X / like X" — a classical literary simile marker, equivalent to ように／よう, often carrying a disparaging or humbly self-deprecating nuance depending on context.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞＋の／動詞辞書形・た形 + ごとし（終止形）
- ～ごとく（連用形、副詞的に使う）
- ～ごとき + 名詞（連体形、名詞を修飾）
- Example base: 光陰矢の如し (time flies like an arrow); 彼ごときに負けるものか (I won't lose to someone like him)

**Typical context:** Formal, written/literary and classical register; appears in proverbs, formal writing, and set phrases. The ごとき form in particular frequently carries a dismissive or disparaging connotation ("a mere X," "someone as insignificant as X"), while the plain ごとし／ごとく is more neutral, simply meaning "like/as."

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **ように／よう** — the modern, everyday equivalent for "like/as if X"; usable in any register, while ごとし／ごとく／ごとき are classical remnants confined to proverbs, formal writing, or deliberately archaic style.
- [**かのようだ**](grammar-かのようだ.md) — "as if X (were the case)"; a modern construction for hypothetical similes, overlapping with ごとし in meaning but far more common in contemporary speech.
- **まがい** — "imitation/pseudo-X" (e.g., 詐欺まがい); shares ごとき's occasional disparaging flavor when describing something as a lesser imitation, though まがい specifically implies fraudulence/imitation rather than simple comparison.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; 本物の／まさにその通りの ("the genuine article / exactly as stated") would contrast with ごとし's "merely resembling" framing.

**Example sentences:**
1. 光陰矢の如し。時間を大切に使いなさい。 (Time flies like an arrow. Use your time wisely.)
2. 彼は何事もなかったかのごとく、平然と振る舞っていた。 (He behaved calmly, as if nothing had happened.)
3. 君ごときに、この仕事が務まるはずがない。 (There's no way someone as inexperienced as you could handle this job.)
4. 彼女は女神の如き美しさを持っていた。 (She possessed a beauty like that of a goddess.)
5. 噂は燎原の火の如く広がった。 (The rumor spread like wildfire.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners should distinguish the three conjugated forms by their grammatical role: ごとし ends a sentence (predicate), ごとく modifies a verb adverbially, and ごとき modifies a following noun — mixing these up is a common error. The ごとき form in particular often signals disdain or humility ("a mere X," "someone as unworthy as X"), a nuance not inherently present in the neutral ように, so learners should watch for this added dismissive charge.
