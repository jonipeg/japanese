# ～など／なんて／なんか {#など・なんて・なんか}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～など／なんて／なんか

**Furigana form:** ～など／なんて／なんか (no special kanji readings needed)

**Meaning:** "Things like X, and so on" — used for non-exhaustive listing or vague reference. なんて and なんか additionally express disdain, surprise, or self-deprecating modesty ("X of all things," "something like X [which is unimpressive]").

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + など／なんか + (particle): 果物など好きです (I like things like fruit)
- 名詞／文 + なんて + (感情・評価): 彼が来るなんて驚いた (I'm surprised that he's coming, of all things)
- 私なんか／私なんて: modest self-reference ("someone like me")

**Typical context:** など is neutral-to-formal and usable in writing; なんて and なんか are casual/conversational and carry emotional coloring (surprise, contempt, humility).

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**とか**](grammar-とか.md) — similarly casual listing, but without the disdain/surprise nuance of なんて.
- [**や**](grammar-や.md) — neutral, formal-friendly listing equivalent to など.
- **くらい／ぐらい** — can express minimization ("at least this much"), overlapping with なんか's belittling tone in some contexts.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 休日は読書や映画鑑賞など楽しんでいます。
   (On days off, I enjoy things like reading and watching movies.)
2. 彼が試験に落ちるなんて、信じられない。
   (I can't believe that he failed the exam, of all things.)
3. 私なんかにできるわけがない。
   (There's no way someone like me could do it.)
4. ゲームなんかより、勉強した方がいいよ。
   (You'd be better off studying than playing games, honestly.)
5. 野菜や果物など、新鮮な食材を使っています。
   (We use fresh ingredients like vegetables and fruit.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** など is neutral and safe in all registers, but なんて/なんか should be avoided in formal writing or polite business speech since they carry emotional or belittling connotations. Learners often use なんか as a filler ("like, um") in speech, which is a separate colloquial usage from its belittling/listing grammatical function covered here.
