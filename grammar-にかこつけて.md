# ～にかこつけて {#にかこつけて}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～に託けて

**Furigana form:** ～にかこつけて

**Meaning:** "Using X as an excuse/pretext" — describes doing something by exploiting an unrelated or loosely related circumstance as a convenient justification.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + にかこつけて
- Example base: 大学院進学 → 大学院進学にかこつけて

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal, usable in both speech and writing; carries a critical or suspicious nuance, implying the stated reason is not the true motivation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**にかまけて**](grammar-にかまけて.md) — "being preoccupied with X (to the neglect of other things)"; related in sound/shape but distinct meaning — かまける is genuine absorption, while かこつける is a deliberate, often insincere pretext.
- **を口実に** — "using X as a pretext/excuse"; near-synonymous and commonly interchangeable, perhaps slightly more direct/common than the somewhat literary にかこつけて.
- **を理由に** — "citing X as the reason"; more neutral, not necessarily implying the reason is false or merely convenient, unlike にかこつけて's implicit suspicion.

**Opposite grammar structures:** 本当の理由で ("for the genuine/true reason") expresses the reverse — acting from a sincere, stated motive rather than a pretext.

**Example sentences:**
1. 大学院進学に託けて今の会社を辞めてしまった。 (Using graduate school as a pretext, he quit his current job.)
2. 彼は出張にかこつけて、観光を楽しんでいたらしい。 (It seems he used the business trip as a pretext to enjoy sightseeing.)
3. 体調不良にかこつけて、面倒な会議を欠席した。 (Using poor health as an excuse, she skipped the troublesome meeting.)
4. 友人の結婚祝いにかこつけて、高級レストランで豪華な食事をした。 (Using a friend's wedding celebration as a pretext, we had a lavish meal at an upscale restaurant.)
5. 引っ越しの手伝いにかこつけて、彼女に会いに行った。 (Using helping with the move as a pretext, he went to see her.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** にかこつけて always implies the stated reason is a convenient cover for the speaker's true (often self-serving or indulgent) motive — it should not be used for a sincere, straightforward reason, where を理由に or ので would be more appropriate. Don't confuse it with にかまけて (preoccupation/neglect) despite the similar にか... shape; かこつける specifically involves using a circumstance as an excuse, not simply being absorbed in it.
