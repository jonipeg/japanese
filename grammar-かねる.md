# ～かねる {#かねる}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～かねる

**Furigana form:** ～かねる

**Meaning:** "Cannot bring oneself to do X" — a polite, formal way of expressing inability or unwillingness, frequently used to politely decline, refuse, or express difficulty, especially in business and service contexts.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ます形（stem）+ かねる
- 答える → お答えしかねます
- 賛成する → 賛成しかねます

**Typical context:** Formal, polite spoken and written register — very common in customer service responses, business correspondence, and formal refusals. Not used in casual conversation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **にくい** — "difficult to do," a more general expression of difficulty, without かねる's nuance of reluctance or formal refusal.
- [**わけにはいかない**](grammar-わけにはいかない.md) — "cannot do X" due to social or moral obligation; a stronger, more explicit refusal than かねる's softer hedging.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite — the straightforward affirmative counterpart is simply できる.

**Example sentences:**
1. その件についてはお答えしかねます。
   (I'm unable to answer regarding that matter.)
2. 申し訳ございませんが、ご依頼には応じかねます。
   (I'm sorry, but we cannot comply with your request.)
3. このような条件では契約しかねます。
   (Under these conditions, we cannot sign the contract.)
4. 彼の意見には賛成しかねる。
   (I find it hard to agree with his opinion.)
5. 急な変更には対応しかねます。
   (We are unable to accommodate such a sudden change.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Don't confuse かねる (polite inability/reluctance) with かねない (risk of a bad possibility) — they look nearly identical but have opposite meanings. かねる is almost exclusively formal/polite register; using it in casual speech sounds unnaturally stiff.
