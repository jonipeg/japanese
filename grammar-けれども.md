# けれども {#けれども}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** けれども (no kanji; always written in kana)

**Furigana form:** けれども

**Meaning:** "But, however, although" — a polite-neutral contrastive conjunction, softer in tone than でも.

**Conjugation:** Sentence-initial conjunction, no conjugation needed — attaches at the sentence boundary, connecting the previous sentence to the next. Also very commonly attaches directly to the end of a clause mid-sentence (plain/polite form + けれども) to join two contrasting clauses in one sentence, which is its most frequent use.

**Typical context:** Polite-neutral register, usable in both spoken and written Japanese. It sits between でも (casual spoken) and しかし (formal written) — polished enough for polite conversation or soft-spoken writing, but not as stiff as しかし. Often shortened to けど (casual) or けども in speech; the full けれども form itself already signals a slightly more careful, polite tone than けど.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**だけど**](grammar-だけど.md) — very casual spoken "but"; more informal than けれども.
- [**でも**](grammar-でも.md) — casual spoken "but," most common everyday equivalent; less polished than けれども.
- [**しかし**](grammar-しかし.md) — standard written/formal "however"; more formal and terse than けれども.
- [**だが**](grammar-だが.md) — literary/written, terse, narrator or male-speech register; more abrupt than けれども.
- [**それなのに**](grammar-それなのに.md) — adds emotional nuance of surprise/frustration; けれども is more neutral and polite, without that emotional charge.
- [**一方**](grammar-一方.md) — contrasts two parallel facts/perspectives in a structural way; けれども directly negates/softens the preceding statement instead.

**Opposite grammar structures:** そして/また (additive conjunctions) oppose けれども structurally by continuing rather than contrasting the preceding idea.

**Example sentences:**
1. このレストランは美味しいけれども、値段が高い。
   (This restaurant is delicious, but it's expensive.)
2. 行きたかったけれども、時間がなかった。
   (I wanted to go, but I didn't have time.)
3. 彼は若い。けれども、とても経験豊富だ。
   (He is young. However, he is very experienced.)
4. 何度も電話したけれども、誰も出なかった。
   (I called many times, but no one answered.)
5. 分かってはいるけれども、なかなか実行できない。
   (I understand it, but it's hard to actually put into practice.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often treat けれども as interchangeable with でも, but けれども is more commonly used mid-sentence attached to a clause (〜けれども、〜), while でも typically starts a new sentence. Also, けれども is politer and more measured than the very casual だけど — using だけど in a formal letter or presentation would sound too blunt, whereas けれども remains appropriate. Don't confuse its soft, neutral contrast with それなのに's emotional "and yet" nuance.
