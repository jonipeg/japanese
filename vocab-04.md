## 孤児 (こじ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Orphan

**Similar words:**
- 遺児 (いじ) — specifically a child bereaved by a parent's death (e.g. in an accident/disaster); 孤児 is the general term and can imply abandonment too.
- みなしご — a softer, more colloquial/literary word for orphan, often used in children's stories; 孤児 is the formal/administrative term.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite (contrast concept: 両親がいる子 — a child with both parents).

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は幼い頃に両親を亡くし、孤児として育った。
   (He lost his parents when he was young and was raised as an orphan.)
2. 戦争は多くの孤児を生み出した。
   (The war produced many orphans.)
3. その孤児院では、子供たちが温かく見守られている。
   (At that orphanage, the children are watched over warmly.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes use 孤児 loosely for any lonely or abandoned feeling; it strictly refers to children who have lost their parents, not adults or figurative "orphaned" objects/projects (for the latter, Japanese uses different metaphors).

## コツ (こつ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Knack

**Similar words:**
- 秘訣 (ひけつ) — a "secret" to success, more formal and often used for life/career advice; コツ is casual, hands-on know-how.
- [要領](vocab-35.md#要領-ようりょう) (ようりょう) — the "gist" or efficient way of doing something; overlaps with コツ but can also mean general procedural understanding, not just a skill trick.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 自転車に乗るコツをつかんだら、すぐに上手になった。
   (Once I got the knack of riding a bike, I quickly got good at it.)
2. 料理のコツは、火加減をうまく調節することだ。
   (The knack of cooking is adjusting the heat well.)
3. 彼にコツを教えてもらったら、簡単にできるようになった。
   (After he taught me the trick, I was able to do it easily.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** コツ is almost always written in katakana even though it has an obscure kanji (骨); learners should not confuse it with 骨 (ほね, bone) despite sharing the character historically. Also コツをつかむ ("grasp the knack") is the set phrase — avoid直訳 like 取る.

## 詐欺 (さぎ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Fraud

**Similar words:**
- 偽り (いつわり) — a lie or falsehood in general; broader and less legal than 詐欺, which specifically implies deceiving someone for monetary/material gain.
- 騙し (だまし) — the act of tricking/deceiving someone, more colloquial; 詐欺 is the formal, often criminal/legal term.

**Opposite words:** 正直 (しょうじき, honesty) — not a true antonym but the conceptual opposite of deceptive behavior.

**Example sentences:**
1. その男は結婚詐欺で逮捕された。
   (The man was arrested for marriage fraud.)
2. インターネット詐欺の被害が増えている。
   (Damage from internet fraud is increasing.)
3. 彼女は詐欺に遭って、全財産を失った。
   (She fell victim to fraud and lost all her assets.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 詐欺 implies a crime with intent to deceive for gain (often prosecutable); learners should not use it for an innocent mistake or simple exaggeration — use 嘘 (うそ) for a plain lie instead.

## 強いる (しいる)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To force

**Similar words:**
- 強制する (きょうせいする) — the noun-based verb form for "to force/compel," more bureaucratic/legal in tone; 強いる is more literary and often used with abstract hardships (苦労を強いる).
- 押し付ける (おしつける) — to push/foist something onto someone, implying unwanted imposition rather than outright compulsion.

**Opposite words:** No direct single-word opposite (contrast: 自由にさせる — to let someone act freely).

**Example sentences:**
1. 会社は従業員に長時間労働を強いた。
   (The company forced long working hours on its employees.)
2. 戦争は多くの人々に犠牲を強いた。
   (The war forced sacrifice upon many people.)
3. 彼女に無理を強いるようなことはしたくない。
   (I don't want to force her into doing something unreasonable.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 強いる takes the pattern 〜に〜を強いる (force [hardship] on [person]) and is almost always used in formal/written contexts about difficult or burdensome situations, not casual requests — using it for trivial requests ("I forced him to come to the party") sounds overly dramatic.

## 在庫 (ざいこ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Inventory

**Similar words:**
- 棚卸し (たなおろし) — stocktaking, the process of counting inventory; 在庫 is the stock itself, not the counting process.
- ストック — a loanword used casually for "stock" of goods, often smaller scale than 在庫 which is the standard business term.

**Opposite words:** 品切れ (しなぎれ, out of stock) is the practical opposite state.

**Example sentences:**
1. この商品は在庫がありますか。
   (Is this item in stock?)
2. 在庫が切れたので、追加注文をした。
   (We ran out of stock, so we placed an additional order.)
3. 倉庫には大量の在庫が保管されている。
   (A large amount of inventory is stored in the warehouse.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 在庫 refers to the goods/stock itself (countable concept, "do you have stock"), not the warehouse or the shelf; learners sometimes misuse it to mean "storage space."

## 花壇 (かだん)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Flower bed

**Similar words:**
- 花畑 (はなばたけ) — a flower field, larger and more natural/open than a 花壇, which is a cultivated, often bordered garden bed.
- 庭園 (ていえん) — a garden in general (can include flower beds, trees, paths); 花壇 is just the flower-planted section.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 公園の花壇にはたくさんのチューリップが植えられている。
   (Many tulips are planted in the park's flower bed.)
2. 彼女は庭に小さな花壇を作った。
   (She made a small flower bed in her garden.)
3. 学校の花壇の手入れは生徒たちが担当している。
   (The students are in charge of tending the school's flower bed.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 花壇 specifically implies a defined, often raised or bordered planting area (commonly in parks, schools, or front yards) — not a wild field of flowers, which would be 花畑.

## 知覚 (ちかく)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Perception

**Similar words:**
- [感覚](vocab-13.md#感覚-かんかく) (かんかく) — sense/sensation, the more everyday word for feeling things through the senses; 知覚 is the more academic/technical term used in psychology and philosophy.
- [認識](#認識-にんしき) (にんしき) — cognitive recognition/awareness, a higher-level mental processing than raw sensory 知覚.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite (near-opposite concept: 無感覚 — numbness/insensitivity).

**Example sentences:**
1. 人間の知覚は五感を通じて外界の情報を受け取る。
   (Human perception receives information about the outside world through the five senses.)
2. 彼は痛みに対する知覚が鈍くなっていた。
   (His perception of pain had become dulled.)
3. 知覚心理学は、人がどのように世界を認識するかを研究する。
   (Perceptual psychology studies how people perceive the world.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 知覚 is a technical/academic register word (psychology, philosophy, biology); in everyday conversation about "noticing" or "feeling" something, 感じる or 気づく is far more natural than 知覚する.

## 五感 (ごかん)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Five senses

**Similar words:**
- [感覚](vocab-13.md#感覚-かんかく) (かんかく) — sense/sensation in general, the broader umbrella term; 五感 specifically enumerates the five classical senses (sight, hearing, smell, taste, touch).
- [直感](vocab-28.md#直感-ちょっかん) (ちょっかん) — intuition, a "sixth sense" type of immediate understanding, distinct from the physical 五感.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. この料理は五感すべてで楽しめるように作られている。
   (This dish is made to be enjoyed with all five senses.)
2. 自然の中で過ごすと、五感が研ぎ澄まされる気がする。
   (Spending time in nature seems to sharpen the five senses.)
3. 赤ちゃんは五感を通して世界を学んでいく。
   (Babies learn about the world through their five senses.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 五感 is a fixed set phrase referring exactly to the five classical senses; it's often paired with 研ぎ澄ます (to sharpen) or 刺激する (to stimulate) in marketing/descriptive language about food, art, or nature experiences.

## 入力 (にゅうりょく)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Input

**Similar words:**
- [記入](vocab-14.md#記入-きにゅう) (きにゅう) — to fill in/write in (e.g., a form by hand), more about writing than data entry; 入力 is typically digital/keyboard-based entry.
- インプット — a loanword cognate used more casually or in business jargon (e.g. "gathering input/information"), whereas 入力 is the standard technical term for data entry.

**Opposite words:** 出力 (しゅつりょく, output) is the direct antonym.

**Example sentences:**
1. パスワードを入力してください。
   (Please enter your password.)
2. データの入力ミスで、計算結果が間違っていた。
   (Due to a data input error, the calculation result was wrong.)
3. このフォームに必要事項を入力してから送信してください。
   (Please enter the required information into this form before submitting.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 入力 almost always pairs with 出力 as the standard IT/engineering pair (input/output); learners should not substitute 記入 when talking about typing into a computer system — 記入 is for handwritten forms.

## 構築 (こうちく)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Construction

**Similar words:**
- [建設](vocab-18.md#建設する-けんせつ) (けんせつ) — physical construction of buildings/infrastructure; 構築 is used more abstractly for building systems, relationships, or frameworks (e.g., システムを構築する).
- [築く](vocab-01.md#築く-きずく) (きずく) — a more literary verb meaning "to build up" (e.g., a fortune, a relationship), overlapping with the abstract sense of 構築.

**Opposite words:** 破壊 (はかい, destruction) is the conceptual opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 新しいシステムを構築するのに半年かかった。
   (It took half a year to build the new system.)
2. 二国間の信頼関係を構築することが重要だ。
   (Building a relationship of trust between the two countries is important.)
3. 彼はゼロから事業を構築した。
   (He built the business up from scratch.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 構築 is used for abstract or systemic "building" (networks, theories, relationships, organizations) — not for physical buildings, where 建設 or 建築 is correct. Mixing these up is a very common learner error.

## 抑圧 (よくあつ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Repression

**Similar words:**
- [抑制](vocab-36.md#抑制-よくせい) (よくせい) — suppression/control of something (e.g., emotions, growth), often more neutral/self-directed; 抑圧 implies a stronger, often external and oppressive force.
- 弾圧 (だんあつ) — crackdown/oppression, specifically used for political or state suppression of people/movements; 抑圧 can be psychological or social as well as political.

**Opposite words:** 解放 (かいほう, liberation) is the direct antonym.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女は長年、感情を抑圧して生きてきた。
   (She has lived for years repressing her emotions.)
2. 独裁政権による抑圧に、人々は耐えられなくなった。
   (People could no longer bear the repression by the dictatorial regime.)
3. 抑圧された欲求は、別の形で表れることがある。
   (Repressed desires can manifest in different forms.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 抑圧 often appears in psychology (Freudian "repression") as well as in political contexts; learners should distinguish it from 抑制, which is milder and can be positive (self-control), while 抑圧 usually carries a negative, oppressive connotation.

## 攻撃性 (こうげきせい)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Aggressiveness

**Similar words:**
- 攻撃的 (こうげきてき) — the adjectival form "aggressive" describing behavior/attitude directly; 攻撃性 is the abstract noun "aggressiveness" as a trait or tendency.
- 積極性 (せっきょくせい) — proactiveness/positive assertiveness, a much more neutral-to-positive trait than the hostile connotation of 攻撃性.

**Opposite words:** 温厚さ (おんこうさ, gentleness/mildness) is a natural opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. そのスポーツ選手は攻撃性の高いプレースタイルで知られている。
   (That athlete is known for a highly aggressive playing style.)
2. ストレスが攻撃性を高めることがある。
   (Stress can increase aggressiveness.)
3. 子供の攻撃性には、環境要因が大きく関わっている。
   (Environmental factors are heavily involved in children's aggressiveness.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes confuse 攻撃性 (aggressiveness, a trait) with 積極性 (proactiveness, a positive trait); 攻撃性 generally carries a negative/hostile nuance, so praising someone's "積極性" is a compliment, but praising their "攻撃性" would sound odd or even critical.

## 手薄 (てうす)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Shorthanded

**Similar words:**
- 人手不足 (ひとでぶそく) — labor shortage, a more general/formal term for lacking workers; 手薄 can also describe thin defenses, low stock, or weak coverage more broadly, not just staffing.
- 不十分 (ふじゅうぶん) — insufficient, a general word for inadequacy; 手薄 specifically implies a "thinning out" of resources/people in a particular area.

**Opposite words:** 手厚い (てあつい, thorough, generous coverage) is the direct antonym.

**Example sentences:**
1. 夜間は警備が手薄になるので注意が必要だ。
   (Security becomes thin at night, so caution is needed.)
2. このチームは守備が手薄だ。
   (This team's defense is thin/weak.)
3. 人手が手薄な部署に応援を送った。
   (We sent support to the understaffed department.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 手薄 isn't limited to staffing — it's often used for "thin" defense in sports, weak security coverage, or low inventory. Its antonym 手厚い (generous, thorough care/treatment) is a useful pair to learn together.

## 局面 (きょくめん)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Phase, situation

**Similar words:**
- 状況 (じょうきょう) — situation/circumstances in general, neutral and broad; 局面 implies a critical turning point or phase within a larger unfolding process (often used in shogi/chess or politics).
- 場面 (ばめん) — a scene (literal or figurative, like in a movie or story); 局面 is more abstract, referring to a stage/juncture in a situation rather than a visual scene.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 交渉は重要な局面を迎えている。
   (The negotiations are reaching a critical phase.)
2. 将棋の終盤の局面で形勢が逆転した。
   (The situation turned around in the endgame phase of the shogi match.)
3. 新しい局面を打開するためのアイデアが必要だ。
   (New ideas are needed to break through this new phase/situation.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 局面 carries a sense of a critical or decisive juncture (often used with 重要な, 新たな, 打開する); it's more dramatic than plain 状況, so using it for a mundane, everyday situation can sound overly grand.

## 領域 (りょういき)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Domain

**Similar words:**
- 分野 (ぶんや) — field/area of study or work, used more for academic/professional specialization; 領域 is broader and can mean physical territory, jurisdiction, or abstract domains.
- 範囲 (はんい) — scope/range/extent, focused on boundaries or limits rather than a defined area of expertise or territory.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. この問題は専門外の領域だ。
   (This issue is outside my area of expertise.)
2. その国は領域をめぐって隣国と対立している。
   (That country is in conflict with its neighbor over territory.)
3. AI技術は医療の領域にも応用されている。
   (AI technology is also being applied in the medical domain.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 領域 can mean physical territory (国の領域) or abstract domain/field (専門領域); learners should pick 分野 when specifically discussing academic/professional specialties in a conversational context, as 領域 can sound more formal or geographic.

## 磨く (みがく)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To hone

**Similar words:**
- [鍛える](vocab-14.md#鍛える-きたえる) (きたえる) — to train/forge (body, spirit, skills) through rigorous practice; 磨く implies refining/polishing something that already has some quality, while 鍛える implies building up strength from scratch.
- 洗練する (せんれんする) — to refine/sophisticate (style, taste, manners); more about elegance and polish than active skill practice.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite (contrast: 荒らす — to roughen/damage, 鈍らせる — to dull).

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女は日々の練習で演技力を磨いている。
   (She hones her acting skills through daily practice.)
2. 靴を磨いてから出かけた。
   (I polished my shoes before going out.)
3. もっと技術を磨かなければ、プロにはなれない。
   (You can't become a pro unless you hone your skills more.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 磨く literally means "to polish" (shoes, teeth, gems) but is frequently used figuratively for honing skills/abilities (腕を磨く, 技術を磨く) — learners sometimes default to 練習する for skill improvement and miss this more vivid, idiomatic alternative.

## 被害妄想 (ひがいもうそう)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Persecution complex

**Similar words:**
- 妄想 (もうそう) — delusion in general (not necessarily about being victimized); 被害妄想 is the specific delusion of being persecuted/targeted by others.
- 疑心暗鬼 (ぎしんあんき) — a four-character idiom meaning paranoid suspicion of everything, a milder/more everyday version of distrust compared to the clinical-sounding 被害妄想.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は被害妄想がひどく、みんなが自分を陥れようとしていると思い込んでいる。
   (He has a severe persecution complex and is convinced everyone is trying to bring him down.)
2. ストレスが重なると、被害妄想に陥ることがある。
   (When stress piles up, one can fall into a persecution complex.)
3. それは被害妄想ではなく、実際に起きていることだ。
   (That's not a persecution complex — it's actually happening.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 被害妄想 is often used both clinically and colloquially/half-jokingly ("you're being paranoid"); learners should note it specifically means believing oneself to be a victim/target, not generic anxiety or worry.

## 普遍性 (ふへんせい)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Universality

**Similar words:**
- 一般性 (いっぱんせい) — generality, applying broadly but with a more mundane, statistical nuance; 普遍性 implies a deeper, timeless, philosophical universality (true across all cases/cultures/times).
- 共通性 (きょうつうせい) — commonality, shared traits between specific things; narrower in scope than the all-encompassing 普遍性.

**Opposite words:** 特殊性 (とくしゅせい, particularity/specialness) is the direct antonym.

**Example sentences:**
1. この物語には人間の普遍性を描いたテーマがある。
   (This story has a theme depicting the universality of human nature.)
2. 彼の理論は普遍性に欠けるという批判を受けた。
   (His theory was criticized for lacking universality.)
3. 愛というテーマには時代を超えた普遍性がある。
   (The theme of love has a universality that transcends eras.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 普遍性 is a philosophical/academic register word implying something is true always and everywhere; don't confuse it with 一般性, which merely means "commonly applicable" without the deeper timeless connotation.

## 描写 (びょうしゃ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Depiction

**Similar words:**
- [表現](vocab-32.md#表現-ひょうげん) (ひょうげん) — expression in general (any form: art, words, gestures); 描写 specifically refers to depicting/describing scenes, characters, or emotions in detail, usually in writing or art.
- 叙述 (じょじゅつ) — narration/description in formal writing, more about recounting events in sequence than vividly depicting a scene or image.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. この小説は心理描写が非常に巧みだ。
   (This novel's psychological depiction is extremely skillful.)
2. 作者は自然の風景を美しく描写している。
   (The author depicts the natural scenery beautifully.)
3. その映画は戦争の悲惨さをリアルに描写していた。
   (The movie realistically depicted the tragedy of war.)
(see also 羅列 below — naming things in a list is not the same as vividly describing them)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 描写 implies vivid, detailed portrayal through words or art (風景描写, 心理描写); it's often contrasted with 羅列 (a mere listing of facts/items without depth) — good writing uses 描写, not 羅列.

## 羅列 (られつ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Enumeration

**Similar words:**
- 列挙 (れっきょ) — to enumerate/list out explicitly and often with purpose (e.g., listing reasons); 羅列 often carries a slightly negative nuance of listing things haphazardly or without structure.
- [描写](#描写-びょうしゃ) (びょうしゃ) — vivid depiction; often used as a contrast — writing that is just 羅列 (a flat list of facts) lacks the depth of 描写.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite (contrast: 描写 — vivid depiction, as opposed to flat listing).

**Example sentences:**
1. この作文は事実の羅列にすぎず、深みがない。
   (This essay is nothing but a list of facts and lacks depth.)
2. 彼のスピーチは専門用語の羅列で、聞いていて退屈だった。
   (His speech was a string of jargon and was boring to listen to.)
3. 単語を羅列するだけでは、文章とは言えない。
   (Just stringing words together doesn't make it a proper sentence.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 羅列 usually carries a critical, negative nuance (a disorganized or shallow list), unlike the more neutral 列挙; learners should avoid using 羅列 to praise a well-organized list — use 列挙 or リスト for neutral listing.

## 暗唱 (あんしょう)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Memorization/recitation

**Similar words:**
- [暗記](vocab-07.md#暗記-あんき) (あんき) — to memorize (information in general, e.g. for a test); 暗唱 specifically means to recite something memorized aloud, usually a passage, poem, or speech.
- [朗読](vocab-36.md#朗読-ろうどく) (ろうどく) — to read aloud (from a text in hand), whereas 暗唱 is reciting from memory without looking at the text.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 子供たちは詩を暗唱する練習をしている。
   (The children are practicing reciting a poem from memory.)
2. 彼はスピーチ全文を暗唱できるほど練習した。
   (He practiced so much he could recite the entire speech from memory.)
3. 学校で古典の一節を暗唱させられた。
   (We were made to recite a passage of classical literature at school.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 暗唱 requires reciting without looking at the text (from memory); learners often confuse it with 朗読 (reading aloud from the page) — the key distinguishing feature is memory vs. reading.

## 猛獣 (もうじゅう)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Wild beast

**Similar words:**
- 野獣 (やじゅう) — wild/savage beast, similar but with more emphasis on wildness/untamed nature in general; 猛獣 specifically emphasizes ferocity and danger (lions, tigers).
- [獣](vocab-17.md#獣-けだもの) (けもの) — a general, more neutral word for "beast/animal," without the ferocious connotation of 猛獣.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite (contrast: 家畜 — domesticated livestock, ペット — pet).

**Example sentences:**
1. 動物園には猛獣コーナーがあり、子供たちに人気だ。
   (The zoo has a wild beast corner that's popular with children.)
2. 猛獣に襲われないよう、十分に注意してください。
   (Please be careful not to be attacked by wild beasts.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 職務 is formal/bureaucratic language for the duties tied to a specific job/position (often in legal, governmental, or corporate contexts); using it for casual daily work talk ("my job duties today") would sound stiff — 仕事 is more natural there.

**Meaning:** Baby face

**Similar words:**
- 童顔童顔 (compare) 幼い顔 (おさないかお) — a childlike face, a descriptive phrase rather than the fixed noun term 童顔; 童顔 specifically refers to an adult whose face looks youthful/childlike despite their actual age.
- 若く見える (わかくみえる) — to look young, a broader phrase describing overall youthful appearance, not specifically the "baby-faced" quality that 童顔 captures.

**Opposite words:** 面長 (おもなが, oblong face) is not a true antonym but a contrasting face-shape descriptor; 老け顔 (ふけがお, an older-looking face) is a closer conceptual opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は童顔なので、実年齢より若く見られる。
   (Because he has a baby face, he's seen as younger than his actual age.)
2. 童顔の女優が、学生役を演じることが多い。
   (Actresses with baby faces often play student roles.)
3. 童顔だとよく言われるが、もう40歳だ。
   (I'm often told I have a baby face, but I'm already 40.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 童顔 specifically describes an adult's youthful-looking face (a compliment or neutral observation about appearance vs. actual age) — it's not used to describe an actual child's face, since children's faces are simply "child-like" by default, not notably so.

## 面長 (おもなが)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Oblong face

**Similar words:**
- 細面 (ほそおもて) — a slender/narrow face, similar but emphasizing thinness/narrowness rather than overall length as 面長 does.
- 丸顔 (まるがお) — round face, the contrasting face-shape category often discussed alongside 面長 in beauty/fashion contexts.

**Opposite words:** 丸顔 (まるがお, round face) is the natural contrasting term.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女は面長なので、どんな髪型も似合う。
   (She has an oblong face, so any hairstyle suits her.)
2. 面長の人には横に広がる髪型がおすすめだ。
   (For people with oblong faces, hairstyles with width are recommended.)
3. 面長と丸顔では、似合うメガネのフレームが違う。
   (The glasses frames that suit oblong faces differ from those for round faces.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 面長 is a neutral, descriptive term used in beauty/fashion contexts (hairstyles, makeup, glasses recommendations) about face shape — it's not inherently negative, just descriptive, unlike some English connotations of "long face" (meaning sad expression), which doesn't apply here.

## 貧弱 (ひんじゃく)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Frail, feeble

**Similar words:**
- 虚弱 (きょじゃく) — weak/delicate constitution, specifically about poor physical health/susceptibility to illness; 貧弱 is broader, describing something (body, content, equipment) as meager, scanty, or underdeveloped, not just health-related.
- ひ弱 (ひよわ) — frail/delicate (often describing children or a person's build), a softer, more casual synonym for physical frailty than 貧弱, which can also carry negative judgmental connotations.

**Opposite words:** 逞しい (たくましい, strong/robust) is a natural opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は体格が貧弱で、よくいじめられた。
   (He had a frail build and was often bullied.)
2. その説明は根拠が貧弱で説得力がない。
   (That explanation has weak grounds and isn't persuasive.)
3. 設備が貧弱なため、十分な訓練ができない。
   (Because the equipment is meager/inadequate, sufficient training can't be done.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 貧弱 can describe physical frailty (貧弱な体格) but is equally common describing inadequate/meager non-physical things (貧弱な根拠 — weak grounds/evidence, 貧弱な設備 — poor equipment) — it often carries a critical, negative judgmental tone, unlike the more neutral/sympathetic ひ弱.

## ぼっちゃり (ぼっちゃり)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Plump

**Similar words:**
- ぽっちゃり — a near-identical, very common variant spelling/pronunciation with the same meaning (slightly plump, in a cute/endearing way); the two are used almost interchangeably, with ぽっちゃり being somewhat more common in casual speech.
- 太っている (ふとっている) — to be fat, a more blunt, direct, and potentially less polite descriptor than the softer, more affectionate ぼっちゃり/ぽっちゃり.

**Opposite words:** すらっと (slim/slender) is the natural opposite in this vocabulary set.

**Example sentences:**
1. あの赤ちゃんはぽっちゃりしていて可愛い。
   (That baby is plump and cute.)
2. 彼女は昔よりぽっちゃりした気がする。
   (I feel like she's gotten a bit plumper than before.)
3. ぽっちゃり体型を気にする女性は多い。
   (Many women are self-conscious about having a plump figure.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ぼっちゃり/ぽっちゃり is a soft, affectionate, non-offensive way to describe plumpness (often used for babies, or self-deprecatingly) — much gentler than 太っている (fat), which can sound blunt or even rude depending on context; learners should use the softer term when being polite/kind about body size.

## すらっと (すらっと)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Slim, slender

**Similar words:**
- スラリと — a near-identical katakana variant with the same meaning (slender, willowy), used interchangeably with すらっと.
- 痩せている (やせている) — to be thin/skinny, a more neutral/clinical descriptor of body weight; すらっと specifically implies an attractive, graceful slenderness (often with height), not just thinness.

**Opposite words:** ぼっちゃり/ぽっちゃり (plump) is the natural opposite in this vocabulary set.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女はすらっとした美しい脚をしている。
   (She has beautifully slender legs.)
2. モデルのようにすらっとした体型に憧れる。
   (I admire a slender figure like a model's.)
3. 彼はすらっと背が高く、スーツがよく似合う。
   (He's tall and slender, and suits look great on him.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** すらっと implies an attractive, graceful slenderness (often combined with tallness — すらっと背が高い) rather than plain thinness; it carries a positive, admiring connotation, unlike the more neutral/clinical 痩せている, which can even sound slightly negative (underweight) in some contexts.

## 水玉 (みずたま)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Polka dot

**Similar words:**
- ドット — a loanword for "dot," sometimes used interchangeably in fashion contexts (ドット柄), though 水玉 (literally "water drop/ball") is the more traditional native Japanese term for the polka-dot pattern.
- [花柄](#花柄-はながら) (はながら) — floral pattern, a different common clothing pattern category often mentioned alongside 水玉 when describing design styles.

**Opposite words:** 無地 (むじ, plain/solid color) is a natural opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女は水玉模様のワンピースを着ている。
   (She's wearing a polka-dot dress.)
2. 水玉柄のネクタイは、ビジネスシーンでも使いやすい。
   (Polka-dot ties are easy to use even in business settings.)
3. 子供用の傘には水玉模様が人気だ。
   (Polka-dot patterns are popular for children's umbrellas.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 水玉 literally evokes "water drops/balls" as the visual metaphor for polka dots — it's always used with 模様 or 柄 (pattern) attached (水玉模様, 水玉柄) when describing fabric/clothing designs, rather than standing alone as an adjective.

## 花柄 (はながら)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Floral pattern

**Similar words:**
- 花模様 (はなもよう) — floral pattern/design, a near-synonym used interchangeably with 花柄, though 花柄 is slightly more common in casual fashion contexts.
- [水玉](#水玉-みずたま) (みずたま) — polka dot, a different common pattern type often contrasted with 花柄 in clothing descriptions.

**Opposite words:** 無地 (むじ, plain/solid color) is a natural opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 春らしい花柄のワンピースを買った。
   (I bought a spring-like floral dress.)
2. このカーテンは花柄で、部屋が明るく見える。
   (These curtains have a floral pattern, making the room look bright.)
3. 花柄のハンカチをプレゼントにもらった。
   (I received a floral-pattern handkerchief as a gift.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 柄 (がら) is the general suffix for "pattern/design" (水玉柄, 花柄, チェック柄 — plaid), so 花柄 literally means "flower pattern" — learners should recognize 柄 as a productive word-building element for describing fabric/clothing designs.

## 地味 (じみ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Plain, subdued

**Similar words:**
- [質素](#質素-しっそ) (しっそ) — frugal/modest, describing a simple lifestyle or spending habits rather than visual appearance/style, which is 地味's main domain.
- シンプル — a loanword for "simple," often more positively connotated (minimalist, clean) than 地味, which can carry a mildly negative nuance of being dull/unremarkable.

**Opposite words:** 派手 (はで, showy/flashy) is the direct antonym.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女はいつも地味な服装をしている。
   (She always wears plain/subdued clothing.)
2. 地味な仕事だが、やりがいを感じている。
   (It's an unglamorous job, but I find it rewarding.)
3. もう少し地味な色のバッグを探している。
   (I'm looking for a bag in a more subdued color.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 地味 describes understated, unremarkable appearance or style (clothes, color, even personality/work) — it's the direct opposite of 派手 and the pair (地味⇔派手) is one of the most fundamental descriptive adjective pairs in Japanese, frequently tested and used.

## 派手 (はで)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Showy, flashy

**Similar words:**
- [華やか](vocab-01.md#華やか-はなやか) (はなやか) — gorgeous/glamorous, a more positive, elegant form of flashiness/brightness; 派手 can be neutral-to-negative (over-the-top, garish) depending on context, while 華やか is almost always complimentary.
- [目立つ](vocab-19.md#目立つ-めだつ) (めだつ) — to stand out/be conspicuous, a broader term for drawing attention, which may or may not be due to flashy appearance specifically (as 派手 implies).

**Opposite words:** 地味 (じみ, plain/subdued) is the direct antonym.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女は派手な格好で登場した。
   (She appeared in flashy/showy attire.)
2. そのパーティーはとても派手だった。
   (That party was very flashy/extravagant.)
3. 彼は派手な性格で、いつも注目を集める。
   (He has a flashy personality and always draws attention.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 派手 and 地味 form the essential contrasting pair for describing visual style/flashiness (clothes, personality, events) — 派手 can lean negative (garish, over-the-top) or simply descriptive depending on tone, while 華やか is reserved for positive, elegant glamour.

## しゃがむ (しゃがむ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To crouch

**Similar words:**
- かがむ — to bend down/stoop, a broader term for bending the body forward or down, which may or may not involve fully crouching with bent knees like しゃがむ.
- 膝を曲げる (ひざをまげる) — to bend one's knees, a descriptive phrase for part of the physical action involved in しゃがむ, but not a single dedicated verb for the full crouching posture.

**Opposite words:** 立つ (たつ, to stand) is a natural opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は地面に落ちた物を拾うためにしゃがんだ。
   (He crouched down to pick up something that had fallen on the ground.)
2. 子供はしゃがんで虫を観察していた。
   (The child crouched down observing an insect.)
3. 長時間しゃがんでいたので、足がしびれた。
   (My legs went numb from crouching for a long time.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** しゃがむ specifically means to crouch down low with bent knees (often to look at something on the ground or to hide) — distinct from かがむ, which more broadly means to bend/stoop and can include just bending at the waist without fully lowering the body.

## うつむく (うつむく)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To look down

**Similar words:**
- 俯く (うつむく) — same word, just showing the kanji form; written with kanji in more formal/literary contexts, while hiragana is common in casual writing.
- 下を向く (したをむく) — to face downward, a more literal, descriptive phrase for the same physical action, whereas うつむく is a single dedicated verb often implying an emotional state (shame, sadness, shyness) behind the downward gaze.

**Opposite words:** 見上げる (みあげる, to look up) is a natural opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女は恥ずかしそうにうつむいた。
   (She looked down, seeming embarrassed.)
2. 叱られて、彼はうつむいたまま何も言わなかった。
   (Having been scolded, he said nothing, staying looking down.)
3. うつむいて歩いていたら、人にぶつかってしまった。
   (I was walking with my head down and bumped into someone.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** うつむく almost always implies an emotional undertone (shame, sadness, dejection, shyness) behind the physical act of looking down — it's rarely used for a neutral, purposeful downward glance (like checking your phone), where 下を見る would be more fitting.

## 寝転ぶ (ねころぶ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To lie down

**Similar words:**
- 横になる (よこになる) — to lie down/recline, a more neutral, polite way to describe lying down (often for resting or when unwell); 寝転ぶ specifically implies casually flopping/sprawling down, often on the floor or grass, in a relaxed or careless manner.
- 寝る (ねる) — to sleep/go to bed, implying the act of sleeping, whereas 寝転ぶ just means assuming a lying-down position, not necessarily to sleep.

**Opposite words:** 起き上がる (おきあがる, to get up) is a natural opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は芝生の上に寝転んで、空を眺めていた。
   (He lay down on the grass, gazing at the sky.)
2. 疲れたので、ソファに寝転んで休んだ。
   (I was tired, so I lay down on the sofa and rested.)
3. 子供たちは床に寝転んでテレビを見ていた。
   (The children were lying on the floor watching TV.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 寝転ぶ implies a casual, relaxed sprawling lie-down (on grass, floor, sofa), often for leisure rather than formal rest or sleep; it's more informal/physical than 横になる, which is the polite, neutral phrase often used when suggesting someone rest (e.g., when sick).

## あおむけ (あおむけ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Face up

**Similar words:**
- うつぶせ — face down/prone, the direct opposite posture, often paired with あおむけ when describing sleeping or lying positions.
- 仰向け (あおむけ) — same word written in kanji; 仰 means "to look up/face upward," clarifying the body orientation (lying on one's back, facing the ceiling/sky).

**Opposite words:** うつぶせ (face down) is the direct antonym.

**Example sentences:**
1. 赤ちゃんをあおむけに寝かせてください。
   (Please lay the baby down face up.)
2. 彼はあおむけになって、天井を見つめていた。
   (He lay on his back, staring at the ceiling.)
3. 水の上であおむけに浮かぶと、体が楽になる。
   (Floating on your back on the water relaxes the body.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** あおむけ (face up) and うつぶせ (face down) are a crucial paired vocabulary set for describing sleeping/lying positions, especially relevant in medical and childcare contexts (e.g., infant sleep safety guidance); learners should memorize both together since they're frequently contrasted.
