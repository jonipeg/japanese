# ～といわず…といわず {#といわず…といわず}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～といわず…といわず

**Furigana form:** ～といわず…といわず

**Meaning:** "Without distinguishing between X and Y, the situation is the same everywhere / all the time" — emphasizes that something applies indiscriminately across the board, often describing a messy state or a constant occurrence.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + といわず
- 顔といわず手といわず、泥だらけだ — "his face, his hands, everything was covered in mud"

**Typical context:** Written and spoken descriptive register, often with a negative or exasperated nuance — describing something covering an entire area or happening constantly without exception.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～も…も** — plain "both X and Y," lacking the "indiscriminately everywhere" totality nuance.
- [**～にかかわらず**](grammar-にかかわらず・にもかかわらず.md) — "regardless of X," a broader concessive use not limited to paired spatial/temporal examples.
- [**～といい…といい**](grammar-といい…といい.md) — an evaluative judgment based on two aspects (see that entry), whereas といわず is about uniform coverage or occurrence, often with messier or more negative connotations.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a phrase emphasizing a clear distinction, like "Xだけで、Yは違う" ("only X, not Y"), contrasts with this pattern's claim of no distinction.

**Example sentences:**
1. 砂浜で遊んでいた子供たちは、手といわず足といわず全身砂だらけだ。
   (The children who'd been playing on the beach were covered in sand from head to toe — hands, feet, everywhere.)
2. 室内で犬を飼っているので、廊下といわず部屋の中といわず家中犬の毛が落ちている。
   (Since we keep a dog indoors, dog hair is scattered all over the house — the hallway, the rooms, everywhere.)
3. 営業マンの島田さんは平日といわず週末といわず休む暇なく社外に出て働いている。
   (Shimada, the salesman, is out working constantly, with no time to rest — weekdays, weekends, it makes no difference.)
4. この公園は春といわず秋といわず、一年中観光客でにぎわっている。
   (This park is bustling with tourists year-round — spring, autumn, it doesn't matter.)
5. 引っ越しの荷物は、玄関といわずリビングといわず家中に積み上げられていた。
   (The moving boxes were piled up everywhere in the house — the entryway, the living room, you name it.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern typically pairs two spatially or temporally related nouns (hands and feet, day and night), rather than unrelated items. Unlike といい…といい, it can be followed by verb sentences as well as state descriptions, and it tends to carry a negative or messy connotation. It cannot be followed by a negative sentence or a request/suggestion.
