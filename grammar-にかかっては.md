# ～にかかっては {#にかかっては}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～にかかっては

**Furigana form:** ～にかかっては *(掛かっては — conventionally written in kana here)*

**Meaning:** "When it comes to X('s hands/skill/power)" — expresses that once something falls under the influence, skill, or control of X, a particular (often surprising, impressive, or ironic) outcome inevitably follows.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 (noun, typically a person or their "hands"/skill) + にかかっては
- Common pattern: [person]の手にかかっては, or directly [person]にかかっては, describing that person's characteristic effect on a situation.

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal spoken and written register; often carries an ironic or admiring tone (e.g., praising someone's exceptional skill, or commenting wryly on someone's notorious habit). Not rude, but has a slightly rhetorical, storytelling flavor.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **を前提に (をぜんていに)** — means "on the premise/assumption of X"; the source material explicitly confirms students confused these two on a graded test (Q12) — を前提に is about a stated basis/condition for an action or plan, while にかかっては is about an outcome being determined by someone's hands/skill/influence. They are not interchangeable: を前提に sets up a premise for planning, whereas にかかっては describes a (often ironic) causal effect of someone's involvement.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though a phrase like ～をもってしても ("even with X, [still fails]") conceptually inverts the expectation — にかかっては implies guaranteed success/effect due to someone's skill, while をもってしても implies that even a given strength is insufficient. No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 泥棒の手にかかってはこんな鍵、簡単に開けられてしまうでしょうね。
   (In the hands of a thief, a lock like this would surely be opened easily.)
2. 田中課長の饒舌にかかっては誰も勝てない。
   (When it comes to Section Chief Tanaka's eloquence, nobody can win against him.)
3. 名シェフの手にかかれば、どんな食材も絶品料理に変わる。
   (In the hands of a master chef, any ingredient turns into an exquisite dish.)
4. あの弁護士にかかっては、どんな難しい裁判も勝訴に持っていける。
   (When it comes to that lawyer, he can turn even the toughest trial into a win.)
5. 子供の純粋な質問にかかっては、大人も答えに窮することがある。
   (When it comes to a child's innocent questions, even adults sometimes struggle to answer.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The source material explicitly documents a confirmed student wrong-answer (Q12): a student mistakenly chose にかかっては where を前提に was correct. The critical distinction: を前提に introduces the basis/assumption underlying a plan or statement ("on the premise that..."), while にかかっては describes an outcome that is determined by, or dependent on, someone's particular hands/skill/influence, often with an ironic or admiring flavor. Students should check whether the sentence is establishing a premise for a plan (を前提に) versus describing a skill-dependent outcome (にかかっては).
