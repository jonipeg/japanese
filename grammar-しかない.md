# ～しかない {#しかない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～しかない

**Furigana form:** ～しかない (no special kanji reading)

**Meaning:** "Have no choice but to X / there's nothing to do but X / only X" — expresses that, given the circumstances, a single option remains and must be taken.

**Conjugation:**
- Verb (dictionary form) + しかない
- 名詞 + しかない
- もう諦めるしかない (There's nothing to do but give up now)
- これしかない (This is all there is / this is the only option)

**Typical context:** Common in casual and neutral spoken Japanese, also appears in writing; less formal than ざるを得ない, more direct and resigned in tone.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**ざるを得ない**](grammar-ざるを得ない.md) — more formal, emphasizes being forced against one's will by external circumstance.
- **ほかない／ほかはない** — nearly synonymous, slightly more formal/written variant of しかない.
- **だけだ** — "that's all," can overlap in limiting-option contexts but doesn't always carry the "no choice" nuance.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though 他にも方法がある ("there are other ways too") contrasts conceptually.

**Example sentences:**
1. 電車がないので、歩いて帰るしかない。
   (Since there's no train, I have no choice but to walk home.)
2. こうなったら、もう正直に話すしかない。
   (Given this situation, there's nothing to do but tell the truth.)
3. 薬が効かないなら、手術するしかない。
   (If the medicine doesn't work, surgery is the only option.)
4. 彼を説得するには、直接会って話すしかない。
   (To convince him, the only way is to meet and talk directly.)
5. お金がないから、安い店で我慢するしかない。
   (Since I have no money, I have no choice but to make do with a cheap store.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** しかない is more casual/neutral than ざるを得ない, which carries stronger formality and reluctance; in daily conversation, しかない is the more natural, commonly used choice. Don't confuse with しか + negative in simple limiting sentences like これしか食べない ("I only eat this") — that's the basic しか...ない limiting pattern, a related but simpler structure.
