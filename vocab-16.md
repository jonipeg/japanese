## クーラー
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** an air conditioner, a cooler

**Similar words:**
- エアコン（えあこん）— the more modern, commonly used term for "air conditioner" (covers both heating and cooling); クーラー traditionally implies cooling only.
- [扇風機](vocab-23.md#扇風機-せんぷうき)（せんぷうき）— "electric fan," a different, non-refrigerated cooling device.

**Opposite words:**
- ヒーター（ひーたー）— heater

**Example sentences:**
1. 暑いのでクーラーをつけた。— It's hot, so I turned on the air conditioner.
2. クーラーの効きが悪い。— The air conditioner isn't cooling well.
3. クーラー病にならないよう気をつけて。— Be careful not to get sick from excessive air conditioning.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** クーラー traditionally refers to cooling-only units, while エアコン (which can heat and cool) has become the more common general term in modern usage — in everyday speech, many Japanese speakers now use エアコン even where クーラー was once standard.

## 区画 (くかく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** division, section, block (especially of land, as in city planning)

**Similar words:**
- [区分](#区分する-くぶん)（くぶん）— "classification/division," broader, can apply to categorizing anything, not just physical land.
- 街区（がいく）— "city block," specifically an urban block shape.
- [区域](vocab-15.md#区域-くいき)（くいき）— "area/zone," a designated area, often larger or less precisely bounded than a 区画.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 土地を区画整理した。— The land was reorganized into sections (land readjustment).
2. 駐車場の区画に車を停めた。— I parked the car in a designated parking spot.
3. 新しい住宅区画が開発された。— A new residential block was developed.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 区画 commonly appears in 区画整理 (land readjustment/replotting), a specific Japanese urban planning term — it refers to a physically delineated plot or section, distinct from the more abstract/general 区分 (classification).

## 区間 (くかん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a section, a segment (especially of a route, railway line, or road)

**Similar words:**
- 範囲（はんい）— "range/scope," broader and more abstract, not limited to physical routes.
- 路線（ろせん）— "train/bus line/route," the whole line rather than just a segment of it.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. この区間は運休している。— Service is suspended on this section (of the train line).
2. 渋滞している区間を避けて迂回した。— I took a detour to avoid the congested stretch.
3. 工事のため一部区間が通行止めだ。— Part of the route is closed due to construction.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 区間 is the standard word for a specific stretch/segment of a train line or road (e.g., in train announcements: この区間は各駅に停車します) — it's a must-know word for reading transit information in Japan.

## 釘 (くぎ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a nail

**Similar words:**
- ねじ（螺子）— "screw," a different type of fastener.
- ピン（ぴん）— "pin," smaller and used for different purposes.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 壁に釘を打って絵を飾った。— I hammered a nail into the wall and hung a picture.
2. 釘を踏んでけがをした。— I stepped on a nail and got hurt.
3. 古い釘が錆びていた。— The old nail was rusted.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The idiom 釘を刺す (literally "to drive in a nail") means "to give someone a firm warning/make sure they don't forget" — knowing this figurative usage is key, since it's far more common in conversation than the literal nail itself.

## 区切り (くぎり)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a break, a pause, a natural dividing point or end (of a task, period, or piece of text)

**Similar words:**
- 節目（ふしめ）— "milestone/turning point," often marks a significant life or career juncture, more symbolic than a simple pause.
- [休憩](vocab-15.md#休憩-きゅうけい)（きゅうけい）— "a break/rest," specifically a pause for resting, not a structural dividing point.
- 段落（だんらく）— "paragraph," a specific structural division in writing.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. ここで一区切りつけよう。— Let's wrap up this section here.
2. 仕事の区切りがいいところで休憩する。— I'll take a break at a good stopping point in my work.
3. 文章に区切りをつけて読みやすくした。— I added breaks to the text to make it easier to read.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 区切り often appears in the phrase 区切りをつける/がいい ("to bring to a natural pause/conclusion") describing a sensible stopping point in an ongoing task — it's more about structural pacing than taking a literal rest.

## 区切る (くぎる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to partition, divide, punctuate (mark off sections)

**Similar words:**
- [分ける](vocab-36.md#分ける-わける)（わける）— "to divide/separate," more general-purpose, not specific to marking sections within a continuum.
- 仕切る（しきる）— "to partition (physically, e.g., with a wall/curtain)," or "to take charge of/run something."
- 句切る（くぎる）— an alternate kanji specifically for punctuating sentences (pausing at phrase breaks), a specialized usage overlapping with 区切る.

**Opposite words:**
- つなげる（繋げる）— to connect, join

**Example sentences:**
1. 部屋をカーテンで区切った。— I partitioned the room with a curtain.
2. 文章を読点で区切る。— Punctuate the sentence with commas.
3. 一年を四半期に区切って管理する。— Manage the year by dividing it into quarters.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 区切る can apply to both physical space (partitioning a room) and abstract/temporal divisions (dividing time periods, punctuating text) — it's versatile, but always implies creating a clear boundary between sections.

## くぐる
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to pass/slip under (something), to go through (a gate, barrier)

**Similar words:**
- 通り抜ける（とおりぬける）— "to pass through," more general, not specifically about going under something.
- すり抜ける（すりぬける）— "to slip through (a narrow gap)," emphasizes narrowly evading/squeezing through.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 鳥居をくぐって神社に入った。— I passed under the torii gate and entered the shrine.
2. トンネルをくぐって反対側に出た。— I went through the tunnel and came out the other side.
3. 法の網をくぐる方法を考えた。— He thought of a way to slip through the loopholes of the law.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Beyond the literal "pass under" (鳥居をくぐる), くぐる has a figurative use meaning "to evade/slip past" rules or difficulties (法の網をくぐる "to evade the law," 危機をくぐり抜ける "to survive through a crisis") — don't limit your understanding to just the physical action.

## 臭い (くさい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to stink, smell bad; (figuratively) suspicious, fishy

**Similar words:**
- [匂う](vocab-30.md#匂う-におう)（におう）— neutral "to smell" (can be good or bad depending on context), whereas 臭い specifically implies a bad smell.
- 怪しい（あやしい）— "suspicious/dubious," the more common word for suspecting something is wrong, without the smell metaphor.

**Opposite words:**
- いい匂い（いいにおい）— a nice smell

**Example sentences:**
1. この靴下、臭い。— These socks stink.
2. 何だか話が臭いな。— Something about this story seems fishy.
3. ゴミ箱が臭いので毎日捨てている。— The trash can smells, so I take it out every day.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 臭い is written with a different kanji than 匂い (both read with くさ../にお.. roots) and specifically connotes an unpleasant odor, while also serving as slang for "suspicious" (あいつ何か臭いな, "that guy seems shady") — don't use it for pleasant smells.

## 鎖 (くさり)

- 紐（ひも）— "string/cord," thinner and more flexible than a chain.

## 腐る (くさる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to rot, spoil, go bad (of food); (figuratively) to become demoralized/sour

**Similar words:**
- 傷む（いたむ）— "to spoil/deteriorate," often milder, used for food going slightly bad or items getting damaged.
- 朽ちる（くちる）— "to decay/rot," more literary, often for wood or structures decaying over long time.
- [落ち込む](vocab-10.md#落ち込む-おちこむ)（おちこむ）— "to feel down," the figurative equivalent without the food-spoiling metaphor.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 暑さで食べ物がすぐ腐る。— Food spoils quickly in the heat.
2. 彼は失敗しても腐らずに頑張った。— Even after failing, he didn't get demoralized and kept trying.
3. この牛乳、もう腐っているかもしれない。— This milk might have already gone bad.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 腐る has a common figurative extension meaning "to become sulky/demoralized/give up in frustration" (腐らずに頑張る, "keep trying without getting discouraged") — this non-literal usage is very common in encouragement/motivational contexts.

## くしゃみ
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a sneeze

**Similar words:**
- せき（咳）— "a cough," a different respiratory reflex.
- [あくび](vocab-06.md#あくび-あくび)（欠伸）— "a yawn," another involuntary action, unrelated physiologically.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 花粉症でくしゃみが止まらない。— I can't stop sneezing because of hay fever.
2. 大きなくしゃみをしてびっくりした。— I was startled by a big sneeze.
3. 誰かが私の噂をしているのか、くしゃみが出た。— I sneezed — maybe someone's talking about me (referencing the superstition).

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** There's a traditional Japanese superstition that sneezing means someone is talking about you behind your back — this cultural reference (誰かが噂している) often appears alongside くしゃみ in casual conversation.

## 苦情 (くじょう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a complaint, a grievance

**Similar words:**
- クレーム（くれーむ）— loanword "claim/complaint," very commonly used in customer service contexts (note: false friend — not "claim" in the English legal sense).
- 文句（もんく）— "complaint/grumbling," more casual, often about minor personal grievances.
- 抗議（こうぎ）— "protest," a stronger, more formal objection, often collective.

**Opposite words:**
- 称賛（しょうさん）— praise, commendation

**Example sentences:**
1. 近隣住民から苦情が寄せられた。— Complaints came in from neighboring residents.
2. 苦情処理の担当者と話した。— I spoke with the person in charge of handling complaints.
3. 騒音について苦情を言った。— I complained about the noise.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 苦情 is the formal word used in customer service/business contexts (苦情処理 "complaint handling," お客様からの苦情 "customer complaints") — クレーム is the more casual spoken equivalent, and English speakers should note クレーム does NOT mean "claim" as in an insurance claim.

## 苦心 (くしん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** taking pains, working hard with effort/ingenuity (often to overcome a difficulty)

**Similar words:**
- 苦労（くろう）— "hardship/trouble," broader, about enduring difficulty generally, not necessarily tied to creative problem-solving.
- [工夫](vocab-02.md#工夫-くふう)（くふう）— "ingenuity/devising a way," focuses on the clever solution itself rather than the effortful struggle.
- [努力](vocab-30.md#努力-どりょく)（どりょく）— "effort," general hard work, without the specific connotation of struggling with a difficult problem.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. この作品には彼の苦心の跡が見える。— You can see traces of his painstaking effort in this work.
2. 苦心して完成させたプロジェクトだ。— It's a project I completed only after much effort and struggle.
3. 苦心の末、ついに解決策を見つけた。— After much toil, I finally found a solution.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 苦心 specifically implies effortful struggle combined with creative problem-solving (often in 苦心の末 "after much painstaking effort," or 苦心の作 "a work born of great effort") — it's more specific than generic 苦労 (hardship) or 努力 (effort).

## 屑 (くず)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** waste, rubbish, scraps; (figuratively, harsh) a worthless person

**Similar words:**
- ごみ（ゴミ）— "trash/garbage," the general everyday word for waste.
- 残骸（ざんがい）— "wreckage/debris," remains of something destroyed, more dramatic.
- 廃棄物（はいきぶつ）— "waste material," formal/technical term, often industrial.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 紙屑をゴミ箱に捨てた。— I threw the paper scraps in the trash can.
2. 鉄屑を集めてリサイクルに出した。— I collected scrap metal and sent it for recycling.
3. あんな人間の屑とは関わりたくない。— I don't want to associate with such a worthless person.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** While literally "scraps/waste" (紙屑 paper scraps, 鉄屑 scrap metal), 屑 is also used as a harsh insult toward a person (人間の屑, "human trash/scum") — be aware this usage is quite offensive and not appropriate in polite conversation.

**Similar words:**

## 崩す (くずす)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to destroy, demolish, break down; to break (a large bill into smaller change)

**Similar words:**
- 壊す（こわす）— "to break/destroy," more general and physical, without the specific "break a bill" usage.
- [倒す](vocab-25.md#倒す-たおす)（たおす）— "to knock down/topple," implies something falling over rather than crumbling apart.
- [崩れる](#崩れる-くずれる)（くずれる）— the intransitive counterpart, "to collapse/crumble (on its own)," vs. 崩す which is transitive (someone actively causes it).

**Opposite words:**
- [築く](vocab-01.md#築く-きずく)（きずく）— to build, construct

**Example sentences:**
1. 一万円札を崩してもらえますか。— Could you break this 10,000 yen bill for me?
2. 地震で建物が崩された。— The building was destroyed by the earthquake.
3. 体調を崩して会社を休んだ。— I fell ill (lit. broke down my condition) and took the day off work.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 崩す has two very different everyday uses — "breaking a large bill into smaller change" (お札を崩す) and "to fall ill/lose one's balance of health" (体調を崩す) — both are extremely common idioms learners should memorize directly, as they don't translate literally from "destroy."

## 距離 (きょり)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a distance, an interval (physical or figurative/emotional)

**Similar words:**
- 間隔（かんかく）— "interval/spacing," often for distance between repeated things (e.g., time intervals, spacing between objects).
- 隔たり（へだたり）— "a gap/separation," more literary, often figurative (emotional or ideological distance).

## 嫌 (きらい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** dislike, hate; disagreeable (na-adjective)

**Similar words:**
- 嫌い（きらい）— same word; note the homograph 嫌い（きらい, na-adjective "dislike"）vs. the suffix ～嫌い（ぎらい, "tendency to dislike," e.g. 食わず嫌い "disliking without trying").
- 苦手（にがて）— "bad at/not fond of," softer, often implies difficulty or awkwardness rather than outright dislike.
- 嫌悪（けんお）— "disgust/abhorrence," much stronger and more formal than casual 嫌い.

**Opposite words:**
- 好き（すき）— like, fond of

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は野菜が嫌いだ。— He dislikes vegetables.
2. 虫が嫌いで触れない。— I hate bugs and can't touch them.
3. そんな嫌な言い方をしないで。— Don't speak in such a nasty way.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** As a suffix (～嫌い, read ぎらい with rendaku), it means "a tendency to dislike X" (負けず嫌い "hates to lose," 食わず嫌い "disliking something without having tried it") — note the reading shifts from きらい to ぎらい in this compound form.

## 嫌う (きらう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to dislike, detest, loathe

**Similar words:**
- [嫌がる](vocab-07.md#嫌がる-いやがる)（いやがる）— "to show reluctance/displeasure (visibly)," more about outward resistance than inner feeling.
- [憎む](vocab-30.md#憎む-にくむ)（にくむ）— "to hate/resent," much stronger, implies deep hostility.
- [避ける](vocab-36.md#避ける-よける)（さける）— "to avoid," behavioral avoidance, which may or may not stem from dislike.

**Opposite words:**
- 好む（このむ）— to like, prefer

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は人混みを嫌う。— He dislikes crowds.
2. その提案は多くの人に嫌われた。— That proposal was disliked by many people.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 嫌う is the verb form of 嫌い, used in more formal/written contexts (嫌われる, passive "to be disliked/hated") — in casual spoken Japanese, people more often just use the na-adjective 嫌い rather than conjugating 嫌う directly.

## 気楽 (きらく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** carefree, easygoing, relaxed (without worry or pressure)

**Similar words:**
- のんき（呑気）— "carefree/easy-going," similar but can carry a slightly negative nuance of being too laid-back/oblivious.
- 気軽（きがる）— "casual, without hesitation," more about ease of action (e.g., feeling free to ask/do something) than a general life attitude.
- 楽観的（らっかんてき）— "optimistic," about outlook on outcomes rather than a relaxed lifestyle/attitude.

**Opposite words:**
- 神経質（しんけいしつ）— nervous, highly strung

**Example sentences:**
1. 気楽に考えればいいよ。— Just think about it casually/don't stress.
2. 一人暮らしは気楽だ。— Living alone is carefree.
3. 気楽な気持ちで試験に臨んだ。— I approached the exam with a relaxed mindset.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 気楽 and 気軽 are easily confused — 気楽 describes a relaxed mental state/lifestyle (stress-free), while 気軽 describes ease of taking an action without hesitation (気軽に聞いてください, "feel free to ask").

## 煌びやか (きらびやか)

## 限り (きり)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a limit, an end; (as a suffix, ～限り) as far as/to the extent that, or "only/just"

**Similar words:**
- [限界](vocab-18.md#限界-げんかい)（げんかい）— "limit/boundary," more about the maximum capacity, often used for abstract capability limits.
- 以上（いじょう）— "given that / since," a related grammatical pattern for "as long as a condition holds."
- だけ（だけ）— "only," simpler and more casual way to express exclusivity/limitation.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 力の限り頑張った。— I did my best with all my strength (to the limit).
2. 今日限りでこの店は閉店する。— This shop will close for good, ending today.
3. 私の知る限り、彼は正直な人だ。— As far as I know, he is an honest person.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 暮れ alone (without 年の) can mean either "dusk" or "year's end" depending on context — in modern usage 年末 is more common/unambiguous for "year-end," while 暮れ often appears in more literary or seasonal expressions (お歳暮 "year-end gift," itself derived from this word).

## グレー (グレー)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** gray, grey

**Similar words:**
- 灰色（はいいろ）— the native Japanese word for "gray," used more in formal/descriptive writing; グレー is more common in fashion/design contexts.
- 鼠色（ねずみいろ）— "mouse-gray," a traditional, slightly old-fashioned name for a grayish color.

**Example sentences:**
1. グレーのスーツを着ている。— He's wearing a gray suit.
2. 彼の返答はグレーな感じだった。— His answer felt ambiguous (lit. "gray," i.e. neither clearly yes nor no).
3. グレーの空が広がっていた。— A gray sky stretched overhead.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** グレー is also used figuratively for "ambiguous/borderline" situations (グレーゾーン "gray area," legally or ethically unclear) — this abstract sense parallels English "gray area" and is common in business/legal Japanese.

## クレーン (クレーン)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a crane (construction machine/truck)

**Similar words:**
- 起重機（きじゅうき）— the formal/technical Japanese term for "crane," used in engineering/legal documents; クレーン is the common everyday term.

**Example sentences:**
1. 工事現場で大きなクレーンが稼働していた。— A large crane was operating at the construction site.

3. クレーンの操作には資格が必要だ。— Operating a crane requires a license.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** クレーン refers to the construction/lifting machine; it's unrelated to the English homograph "crane" the bird, which in Japanese is 鶴（つる）— a common point of confusion for English speakers directly translating.

## くれぐれも (くれぐれも)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** be sure to, please do take care (emphatic adverb for earnest requests)

**Similar words:**
- [どうか](vocab-29.md#どうか)（どうか）— "please," a general polite request marker, less emphatic/repetitive in tone than くれぐれも.
- [必ず](vocab-13.md#必ず-かならず)（かならず）— "without fail, certainly," emphasizes certainty of action rather than earnest care/concern.

**Example sentences:**
1. くれぐれもお体を大切にしてください。— Please be sure to take good care of your health.
2. くれぐれもよろしくお伝えください。— Please be sure to give them my regards.
3. くれぐれも油断しないように。— Please make sure not to let your guard down.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** くれぐれも is used specifically in earnest, often slightly worried, repeated requests for someone's wellbeing or caution — it has a warm, formal politeness and is commonly used in letters/parting words, not for ordinary requests about unrelated tasks.

## 暮れる (くれる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to get dark, to grow late (of the day); (figuratively) to be at a loss, immersed

**Similar words:**
- 日が沈む（ひがしずむ）— "the sun sets," a more literal/concrete description of the same phenomenon.
- 更ける（ふける）— "to grow late" (of night specifically), a related but narrower word for deep night rather than dusk.

**Example sentences:**
1. 秋は日が暮れるのが早い。— In autumn, it gets dark early.
2. 途方に暮れてどうすればいいか分からなかった。— I was at a loss and didn't know what to do.
3. 彼は悲しみに暮れていた。— He was sunk in grief.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Beyond the literal "getting dark" meaning, 暮れる has important figurative uses in set phrases like 途方に暮れる ("to be at a loss") and 悲しみに暮れる ("to be overcome with grief") — learners should memorize these fixed expressions rather than expect a literal "darkness" meaning each time.

## 加える (くわえる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to add, to join (something to a set/total); to inflict (e.g. damage)

**Similar words:**
- [足す](vocab-25.md#足す-たす)（たす）— "to add" in a simple arithmetic/everyday sense (e.g. adding numbers or ingredients), more casual than 加える.
- 追加する（ついかする）— "to add on/supplement," often used for adding items to an existing list or order.
- 含める（ふくめる）— "to include," emphasizing something being counted as part of a whole rather than newly added.

**Example sentences:**
1. スープに塩を少し加えた。— I added a little salt to the soup.
2. メンバーに新しい人を加えることにした。— We decided to add a new person to the members.
3. 相手に攻撃を加えた。— They inflicted an attack on the opponent.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 加える can mean literal addition (ingredients, numbers, members) but also appears in the more formal/written idiom 攻撃を加える ("to inflict an attack") or 制裁を加える ("to impose sanctions") — this "to inflict/impose" sense is distinct from simple addition and often missed by learners.

## 詳しい (くわしい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** detailed, particular; to be well informed about (a subject)

**Similar words:**
- 細かい（こまかい）— "fine/detailed," often about physical fineness or minute detail, rather than depth of knowledge.
- 精通している（せいつうしている）— "to be well-versed in," a more formal synonym for the "knowledgeable about" sense of 詳しい.

**Opposite words:**
- 疎い（うとい）— ignorant of, uninformed about

**Example sentences:**
1. もっと詳しい情報が欲しい。— I want more detailed information.
2. 彼は歴史に詳しい。— He is knowledgeable about history.
3. 詳しく説明してください。— Please explain in detail.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 詳しい has two related but distinct uses — describing information as "detailed" (詳しい情報) and describing a person as "knowledgeable about X" using 〜に詳しい — learners sometimes try to use に incorrectly with the first sense or forget it's required with the second.
