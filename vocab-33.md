## ヨット (よっと)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** yacht, sailboat

**Similar words:**
- 船（ふね）— "boat/ship," the general term for any watercraft; ヨット specifically refers to a sailboat/yacht used for leisure or racing.
- クルーザー — "cruiser (boat)," a loanword for a larger motor-powered leisure boat; ヨット specifically implies a sailboat.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 夏休みにヨットに乗りに行った。— I went sailing during summer vacation.
2. 彼はヨットレースに参加している。— He participates in yacht races.
3. 海辺に白いヨットが浮かんでいる。— White yachts are floating by the seaside.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ヨット specifically refers to a sailing yacht/sailboat (from English "yacht"), not a general motorboat — in Japanese it strongly evokes sailing/wind-powered boats rather than luxury motor yachts, which is a slight difference from the broader English usage.

## 酔っ払い (よっぱらい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a drunk, a drunken person

**Similar words:**
- [酔う](vocab-35.md#酔う-よう)（よう）— "to get drunk," the base verb from which 酔っ払い derives; 酔っ払い is the noun referring to a person in that drunken state.
- 泥酔（でいすい）— "dead drunk, severe intoxication," more formal/clinical term for extreme drunkenness; 酔っ払い is the casual everyday word for a drunk person.

**Opposite words:**
- しらふ — sober

**Example sentences:**
1. 駅前に酔っ払いが座り込んでいた。— A drunk was sitting on the ground in front of the station.
2. 酔っ払い運転は絶対にしてはいけない。— You must never drive drunk.
3. 彼は酔っ払いになると陽気になる。— He becomes cheerful when he gets drunk.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 酔っ払い is casual/colloquial and can sound slightly derogatory or humorous depending on tone — it's the noun form (a drunk person), while the verb for "to get drunk" is 酔っ払う or simply 酔う; 酔っ払い運転 is a commonly seen compound meaning "drunk driving."

## 予定 (よてい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** plan, schedule

**Similar words:**
- [計画](vocab-17.md#計画する-けいかく)（けいかく）— "a plan," often more detailed/deliberate, used for larger projects; 予定 is more about a scheduled time/event, simpler and more everyday.
- スケジュール — "schedule," a loanword often used interchangeably with 予定 in casual/business contexts, especially for a list of appointments.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 明日は会議の予定がある。— I have a meeting scheduled for tomorrow.
2. 来月、引っ越す予定です。— I'm planning to move next month.
3. 予定が変更になった。— The schedule has changed.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 〜予定です is an extremely common grammatical pattern for stating future plans (旅行に行く予定です = "I'm planning to go on a trip"), softer and more tentative than つもり (intention) — it implies something scheduled/arranged rather than a firm personal resolve.

## 与党 (よとう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** the ruling/government party

**Similar words:**
- [政権](vocab-22.md#政権-せいけん)（せいけん）— "the administration/regime in power," broader, referring to the governing body as a whole; 与党 specifically refers to the political party (or coalition) holding power in parliament.
- [政党](vocab-22.md#政党-せいとう)（せいとう）— "a political party," general term for any party; 与党 specifically denotes the one(s) currently in government.

**Opposite words:**
- 野党（やとう）— the opposition party

**Example sentences:**
1. 与党が選挙で過半数を獲得した。— The ruling party won a majority in the election.
2. 与党と野党の対立が激化している。— The conflict between the ruling and opposition parties is intensifying.
3. 与党内でも意見が分かれている。— Opinions are divided even within the ruling party.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 与党 and its direct opposite 野党 are almost always learned and used as a pair in Japanese political reporting — mixing them up is a common beginner error, so associating them together (与党 = in power, 野党 = "in the fields/out of power") helps retention.

## 夜中 (よなか)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** midnight, the dead of night

**Similar words:**
- [深夜](vocab-22.md#深夜-しんや)（しんや）— "late night/dead of night," more formal/written, often used in TV schedules (深夜番組 = late-night show); 夜中 is the everyday spoken equivalent.
- 真夜中（まよなか）— "the middle of the night," emphasizes the very peak of nighttime, often used for dramatic emphasis; 夜中 is more general for "during the night."

**Opposite words:**
- 真昼（まひる）— broad daylight, midday

**Example sentences:**
1. 夜中に電話が鳴って驚いた。— I was startled when the phone rang in the middle of the night.
2. 夜中まで勉強していた。— I was studying until late at night.
3. 彼は夜中にこっそり家を出た。— He quietly left the house in the dead of night.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 夜中 is a common, casual everyday word for "during the night / late at night," while 深夜 is more formal/official (used in broadcasting schedules, legal contexts like 深夜営業 = late-night business hours) — choose based on register.

## 世の中 (よのなか)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** the world, life, the times, society

**Similar words:**
- [社会](vocab-20.md#社会-しゃかい)（しゃかい）— "society," a more formal/academic term for the structured social system; 世の中 is more colloquial and philosophical, often used to comment on life/the way things are.
- [世界](vocab-23.md#世界-せかい)（せかい）— "the world," refers to the globe/world at large (geographically or broadly); 世の中 is more about the human social world and how it functions.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 世の中はそんなに甘くない。— The world isn't that easy/forgiving.
2. 世の中が便利になった。— The world (society) has become more convenient.
3. そういう世の中の流れには逆らえない。— You can't go against that kind of societal trend.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 世の中 often appears in reflective or philosophical statements about how life/society works (世の中はそんなものだ = "that's just how the world is"), giving it a more experiential, folk-wisdom tone compared to the more analytical/academic 社会.

## 予備 (よび)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a spare, a reserve, a backup

**Similar words:**
- スペア — "spare," a loanword often used for physical spare parts (タイヤのスペア = spare tire); 予備 is broader and more formal, applying to people, plans, and supplies too (予備知識 = background knowledge).
- バックアップ — "backup," a loanword specifically common in digital/IT contexts; 予備 is the general-purpose native term.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 予備の電池を持ってきた。— I brought spare batteries.
2. 面接の前に予備知識を身につけておいた。— I equipped myself with background knowledge before the interview.
3. 予備校に通って受験勉強をしている。— I attend a prep school to study for exams.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 予備 appears in many useful compounds beyond "spare object" — 予備校 (cram/prep school for exam retakers), 予備知識 (background knowledge), 予備軍 (a "reserve army," used figuratively for people at risk of a condition, e.g. 糖尿病予備軍 = "prediabetic") — these extended uses go beyond the literal "spare" meaning.

## 呼びかける (よびかける)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to call out to, to appeal to

**Similar words:**
- [訴える](vocab-08.md#訴える-うったえる)（うったえる）— "to appeal/plead," often emotionally or legally, and can be more intense/urgent; 呼びかける is more about actively calling for attention/action from a group.
- アピールする — "to appeal/promote," a loanword often used in advertising/self-promotion contexts; 呼びかける is more about urging people toward a collective action (e.g. 協力を呼びかける = to call for cooperation).

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 市民に節電を呼びかけた。— We appealed to citizens to conserve electricity.
2. 彼は道で困っている人に呼びかけた。— He called out to someone in trouble on the street.
3. 政府は国民に協力を呼びかけている。— The government is calling on the public for cooperation.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 呼びかける frequently appears in public appeals and announcements (節電を呼びかける, 注意を呼びかける) directed at a group/the public, distinct from simply calling an individual's name, which would just use 呼ぶ.

## 呼び止める (よびとめる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to call out and stop (someone)

**Similar words:**
- 止める（とめる）— "to stop (something/someone)," general verb; 呼び止める specifically combines calling out verbally with the act of making someone halt.
- 引き止める（ひきとめる）— "to hold back/detain (someone from leaving)," implies physically or persuasively preventing departure; 呼び止める is specifically the verbal call that causes the stop.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 警察官が通行人を呼び止めた。— A police officer called out and stopped a passerby.
2. 彼女に呼び止められて振り返った。— I turned around after being called out to and stopped by her.
3. 店員に呼び止められて商品の説明を受けた。— I was stopped by a store clerk and given an explanation of the product.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 呼び止める is a compound verb combining 呼ぶ (to call) + 止める (to stop), used specifically for situations where someone verbally calls out to make another person pause/stop walking — commonly seen in contexts involving police, store clerks, or someone trying to get another's attention from behind.

## 呼ぶ (よぶ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to call (someone's name, to invite, to summon)

**Similar words:**
- 招く（まねく）— "to invite," more formal, used for inviting guests to an event; 呼ぶ is more general and casual, covering calling someone's name or summoning them too.
- [名付ける](vocab-30.md#名付ける-なづける)（なづける）— "to name," used for giving something a name; 呼ぶ can also mean "to refer to/call something by a name" (〜と呼ぶ).

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 名前を呼ばれて振り向いた。— I turned around when my name was called.
2. 友達を家に呼んだ。— I invited friends over to my house.
3. これは日本語で何と呼びますか。— What is this called in Japanese?

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 呼ぶ has three distinct common uses that learners should keep separate: calling someone's name, inviting someone over, and referring to something by a name/term (〜と呼ばれている = "is called ~") — context determines which sense applies.

## 余分 (よぶん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a surplus, an extra amount, excess

**Similar words:**
- [余計](vocab-36.md#余計-よけい)（よけい）— "excessive/unnecessary," often with a negative nuance of something being unwanted or uncalled for (余計なお世話 = "none of your business"); 余分 is more neutral, simply meaning "extra."
- 余り（あまり）— "leftover/remainder," what's left after using/consuming something; 余分 is more about having more than needed from the start.

**Opposite words:**
- 不足（ふそく）— shortage, insufficiency

**Example sentences:**
1. 余分なお金はない。— I don't have any extra money.
2. 荷物に余分なスペースがある。— There's extra space in the luggage.
3. 余分に買っておいた方がいい。— It's better to buy extra.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 余分 is neutral ("extra/surplus"), while its near-homophone cousin 余計 often carries a negative, unwanted connotation ("uncalled for," as in 余計なことを言うな = "don't say unnecessary/uncalled-for things") — mixing these up can unintentionally sound rude.

## 予報 (よほう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a forecast (especially weather)

**Similar words:**
- [予測](vocab-32.md#予測-よそく)（よそく）— "forecast/estimate," broader and more data/analysis based, used in economics, science, etc.; 予報 is specifically the standard term for official forecasts, especially weather.
- 天気予報（てんきよほう）— "weather forecast," the full standard compound term that 予報 is most often shorthand for in daily conversation.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 今日の天気予報は雨だった。— Today's weather forecast said rain.
2. 台風の予報が出ている。— A typhoon forecast has been issued.
3. 予報が外れて晴れた。— The forecast was wrong and it turned out sunny.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 予報 in everyday conversation almost always refers to the weather forecast specifically (short for 天気予報), while 予測 is the more general term used for forecasting in business, science, or other data-driven contexts — don't use 予報 for things like sales forecasts.

## 予防 (よぼう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** prevention (especially of disease/accidents)

**Similar words:**
- 防止（ぼうし）— "prevention," a close synonym used for preventing specific negative events (事故防止 = accident prevention); 予防 leans more toward health/medical prevention (予防接種 = vaccination).
- [対策](vocab-25.md#対策-たいさく)（たいさく）— "countermeasure," broader, about measures taken in response to or anticipation of a problem; 予防 specifically means stopping something before it occurs.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 風邪の予防にはうがいが効果的だ。— Gargling is effective for preventing colds.
2. 予防接種を受けてきた。— I went and got a vaccination.
3. 火災予防のために点検を行った。— We conducted an inspection for fire prevention.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 予防 is most strongly associated with health and safety (予防接種 = vaccination, 予防医学 = preventive medicine), while 防止 is used more broadly for preventing any kind of undesirable event (犯罪防止 = crime prevention) — the two overlap but have different typical collocations.

## 読み (よみ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** reading; also insight, a read on a situation

**Similar words:**
- 読解（どっかい）— "reading comprehension," a more formal/academic term specifically for understanding written text; 読み is broader and can also mean intuition/insight into a situation.
- 洞察（どうさつ）— "insight," a formal term for perceptive understanding; 読み in its figurative sense (readingの読みが深い = "has deep insight/foresight") is a more casual equivalent.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. この漢字の読みがわからない。— I don't know the reading of this kanji.
2. 彼は将棋の読みが鋭い。— He has sharp foresight in shogi.
3. 彼の読みは外れた。— His prediction/read on the situation was wrong.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Beyond its literal meaning "reading" (of a word or kanji), 読み is commonly used figuratively to mean "insight" or "a read on a situation" (特に将棋や相場で, as in 読みが当たる/外れる = "the prediction hit/missed") — this extended meaning is important for natural comprehension.

## 読み上げる (よみあげる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to read out loud, to read through (aloud, often formally)

**Similar words:**
- 朗読する（ろうどくする）— "to read aloud (expressively)," often for literature/poetry with artistic delivery; 読み上げる is more neutral/functional, used for announcements, names, documents.
- 発表する（はっぴょうする）— "to announce/present," broader, doesn't necessarily involve reading a prepared text aloud; 読み上げる specifically implies vocalizing written text from start to finish.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 司会者が受賞者の名前を読み上げた。— The emcee read out the winner's name.
2. 判決文が法廷で読み上げられた。— The verdict was read out in court.
3. 彼は原稿をそのまま読み上げるだけだった。— He just read the manuscript aloud as it was.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 読み上げる implies reading a complete text aloud from beginning to end, often in a formal/official setting (names at a ceremony, a verdict, an announcement) — it's not used for casual reading aloud to oneself, which would just be 読む.

## 蘇る (よみがえる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to revive, to come back to life, to be resurrected

**Similar words:**
- 復活する（ふっかつする）— "to revive/make a comeback," broader, used for trends, careers, and literal resurrection; 蘇る is more literary/poetic and often describes memories or feelings coming back vividly.
- 回復する（かいふくする）— "to recover," about returning to a prior healthy/normal state; 蘇る implies something that had died or been lost coming back, a stronger image.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 昔の記憶が鮮やかに蘇った。— Old memories vividly came back to me.
2. 枯れた木が蘇った。— The withered tree came back to life.
3. 伝説の英雄が蘇るという物語だ。— It's a story about a legendary hero being resurrected.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 蘇る is frequently used figuratively for memories, feelings, or traditions "coming back to life" vividly (記憶が蘇る), not just literal biological resurrection — it has a more poetic/literary flavor than the more neutral, everyday 復活する.

## 寄りかかる (よりかかる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to lean against, to depend/rely on

**Similar words:**
- [もたれる](vocab-34.md#もたれる-もたれる) — "to lean against/rest on," very close synonym, slightly more casual; 寄りかかる can be used both literally (leaning against a wall) and figuratively (depending on someone).
- [頼る](vocab-26.md#頼る-たよる)（たよる）— "to rely/depend on," specifically about depending on someone for help/support; 寄りかかる figuratively implies a heavier, sometimes criticized reliance (親に寄りかかる = "leaning/relying too much on one's parents").

**Opposite words:**
- 自立する（じりつする）— to become independent

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は壁に寄りかかって休んでいた。— He was resting, leaning against the wall.
2. いつまでも親に寄りかかってはいけない。— You shouldn't keep depending on your parents forever.
3. 木に寄りかかって本を読んだ。— I read a book leaning against a tree.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 寄りかかる has both a literal physical meaning (leaning one's body against something) and a figurative meaning (over-relying on someone/something, often with a mildly critical nuance of dependency) — context determines which sense is meant.

## より (より)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** than (comparison); from, since (formal, as a particle)

**Similar words:**
- から — "from/since," the everyday equivalent particle for origin/starting point; より is more formal/written, often used in official notices (10時より開始 = "starting from 10 o'clock").
- 〜に比べて（にくらべて）— "compared to," an alternative phrase for comparisons; より attaches directly to a noun to form a comparative (AよりBのほうが〜).

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 今日は昨日より暑い。— Today is hotter than yesterday.
2. 会議は午後1時より開始します。— The meeting will start from 1pm.
3. 彼は誰よりも努力した。— He worked harder than anyone.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** より has two quite different grammatical functions that learners often conflate: the comparison particle ("than," AよりB) and the formal particle meaning "from/since" (used in official announcements, letters — similar to から but more formal) — the second use is much rarer in casual speech.

## 世論 (よろん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** public opinion

**Similar words:**
- 輿論（よろん）— older/formal written variant of the same word, same reading, used in more academic or legal contexts.
- 民意（みんい）— "the will of the people," focuses on the people's collective intent rather than a measured "opinion."
- [アンケート](vocab-07.md#アンケート-アンケート)（あんけーと）— a survey/questionnaire, the tool used to measure 世論 rather than the opinion itself.

**Opposite words:**
- 私見（しけん）— personal/private opinion, as opposed to public opinion

**Example sentences:**
1. 世論の動向を注視する必要がある。— We need to closely watch the trends in public opinion.
2. その政策は世論の強い反発を招いた。— That policy provoked strong public backlash.
3. 世論調査によると、支持率は下がっている。— According to the public opinion poll, approval ratings are falling.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Note the reading is よろん, not せろん (though せろん is also an accepted reading in some dictionaries) — learners often mispronounce it. It's almost always used in the set phrase 世論調査 (public opinion poll) or 世論を喚起する (to arouse public opinion).

## 弱まる (よわまる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to grow weak, to weaken (intransitive)

**Similar words:**
- [弱る](#弱る-よわる)（よわる）— to grow weak/debilitated, often used for living things (health, strength), while 弱まる is used more for abstract forces (wind, pain, feelings).
- [衰える](vocab-10.md#衰える-おとろえる)（おとろえる）— to decline/decay over time, broader and often implies a long-term, irreversible decline.
- 和らぐ（やわらぐ）— to soften/ease, focuses on intensity becoming gentler rather than strength disappearing.

**Opposite words:**
- 強まる（つよまる）— to intensify, grow stronger

**Example sentences:**
1. 台風の勢いが次第に弱まってきた。— The typhoon's intensity has gradually weakened.
2. 時間が経つにつれ、痛みは弱まった。— As time passed, the pain weakened.
3. 彼への反対の声は徐々に弱まっている。— The voices of opposition against him are gradually weakening.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This is intransitive — the subject itself weakens (風が弱まる). Don't confuse with the transitive 弱める (to weaken something), which takes a direct object (を).

## 弱める (よわめる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to weaken (something), to turn down/lessen

**Similar words:**
- [弱まる](#弱まる-よわまる)（よわまる）— the intransitive pair; 弱める always requires an object being acted upon.
- [和らげる](vocab-34.md#和らげる-やわらげる)（やわらげる）— to soften/alleviate (pain, tension), more about reducing intensity gently than reducing raw strength.
- [緩める](vocab-35.md#緩める-ゆるめる)（ゆるめる）— to loosen/relax (a rule, grip, pace), broader usage beyond "weakening."

**Opposite words:**
- 強める（つよめる）— to strengthen, intensify

**Example sentences:**
1. 火を弱めてもう少し煮込んでください。— Please turn down the heat and simmer it a bit longer.
2. 批判の声を弱めるために声明を出した。— They issued a statement to weaken the voices of criticism.
3. 薬で痛みを弱めることができた。— The medicine was able to lessen the pain.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Transitive verb — always takes を (火を弱める). A common cooking-context usage is 火を弱める "turn down the heat," which learners sometimes confuse with 弱まる.

## 弱る (よわる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to grow weak, lose strength; to be in a fix, be perplexed

**Similar words:**
- [弱まる](#弱まる-よわまる)（よわまる）— used for abstract forces/intensities weakening, while 弱る is used for living things' health/vitality weakening.
- 衰弱する（すいじゃくする）— a more formal/medical term for becoming debilitated, often used for serious illness.
- 困る（こまる）— to be troubled/at a loss, overlaps with 弱る's secondary meaning of "being perplexed," but 困る is more neutral and common.

**Opposite words:**
- 強まる（つよまる）— to grow strong(er) (as a force)

**Example sentences:**
1. 祖父は病気で体が弱ってきた。— My grandfather's body has grown weak from illness.
2. 急に雨が降ってきて弱ったなあ。— It suddenly started raining — what a bind I'm in.
3. 植物は水不足で弱っている。— The plant is weakening from lack of water.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 弱ったな(あ) is a common colloquial exclamation meaning "oh no, this is a problem" — learners often only learn the "physically weaken" sense and miss this idiomatic "troubled" usage.

## 類推 (るいすい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** analogical inference, analogy-based reasoning

**Similar words:**
- [推測](vocab-22.md#推測-すいそく)（すいそく）— "guess/speculation," more general inference without necessarily using analogy as the method.
- 比喩（ひゆ）— "metaphor/analogy" as a rhetorical device in language, not a reasoning process like 類推.
- [連想](vocab-34.md#連想-れんそう)（れんそう）— "association" (of ideas), a looser mental link rather than a structured logical inference.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 過去の事例から類推して対策を考えた。— I thought of countermeasures by analogical inference from past cases.
2. この単語の意味は文脈から類推できる。— The meaning of this word can be inferred analogically from context.
3. 専門家は類推によって未知の現象を説明した。— The expert explained the unknown phenomenon through analogical reasoning.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 類推する is a fairly academic/formal verb meaning to reason from known similar cases to an unknown one — it's more intellectual/logical than casual 推測する (guessing), and appears often in essays, science, and linguistics.

## ルーズ (るーず)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** loose, sloppy, careless (especially with money or time)

**Similar words:**
- [だらしない](vocab-26.md#だらしない) — native Japanese word for "slovenly/sloppy," broader (covers appearance, habits) and more commonly used than the loanword ルーズ.
- いい加減（いいかげん）— "irresponsible/halfhearted," emphasizes a lack of seriousness or care, can apply to attitude generally.
- [几帳面](vocab-01.md#几帳面-きちょうめん)（きちょうめん）— the opposite trait, meticulous and precise, often contrasted with ルーズ in describing personality.

**Opposite words:**
- [几帳面](vocab-01.md#几帳面-きちょうめん)（きちょうめん）— meticulous, precise
- 厳格（げんかく）— strict, rigorous

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は時間にルーズな性格だ。— He has a personality that's loose about time (always late).
2. お金にルーズな人とは付き合いたくない。— I don't want to associate with people who are careless with money.
3. 彼女の仕事はいつもルーズで困る。— Her work is always sloppy, and it's a problem.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ルーズ is almost always used with に to describe being careless "about/with" something specific (時間にルーズ, お金にルーズ) — it's rarely used as a standalone descriptor without specifying the domain of carelessness.

## 留守番 (るすばん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** looking after the house while others are out; (by extension) answering machine

**Similar words:**
- 留守（るす）— simply means "being out/away from home"; 留守番 specifically refers to the person (or role) staying behind to watch the house.
- 子守（こもり）— "babysitting," a related caretaking role but specifically for children, not the house itself.
- ハウスシッター — "house sitter" (loanword), a more Western/formal concept of paid house-sitting, less common in everyday Japanese usage than 留守番.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 両親が旅行に行くので、犬の留守番をすることになった。— Since my parents are going on a trip, I'm going to be the one watching the dog at home.
2. 子供に留守番をさせるのは少し心配だ。— I'm a bit worried about leaving the kids to watch the house alone.
3. 留守番電話にメッセージを残してください。— Please leave a message on the answering machine.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 留守番電話 (answering machine/voicemail) is a very common compound derived from 留守番 — learners sometimes think 留守番 alone means "voicemail," but by itself it specifically means the act/role of staying home to watch the place.

## 零 (れい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** zero

**Similar words:**
- ゼロ — the far more commonly used loanword for "zero" in everyday speech; 零 is the native kanji reading, more formal/literary, often seen in set phrases (零点, 零時).
- 無（む）— "nothingness/non-existence," a broader philosophical/abstract concept rather than the numeral zero specifically.
- マイナス — "minus/negative," related to numbers below zero, not zero itself.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 気温が零度を下回った。— The temperature dropped below zero degrees.
2. テストで零点を取ってしまった。— I ended up getting a zero on the test.
3. 午前零時に花火が始まった。— The fireworks started at midnight (zero hour).

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 零 is mostly reserved for set formal expressions like 零度 (zero degrees), 零時 (midnight/zero hundred hours), and 零点 (zero points) — for everyday counting or casual references to "zero," ゼロ is used instead.

## 礼儀 (れいぎ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** courtesy, manners, etiquette

**Similar words:**
- マナー — the loanword "manners," often used for more casual/modern social etiquette (e.g. table manners, phone manners); 礼儀 is broader and carries a more traditional/moral weight.
- 作法（さほう）— "etiquette/protocol," often refers to specific formal procedures (tea ceremony, table setting) rather than general politeness.
- 敬意（けいい）— "respect," the underlying attitude, whereas 礼儀 is the outward behavioral expression of that respect.

**Opposite words:**
- 無礼（ぶれい）— rudeness, discourtesy

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は礼儀正しい青年だ。— He's a very courteous young man.
2. 目上の人には礼儀を尽くすべきだ。— One should show full courtesy to one's superiors.
3. 礼儀を知らない態度に周りが驚いた。— Everyone around was surprised by his lack of manners.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 礼儀 often appears in the set phrase 礼儀正しい (polite/well-mannered) and 礼儀を守る/尽くす — it emphasizes social propriety rooted in respect for hierarchy and tradition, more formal than casual マナー.

## 礼儀正しい (れいぎただしい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** polite, courteous, well-mannered

**Similar words:**
- 丁寧（ていねい）— "polite/careful," often describes polite speech or careful work, narrower than the overall mannered behavior implied by 礼儀正しい.
- 上品（じょうひん）— "elegant/refined," focuses more on gracefulness and class than strict adherence to etiquette.
- きちんとした — "proper/orderly," broader term for being well put-together, not specifically about politeness toward others.

**Opposite words:**
- [失礼](vocab-21.md#失礼-しつれい)（しつれい）— rude, impolite
- 無礼（ぶれい）— discourteous

**Example sentences:**
1. あの子はいつも礼儀正しく挨拶する。— That child always greets people politely.
2. 礼儀正しい態度は好印象を与える。— A polite attitude gives a good impression.
3. 彼は目上の人に対して礼儀正しく振る舞う。— He behaves politely toward his superiors.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 礼儀正しい is an i-adjective describing a person's behavior/attitude, not an action itself — it's often used to praise someone's upbringing or character, especially regarding how they treat people of higher status.

## 例外 (れいがい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** an exception

**Similar words:**
- 特例（とくれい）— "special case/exception," often implies an officially sanctioned exception to a rule, more formal/administrative.
- 異例（いれい）— "unprecedented/unusual," emphasizes rarity or being out of the ordinary rather than simply being an exception to a stated rule.
- 別格（べっかく）— "in a class of its own," implies something is treated specially due to its exceptional status, not necessarily tied to breaking a rule.

**Opposite words:**
- [原則](vocab-18.md#原則-げんそく)（げんそく）— principle, general rule
- [通常](vocab-04.md#通常-つうじょう)（つうじょう）— normal, usual

**Example sentences:**
1. この規則には例外がない。— There are no exceptions to this rule.
2. 彼は例外として特別に許可された。— He was granted permission as a special exception.
3. 例外を除いて、全員が参加しなければならない。— Except for exceptions, everyone must participate.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The common phrase 例外なく (without exception) and 例外として (as an exception) are useful set expressions — 例外 pairs naturally with 原則 (principle) in contrasting "the rule" vs "the exception."

## 冷夏 (れいか)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a cold summer, cool summer (unusually low temperatures)

**Similar words:**
- 猛暑（もうしょ）— "extreme heat," the opposite phenomenon of an unusually hot summer.
- 冷害（れいがい）— "cold damage" to crops, often resulting from a 冷夏, a related but distinct agricultural consequence.
- 涼しい夏（すずしいなつ）— simply "a cool summer" in plain descriptive language, less technical/meteorological than 冷夏.

**Opposite words:**
- 猛暑（もうしょ）— extreme heat, scorching summer

**Example sentences:**
1. 今年は冷夏で、エアコンの売り上げが落ちた。— This year was a cold summer, so air conditioner sales dropped.
2. 冷夏の影響で米の収穫量が減った。— Due to the cold summer, rice harvests decreased.
3. 気象庁は冷夏になる可能性を発表した。— The Meteorological Agency announced the possibility of a cold summer.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 冷夏 is a specific meteorological/agricultural term referring to a summer season that's abnormally cool compared to average — it's commonly discussed in relation to crop yields (especially rice), not just casual weather chat.

## 冷酷 (れいこく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** cold-bloodedness, cruelty, heartlessness

**Similar words:**
- 残酷（ざんこく）— "cruel/brutal," often describes specific cruel acts or scenes (violence), while 冷酷 describes an emotionless, calculating personality trait.
- 非情（ひじょう）— "unfeeling/merciless," similar to 冷酷 but can also describe circumstances (non-human) as merciless, not just people.
- [冷淡](#冷淡-れいたん)（れいたん）— "cold-hearted/indifferent," milder than 冷酷 — 冷淡 is more about lack of warmth/interest, while 冷酷 implies active cruelty.

**Opposite words:**
- 温情（おんじょう）— warmth, compassion
- 慈悲（じひ）— mercy, compassion

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼の冷酷な態度に誰もが震え上がった。— Everyone trembled at his cold-blooded attitude.
2. その冷酷な決断は多くの人を傷つけた。— That cold-blooded decision hurt many people.
3. 冷酷な犯人が逮捕された。— The cold-blooded criminal was arrested.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 冷酷 implies deliberate, calculating cruelty or lack of mercy — stronger and more negative than 冷淡, which just means emotionally distant or indifferent without necessarily being cruel.

## 冷淡 (れいたん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** cold-hearted, indifferent by nature, unsympathetic

**Similar words:**
- [冷酷](#冷酷-れいこく)（れいこく）— "cold-blooded/cruel," stronger and implies active cruelty, whereas 冷淡 is more passive indifference or lack of warmth.
- 無関心（むかんしん）— "indifference/lack of interest," focuses on apathy toward a topic rather than coldness in personal relationships.
- [そっけない](vocab-24.md#そっけない) — more casual/colloquial word for "curt/blunt," describing a brusque manner in speech or behavior, less formal than 冷淡.

**Opposite words:**
- 親切（しんせつ）— kind, considerate
- 温かい（あたたかい）— warm (figuratively, warm-hearted)

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女は彼に冷淡な態度を取った。— She took a cold, indifferent attitude toward him.
2. 冷淡な返事にがっかりした。— I was disappointed by the indifferent reply.
3. 彼は人に対して冷淡すぎる。— He's too cold-hearted toward people.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 冷淡 describes an emotional distance or lack of warmth/sympathy in how someone treats others — it's often used for romantic or interpersonal contexts (冷淡な態度 toward a partner) rather than describing outright cruelty.

## 冷静 (れいせい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** calm, composed, level-headed

**Similar words:**
- [落ち着く](vocab-10.md#落ち着く-おちつく)（おちつく）— "to calm down/settle," a verb describing the process of becoming calm, whereas 冷静 describes a state or trait of composure.
- 平静（へいせい）— "tranquility/calmness," slightly more literary/formal, often used for describing an unshaken mental state during crisis.
- クール — casual loanword for being "cool-headed," often with a stylish/detached connotation rather than purely composed reasoning.

**Opposite words:**
- [興奮](vocab-19.md#興奮する-こうふん)（こうふん）— excitement, agitation
- [動揺](vocab-29.md#動揺-どうよう)（どうよう）— agitation, being flustered

2. 彼は冷静な判断力を持っている。— He has calm, level-headed judgment.
3. どうか冷静になってください。— Please calm down (be composed).

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 冷静 describes mental composure especially under pressure or stress — commonly used in phrases like 冷静に考える (think calmly) and 冷静さを失う (lose one's composure); it's a positive trait, unlike the negative coldness of 冷淡/冷酷.

## 冷蔵 (れいぞう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** cold storage, refrigeration

**Similar words:**
- [冷凍](vocab-34.md#冷凍-れいとう)（れいとう）— "freezing," storage below freezing point, a colder/more intense form of preservation than 冷蔵.
- 保冷（ほれい）— "keeping cool," often used for portable cooling (coolers, insulated bags) rather than fixed refrigeration units.
- クーリング — casual loanword "cooling," more general and less specific to food storage.

**Opposite words:**
- 常温（じょうおん）— room temperature (storage)

**Example sentences:**
1. この食品は冷蔵保存してください。— Please store this food under refrigeration.
2. 冷蔵庫で冷蔵しておいた野菜を使った。— I used vegetables that had been kept refrigerated in the fridge.
3. 冷蔵技術の進歩で食品の鮮度が保てるようになった。— Advances in refrigeration technology have made it possible to keep food fresh.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 冷蔵 (refrigerate, above freezing) is distinct from 冷凍 (freeze, below freezing) — mixing them up on food packaging instructions could literally lead to spoiled or improperly stored food.
