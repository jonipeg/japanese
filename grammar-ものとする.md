# ～ものとする {#ものとする}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ものとする

**Furigana form:** ～ものとする

**Meaning:** "It shall be deemed/stipulated that X" — a formal convention used to establish a rule, regulation, or official stipulation, common in legal and contractual language.

**Conjugation:**
- Verb-辞書形 + ものとする
- Example: 本契約は日本法に準拠するものとする (This contract shall be governed by Japanese law).

**Typical context:** Exclusively formal written register — legal documents, contracts, bylaws, official regulations. Never used in casual speech; sounds extremely stiff and bureaucratic outside of formal/legal writing.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **こととする** — "it is decided that X," used for formal decisions/resolutions (e.g., committee decisions) rather than binding legal stipulations — softer and more procedural than ものとする.
- **べきである** — "should be X," a normative judgment about what ought to happen, rather than an official stipulation of what shall be the rule.
- **とみなす** — "to deem/regard as X," used for classification or legal interpretation of a specific case, rather than establishing a general rule.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 本規約の変更は、会員への事前通知をもって有効とするものとする。
   (Changes to these terms shall be deemed effective upon prior notice to members.)
2. 会議は毎月第一月曜日に開催するものとする。
   (Meetings shall be held on the first Monday of every month.)
3. 損害が生じた場合、甲がその責任を負うものとする。
   (In the event damage occurs, Party A shall bear the responsibility.)
4. 本契約の有効期間は一年とし、自動更新されるものとする。
   (This contract's term shall be one year, and shall be automatically renewed.)
5. 未成年者が参加する場合は、保護者の同意を得るものとする。
   (When a minor participates, the consent of a guardian shall be obtained.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Students sometimes mistake this for a simple "decide to do X" (こととする) when it actually carries binding, rule-like force — closer to "shall" in formal English legal writing. Because it almost never appears outside legal/contractual contexts, if a sentence has a casual or everyday topic, ものとする is likely the wrong choice even if grammatically possible.
