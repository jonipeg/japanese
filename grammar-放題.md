# ～放題 {#放題}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～放題

**Furigana form:** ～ほうだい

**Meaning:** "As much as one wants, unrestrained" — attaches to a verb stem to indicate an action done without limit or restriction (e.g., 食べ放題, "all-you-can-eat"; 飲み放題, "all-you-can-drink").

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ます形 (verb ます-stem, stem only) + 放題
- Inferred from common usage since no example sentence is given in the source: 食べ放題 (eat as much as you want), 飲み放題 (drink as much as you want), 言いたい放題 (say whatever one wants, unrestrained), 伸び放題 (grown out of control/unchecked, e.g., of hair or grass)

**Typical context:** Casual-to-neutral spoken and written Japanese — extremely common in everyday contexts, especially restaurant/service advertising (食べ放題, 飲み放題) and informal complaints about something left unchecked (やりたい放題 can carry a negative, "doing whatever one pleases without regard for others" connotation). Not rude on its own, though when used to criticize someone's unchecked behavior (やりたい放題) it carries a disapproving tone.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **放題 vs まくる** — まくる (e.g., 食べまくる) emphasizes doing an action intensely/repeatedly ("eating like crazy"), while 放題 emphasizes the absence of a limit/restriction (unlimited quantity available), a subtly different nuance (manner-of-intensity vs. no-restriction).
- **かぎりに (を限りに)** — "as of X (a cutoff point)"; unrelated meaning — marks an ending point in time rather than unrestrained quantity.
- **次第 (しだい)** — "as soon as X / depending on X"; unrelated grammatically and semantically, only superficially similar as a noun-attaching suffix.

**Opposite grammar structures:** 制限付き ("with restrictions/limited") functions as a conceptual opposite, describing something bounded rather than unlimited.

**Example sentences (no example sentence given in source for this entry; first two below are standard usages, remaining are original):**
1. このレストランは2000円で食べ放題だ。
   (At this restaurant, you can eat all you want for 2000 yen.)
2. 彼はいつも言いたい放題で、周りの気持ちを考えない。
   (He always says whatever he wants, without considering others' feelings.)
3. 庭の草が伸び放題になっている。
   (The grass in the garden has grown completely unchecked.)
4. 今日は飲み放題のコースを予約した。
   (Today we booked an all-you-can-drink course.)
5. 子供におもちゃを買い放題にさせるのは良くないと思う。
   (I don't think it's good to let children buy as many toys as they want.)

A key nuance for learners: 放題 following a desire-form like たい (食べたい放題, 言いたい放題) often shifts toward a negative, "doing whatever one feels like, unchecked" connotation, whereas plain ます-stem + 放題 (食べ放題, 飲み放題) is neutral-to-positive and mainly used in commercial "all-you-can..." contexts.
