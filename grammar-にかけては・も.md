# ～にかけては/も {#にかけては・も}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～にかけて(は)/も

**Furigana form:** ～にかけて(は)/も

**Meaning:** "When it comes to X (one's area of skill/forte)" — praises someone's particular expertise or strength. The も variant means "even in terms of X" (adding another area where a quality holds, often negatively: "even in X, he's no good").

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + にかけては — 料理にかけては (when it comes to cooking)
- 名詞 + にかけても — スピードにかけても (even in terms of speed)

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal, often used when complimenting a particular skill; common in spoken praise and written profiles/introductions.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **については** — neutral "regarding X," lacks the "forte/strength" nuance.
- [**において**](grammar-において.md) — formal "in X (a field/situation)," more neutral, no implication of being especially skilled.
- **に関しては** — "as for/concerning X," neutral topic marker without the praise nuance.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though にかけては is itself often reserved for strengths, contrasting with phrases describing weaknesses like が苦手で.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は絵を描くことにかけては、クラスで一番だ。
   (When it comes to drawing, he's the best in the class.)
2. 営業成績にかけては、彼女の右に出る者はいない。
   (When it comes to sales performance, no one surpasses her.)
3. スピードにかけても正確さにかけても、彼は誰にも負けない。
   (Neither in speed nor in accuracy does he lose to anyone.)
4. この店はラーメンの味にかけては、町で評判だ。
   (This shop is famous in town when it comes to ramen flavor.)
5. 几帳面さにかけても、彼は社内で有名だ。
   (Even in terms of meticulousness, he's well known in the company.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** にかけては is almost always used for something positive/a point of pride; using it for a clearly negative trait sounds odd unless intentionally ironic. Learners sometimes confuse it with については, which is purely topical and carries no "specialty" nuance.
