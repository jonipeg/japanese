# ～ざるを得ない {#ざるを得ない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ざるを得ない

**Furigana form:** ～ざるをえない

**Meaning:** "Cannot help but do X / have no choice but to do X" — expresses that the speaker is forced into an action against their own will due to circumstances, often with a reluctant nuance.

**Conjugation:**
- Verb (ない form, dropping ない) + ざるを得ない
- 行く → 行かざるを得ない
- する → せざるを得ない (irregular)
- 会社の方針なので、従わざるを得ない (Since it's company policy, I have no choice but to comply)

**Typical context:** Formal written and spoken Japanese — business, news, essays; conveys a somewhat reluctant or resigned tone about an unavoidable action.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**しかない**](grammar-しかない.md) — "have no choice but to," more casual/general, less formal register.
- **なければならない** — "must do X," expresses obligation/necessity, but lacks the specific "reluctantly forced by circumstance" nuance.
- [**わけにはいかない**](grammar-わけにはいかない.md) — "can't very well not do X," focuses on social/moral constraints preventing an alternative.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though ～たくてもできない contrasts (wanting to do something but being unable to), as opposed to being forced to do something unwanted.

**Example sentences:**
1. 台風のため、イベントは中止せざるを得なかった。
   (Due to the typhoon, we had no choice but to cancel the event.)
2. 上司の命令なので、残業せざるを得ない。
   (Since it's the boss's order, I have no choice but to work overtime.)
3. 証拠がそろっている以上、彼の主張を認めざるを得ない。
   (Given that the evidence is all there, I have no choice but to accept his claim.)
4. 経営が厳しく、社員を減らさざるを得なかった。
   (With management in a difficult position, we had no choice but to reduce staff.)
5. 体調が悪化し、入院せざるを得なくなった。
   (My condition worsened, and I had no choice but to be hospitalized.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** する conjugates irregularly to せざるを得ない (not "しざるを得ない"). The phrase implies reluctance/lack of personal desire — distinct from ざるをえない's more neutral cousin しかない, which doesn't always carry the same "against my will" feeling.
