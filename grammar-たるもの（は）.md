# ～たるもの（は） {#たるもの（は）}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～たるもの（は）

**Furigana form:** ～たるもの（は）

**Meaning:** States that someone who holds a responsible or superior position/role ought to behave in a manner befitting that status — used to describe the ideal, expected conduct for such a person.

**Conjugation:**
- 名 + たるもの（は）
- 経営者たるものは — "anyone who is to be a manager"

**Typical context:** Formal written register — moralizing or prescriptive statements found in essays, speeches, and formal writing about the duties and conduct expected of people in particular social roles.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～として（当然）** — "as X, naturally..." — a more neutral, less formal way to state what's expected of someone in a given role.
- **～にふさわしく** — "in a manner befitting X" — closely related in meaning, often paired with the same kind of status nouns.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite structure; the sense is inherently prescriptive, so its negation would simply describe what such a person must *not* do.

**Example sentences:**
1. 経営者たるものは、一般的な法律や年金制度について知っておかなければならない。
   (Anyone who is to be a manager must have a working knowledge of general law and the pension system.)
2. 社会人たるもの、あいさつや時間を守ることなど、できて当然だろう。
   (As a member of society, being able to greet people properly and keep to schedules should go without saying.)
3. 紳士たるもの、強く優しくなければならない。
   (A true gentleman must be both strong and kind.)
4. 教師たるもの、常に生徒の手本となる行動を取るべきだ。
   (A teacher, as such, should always set an example for students through their behavior.)
5. リーダーたるものは、どんな困難な状況でも冷静さを失ってはならない。
   (A leader must never lose their composure, no matter how difficult the situation.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Attaches to nouns denoting a position of responsibility or excellence (経営者, 社会人, 紳士, 教師, リーダー, etc.), and the following clause states the ideal behavior expected of that status. It is more prescriptive/moralizing than ～ともあろう, which instead expresses surprise at someone *violating* that standard rather than laying out what they should do.
