# ～てからでないと／てからでなければ {#てからでないとてからでなければ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～てからでないと／てからでなければ

**Furigana form:** ～てからでないと／てからでなければ (no additional kanji readings needed)

**Meaning:** "Unless one does X first, not until after doing X" — states that Y cannot happen until X is completed first, framing X as a required precondition.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞て形 (verb, te-form) + からでないと／からでなければ + (negative-potential consequence)

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal, used in both spoken and written Japanese.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **ないと** — simple casual conditional "if not," lacking the explicit "precondition" framing.
- **前に** — "before," a simple temporal sequencing word without the strong "precondition" nuance.
- [**ないことには**](grammar-ないことには.md) — "unless," a more formal alternative with the same precondition logic.

**Opposite grammar structures:** ～さえすれば ("as long as X," a simple sufficient condition, contrasting in logical direction with the necessary condition expressed here).

**Example sentences:**
1. 医者に相談してからでないと、薬を変えることはできない。
   (You can't change the medication unless you consult a doctor first.)
2. 契約書にサインしてからでないと、作業を始められない。
   (We can't start the work unless the contract is signed first.)
3. 実際に使ってみてからでないと、良さが分からない。
   (You can't understand how good it is unless you actually try using it first.)
4. 上司の許可を得てからでなければ、出張の予定を決められない。
   (We can't decide on the business trip schedule without first getting the boss's approval.)
5. 準備運動をしてからでないと、怪我をする恐れがある。
   (Unless you do warm-up exercises first, there's a risk of injury.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern always pairs with a negative-potential consequence in the main clause (can't do Y). Learners often forget that からでないと requires a negative predicate following it — omitting that negative results in an ungrammatical sentence.
