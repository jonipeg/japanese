# ～を境に {#を境に}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～を境に

**Furigana form:** ～をさかいに

**Meaning:** "With X as the turning point/boundary" — marks a specific event or point in time as the dividing line between a before-state and an after-state, often indicating a notable change.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 (noun) + をさかいに
- Example from source: 喧嘩をさかいに (with the fight/argument as the turning point)

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal, usable in both written and spoken Japanese. No strong politeness/rudeness connotation; commonly used in narrative description of life changes, relationship shifts, or historical turning points. Slightly more formal/literary in tone than everyday casual speech, but not restricted to only written contexts.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **なみ (並み)** — means "on par with/equivalent to X," a level/standard comparison; this was a confirmed confusion pair on a graded test, where a student picked なみで instead of を境に. The two are unrelated — を境に marks a temporal dividing point, while なみ compares quality/standard.
- [**を機に**](grammar-を機に.md) — "taking X as an opportunity/trigger," emphasizing X as the motivating cause for a deliberate decision; を境に is more neutral and can describe any before/after division, including ones not driven by a conscious choice (e.g., natural events, external changes).
- **以来 (いらい)** — "ever since X," marking a starting point and continuing state afterward; close in spirit, but を境に emphasizes the contrast between before and after, while 以来 emphasizes the continuous span of time since X.
- **を皮切りに** — "starting with X (and continuing in succession)," used for a series of events kicking off from X; emphasizes a sequence beginning, not a before/after contrast.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. あの日の喧嘩をさかいに、彼女とは絶交状態になってしまった。
   (With the fight that day as the turning point, I ended up completely estranged from her.)
2. 祖父が亡くなったのをさかいに、家族の集まりがめっきり減った。
   (With my grandfather's passing as the turning point, family gatherings decreased sharply.)
3. 入社5年目をさかいに、仕事に対する考え方が大きく変わった。
   (With my fifth year at the company as the turning point, my way of thinking about work changed significantly.)
4. この事件をさかいに、会社の安全対策は一変した。
   (With this incident as the turning point, the company's safety measures changed completely.)
5. 結婚をさかいに、彼の生活リズムはすっかり変わった。
   (With his marriage as the turning point, his daily rhythm completely changed.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** On a graded test, a student picked なみで when the correct answer was を境に(さかいに) — the key distinction is that を境に marks a specific point in time dividing a clear before/after contrast, whereas なみ is a static comparison of level or standard with no temporal "turning point" sense. Students should check whether the sentence describes a change occurring at a specific moment (→を境に) rather than a comparison of quality (→なみ).
