## 冷蔵庫 (れいぞうこ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** refrigerator

**Similar words:**
- 冷凍庫（れいとうこ）— "freezer," the sub-compartment or separate appliance for freezing, colder than the 冷蔵庫 section.
- クーラーボックス — "cooler box," a portable insulated container, not a household appliance like 冷蔵庫.
- 保冷庫（ほれいこ）— a cooling storage unit, often smaller or portable, less common a word than 冷蔵庫.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 冷蔵庫に牛乳がまだ残っている。— There's still milk left in the refrigerator.
2. 新しい冷蔵庫を買おうか迷っている。— I'm thinking about whether to buy a new refrigerator.
3. 冷蔵庫のドアをちゃんと閉めてください。— Please close the refrigerator door properly.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 冷蔵庫 refers to the whole appliance (fridge), while the freezer compartment within it is 冷凍庫 — in Japan these are often one combined unit (冷凍冷蔵庫), so context clarifies which part is meant.

## 冷凍 (れいとう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** freezing, refrigeration (below freezing point)

**Similar words:**
- [冷蔵](vocab-33.md#冷蔵-れいぞう)（れいぞう）— "refrigeration," cooling above freezing point, milder than 冷凍's freezing temperatures.
- 急速冷凍（きゅうそくれいとう）— "flash freezing," a specific rapid-freezing technique, a more technical subtype of 冷凍.
- 凍結（とうけつ）— "freezing" in a broader sense (including freezing of water, assets, or relationships figuratively), less specific to food storage than 冷凍.

**Opposite words:**
- 解凍（かいとう）— thawing, defrosting

**Example sentences:**
1. 冷凍食品はとても便利だ。— Frozen food is very convenient.
2. 肉を冷凍しておけば長持ちする。— If you freeze the meat, it will last longer.
3. この魚は冷凍されて輸送されている。— This fish is transported frozen.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 冷凍 (freeze) and its opposite 解凍 (thaw/defrost) are a key pair to remember together — many learners know 冷凍 but forget 解凍 when describing defrosting frozen food.

## レインコート (れいんこーと)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** raincoat

**Similar words:**
- 雨具（あまぐ）— general "rain gear," a broader category including umbrellas and boots, not just a coat.
- カッパ — a more casual/colloquial word for raincoat, especially the simple pull-over kind, derived from Portuguese "capa."
- 傘（かさ）— umbrella, a different but related rain-protection item, not worn like a coat.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 雨が降りそうなのでレインコートを着た。— Since it looked like rain, I wore a raincoat.
2. 子供用のかわいいレインコートを買った。— I bought a cute raincoat for kids.
3. 自転車に乗るときはレインコートが便利だ。— A raincoat is handy when riding a bicycle.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** レインコート and カッパ are often used interchangeably in casual speech, but カッパ feels more old-fashioned/simple (think school rain ponchos), while レインコート suggests a more fashionable, tailored coat.

## 冷房 (れいぼう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** air-conditioning (cooling)

**Similar words:**
- エアコン — the general loanword for "air conditioner" (the appliance itself), covering both cooling and heating; 冷房 specifically refers to the cooling function/mode.
- 暖房（だんぼう）— "heating," the opposite function of the same appliance.
- [クーラー](vocab-16.md#クーラー) — an older/informal loanword specifically for a cooling-only unit, similar to 冷房 but refers to the device rather than the function.

**Opposite words:**
- 暖房（だんぼう）— heating

**Example sentences:**
1. 夏は冷房をつけないと暑くて眠れない。— In summer, if I don't turn on the air conditioning, it's too hot to sleep.
2. オフィスの冷房が効きすぎて寒い。— The office air conditioning is too strong, and it's cold.
3. 冷房病にならないよう気をつけている。— I'm careful not to get "air-conditioning sickness."

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 冷房 specifically refers to the cooling function (as opposed to 暖房, heating) of a climate-control system — エアコン refers to the machine itself that can do both, so 冷房をつける (turn on cooling) is more precise than エアコンをつける when specifically meaning "turn on the AC to cool."

## レース (れーす)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** race (competition); also lace (fabric, same spelling in katakana but different word)

**Similar words:**
- [競争](vocab-15.md#競争-きょうそう)（きょうそう）— "competition," broader term for any kind of competitive contest, not limited to literal races.
- マラソン — "marathon," a specific long-distance running race, a subtype of レース.
- 競走（きょうそう）— homophone of 競争 but specifically means "footrace," closely related to レース in the racing sense.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼はカーレースで優勝した。— He won the car race.
2. 今日はマラソンレースに参加する。— Today I'm participating in a marathon race.
3. このドレスには美しいレースが使われている。— Beautiful lace is used on this dress.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** レース is a homonym in Japanese — it can mean "race" (competition, from English "race") or "lace" (fabric trim, from English "lace"); context (cars/sports vs. clothing/fabric) is essential to distinguish the two unrelated meanings.

## レギュラー (れぎゅらー)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** regular (player on a team), regular member/status

**Similar words:**
- [正式](vocab-22.md#正式-せいしき)（せいしき）— "official/formal," describes official status broadly, not limited to sports team membership.
- [メンバー](#メンバー-めんばー) — "member," more general term for belonging to a group, without implying "starting/regular" status specifically.
- 控え（ひかえ）— "substitute/reserve," the direct opposite within a sports team context — someone not a regular starter.

**Opposite words:**

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** In sports contexts, レギュラー specifically means a "starting/regular" player (as opposed to 控え, a bench player) — it's also used outside sports (e.g. レギュラーコーヒー, regular-size/standard coffee), so the meaning shifts depending on context.

## レクリエーション (れくりえーしょん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** recreation (leisure activity, often organized/group-based)

**Similar words:**
- 娯楽（ごらく）— "entertainment/amusement," broader and more native-feeling term, covering passive entertainment too (movies, games), not just organized group activities.
- [レジャー](vocab-02.md#レジャー) — "leisure," broader concept of free-time activities generally, while レクリエーション often implies organized, purposeful group activities (e.g. at schools, workplaces, elderly care facilities).
- 気分転換（きぶんてんかん）— "change of pace/refreshing one's mood," a more personal, individual concept than group-oriented レクリエーション.

**Opposite words:**
- [労働](vocab-36.md#労働-ろうどう)（ろうどう）— labor, work

**Example sentences:**
1. 老人ホームでレクリエーション活動が行われている。— Recreational activities are held at the elderly care home.
2. 会社の社員旅行でレクリエーションを楽しんだ。— We enjoyed recreational activities during the company employee trip.
3. 学校行事としてレクリエーションの時間が設けられた。— Time for recreation was set aside as a school event.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** レクリエーション in Japanese often specifically implies organized group activities (games, team-building) used in schools, companies, or care facilities — it's narrower than the general English sense of "recreation" as any leisure activity.

## レコーダー (れこーだー)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** recorder (device for recording audio/video)

**Similar words:**
- ビデオデッキ — "video deck/VCR," an older specific type of recording device, narrower than general レコーダー.
- 録音機（ろくおんき）— native-Japanese compound word for "audio recorder," functionally similar to レコーダー but with a more technical/formal feel.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** レコーダー is a broad term covering various recording devices (video recorders, voice/IC recorders) — the specific type is often clarified with a prefix like ビデオレコーダー or ICレコーダー.

## レジスター (れじすたー)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** cash register

**Similar words:**

- [会計](vocab-12.md#会計-かいけい)（かいけい）— "checkout/billing," refers to the act of paying/settling the bill, not the physical machine itself.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. レジスターでお会計をお願いします。— Please settle the bill at the cash register.
2. 新しいレジスターを店に導入した。— We introduced a new cash register at the store.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** In daily conversation, Japanese speakers almost always shorten レジスター to レジ (e.g. レジに並ぶ, line up at the register) — using the full word レジスター sounds slightly stiff/technical in casual contexts.

## レジャー (れじゃー)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** leisure

**Similar words:**
- 娯楽（ごらく）— "entertainment/amusement," a native-Japanese near-equivalent, sometimes used interchangeably but with a slightly more old-fashioned or official tone.
- 休暇（きゅうか）— "vacation/time off," refers to the time away from work itself rather than the activities done during it.
- [レクリエーション](#レクリエーション-れくりえーしょん) — "recreation," often implies organized or purposeful group activities, whereas レジャー is broader and more personal/individual free-time use.

1. 週末はレジャーを楽しむ人が多い。— Many people enjoy leisure activities on weekends.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** レジャー is commonly used in compounds like レジャー施設 (leisure facility) and レジャー産業 (leisure industry) — it leans toward commercial/recreational contexts (theme parks, resorts) rather than simply "relaxing at home."

## 列 (れつ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a row, line, rank, queue

**Similar words:**
- [行列](vocab-16.md#行列-ぎょうれつ)（ぎょうれつ）— "queue/procession," specifically a line of people waiting, a common compound built from 列.
- 並び（ならび）— "a lineup/row," more casual, often describing an arrangement of objects rather than people waiting.
- 系列（けいれつ）— "series/affiliated group" (e.g. corporate keiretsu), a figurative extension of 列 referring to organizational grouping rather than a physical line.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. チケットを買うために長い列に並んだ。— I lined up in a long queue to buy tickets.
2. 生徒たちは列を作って教室に入った。— The students formed a line and entered the classroom.
3. 彼は前から3列目に座っている。— He is sitting in the third row from the front.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 列 alone can mean a line of people/objects, but it's most frequently seen in compounds like 行列 (queue), 列車 (train), and 列島 (archipelago) — as a standalone word it's used for describing rows/ranks (座席の列, rows of seats).

## レストラン (れすとらん)

#to-review

**Meaning:** restaurant

## 列車 (れっしゃ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a train

**Similar words:**
- 電車（でんしゃ）— "electric train," commonly used for local/commuter trains; 列車 is broader and more formal, often used for long-distance or less common train contexts.
- 汽車（きしゃ）— older term specifically for steam-powered trains, now mostly historical or regional usage.
- [新幹線](vocab-21.md#新幹線-しんかんせん)（しんかんせん）— "bullet train/Shinkansen," a specific high-speed train type, a subtype that could also be called 列車.

- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 次の列車は十分後に出発します。— The next train departs in ten minutes.
2. 夜行列車に乗って旅行した。— I traveled by taking an overnight train.
3. 列車が遅延しているとアナウンスがあった。— There was an announcement that the train was delayed.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 列車 is a more formal/written word often used in announcements, schedules, and literature, while 電車 is the everyday word for the commuter trains most people ride — 列車 can also cover freight trains and long-distance trains that 電車 wouldn't typically describe.

## 列島 (れっとう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** archipelago, chain of islands

**Similar words:**
- 諸島（しょとう）— "islands/group of islands," often used for naming a specific group (例: ハワイ諸島), slightly different usage pattern from 列島.
- 島国（しまぐに）— "island nation," refers to a country made up of islands, a political/national concept rather than the geographic chain itself.
- 群島（ぐんとう）— "island cluster," similar to 諸島, emphasizing a cluster/group rather than a long chain shape like 列島.

**Opposite words:**
- [大陸](vocab-25.md#大陸-たいりく)（たいりく）— continent

**Example sentences:**
1. 日本列島は南北に細長い。— The Japanese archipelago is long and narrow from north to south.

3. この列島には多様な生態系が存在する。— This chain of islands has diverse ecosystems.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 列島 specifically emphasizes the elongated, chain-like shape of a group of islands (as in 日本列島, the Japanese archipelago) — it's commonly used in weather reports and geography, distinct from 諸島/群島 which don't carry the same "chain" connotation.

## レバー (ればー)
#vocabulary
#to-review

## 連休 (れんきゅう)

2. 魚油にはいい成分が含まれている。— Fish oil contains good nutrients.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The kanji 油 has two readings: あぶら (standalone, everyday "oil," like cooking oil) and ゆ (used in compounds like 石油, 原油, 魚油)—learners should recognize that compound words typically shift to the ゆ reading rather than あぶら.

## 唯一 (ゆいいつ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** only, sole, unique

**Similar words:**
- 唯一無二（ゆいいつむに）— emphatic four-character idiom, "one and only, unique," stronger than 唯一 alone.
- [独自](vocab-29.md#独自-どくじ)（どくじ）— "original, unique (to oneself)," emphasizes distinctiveness rather than exclusivity/sole existence.
- 一つだけ（ひとつだけ）— casual equivalent, "just one," less formal than 唯一.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は私の唯一の友人だ。— He is my only friend.
2. それが唯一の解決策だった。— That was the only solution.
3. 唯一無二の存在になりたい。— I want to become a one-of-a-kind presence.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 唯一 is formal/written register meaning "the only one (of its kind)"; it's often paired with の to modify a noun (唯一の友人), and the four-character idiom 唯一無二 intensifies the meaning to "utterly unique, irreplaceable."

## 優 (ゆう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** grade A, excellent (academic grading); gentleness (as a prefix)

**Similar words:**
- [優秀](vocab-35.md#優秀-ゆうしゅう)（ゆうしゅう）— "excellent, outstanding," describes overall quality/ability rather than a specific grade.
- A（えー）— the letter grade itself, often used interchangeably with 優 in Japanese universities.
- 良（りょう）— "good," the grade below 優 in the traditional 優・良・可・不可 grading scale.

**Opposite words:**
- [不可](vocab-32.md#不可-ふか)（ふか）— failing grade

**Example sentences:**
1. そのテストで優をもらった。— I got an A (優) on that test.
2. 彼女はすべての科目で優を取った。— She got top grades (優) in every subject.
3. 大学の成績表に優の文字が並んでいる。— The university transcript is lined with "優" grades.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 優 as a grading term is specific to the traditional Japanese university grading scale (優・良・可・不可, roughly A/B/C/F); it's distinct from 優 used as a prefix in words like 優しい (gentle) or 優れる (to excel)—same kanji, different registers and meanings.

## 優位 (ゆうい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** predominance, superiority, advantageous position

**Similar words:**
- 優勢（ゆうせい）— "superiority, predominance," very close synonym, often interchangeable, slightly more about momentum/current advantage in a contest.
- [有利](vocab-35.md#有利-ゆうり)（ゆうり）— "advantageous, favorable," broader, describes favorable conditions rather than a ranked position over others.
- 優越（ゆうえつ）— "superiority," can carry a stronger psychological/social hierarchy connotation (優越感 = superiority complex).

**Opposite words:**
- 劣位（れつい）— inferior position

**Example sentences:**
1. 交渉で優位に立った。— We gained the upper hand in the negotiation.
2. 技術力で他社より優位だ。— We have an advantage over other companies in technology.
3. 試合は終始優位に進んだ。— The match proceeded with us in a dominant position throughout.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 優位 and 優勢 are nearly synonymous and often interchangeable in describing an advantageous position; 優位に立つ (to gain the upper hand) is a common fixed collocation worth memorizing as a set phrase.

## 憂鬱/憂うつ (ゆううつ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** depression, gloom, melancholy

**Similar words:**
- [鬱々](vocab-01.md#鬱々-うつうつ)（うつうつ）— more literary, emphasizes a lingering, stagnant gloomy state; 憂鬱 is the more common everyday word.
- [落ち込む](vocab-10.md#落ち込む-おちこむ)（おちこむ）— "to feel down," a verb for a more transient emotional dip, event-triggered.
- 気分が沈む（きぶんがしずむ）— "to feel low/down," a descriptive phrase rather than a single adjective/noun.

**Opposite words:**
- 爽快（そうかい）— refreshing, exhilarating

**Example sentences:**
1. 月曜日の朝は憂鬱だ。— Monday mornings are depressing.
2. 彼女は最近、憂鬱な気分が続いている。— She's been feeling melancholic lately.
3. 試験のことを考えると憂鬱になる。— Thinking about the exam makes me feel gloomy.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 憂鬱 is both a noun and na-adjective (憂鬱だ, 憂鬱な気分) and is the most common everyday word for feeling depressed/gloomy about a specific situation (like Monday mornings or exams), distinct from clinical depression (うつ病), which is a medical diagnosis.

## 有益 (ゆうえき)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** helpful, useful, beneficial, instructive

**Similar words:**
- 役立つ（やくだつ）— "to be useful/helpful," a verb form, more casual than the na-adjective 有益.
- 有用（ゆうよう）— close synonym "useful, practical," slightly more technical/formal register.
- ためになる — casual equivalent, "beneficial, instructive," common in spoken Japanese.

**Opposite words:**
- 無益（むえき）— useless, unprofitable

**Example sentences:**
1. この講演はとても有益だった。— This lecture was very informative.
2. 有益な情報をありがとうございます。— Thank you for the useful information.
3. 読書は有益な趣味だ。— Reading is a beneficial hobby.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 有益 is formal/written register, common in business and academic contexts (有益な情報, 有益な意見); in casual speech, 役に立つ or ためになる sound more natural, so 有益 might feel slightly stiff in everyday conversation.

## 遊園地 (ゆうえんち)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** an amusement park

**Similar words:**
- テーマパーク — "theme park," often implies a specific themed concept (like Disneyland), a more modern/specific type of 遊園地.
- 動物園（どうぶつえん）— "zoo," a different type of recreational facility, not focused on rides.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 子供たちと遊園地に行った。— I went to the amusement park with the kids.
2. 遊園地には絶叫マシンがたくさんある。— The amusement park has many thrill rides.
3. 夏休みに遊園地で一日中遊んだ。— We played all day at the amusement park during summer vacation.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 遊園地 is the general term for any amusement park with rides, while テーマパーク specifically implies a themed park with a cohesive concept (like Disneyland or Universal Studios)—the two overlap but aren't perfectly synonymous.
