# ～べく {#べく}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～べく

**Furigana form:** ～べく

**Meaning:** "In order to" — expresses that an action is performed with a particular goal in mind; a literary way of indicating purpose. Written-language style.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形 + べく（例外：する → するべく・すべく）
- サッカー選手になるべく — "in order to become a soccer player"

**Typical context:** Formal written Japanese — newspaper articles, biographies, business writing, and formal narration; rarely used in casual conversation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～ために** — the basic, neutral way to express purpose, usable in both speech and writing, much more common in everyday Japanese.
- **～んがため（に）** — a more archaic/literary purpose expression stressing a strong, driving goal, even more formal than べく.
- **～べし** — the classical predicate form べく derives from, meaning "should/must," seen in set phrases and written admonitions.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a result-expressing structure like ～た結果 ("as a result of X") would instead describe an outcome rather than a purpose.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼はサッカー選手になるべく、毎日厳しい練習をしている。
   (In order to become a soccer player, he trains rigorously every day.)
2. 新型の機械を購入するべく、社長はいろいろ調べている。
   (In order to purchase the new model of machine, the president is researching various options.)
3. 介護ロボットを開発すべく、わたしたちは今日も実験を続ける。
   (In order to develop a care-giving robot, we continue our experiments today as well.)
4. 事故の原因を明らかにすべく、専門家による調査が行われた。
   (In order to clarify the cause of the accident, an investigation by experts was carried out.)
5. 地域の活性化を図るべく、市は新しい計画を発表した。
   (In order to revitalize the region, the city announced a new plan.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** べく attaches only to intentional (volitional) verbs — it cannot express an involuntary purpose. The clause that follows must also describe a deliberate, willed action, not something done to or caused by someone else, and the subject of both clauses must be the same person.
