# ～をよそに {#をよそに}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～を余所に

**Furigana form:** ～をよそに

**Meaning:** "Ignoring X / without concern for X" — describes proceeding with an action while disregarding a relevant concern, warning, or others' feelings/expectations.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + をよそに
- Example base: 周囲の心配 → 周囲の心配をよそに

**Typical context:** Formal, written register; often carries a critical nuance, implying the subject is being inconsiderate, reckless, or selfishly focused despite a reasonable concern that should matter. Common in news writing and formal narrative.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **をものともせず（に）** — "undaunted by X"; similarly describes disregarding an obstacle, but をものともせず carries a more admiring, "brave/undeterred" connotation (overcoming a hindrance), while をよそに carries a more critical, "selfishly ignoring others' concern" connotation.
- **も顧みず** — "without regard for X"; close in meaning and also often critical, used for disregarding danger, responsibility, or others' feelings, largely overlapping with をよそに.
- [**にもかかわらず**](grammar-にかかわらず・にもかかわらず.md) — "despite X"; a more neutral general concessive, lacking をよそに's implication that the disregarded thing was a legitimate concern that should have mattered to the subject.

**Opposite grammar structures:** ～を気遣って／～に配慮して ("being considerate of X / showing consideration for X") expresses the reverse — properly heeding a concern rather than ignoring it.

**Example sentences:**
1. 周囲の反対をよそに、彼は海外移住を決めた。 (Ignoring the opposition from those around him, he decided to emigrate abroad.)
2. 住民の不安をよそに、工事は予定通り進められた。 (Disregarding the residents' anxieties, the construction proceeded as scheduled.)
3. 家族の心配をよそに、彼女は危険な登山に挑戦し続けた。 (Ignoring her family's worry, she kept challenging herself with dangerous mountain climbs.)
4. 世間の批判をよそに、その会社は方針を変えなかった。 (Ignoring public criticism, the company did not change its policy.)
5. 周囲の制止をよそに、少年は川へ飛び込んでいった。 (Ignoring the attempts of those around him to stop him, the boy jumped into the river.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** をよそに almost always carries an implicit criticism of the subject for disregarding something that reasonably deserved attention (others' worry, warnings, public opinion) — it should not be used for neutral, consequence-free indifference. Learners should distinguish it from をものともせず, which casts disregarding an obstacle in a more positive, determined light, whereas をよそに usually casts the disregard as careless or selfish.
