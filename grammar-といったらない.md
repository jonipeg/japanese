# ～といったらない {#といったらない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～といったらない

**Furigana form:** ～といったらない

**Meaning:** Expresses that something is to such an extreme degree that words fail to capture it — "indescribably ~," "~ beyond words." Spoken language, used for strong emotional reactions.

**Conjugation:**
- イ形い／ナ形(だ)／名 + といったらない
- だらしないといったらない — "sloppy beyond words"

**Typical context:** Spoken, casual-to-neutral register — used in everyday conversation and anecdotes to emphasize an extreme reaction, whether negative (annoyance, exasperation) or positive (amazement, admiration).

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～の極み（きわみ）** — also expresses an extreme degree, but formal/written rather than spoken, and lacks the emotional "words fail me" nuance.
- **～てしょうがない／～てたまらない** — express a strong feeling the speaker can't help having, but attach to feeling-verbs/adjectives rather than describing the degree of a situation itself.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a phrase like ～というほどでもない ("not really to the extent of ~") would instead downplay the degree of something.

**Example sentences:**
1. あいつはだらしないといったらない。物はよくなくすし、時間にルーズだし……。
   (That guy is sloppy beyond words — he's always losing things, and he's never on time...)
2. あのレストランの料理のおいしいことといったらなかった。今でも忘れられない。
   (The food at that restaurant was indescribably delicious. I still can't forget it.)
3. 富士山の頂上から見た景色の素晴らしさといったら……。いつかきっとまた行きたい。
   (The view from the top of Mt. Fuji was beyond words... I definitely want to go again someday.)
4. 彼の話の長さといったらない。いつも会議が長引く。
   (His talks are endlessly long — meetings always run over because of him.)
5. 試験に落ちたときの悔しさといったらなかった。
   (The frustration I felt when I failed the exam was beyond words.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern expresses the speaker's surprise at how extreme something is — it's not a neutral description. With nouns, the noun is usually a nominalized adjective formed by adding さ (素晴らしさ・だらしなさ・悔しさ), not a plain noun. In casual speech, the final ない is sometimes dropped, trailing off into silence (as in example 3) to let the extremity speak for itself.
