# ～にあって {#にあって}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**1. Kanji version:** ～にあって

**2. Furigana version:** ～にあって

**3. Meaning:** In the midst of / under the circumstances of X — typically an unusual, difficult, or noteworthy situation.

**4. Conjugation (connection form):** 名詞 + にあって

**5. Usage context:** Written, formal/literary register. Used to describe serious, dramatic, or noteworthy circumstances; not used in casual speech.

**6. Similar grammar structures:**
- において — a more neutral general marker of location, time, or field, lacking にあって's nuance of "amid difficult/notable circumstances."
- にしても — "even granting X," a concessive structure, different in function from にあって's circumstantial-setting role.
- のままに — confirmed confusion (student wrongly picked のままに on Q11); unrelated in meaning, as のままに describes passively following along with something, while にあって sets the stage/circumstance for the main clause.

**7. Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**8. Example sentences:**
1. 水も食糧も不足した状況にあって、もっとも大事なのはお互いに助け合うことだ。(In the midst of a situation where both water and food are scarce, the most important thing is to help one another.)
2. 厳しい経済状況にあって、多くの企業がコスト削減を迫られている。(Amid harsh economic conditions, many companies are being forced to cut costs.)
3. この非常事態にあって、冷静さを保つことが何より求められる。(In the midst of this emergency, staying calm is required above all else.)
4. 戦争という極限状態にあって、人々は互いに支え合って生き延びた。(Amid the extreme circumstances of war, people survived by supporting one another.)
5. 情報が錯綜する現代社会にあって、正しい判断力がますます重要になっている。(In the midst of today's society where information is chaotic, sound judgment is becoming ever more important.)

**9. Common mistakes / key nuances:** Confirmed student confusion with のままに on Q11 — にあって marks a circumstantial setting ("in the midst of X, [something happens/is needed]"), while のままに describes passively following along with something ("as it is / in accordance with"). Treat these as unrelated in meaning despite surface similarity in multiple-choice options.
