# ～となると {#となると}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**1. Kanji version:** ～となると

**2. Furigana version:** ～となると

**3. Meaning:** When it comes to X / if it's truly the case that X, then... — draws a serious conclusion or implication from a hypothetical or newly-revealed situation.

**4. Conjugation (connection form):** 動詞・い形・な形・名詞の普通形 + となると

**5. Usage context:** Written and spoken, neutral-to-formal register.

**6. Similar grammar structures:**
- となれば — very similar, slightly more hypothetical/conditional in nuance.
- ともなると — more emphatic, meaning "once it reaches the level of X..." (e.g. 社長ともなると "once you become company president...").
- となって — a DIFFERENT, unrelated form: simply the te-form/connective form of となる, meaning "becomes X, and then..." Confirmed recurring student confusion (Q17, and noted again in later scan notes as a repeated error pattern) — students mistake the conditional discourse marker となると for the plain connective となって.

**7. Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**8. Example sentences:**
1. 銀行に融資を断られたとなると、この会社もそろそろ危ないな。(If it's really come to the point where the bank has refused to lend to them, this company must be in trouble soon.)
2. 彼が本当に辞めるとなると、プロジェクトの進行に大きな影響が出るだろう。(If he really is going to quit, it will have a major impact on the project's progress.)
3. 台風が直撃するとなると、イベントは中止せざるを得ない。(If the typhoon is really going to hit directly, the event will have to be canceled.)
4. 留学するとなると、準備することがたくさんある。(When it comes to actually studying abroad, there's a lot to prepare.)
5. 両親の介護をするとなると、仕事を辞めることも考えなければならない。(If it comes to caring for my parents, I'll have to consider quitting my job too.)

**9. Common mistakes / key nuances:** Confirmed recurring student error (Q17, and flagged again independently in later notes as "pattern already seen"): confusing となると with となって. Remember となると introduces a conditional/inferential clause ("if it truly comes to this, then..."), while となって is just the plain connective form of となる ("turns into X, and then...") with no conditional-inference meaning.
