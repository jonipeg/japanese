## 長方形 (ちょうほうけい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a rectangle

**Similar words:**
- [正方形](vocab-22.md#正方形-せいほうけい)（せいほうけい）— "a square," a special case of rectangle with equal sides.
- 四角形（しかくけい）— "a quadrilateral," the broader category including both squares and rectangles.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 長方形の机を部屋に置いた。— I placed a rectangular desk in the room.
2. この土地は長方形をしている。— This plot of land is rectangular in shape.
3. 長方形の面積は縦×横で求められる。— The area of a rectangle is found by length times width.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** A purely geometric term with a fixed meaning — don't confuse 長方形 (rectangle, unequal sides) with 正方形 (square, equal sides); the kanji 長 (long) is the distinguishing clue.

## 調味料 (ちょうみりょう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a seasoning, condiment

**Similar words:**
- スパイス（スパイス）— "spice," a narrower category of seasoning, usually dried/powdered.
- 薬味（やくみ）— "garnish/accent seasoning" (like grated ginger or green onion), used fresh alongside food rather than cooked in.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. この料理には様々な調味料が使われている。— Various seasonings are used in this dish.
2. 調味料を入れすぎて味が濃くなった。— I added too much seasoning, and the flavor became strong.
3. 醤油や味噌は日本の代表的な調味料だ。— Soy sauce and miso are representative Japanese seasonings.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 調味料 is the general category covering all seasoning agents (salt, soy sauce, sugar, etc.), broader than スパイス which usually refers to aromatic spices specifically.

## 調理 (ちょうり)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** cooking, cookery; to cook/prepare food

**Similar words:**
- 料理（りょうり）— "cooking/cuisine," the more common everyday term, can refer to the dish itself as well as the act of cooking.
- 調理師（ちょうりし）— "professional cook/chef," the qualified title derived from 調理.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 新鮮な魚を調理する。— I prepare/cook the fresh fish.
2. 調理師免許を取得した。— I obtained a professional cooking license.
3. この調理法は簡単だ。— This cooking method is simple.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 調理 is more formal/technical than 料理 and often used in professional or instructional contexts (調理師, 調理法, 調理器具) — 料理 is the word to use for everyday "cooking dinner."

## 調和 (ちょうわ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** harmony, to be in harmony (with), to match

**Similar words:**
- [バランス](vocab-02.md#バランス)（バランス）— loanword "balance," more about equilibrium between elements, while 調和 emphasizes pleasing coexistence/matching.
- [一致](vocab-07.md#一致-いっち)（いっち）— "agreement/coincidence," about matching exactly, more precise/narrow than the softer, aesthetic 調和.

**Opposite words:**
- 不調和（ふちょうわ）— disharmony

**Example sentences:**
1. 自然との調和を大切にした建築だ。— It's architecture that values harmony with nature.

3. 彼らは調和を保ちながら仕事を進めた。— They proceeded with their work while maintaining harmony.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 調和 often describes an aesthetic or relational harmony (colors, nature, team dynamics) rather than exact mathematical/logical agreement — don't confuse with 一致 which implies precise matching.

**Similar words:**

## 直後 (ちょくご)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** immediately after

**Similar words:**
- すぐ後（すぐあと）— casual equivalent "right after," less formal than 直後.
- 間もなく（まもなく）— "soon, shortly," about the near future rather than right after a specific event.

**Opposite words:**
- [直前](#直前-ちょくぜん)（ちょくぜん）— immediately before

**Example sentences:**
1. 事故の直後、救急車が到着した。— Immediately after the accident, an ambulance arrived.
2. 試合終了の直後にインタビューが行われた。— The interview took place immediately after the match ended.
3. 彼は帰国した直後に体調を崩した。— He fell ill right after returning to his country.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 直後 always attaches to a noun/verb with の or た-form + 直後 (事故の直後, 到着した直後) — it can't stand alone as a sentence-final word.

#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** direct, immediate; directly, in person

**Similar words:**
- [直に](vocab-19.md#直に-じかに)（じかに）— casual synonym "directly/firsthand," slightly more colloquial than 直接.

- [間接](vocab-13.md#間接-かんせつ)（かんせつ）— indirect

#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** just before

**Similar words:**
- [寸前](vocab-01.md#寸前-すんぜん)（すんぜん）— "on the verge of, just before," often used for a critical/dramatic moment right before something happens.

## ちょくちょく (ちょくちょく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** occasionally, often, now and then

**Similar words:**
- たまに（たまに）— "occasionally/once in a while," similar frequency but slightly less frequent-sounding than ちょくちょく.
- よく（よく）— "often," more neutral/common, without the casual, chatty tone of ちょくちょく.

**Opposite words:**
- めったに〜ない（めったに〜ない）— rarely

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼はちょくちょくこの店に来る。— He comes to this shop fairly often.
2. 最近ちょくちょく頭痛がする。— I've been getting headaches now and then recently.
3. ちょくちょく連絡を取り合っている。— We keep in touch with each other now and then.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ちょくちょく is casual/colloquial and conveys a friendly, informal tone — inappropriate in formal writing or business contexts, where 頻繁に or 時々 would be preferred.

## 直通 (ちょくつう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** direct, through (e.g., a train or phone line)

**Similar words:**
- 直行（ちょっこう）— "nonstop/direct (travel)," used for flights/trains that go straight to a destination without stopping, close in meaning to 直通.
- ダイレクト（ダイレクト）— loanword "direct," used more in marketing contexts (direct mail, etc.).

**Opposite words:**
- 乗り換え（のりかえ）— transfer, changing trains

**Example sentences:**
1. この電車は空港まで直通です。— This train goes directly to the airport (no transfer needed).
2. 社長室への直通電話がある。— There's a direct phone line to the president's office.
3. 直通バスを利用すれば早く着く。— If you use the direct bus, you'll arrive quickly.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 直通 specifically emphasizes "no transfer/connection needed" for transport or communication lines — distinct from 直行, which emphasizes "non-stop" travel (not making intermediate stops) more than skipping transfers.

## 直面 (ちょくめん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to face, be confronted with (a difficult situation)

**Similar words:**
- 向き合う（むきあう）— "to face/confront," more personal and often used for facing a person or one's own feelings.
- 直視する（ちょくしする）— "to look directly at/face up to," emphasizes not avoiding a harsh reality.

**Opposite words:**
- [回避する](vocab-03.md#回避する-かいひする)（かいひする）— to avoid, evade

**Example sentences:**
1. 会社は深刻な経営危機に直面している。— The company is facing a serious management crisis.

3. 困難に直面しても諦めなかった。— Even when faced with difficulties, I didn't give up.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 直面する is almost always used for facing a serious, often negative situation (crisis, difficulty, problem) — not used for casually "facing" an object or direction (use 向く or 面する instead).

## 著者 (ちょしゃ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** the author

**Similar words:**
- 作者（さくしゃ）— "creator/author," broader and can apply to any creative work (novels, art, music), while 著者 is specific to written works (books).
- 筆者（ひっしゃ）— "the writer (of this piece)," often used self-referentially within an essay or article.

**Opposite words:**
- [読者](vocab-29.md#読者-どくしゃ)（どくしゃ）— the reader

**Example sentences:**
1. この本の著者について調べた。— I looked into the author of this book.
2. 著者からのメッセージが巻末に書かれていた。— A message from the author was written at the end of the book.
3. 著者は長年この分野を研究してきた。— The author has researched this field for many years.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 著者 is specifically for books/written publications — for a general "creator" (e.g., of a film, song, artwork) 作者 is more appropriate; 筆者 is often used reflexively in essays to mean "I, the writer."

## 著書 (ちょしょ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a book, work (that one has authored)

**Similar words:**
- 作品（さくひん）— "a work" in the broad creative sense (could be art, film, novel), while 著書 specifically means a written book one has authored.
- 本（ほん）— plain, general word for "book," without the "authored by" nuance.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼には多くの著書がある。— He has written many books.
2. 最新の著書が話題になっている。— His latest book is generating buzz.
3. この著書は専門家の間で評価が高い。— This book is highly regarded among experts.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 著書 specifically means "a book someone has written/authored," so it's often used with a possessive (彼の著書) — don't use it generically to just mean "a book you're reading" (use 本 for that).

## 直径 (ちょっけい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a diameter

**Similar words:**
- 半径（はんけい）— "radius," exactly half the diameter, often mentioned alongside 直径 in geometry.
- 円周（えんしゅう）— "circumference," the distance around a circle, a related but distinct measurement.

## 散らかす (ちらかす)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to scatter things about, to make a mess, to litter

**Similar words:**
- [散らす](#散らす-ちらす)（ちらす）— more general "to scatter/spread" (petals, enemies, attention); 散らかす is specifically about making a physical space messy.
- [汚す](vocab-36.md#汚す-よごす)（よごす）— to dirty/soil something; focuses on dirt/stains rather than disorganized clutter.
- ばらまく — to scatter things loosely/carelessly (e.g. papers), often with a nuance of spreading widely.

**Opposite words:**
- 片付ける（かたづける）— to tidy up, put away
- 整理する（せいりする）— to organize, sort out

**Example sentences:**
1. 子供が部屋中におもちゃを散らかした。— The child scattered toys all over the room.
2. そんなに書類を散らかさないでください。— Please don't make such a mess with the documents.
3. 彼は机の上をいつも散らかしている。— He always keeps his desk messy.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This is the transitive verb (someone messes something up); 散らかる is the intransitive counterpart (something becomes messy on its own). Learners often mix up which one takes を vs が — 部屋を散らかす (I mess up the room) vs 部屋が散らかる (the room becomes messy).

## 散らかる (ちらかる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to be scattered about, to be in disorder, to be messy

**Similar words:**
- [散らかす](#散らかす-ちらかす)（ちらかす）— the transitive pair: "to make messy" (someone causes it) vs 散らかる "to become messy" (it happens/is in that state).
- 乱れる（みだれる）— to become disordered/disheveled, often used more broadly (hair, schedule, social order) not just physical mess.
- ごちゃごちゃする — mimetic expression for being cluttered/jumbled, more casual/colloquial.

**Opposite words:**
- 片付く（かたづく）— to get tidied up
- [整う](vocab-30.md#整う-ととのう)（ととのう）— to be put in order, arranged

**Example sentences:**
1. 引っ越しの後、部屋がひどく散らかっている。— After the move, the room is terribly messy.
2. 机の上が散らかっていて、何がどこにあるか分からない。— The desk is so cluttered I don't know where anything is.
3. 風で公園に落ち葉が散らかっている。— Fallen leaves are scattered around the park because of the wind.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Intransitive — no direct object. Students sometimes write 部屋を散らかる, which is wrong; the correct pattern is 部屋が散らかる (the room becomes/is messy).

## 散らす (ちらす)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to scatter, to spread, to distract

**Similar words:**
- [散らかす](#散らかす-ちらかす)（ちらかす）— specifically about making a space untidy; 散らす is broader, used for petals, crowds, attention, pain, etc.
- 追い散らす（おいちらす）— to drive off/disperse (a crowd, animals) by chasing.
- 気を散らす（きをちらす）— a set phrase meaning "to distract (one's attention)," a very common collocation of this verb.

**Opposite words:**
- 集める（あつめる）— to gather, collect
- まとめる — to consolidate, bring together

**Example sentences:**
1. 風が桜の花びらを散らした。— The wind scattered the cherry blossom petals.
2. 警察がデモ隊を散らした。— The police dispersed the demonstrators.
3. スマホの通知が気を散らす。— Phone notifications are distracting.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 散らす is transitive and pairs with the intransitive 散る (to scatter/fall on its own, as in cherry blossoms falling). Don't confuse with 散らかす, which specifically implies clutter/mess rather than general dispersal.

## ちらっと
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** at a glance, briefly, in passing (to see, hear, or think of something fleetingly)

**Similar words:**
- ちらりと — a slightly more literary/softer variant with the same meaning, often in written Japanese.
- ちらちら — mimetic word for repeated glimpses/flickering (looking again and again, or light flickering), whereas ちらっと is a single brief instance.
- 一瞬（いっしゅん）— "an instant/moment," more neutral and not tied to glancing specifically.

**Opposite words:**

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Most often appears adverbially as 痛切に感じる/痛切に反省する ("to keenly feel/reflect on") — it's about the intensity of an internal realization, usually something uncomfortable or regretful, not simple "sharp pain" physically.

- 相次いで（あいついで）— "in quick succession," frequently used in news reports for a series of similar events (accidents, incidents) happening close together.
- どんどん — "rapidly, one after another," more casual and can also imply increasing momentum/speed, not just sequential occurrence.

**Opposite words:**
- たまに — occasionally, once in a while
- 一度に（いちどに）— all at once (as opposed to a sequence)

**Example sentences:**

2. 台風の影響で次々に飛行機が欠航した。— Flights were cancelled one after another due to the typhoon.
3. 彼は次々に質問を投げかけた。— He threw out questions one after another.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Emphasizes rapid, continuous succession of events/items — distinguishable from 続けて (one after another, more sequential/orderly) by its nuance of an almost overwhelming, unstoppable flow.

1. この話の続きが気になる。— I'm curious about the continuation of this story.
2. また続きは明日話そう。— Let's continue (the rest of) the story tomorrow.
3. 雨続きで洗濯物が乾かない。— With the continuous rain, the laundry won't dry.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Also forms compounds like 雨続き (continuous rain) or 仕事続き (one task after another) attaching to nouns to mean "a continuous run of ~" — recognize this productive compounding pattern beyond just "sequel to a story."

## 続く (つづく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to continue, to go on, to last

**Similar words:**
- [続ける](#続ける-つづける)（つづける）— the transitive pair: someone continues (keeps doing) something (続ける) vs something continues on its own (続く).
- 持続する（じぞくする）— to persist/be sustained, a more formal term often used for effects, conditions, or efforts lasting over time.
- 長引く（ながびく）— to be prolonged/drag on, often with a negative nuance (something taking longer than expected or desired).

**Opposite words:**
- 途切れる（とぎれる）— to be interrupted, cut off
- 終わる（おわる）— to end, finish

**Example sentences:**
1. 雨はまだ続いている。— The rain is still continuing.
2. この道はずっと続いている。— This road keeps going on and on.
3. 彼の成功は長く続かなかった。— His success didn't last long.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Intransitive — describes something continuing on its own (雨が続く); contrast with transitive 続ける, where a person/agent actively keeps doing something (勉強を続ける) — a common transitive/intransitive confusion point.

## 突く/突っ突く (つつく/つっつく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to poke, to prod, to nudge, to find fault with

**Similar words:**
- 突く（つく）— the base verb "to poke/thrust/stab" more broadly, while つつく/つっつく specifically implies repeated light poking/prodding.
- つまむ — to pinch/pick up with fingers (like picking up food), a different hand action than poking.
- あら探しをする（あらさがしをする）— to nitpick/find fault, the figurative extension overlapping with つつく's "find fault with" sense.

**Opposite words:**
- なでる — to stroke, pet gently

**Example sentences:**
1. 鳥がくちばしで餌をつついている。— The bird is pecking at its food with its beak.
2. 友達の肩をつついて注意を引いた。— I poked my friend's shoulder to get their attention.
3. 彼の失敗をいちいちつつくのはやめなさい。— Stop nitpicking every single one of his mistakes.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Beyond the literal "poke/peck," it's frequently used figuratively for "picking on/criticizing small faults" (あらをつつく, 失敗をつつく) — a nuance easy to miss if you only learn the literal physical meaning.

## 続ける (つづける)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to continue, to go on, to keep (doing something)

**Similar words:**
- [続く](#続く-つづく)（つづく）— the intransitive pair: 続ける is the active "to keep doing X" while 続く is "X continues on its own."
- 〜続ける — the auxiliary verb usage attached to another verb's stem (e.g. 話し続ける "to keep talking"), an extremely common grammatical pattern.
- 持続する（じぞくする）— to sustain/maintain over time, more formal, often for conditions or efforts rather than an ongoing personal action.

**Opposite words:**
- やめる — to stop, quit
- 中断する（ちゅうだんする）— to interrupt, suspend

**Example sentences:**
1. 毎日運動を続けている。— I keep up exercising every day.
2. 彼は夢を諦めずに努力を続けた。— He continued his efforts without giving up on his dream.
3. 彼女は一時間も話し続けた。— She kept talking for a whole hour.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Very commonly used as an auxiliary attached directly to a verb's ます-stem (動詞の連用形+続ける) to mean "keep on doing ~" — e.g. 泣き続ける (keep crying), 増え続ける (keep increasing) — a key grammar pattern at this level.

## 慎む (つつしむ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to refrain (from), to be discreet/careful, to exercise restraint

**Similar words:**
- 控える（ひかえる）— to refrain from/hold back on (something, often for health or courtesy reasons), a very close synonym, perhaps slightly more common in everyday use.
- 自粛する（じしゅくする）— to voluntarily restrain oneself (often in response to social pressure or circumstances), a more modern, socially-charged term.
- 謹む（つつしむ）— alternate kanji, used specifically in formal/respectful expressions like 謹んで (humbly, respectfully), related in root meaning but more about reverent formality.

2. 医者に飲酒を慎むよう言われた。— The doctor told me to refrain from drinking.
3. 彼は軽率な発言を慎んだ。— He refrained from making careless remarks.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Watch the kanji distinction: 慎む (to refrain/be discreet in behavior) vs 謹む (to do something humbly/respectfully, as in 謹んで申し上げます) — both read つつしむ but are used in different registers and contexts.

## 突っ張る (つっぱる)

**Meaning:** to prop up, to brace, to stand firm, to feel taut/stiff (muscles)

**Similar words:**

- 強がる（つよがる）— to act tough/put on a brave front, the figurative extension of 突っ張る when describing stubborn attitude.

## 包む (つつむ)
#vocabulary

**Similar words:**

## 勤まる (つとまる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to be fit for, to be qualified for (a job/role), to be able to serve in a position

**Similar words:**
- 務まる（つとまる）— alternate kanji, essentially the same meaning, used interchangeably depending on the specific nuance of 勤め vs 務め.
- 適している（てきしている）— to be suited/appropriate for, a more general expression of fitness for a role or task.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This is the potential-like intransitive form related to 勤める (to work/serve) — it describes whether a person is capable/suited for a role, often used with a questioning or doubtful nuance (〜が勤まるだろうか), not simply "to work at a job."

## 勤め/務め (つとめ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a job, work, one's duty

**Similar words:**
- 仕事（しごと）— the general, everyday word for "job/work," much more common than the somewhat formal 勤め.
- [義務](vocab-14.md#義務-ぎむ)（ぎむ）— duty/obligation in a more formal, often legal or moral sense, overlapping with 務め's "duty" meaning.
- [職務](vocab-04.md#職務-しょくむ)（しょくむ）— one's official duties/responsibilities at work, a more formal/bureaucratic term than 勤め.

**Opposite words:**
- 休み（やすみ）— rest, time off

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女は銀行に勤めを持っている。— She has a job at a bank.
2. 親の勤めとして子供を育てる。— Raising children is a parent's duty.
3. 長年の勤めを終えて退職した。— He retired after many years of service.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Two kanji carry slightly different nuance: 勤め leans toward "employment/job" (where one works), while 務め leans toward "duty/responsibility" (what one is obligated to do) — context determines which fits, though both share the reading つとめ.

## 努めて (つとめて)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** to the best of one's ability, as much as possible, making an effort to

**Similar words:**
- できるだけ — "as much as possible," a more common, casual equivalent used in everyday speech.
