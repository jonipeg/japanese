# ～てたまるか／たまるか {#てたまるか・たまるか}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**1. Kanji version:** ～て堪るか／堪るか (rare kanji; usually written in hiragana)

**2. Furigana version:** ～てたまるか／たまるか

**3. Meaning:** There's no way I'll let X happen! / I absolutely refuse to accept X — a strong refusal or indignant exclamation.

**4. Conjugation (connection form):** 動詞て形 + たまるか (also directly after some predicates without て, as in 負けてたまるか which still uses て形 of 負ける)

**5. Usage context:** Spoken, strongly emotional register. Masculine or forceful tone; expressed as a rhetorical question conveying strong emotion/defiance, often in exclamatory or confrontational contexts. Not appropriate in polite/formal speech.

**6. Similar grammar structures:**
- ものか — a very close synonym expressing strong rhetorical negation ("as if I'd do X!"); both are emotionally charged refusals.
- てはいられない — means "can't just stay in a state," a different nuance focused on inability to remain passive rather than refusal/indignation.
- わけがない — "there's no way X (is true)," a more neutral logical denial lacking たまるか's emotional indignation.

**7. Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; conceptually 〜ても構わない ("I don't mind if X happens") expresses the opposite attitude of acceptance.

**8. Example sentences:**
1. 失恋ぐらいで泣いてたまるか。(As if I'd cry over something like a broken heart!)
2. 「俺はもう10年以上将棋をやってるんだ。負けてたまるか」と父は言った。(“I've been playing shogi for over ten years. There's no way I'll lose,” my father said.)
3. こんな理不尽な扱いを受けて、黙っていられてたまるか。(There's no way I can stay silent after being treated so unfairly!)
4. あいつにだけは、絶対に負けてたまるか。(There's no way I'll lose to that guy, of all people!)
5. こんなところで諦めてたまるか。(There's no way I'm giving up at a point like this!)

**9. Common mistakes / key nuances:** Students sometimes misread this as a literal question about endurance rather than recognizing it as a rhetorical exclamation of strong refusal/indignation. It is also easily confused with ものか since both express emphatic emotional negation — the difference is largely stylistic/contextual rather than structural, so register and tone of the surrounding text should guide the choice.
