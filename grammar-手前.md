# ～手前 {#手前}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～手前

**Furigana form:** ～てまえ

**Meaning:** "Given that X (would see/judge me)" — because the speaker is conscious of a certain standpoint, person, or prior statement, they feel they must act accordingly, or else lose face or be evaluated poorly.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞-の／動詞辞書形・た形・ている形 + 手前
- 約束した手前 — "given that I promised [it]"
- 子供たちの手前 — "given that it's in front of the children"

**Typical context:** Spoken and written, neutral-to-formal register — used when explaining one's own constrained behavior due to social appearances, reputation, or a prior commitment; common in self-reflective or explanatory narration.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～以上（は）** — "given that X is the case," also expresses a resulting obligation, but based on logical circumstance rather than social appearance/face.
- [**～からには**](grammar-からには.md) — similar "since X, therefore must Y" structure, emphasizing determination rather than concern over others' evaluation.
- **～建前上** — refers to "on the surface/officially," sharing the idea of appearances mattering, but used more for institutional stances than personal face.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a phrase like 誰に見られてもいいから ("even if anyone watches, it's fine") would instead express indifference to others' evaluation.

**Example sentences:**
1. 5月末までに問題を解決すると約束した手前、どうしても頑張らなければならない。
   (Having promised to resolve the problem by the end of May, I absolutely have to work hard at it now.)
2. いつも手伝ってもらっている手前、今回はこちらから手伝いを申し出なければ……。
   (Since he's always the one helping me, this time I have to be the one to offer help...)
3. 子供たちの手前、父親がこんな酔っ払った姿で帰宅しては体裁が悪い。
   (With the children watching, it looks bad for the father to come home this drunk.)
4. ご近所の手前、家に警察官が来たことは知られたくない。
   (Given the neighbors' eyes, I don't want it known that a police officer came to my house.)
5. 部下に厳しく指導してきた手前、自分がミスをするわけにはいかない。
   (Having strictly trained my subordinates, I can't very well make a mistake myself.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This is always about the speaker's own sense of social obligation stemming from a prior statement, position, or an observing party — it describes self-imposed pressure, not someone else's. The clause that follows usually expresses a constraint on the speaker's own behavior, often using phrases like ～わけにはいかない、～なければならない、or ～ざるをえない.
