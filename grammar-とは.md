# ～とは {#とは}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～とは

**Furigana form:** ～とは

**Meaning:** Expresses the speaker's strong surprise, admiration, or exasperation at a fact just stated — "to think that ~," "what a ~."

**Conjugation:**
- 普通形（ナ形(だ)・名(だ)） + とは
- こんな立派なレポートをたった1日で仕上げたとは — "to think he finished such an impressive report in just one day"

**Typical context:** Both written and spoken, neutral-to-formal register — used when reacting to a surprising fact, often in narration, dialogue, or exclamatory remarks.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**～なんて**](grammar-など・なんて・なんか.md) — a more casual/colloquial equivalent expressing the same kind of surprise or disbelief.
- **～とは思わなかった** — explicitly states "I didn't think that ~," often combined with とは for emphasis.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; stating a fact without any surprise would simply use a plain declarative sentence with no とは attached.

**Example sentences:**
1. 鳥の足が2本であることさえ知らない子供がいるとは驚きだ。
   (It's astonishing that there are children who don't even know birds have two legs.)
2. こんな立派なレポートをたった1日で仕上げたとはすごい。
   (To think he finished such an impressive report in just one day — amazing!)
3. この先に滝があると聞いたのでこんなに歩いてきたのに、ここで行き止まりとは……。
   (I heard there was a waterfall further ahead so I walked all this way, and to think it's a dead end here...)
4. あんなに元気だった彼が突然入院するとは、信じられない。
   (To think that someone as energetic as him would suddenly end up hospitalized — I can't believe it.)
5. 一晩でこんなに雪が積もるとは思わなかった。
   (I never imagined this much snow would pile up in just one night.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern attaches to a clause describing the fact the speaker found surprising, and is typically followed by a sentence expressing the speaker's reaction (驚きだ・すごい・信じられない, etc.) — though that follow-up can be omitted, trailing off as in example 3. It's normally not used about the speaker's own actions or circumstances, since it implies genuine surprise at something external or unexpected.
