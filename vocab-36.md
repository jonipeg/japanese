## 抑制 (よくせい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** restraint, repression, inhibition

**Similar words:**
- [抑圧](vocab-04.md#抑圧-よくあつ)（よくあつ）— suppression/oppression, often stronger and more about forcibly holding something down (emotions, people).
- 制御（せいぎょ）— control (often technical/mechanical), more neutral than the emotionally-loaded 抑制.

**Example sentences:**
1. 感情を抑制するのは難しい。— It's difficult to restrain one's emotions.
2. 物価の上昇を抑制する政策がとられた。— Policies were adopted to curb the rise in prices.
3. この薬は食欲を抑制する効果がある。— This medicine has the effect of suppressing appetite.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 抑制 is used broadly for holding back emotions, economic trends, or bodily functions (appetite, growth) — it's more neutral/clinical than 抑圧, which implies a heavier, often oppressive force.

## 欲張り (よくばり)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** greed; a greedy person

**Similar words:**
- 強欲（ごうよく）— avarice/extreme greed, a stronger and more formal/negative term.
- 貪欲（どんよく）— voracious/insatiable desire, often used for ambition (can be neutral-to-positive in context, e.g. 貪欲に学ぶ).

**Example sentences:**
1. そんなに欲張りなことを言ってはいけない。— You shouldn't say such greedy things.
2. 彼は欲張りで、いつも人の分まで欲しがる。— He's greedy and always wants more than his share.
3. 欲張りすぎて両方とも失敗した。— I was too greedy and ended up failing at both.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 欲張り can function as both a noun describing greed/a greedy person and as a na-adjective (欲張りな人) — unlike 貪欲, which can carry a positive nuance in ambition contexts, 欲張り is almost always negative/critical.

## 欲深い (よくふかい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** acquisitive, avid, grabby, greedy

**Similar words:**
- [欲張り](#欲張り-よくばり)（よくばり）— a very close synonym, perhaps slightly more common in casual speech.
- がめつい — a colloquial, often harsher word for being stingy/grasping about money.

**Example sentences:**
1. 欲深い人は信用されにくい。— Greedy people are hard to trust.
2. 彼の欲深い性格が周りを困らせている。— His greedy personality troubles those around him.
3. 欲深いことをすると後で後悔する。— If you act greedily, you'll regret it later.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 欲深い is an i-adjective (unlike the na-adjective/noun 欲張り), describing an ingrained character trait of greediness — both words are near-synonyms but differ in part of speech, which affects conjugation.

## 欲望 (よくぼう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** greed, desire

**Similar words:**
- [欲](vocab-35.md#欲-よく)（よく）— the blunt, shorter form of the same concept; 欲望 is the fuller noun, often used in more formal/psychological discussion.
- 願望（がんぼう）— a wish/aspiration, generally more positive and less base than 欲望.

**Example sentences:**
1. 人間の欲望には限りがない。— Human desire knows no limits.
2. 物質的な欲望を捨てて生きる。— He lives having abandoned material desires.
3. 権力への欲望が彼を変えてしまった。— The desire for power changed him.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 欲望 is the standard noun for "desire/greed" in formal and philosophical discussion (e.g., 欲望に負ける, "to give in to desire") — distinguish from 願望, which leans toward aspirations/hopes rather than base cravings.

## 余計 (よけい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** extra, more than necessary, surplus, unnecessary

**Similar words:**
- [余分](vocab-33.md#余分-よぶん)（よぶん）— extra/surplus, very close synonym, often interchangeable.
- 無駄（むだ）— wasteful/futile, implies something is pointless rather than just "extra."

**Example sentences:**
1. 余計なことを言ってしまった。— I said something unnecessary (that I shouldn't have).
2. 余計なお世話だ。— That's none of your business (lit. "unnecessary meddling").
3. 心配しすぎると余計に疲れる。— If you worry too much, you get even more tired.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 余計 can function as a na-adjective ("unnecessary/uncalled-for," often in the idiom 余計なお世話) or as an adverb meaning "even more" (余計に疲れる) — two distinct usage patterns worth memorizing separately.

## 避ける (よける)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to avoid, shun, evade, keep clear of

**Similar words:**
- [避ける](#避ける-よける)（さける）— same kanji, different reading! さける means "to avoid" in a more general/figurative sense (avoiding topics, people, situations), while よける is more physical (dodging an object).
- かわす — to dodge/evade (a blow, a question), close to よける's physical dodging sense.

**Example sentences:**
1. 車が来たので素早く避けた。— A car was coming, so I quickly dodged out of the way.
2. 雨を避けて屋根の下に入った。— I went under a roof to avoid the rain.
3. ボールを避けきれずに当たってしまった。— I couldn't dodge the ball in time and got hit.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This is a classic homograph trap — 避ける read さける means avoiding something abstract (people, topics, risk), while read よける it means physically dodging/evading something concrete (a car, rain, a ball) — the kanji is identical but readings split the meaning.

## 予言 (よげん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a prediction, a prophecy; to foretell

**Similar words:**
- [予測](vocab-32.md#予測-よそく)（よそく）— a forecast/prediction based on data/reasoning (weather, economy), more analytical than the often mystical 予言.
- [予想](vocab-32.md#予想-よそう)（よそう）— a general expectation/guess about the future, more casual than 予言.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼の予言は的中した。— His prophecy came true.
2. 占い師が未来を予言した。— The fortune teller prophesied the future.
3. 科学者たちはこの現象を予言していた。— Scientists had predicted this phenomenon.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 予言 carries a mystical/prophetic connotation (fortune-telling, religious prophecy) more often than 予測/予想, which are used for reasoned, data-based forecasts (weather, economic predictions) — choosing the right word affects the tone significantly.

## 横切る (よこぎる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to cross (a street), to cut across

**Similar words:**
- 渡る（わたる）— to cross (a street, bridge, river), the more common, neutral verb for crossing.
- 横断する（おうだんする）— to cross/traverse, a more formal/written equivalent, often used for pedestrian crossings (横断歩道).

**Example sentences:**
1. 猫が道路を横切った。— A cat crossed the road.
2. 彼の前を人が横切っていった。— A person cut across in front of him.
3. 不安が心を横切った。— A feeling of unease crossed my mind.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 横切る specifically emphasizes crossing perpendicular to or cutting across a path/line (often implying it's somewhat sudden or in someone's way), while 渡る is the more neutral, standard verb for crossing a street or bridge; 横切る can also be used figuratively (unease "crossing" one's mind).

## よこす
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to send, hand over (to the speaker)

**Similar words:**
- 送る（おくる）— to send in general, neutral about direction; よこす specifically implies sending toward the speaker/here.
- 寄越す — the kanji form of the same word, less commonly written in kanji in modern usage.

**Example sentences:**
1. 返事をよこさなかった。— He didn't send a reply (to me).
2. 彼は手紙一つよこさない。— He doesn't even send a single letter.
3. こっちに荷物をよこしてください。— Please send the package over here (to me).

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** よこす always implies movement toward the speaker (like "kureru" but with a slightly rougher, more direct/colloquial tone, often used in mildly complaining statements like 手紙一つよこさない) — it sounds blunt/informal compared to neutral 送る.

## 汚す (よごす)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to soil, dirty, stain, disgrace

**Similar words:**
- 汚れる（よごれる）— the intransitive counterpart ("[something] becomes dirty"), while 汚す is transitive ("to dirty something").
- 傷つける（きずつける）— to hurt/damage (can be emotional or physical), different but related when discussing "disgracing" someone's honor.

**Example sentences:**
1. 服を泥で汚してしまった。— I got my clothes dirty with mud.
2. 彼は家族の名誉を汚した。— He disgraced his family's honor.
3. 川を汚さないようにしよう。— Let's try not to pollute the river.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Remember the transitive/intransitive pair: 汚す (to dirty something, transitive, takes を) vs. 汚れる (to become dirty, intransitive) — also note the figurative use for "disgracing" one's name/honor (名を汚す).

## 横綱 (よこづな)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a grand champion (sumo wrestler), the highest sumo rank

**Similar words:**
- 大関（おおぜき）— the second-highest sumo rank, just below 横綱.
- チャンピオン — "champion" in general sports (loanword), broader and not specific to sumo's rank system.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼はついに横綱に昇進した。— He was finally promoted to yokozuna.
2. 横綱の土俵入りは見ごたえがある。— The yokozuna's ring-entering ceremony is a sight to behold.
3. 横綱が負けて会場がどよめいた。— The venue buzzed with surprise when the yokozuna lost.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 横綱 is specifically the top rank in sumo's formal hierarchy (not just "a champion" in a loose sense) — it's also used figuratively to mean "the best/top-ranked" in other fields (業界の横綱, "the top dog of the industry").

## 汚れ (よごれ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** dirt, a stain, a spot; grime

**Similar words:**
- シミ — a specific, usually small discoloration stain (e.g. on clothes or skin); 汚れ is more general dirt/grime of any kind.
- [垢](vocab-06.md#垢-あか)（あか）— grime specifically from the body (skin, collar); narrower than 汚れ.
- [汚す](#汚す-よごす)（よごす）— the transitive verb "to make dirty"; 汚れ is the noun form describing the resulting dirt itself.

**Opposite words:**
- [清潔](vocab-22.md#清潔-せいけつ)（せいけつ）— cleanliness, hygienic
- きれい — clean, pretty

**Example sentences:**
1. シャツに汚れがついてしまった。— A stain got on my shirt.
2. この洗剤は頑固な汚れもよく落ちる。— This detergent removes even stubborn dirt well.
3. 窓の汚れを拭き取った。— I wiped off the grime on the window.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 汚れ is a noun (the dirt/stain itself), not a verb — learners sometimes confuse it with 汚す (to dirty something) or 汚い (dirty, adjective). Keep the three forms distinct in usage.

## 予算 (よさん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a budget, an estimate (of cost)

**Similar words:**
- 見積もり（みつもり）— a cost estimate/quote from a vendor; 予算 is the amount one plans to spend, not a quote received.
- [経費](vocab-17.md#経費-けいひ)（けいひ）— actual expenses/costs incurred; 予算 is the planned allocation before spending happens.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 今月の予算を超えてしまった。— I went over this month's budget.
2. 会社は新しいプロジェクトに予算を組んだ。— The company allocated a budget for the new project.
3. 予算内でできるだけ良いものを買いたい。— I want to buy the best thing I can within budget.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 予算 refers to planned/allotted money, often paired with 組む (to set up a budget) or 超える (to exceed it) — don't confuse with 費用 (actual cost) or 値段 (price of an item).

## 良し (よし)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** good, fine, all right (classical/set-phrase form of 良い)

**Similar words:**
- 良い（よい/いい）— the standard adjective "good"; 良し is its old/literary conclusive form, now mostly fossilized in phrases.
- [オーケー](vocab-09.md#オーケー-オーケー) — casual "okay," used in spoken modern contexts where 良し sounds more formal or decisive.

**Opposite words:**
- 悪し（あし）— bad, poor (equally archaic, seen in 良し悪し)

**Example sentences:**
1. 「よし、やるぞ！」と彼は気合を入れた。— "All right, let's do this!" he said, psyching himself up.
2. これでよしとしよう。— Let's call this good enough.
3. 善は急げ、善し悪しを考える前に始めよう。— Good deeds should be done quickly — let's start before weighing pros and cons.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 良し rarely stands alone in modern conversation except as the interjection よし (meaning "alright!/okay, here goes"); as a formal noun/classical adjective it mainly survives in set expressions like 良し悪し.

## 善し悪し (よしあし)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** good and bad (points), merits and demerits, whether something is good or bad

**Similar words:**
- 一長一短（いっちょういったん）— "one merit, one flaw," used for comparing two options each with pros and cons; 善し悪し is broader, often about judging a single thing's overall quality.
- メリット・デメリット — the loanword pair for "pros and cons," more casual/business register than 善し悪し.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 物事には善し悪しがある。— Everything has its good and bad points.
2. この薬は効果があるが、副作用との善し悪しを考える必要がある。— This medicine is effective, but we need to weigh the pros and cons against its side effects.
3. 人の善し悪しを一言で判断するのは難しい。— It's hard to judge whether a person is good or bad in a single word.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 善し悪し is read よしあし, not ぜんしあくし — a common reading mistake since both kanji have common on'yomi readings that don't apply here.

## らい
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** next, following (prefix, e.g. 来月 "next month," 来日 "visit to Japan," 来客 "visitor")

**Similar words:**
- [次](vocab-28.md#次-つぎ)（つぎ）— "next" as a standalone word for the next item/turn; らい is a bound prefix attached to time/event nouns (来週, 来年).
- 翌（よく）— another "next/following" prefix, but used more for the day/year immediately after a specified reference point (翌日, 翌年), whereas 来 is anchored to the present (来週 = the week after now).

**Opposite words:**
- 去（きょ）— prefix meaning "last/past" (去年 "last year")

**Example sentences:**
1. 来月、大阪に出張する予定です。— I'm planning a business trip to Osaka next month.
2. あの俳優が来日するというニュースを見た。— I saw news that actor is visiting Japan.
3. 来客があるので部屋を片付けておいた。— I tidied the room since we're having a visitor.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 来 as a prefix (らい) only attaches before the reference word (来週, 来年), never after; don't confuse with 来る (くる, "to come") which is a full verb with different readings.

## 止す (よす)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to stop, give up, cancel (an action or plan)

**Similar words:**
- やめる — the far more common verb for "to stop/quit"; 止す is a more old-fashioned/literary variant with the same meaning.
- 中止する（ちゅうしする）— to cancel a planned event formally; よす is more personal/colloquial, about choosing not to do something.

**Opposite words:**
- [続ける](vocab-28.md#続ける-つづける)（つづける）— to continue
- [始める](vocab-31.md#始める-はじめる)（はじめる）— to start

**Example sentences:**
1. そんな危ないことは止したほうがいい。— You'd better stop doing such a dangerous thing.
2. 彼は酒を止すことに決めた。— He decided to give up drinking.
3. 旅行の計画は止すことにした。— We decided to cancel the travel plans.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 止す is read よす, easily confused visually with 止める (とめる/やめる) which uses the same kanji 止 but different readings and nuances — in modern speech やめる is used almost exclusively, with よす sounding dated or regional.

## 夜更け (よふけ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** late at night, the dead of night

**Similar words:**
- [深夜](vocab-22.md#深夜-しんや)（しんや）— a more neutral/formal word for "late night" (e.g. 深夜番組 late-night TV); 夜更け has a more literary, atmospheric feel.
- 真夜中（まよなか）— "midnight," a specific point in time, while 夜更け refers to the late stretch of night more generally.

**Opposite words:**
- 真昼（まひる）— broad daylight, high noon

**Example sentences:**
1. 夜更けに電話が鳴った。— The phone rang late at night.
2. 彼女は夜更けまで勉強していた。— She was studying until late at night.
3. 夜更けの静けさが好きだ。— I like the stillness of the late-night hours.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 夜更け is literary/descriptive, often used in narration or poetic contexts; in everyday casual speech 夜遅く (よるおそく) is more common.

## 嫁 (よめ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** bride; daughter-in-law

**Similar words:**
- [花嫁](vocab-31.md#花嫁-はなよめ)（はなよめ）— specifically "bride" on her wedding day; 嫁 more broadly also means "daughter-in-law" within a family.
- 妻（つま）— one's own wife, a neutral term; 嫁 can casually mean "wife" in some regions/registers but formally centers on the in-law relationship.

**Opposite words:**
- 婿（むこ）— groom, son-in-law

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女は来月、嫁に行く。— She's getting married (lit. "going as a bride") next month.
2. うちの嫁はとても料理が上手だ。— My daughter-in-law (or, colloquially, "my wife") is very good at cooking.
3. 姑と嫁の関係はよく話題になる。— The mother-in-law and daughter-in-law relationship is often a topic of discussion.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Using 嫁 to mean "my wife" is common in casual spoken Japanese but can sound rough or old-fashioned to some listeners, especially in formal contexts — 妻 is the safer, standard term for one's own wife.

## 予約 (よやく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** reservation, booking, appointment

**Similar words:**
- 約束（やくそく）— a promise or appointment between people; 予約 specifically means booking a slot/table/ticket with a business or service.
- アポ（アポイントメント）— a business appointment, often used in work contexts; narrower than the general-purpose 予約.

**Opposite words:**
- キャンセル — cancellation

**Example sentences:**
1. レストランを予約した。— I made a reservation at the restaurant.
2. 病院の予約を忘れないでください。— Please don't forget your hospital appointment.
3. チケットはオンラインで予約できます。— You can reserve tickets online.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 予約 pairs with する/を入れる/を取る (to make a reservation), not 作る; also distinguish from 約束, which is about a personal promise, not a service booking.

## 夜 (よる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** night, evening

**Similar words:**
- 晩（ばん）— also means "evening/night," often used in greetings (今晩は) and slightly more about the evening meal-time period; 夜 is the broader, more neutral term for nighttime.
- 夕方（ゆうがた）— early evening/dusk, the transition period before full night falls.

**Opposite words:**
- 昼（ひる）— daytime

**Example sentences:**
1. 夜になると星がよく見える。— When night falls, the stars are clearly visible.
2. 彼は夜遅くまで働いている。— He works until late at night.
3. 今夜は家でゆっくり過ごしたい。— I want to spend tonight relaxing at home.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 夜 and 晩 overlap but aren't fully interchangeable — 今夜 ("tonight") is natural, while こんばん (今晩) is used in the set greeting こんばんは; don't substitute freely in all compounds.

## 喜び/歓び (よろこび)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** joy, pleasure, happiness, delight

**Similar words:**
- 嬉しさ（うれしさ）— the noun form of 嬉しい, more about a personal felt happiness in the moment; 喜び is broader and can describe shared or deeper joy (e.g. the joy of a life event).
- 幸せ（しあわせ）— overall happiness/well-being as a state of life, whereas 喜び is more about a specific joyful feeling or occasion.

**Opposite words:**
- 悲しみ（かなしみ）— sadness, sorrow

**Example sentences:**
1. 子供の誕生は家族にとって大きな喜びだった。— The birth of the child was a great joy for the family.
2. 彼は合格の知らせに喜びを隠せなかった。— He couldn't hide his joy at the news of passing.
3. 仕事の喜びを感じる瞬間がある。— There are moments when I feel the joy of my work.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 歓び is a less common kanji variant used especially for celebratory/public joy (e.g. 祝賀の歓び); in everyday writing 喜び is the standard choice.

## 喜ぶ/歓ぶ (よろこぶ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to be glad, be pleased, rejoice

**Similar words:**
- 嬉しがる（うれしがる）— to show signs of being happy (more about outward behavior); 喜ぶ is the general verb for feeling/expressing joy.
- 喜ばしい（よろこばしい）— the adjective form meaning "gladdening, happy (news, event)," describing the event rather than a person's reaction.

**Opposite words:**
- [悲しむ](vocab-13.md#悲しむ-かなしむ)（かなしむ）— to feel sad, grieve

**Example sentences:**
1. 両親は私の合格をとても喜んでくれた。— My parents were very pleased about my passing.
2. 彼女はプレゼントをもらって喜んだ。— She was delighted to receive the present.
3. みんなが喜ぶようなサプライズを計画している。— I'm planning a surprise that will make everyone happy.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 喜ぶ describes someone else's or one's own emotional reaction to a positive event — it's often used with で/に causes (〜に喜ぶ, 〜を喜ぶ); don't confuse the transitive/intransitive usage patterns with 喜ばせる (to make someone glad).

## 宜しい (よろしい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** all right, fine, good (polite/formal form of いい)

**Similar words:**
- いい — the plain, everyday word for "good/fine"; よろしい is its polite register, used toward superiors or customers.
- 結構です（けっこうです）— "that's fine/no thank you," a polite phrase that can express acceptance or polite refusal depending on context, unlike よろしい which is a straightforward politeness upgrade of いい.

**Opposite words:**
- いけない — not good, must not

**Example sentences:**
1. こちらにサインをいただいてもよろしいでしょうか。— Would it be all right for you to sign here?
2. 「これでよろしいですか」「はい、結構です」— "Is this all right?" "Yes, that's fine."
3. お名前を伺ってもよろしいですか。— May I ask your name?

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** よろしい is formal/polite register — using it casually with friends sounds stiff or even sarcastic; it commonly appears in the pattern 〜てもよろしいですか (may I...?) in service and business contexts.

## 弱い (よわい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** weak (physically, in strength, ability, or resistance)

**Similar words:**
- もろい — "fragile, brittle," describing something that breaks easily, more about physical durability than general strength.

## 悪者 (わるもの)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** villain, bad person, the "bad guy"

**Similar words:**
- 悪人（あくにん）— a more formal/serious term for "evil person," often used in moral or legal discussions; 悪者 is more casual and commonly used for a "bad guy" in stories or blame-assigning situations.
- 犯人（はんにん）— the culprit/perpetrator of a specific crime, narrower and more factual than the broader, sometimes narrative-framed 悪者.

**Opposite words:**
- 善人（ぜんにん）— a good, virtuous person

**Example sentences:**
1. この映画の悪者はとても魅力的だ。— The villain in this movie is very charismatic.
2. 彼はいつも悪者にされてしまう。— He always ends up being made the bad guy.
3. 悪者を倒すのがヒーローの役目だ。— Defeating the villain is the hero's job.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 悪者 often appears in the idiom 悪者にされる ("to be made the scapegoat/bad guy," even if not truly at fault) — this usage extends beyond literal villainy to social blame-shifting situations.

## 我 (われ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** oneself, I, ego, self

**Similar words:**
- 自分（じぶん）— "oneself," the everyday, neutral reflexive term; 我 is more literary/philosophical, often used in discussions of ego, identity, or self-assertion (我を忘れる, 我が強い).
- 私（わたし）— the standard first-person pronoun "I," far more common in daily speech than the formal/literary 我.

**Opposite words:**
- 他（た）— others, other

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は我を忘れて夢中になった。— He lost himself (forgot his ego) and became absorbed in it.
2. 我が強い人は周りと衝突しやすい。— People with a strong ego tend to clash with those around them.
3. 我以外、皆師なり、という言葉を大切にしている。— I cherish the saying, "everyone but myself is my teacher."

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 我 is not used as an everyday first-person pronoun like 私 or 僕 — it appears mainly in set phrases about ego/self-assertion (我が強い, 我を忘れる) or formal/classical speech, so using it casually as "I" sounds unnatural.

## 我々 (われわれ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** we, our (formal, often used in speeches or writing)

**Similar words:**
- 私たち（わたしたち）— the standard, everyday "we," used in most contexts; 我々 is more formal/stiff, typically used in speeches, official statements, or by groups asserting collective identity.
- 僕ら（ぼくら）— a casual, masculine "we/us," the opposite register from the formal 我々.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 我々はこの計画を支持する。— We support this plan.
2. 我々の努力が実を結んだ。— Our efforts have borne fruit.
3. 我々人類は地球を守る責任がある。— We humans have a responsibility to protect the Earth.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 我々 sounds notably formal and somewhat authoritative/collective (often used by politicians, executives, or in formal writing) — using it in casual daily conversation with friends can sound stiff or pompous; 私たち is the safer everyday choice.
