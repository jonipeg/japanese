# ～だらけ {#だらけ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～だらけ

**Furigana form:** ～だらけ (no kanji)

**Meaning:** "Covered in, full of X" — usually carries a negative or messy connotation (e.g., mistakes, dirt, debt).

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 (noun) + だらけ

**Typical context:** Casual-to-neutral spoken and written Japanese, with a mildly negative nuance.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**まみれ**](grammar-まみれ.md) — similar meaning, "covered in" (often a liquid or dirty substance), more literary in tone.
- **いっぱい** — "full of," neutral or even positive, lacking the negative connotation of だらけ.
- **ばかり** — "nothing but," focuses on repetition rather than physical coverage.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though きれいな／清潔な ("clean") contrasts conceptually.

**Example sentences:**
1. この作文は間違いだらけだ。
   (This essay is full of mistakes.)
2. 子供は泥だらけになって帰ってきた。
   (The child came home covered in mud.)
3. 彼は借金だらけで、生活が苦しいらしい。
   (He's drowning in debt, and his life seems hard.)
4. 彼の部屋は本だらけで、足の踏み場もない。
   (His room is full of books — there's no place to even step.)
5. 傷だらけの車を見て、事故の大きさが分かった。
   (Seeing the car covered in scratches, I understood how serious the accident was.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** だらけ almost always carries a negative nuance of excess or messiness; using it for pleasant abundance (e.g., 花だらけ for a beautiful garden) sounds odd — いっぱい or ばかり are better choices in positive contexts.
