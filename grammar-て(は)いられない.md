# ～て（は）いられない {#て（は）いられない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～て（は）いられない

**Furigana form:** ～て（は）いられない

**Meaning:** "Can't afford to keep on doing X" — expresses that due to a lack of time or emotional composure, one cannot remain in a certain state or keep doing a certain thing; implies the need to act immediately instead.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞て形 + は + いられない
- のんびりしてはいられない — "can't afford to just relax"
- 黙っていられなかった — "couldn't stay silent"

**Typical context:** Spoken and casual written register — used when expressing urgency, impatience, or a resolve to switch from passivity to action; common in personal narration and motivational self-talk.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**～わけにはいかない**](grammar-わけにはいかない.md) — "can't very well do X," expressing that an action is socially or morally impermissible, rather than impossible due to urgency/lack of composure.
- **～ないではいられない** — "can't help but do X," expressing an uncontrollable urge to act, which is a related but distinct nuance (compulsion rather than lack of time).
- [**～どころではない**](grammar-どころではない.md) — "it's not the time/situation for X," similarly conveys that circumstances don't allow for a relaxed or unrelated activity, often with a stronger sense of being overwhelmed.

**Opposite grammar structures:** ～ていられる — the plain potential form, meaning one *can* remain in a given state (e.g., 黙っていられる, "can stay silent"), describing the ability to maintain composure or inaction.

**Example sentences:**
1. やると決めたら、のんびりしてはいられない。今すぐ準備を始めよう。
   (Once I've decided to do it, I can't just relax. Let's start preparing right now.)
2. あれだけひどいことを言われて、わたしも黙っていられなかった。
   (After being told something that awful, I couldn't stay silent either.)
3. 愚痴を言ったり泣いたりしてはいられない。解決策を考えなくては。
   (I can't keep complaining and crying. I have to think of a solution.)
4. 締め切りが明日に迫っているのだから、こんなところでのんびりしてはいられない。
   (The deadline is tomorrow, so I can't afford to be relaxing somewhere like this.)
5. 彼が困っているのを見て、わたしも何もせずにはいられなかった。
   (Seeing him in trouble, I couldn't just sit there doing nothing either.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The subject is usually the speaker (first person), since this pattern expresses a personal sense of urgency rather than an objective description of someone else's situation. It conveys an internal push to stop the current state and move to action, so it reads oddly when attached to actions with no implied urgency or when describing a third party's situation neutrally.
