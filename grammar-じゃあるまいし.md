# ～では／じゃあるまいし {#じゃあるまいし}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～では／じゃあるまいし

**Furigana form:** ～では／じゃあるまいし

**Meaning:** "It's not like X, so..." — points out that the listener/subject is not in a particular (often implausible or excusable) category, and therefore a certain behavior or excuse doesn't make sense, usually delivered with mild scolding or sarcasm.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + ではあるまいし／じゃあるまいし
- Example base: 子供 → 子供じゃあるまいし

**Typical context:** Spoken, casual-to-neutral register; conveys a scolding, exasperated, or sarcastic tone, often used to criticize someone's behavior as inappropriate for their actual status (e.g., "you're not a child, so...").

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **ではないのだから** — plain "since it's not X"; the more neutral, straightforward equivalent, lacking じゃあるまいし's sharper, more rhetorical scolding edge.
- [**くせに**](grammar-くせに.md) — "even though X (should know better)"; shares a critical, scolding tone but くせに criticizes a contradiction between someone's status/knowledge and their actual behavior, while じゃあるまいし specifically denies that an excuse-providing category applies to them.
- [**まい**](grammar-まい.md) (classical negative volitional, as the base of あるまい) — the archaic negative form underlying this construction; recognizing まい as "will surely not be" helps parse あるまいし as "surely it's not the case that X, so..."

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a sympathetic acknowledgment (子供だから仕方ない, "since they're a child, it can't be helped") would contrast with じゃあるまいし's denial of that excusing category.

**Example sentences:**
1. 子供じゃあるまいし、そんなわがままを言うのはやめなさい。 (You're not a child, so stop being so selfish.)
2. 初めてじゃあるまいし、もっと落ち着いてやりなさい。 (It's not like it's your first time, so do it more calmly.)
3. 魔法使いじゃあるまいし、一瞬で問題を解決できるわけがない。 (It's not like you're a wizard, so there's no way you can solve the problem in an instant.)
4. 学生じゃあるまいし、もう少し責任を持って行動してほしい。 (You're not a student anymore, so I'd like you to act with a bit more responsibility.)
5. 神様じゃあるまいし、すべてを完璧にこなせるわけがない。 (You're not a god, so there's no way you can do everything perfectly.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** じゃあるまいし is almost always used to scold or push back against someone's behavior by denying that an excusing category (childhood, inexperience, superhuman ability) applies to them — it carries an implicit "so act accordingly" message in the following clause. This is a casual, somewhat blunt or sarcastic expression; it should be used carefully, as it can come across as condescending depending on tone and relationship.
