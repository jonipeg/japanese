## 携[わ]る (たずさわる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to participate in, take part in, be concerned/involved with (often work or a project)

**Similar words:**
- 関わる（かかわる）— "to be related to/involved with," broader and more common, usable for both positive and negative involvement.
- 従事する（じゅうじする）— formal "to be engaged in (an occupation)," specifically about one's job/profession.
- 参加する（さんかする）— "to participate in," often for a one-time event rather than ongoing work involvement.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は長年医療に携わってきた。— He has been involved in medical work for many years.
2. この研究に携わる研究者は多い。— Many researchers are involved in this research.
3. 教育に携わる仕事に就きたい。— I want to have a job involved in education.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** A formal, often resume/professional-context verb implying sustained, meaningful involvement in a field or project — not used for trivial or momentary participation (use 参加する instead).

## 只/唯 (ただ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** ordinary, common; (adverb) merely, simply, just, only; free (of charge)

**Similar words:**
- [単に](#単に-たんに)（たんに）— "merely/simply," a more formal written synonym for the adverbial "just/only" sense.
- 無料（むりょう）— "free of charge," the more formal/common term specifically for the "free" meaning of ただ.
- 普通（ふつう）— "ordinary/normal," overlaps with the "common, unremarkable" sense of ただ (ただの人 = an ordinary person).

**Opposite words:**
- [有料](vocab-35.md#有料-ゆうりょう)（ゆうりょう）— paid, chargeable

**Example sentences:**
1. これはただの風邪だから心配しないで。— This is just a common cold, so don't worry.
2. ただ黙って見ているだけだった。— I just silently watched, nothing more.
3. このイベントの入場はただです。— Admission to this event is free.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Highly polysemous word — can mean "ordinary" (ただの人), "merely/just" (ただ〜だけ), "free of charge" (ただで), or function as a conjunction meaning "however/but" (ただし is related but distinct) — context determines which sense applies.

## 戦い (たたかい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a battle, a war, a fight, a struggle

**Similar words:**
- 戦争（せんそう）— "war," specifically a large-scale armed conflict between nations, more specific than the broader 戦い.
- [争い](vocab-07.md#争い-あらそい)（あらそい）— "dispute/strife," often interpersonal or less violent than 戦い.
- 闘争（とうそう）— "struggle/strife," often used for political or ideological struggles (労働闘争).

**Opposite words:**
- 和平（わへい）— peace (settlement)

**Example sentences:**
1. 両軍の激しい戦いが続いた。— The fierce battle between the two armies continued.
2. 病との戦いに彼は勝った。— He won his battle against illness.
3. これは自分自身との戦いだ。— This is a battle with myself.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Frequently used metaphorically for non-military struggles (against illness, against oneself, in sports/competition) — much broader figurative use than the literal "battle."

## 戦う (たたかう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to fight, to do battle, to make war, to contend

**Similar words:**
- [争う](vocab-07.md#争う-あらそう)（あらそう）— "to dispute/contend," often less violent, can be verbal or competitive disagreement.
- 闘う（たたかう）— alternate kanji, used specifically for struggles against adversity, illness, or injustice (same reading).
- 対戦する（たいせんする）— "to compete against" in a game/match, more neutral/sporting context.

**Opposite words:**
- 和解する（わかいする）— to reconcile

**Example sentences:**
1. 選手たちは最後まで戦った。— The athletes fought until the very end.
2. 彼は病気と戦い続けている。— He continues to fight against his illness.
3. 正義のために戦う覚悟がある。— I'm prepared to fight for justice.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Note the kanji distinction: 戦う (war/combat/competition) vs 闘う (struggle against hardship/disease/injustice) — both read たたかう but convey slightly different nuances depending on the kanji chosen.

## 叩く (たたく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to beat, to knock, to tap, to hit (repeatedly)

**Similar words:**
- 殴る（なぐる）— "to punch/strike (a person)," implies more forceful, often violent hitting.
- ノックする（ノックする）— loanword "to knock," specifically used for knocking on a door.
- 打つ（うつ）— general "to hit/strike," broader and can be a single hit rather than repeated tapping.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. ドアを叩く音が聞こえた。— I heard the sound of knocking on the door.
2. 太鼓を叩いてリズムを取った。— I kept the rhythm by beating the drum.
3. 彼は机を叩いて怒りを表した。— He expressed his anger by slamming the desk.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Also used figuratively/idiomatically in 陰口を叩く (to badmouth someone behind their back) and 口を叩く (to talk/chatter) — not always literal physical hitting.

## 但し (ただし)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** but, however, provided that (introduces a qualification/exception)

**Similar words:**
- しかし（しかし）— general "however/but," more common in everyday speech, introduces plain contrast.
- ただ（ただ）— can also function as "however" in writing, slightly softer than 但し.
- もっとも（もっとも）— "although/to be sure," introduces a qualifying afterthought, similar nuance to 但し.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 参加は自由です。但し、事前申し込みが必要です。— Participation is free. However, advance registration is required.
2. 返品は可能です。但し、レシートが必要です。— Returns are possible. However, a receipt is required.
3. 彼は賛成した。但し条件付きだった。— He agreed. However, it was conditional.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Specifically used to add a condition/exception/proviso to a preceding statement (often in rules, contracts, instructions) — more formal and written than casual しかし, which is a general contrastive conjunction.

## 直ちに (ただちに)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** immediately, directly, at once

**Similar words:**
- すぐに（すぐに）— common, everyday "right away/soon," less formal than 直ちに.
- [即座に](vocab-24.md#即座に-そくざに)（そくざに）— "instantly/on the spot," very formal, emphasizes a quick reaction without delay.
- 速やかに（すみやかに）— "promptly," formal, often used in official instructions/requests.

**Opposite words:**
- [そのうち](vocab-24.md#そのうち)（そのうち）— eventually, sometime later

**Example sentences:**
1. 異常を発見したら直ちに報告してください。— If you discover an abnormality, please report it immediately.
2. 避難勧告が出たら直ちに避難すべきだ。— If an evacuation advisory is issued, you should evacuate immediately.
3. 彼は直ちに行動に移した。— He immediately moved into action.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** A formal, often official/emergency-context word (used in warnings, news, legal text); in casual conversation すぐに is far more natural.

## 畳む (たたむ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to fold (up); to close down (a business)

**Similar words:**
- 折る（おる）— "to fold/bend," used for a single fold (e.g. paper), while 畳む often implies folding something compactly into its final stored form (futon, clothes, umbrella).
- 閉じる（とじる）— "to close," general closing action, overlaps with the "close down (business)" sense.
- 店じまいする（みせじまいする）— "to close up shop (for the day or permanently)," closely related to the figurative "close down" sense of 畳む.

**Opposite words:**
- 広げる（ひろげる）— to unfold, spread out

**Example sentences:**
1. 布団を畳んで押し入れにしまった。— I folded the futon and put it away in the closet.
2. 傘を畳んで傘立てに入れた。— I folded the umbrella and put it in the umbrella stand.
3. 長年続けた店をついに畳むことにした。— We finally decided to close down the shop we'd run for many years.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Beyond literal folding, 店を畳む is a common idiom meaning "to close/shut down a business permanently" — don't translate it literally as "fold the shop."

## 立[ち]上[が]る (たちあがる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to get up, stand up, rise; (figuratively) to take action, rise up

**Similar words:**
- 起きる（おきる）— "to get up (from sleeping/lying down)," different starting posture than standing from sitting.
- [立つ](#立つ-たつ)（たつ）— plain "to stand," 立ち上がる specifically emphasizes the motion of rising from a seated/lying position.
- 奮起する（ふんきする）— "to be spurred into action," closer to the figurative "rise up and act" sense.

**Opposite words:**
- [座る](vocab-22.md#座る-すわる)（すわる）— to sit down

**Example sentences:**
1. 名前を呼ばれて彼は立ち上がった。— When his name was called, he stood up.
2. 国民は不正に対して立ち上がった。— The people rose up against the injustice.
3. 新しいプロジェクトのために立ち上がった組織がある。— There's an organization that was formed (lit. "stood up") for the new project.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Frequently used figuratively for "taking action/rising up" (against injustice, to start a new venture) in addition to the literal physical action — a very productive metaphorical extension.

## 立[ち]去る (たちさる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to leave, to walk away, to depart (from a place)

**Similar words:**
- 去る（さる）— plain "to leave/depart," slightly more literary alone, 立ち去る emphasizes physically walking away from a specific spot.
- 立ち退く（たちのく）— "to vacate/move out (of a place)," often used for being forced to leave (eviction).
- 出て行く（でていく）— casual "to go/get out," more common in everyday speech.

**Opposite words:**
- 近づく（ちかづく）— to approach

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は無言でその場を立ち去った。— He silently walked away from the scene.
2. 警官に注意され、彼らは立ち去った。— Warned by the police officer, they left the area.
3. 犯人は現場からすぐに立ち去った。— The culprit left the scene immediately.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** A somewhat literary/formal verb often used in narrative or news contexts (e.g. describing someone leaving a crime scene) — in casual speech, 帰る or 出て行く is more natural.

## 立[ち]止[ま]る (たちどまる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to stop, to stand still, to halt (while walking)

**Similar words:**
- 止まる（とまる）— general "to stop," used for vehicles, machines, or people, broader than the specifically "mid-walk" sense of 立ち止まる.
- 立ち止まって考える（たちどまってかんがえる）— a common collocation "to stop and think," figuratively meaning to pause and reflect.
- 足を止める（あしをとめる）— "to stop one's feet," a near-synonymous idiom for pausing while walking.

**Opposite words:**
- 歩き続ける（あるきつづける）— to keep walking

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は信号の前で立ち止まった。— He stopped in front of the traffic light.
2. 美しい景色に思わず立ち止まった。— I unconsciously stopped at the beautiful scenery.
3. たまには立ち止まって自分を振り返ることも大切だ。— It's also important to occasionally stop and reflect on yourself.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Frequently used figuratively as 立ち止まって考える ("pause to reflect"), a common idiom encouraging reflection rather than constant forward motion — popular in self-help/motivational writing.

## 立場 (たちば)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** situation, position, standpoint, viewpoint

**Similar words:**
- 立地（りっち）— "location (of a building/business)," completely different meaning despite phonetic similarity — a common confusion point.
- 視点（してん）— "viewpoint/perspective," more about how one perceives/analyzes something than one's social/relational position.
- 身分（みぶん）— "social status/rank," about one's standing in society rather than a situational/relational position.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼の立場を理解する必要がある。— We need to understand his position/situation.
2. 私の立場では、そうは言えない。— From my standpoint, I can't say that.
3. 相手の立場に立って考えることが大切だ。— It's important to think from the other person's perspective.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Extremely common in the set phrase 〜の立場に立つ ("to stand in someone's shoes/position") — don't confuse with 立地 (りっち, location), which is unrelated despite the shared kanji 立.

## 立[ち]寄る (たちよる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to drop by, to stop by, to call in (briefly, on the way somewhere)

**Similar words:**
- 寄る（よる）— plain, casual synonym, slightly less formal, same basic meaning of "to stop by."
- [訪れる](vocab-10.md#訪れる-おとずれる)（おとずれる）— "to visit," implies a more deliberate, often longer visit rather than a brief stop on the way.
- 寄り道する（よりみちする）— "to take a detour," emphasizes going out of one's direct route, similar nuance of incidental stopping.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 帰りにコンビニに立ち寄った。— I dropped by the convenience store on my way home.
2. 出張のついでに実家に立ち寄った。— I stopped by my parents' house while on a business trip.
3. お近くにお越しの際はぜひ立ち寄ってください。— When you're in the area, please do drop by.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Implies a brief, incidental stop while heading somewhere else (not the final destination) — distinct from 訪れる, which can be a planned, standalone visit.

## 立つ (たつ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to stand (up), to rise, to be built/established; to depart (of time/leaving)

**Similar words:**
- 立ち上がる（たちあがる）— "to stand up (from sitting)," emphasizes the motion of rising, while 立つ can also mean simply "to be standing/upright."
- 建つ（たつ）— homophone meaning "to be built" (of a building), different kanji used specifically for structures.
- 経つ（たつ）— another homophone meaning "(time) to pass/elapse" — a common source of kanji confusion.

**Opposite words:**
- [座る](vocab-22.md#座る-すわる)（すわる）— to sit
- 倒れる（たおれる）— to fall down

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は教壇の前に立った。— He stood in front of the podium.
2. この場所に新しいビルが立つ予定だ。— A new building is planned to be built (lit. "stand") on this site.
3. 卒業してから三年が経った。— Three years have passed since graduation.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** One of the most homophone-dense verbs in Japanese: 立つ (stand), 建つ (be built), 経つ (time pass), 発つ (depart on a trip) — all たつ but different kanji; context determines which is meant.

## 抱っこ (だっこ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** picking someone up and holding them, carrying in one's arms (often a child)

**Similar words:**
- [抱く](vocab-25.md#抱く-だく)（だく）— the more general/formal verb "to hold/embrace," 抱っこ is the cuter, more colloquial noun form used especially with children.
- [おんぶ](vocab-11.md#おんぶ)（おんぶ）— "to carry on one's back," a different carrying position/method than 抱っこ (front-facing in arms).
- 抱きしめる（だきしめる）— "to hug tightly," emphasizes emotional closeness rather than the practical act of carrying.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 赤ちゃんが抱っこをせがんだ。— The baby demanded to be picked up.
2. 子供を抱っこしながら買い物をした。— I went shopping while carrying my child in my arms.
3. パパに抱っこしてもらって嬉しそうだ。— The child looks happy being carried by daddy.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** A warm, casual/childlike word (often used by or to small children, and by parents) — far less formal than 抱く; frequently paired with してもらう/する (抱っこする, 抱っこしてもらう).

## 達者 (たっしゃ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** healthy, in good health; also skilled/proficient (at something, often language)

**Similar words:**
- [健康](vocab-18.md#健康-けんこう)（けんこう）— the general, common word for "health/healthy," more neutral and widely used.
- [元気](vocab-18.md#元気-げんき)（げんき）— "energetic/well," more casual/everyday way to ask about or describe someone's condition.
- 巧み（たくみ）— "skillful," overlaps with the secondary "proficient" sense of 達者 (e.g. 口が達者 = good talker).

**Opposite words:**
- 病弱（びょうじゃく）— sickly, frail

**Example sentences:**
1. おかげさまで両親とも達者に暮らしています。— Thankfully, both my parents are living in good health.
2. 彼は口が達者で、誰とでもうまく話せる。— He's a smooth talker and can converse well with anyone.
3. いつまでもお達者でいてください。— Please stay healthy for a long time to come.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Often used as a polite/formal way to inquire about elderly relatives' health (ご達者で, お達者で); the "skilled/proficient" sense is specifically common in the idiom 口が達者 ("silver-tongued/glib").

## 脱出 (だっしゅつ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** escape (from a dangerous or confining situation)

**Similar words:**
- 逃げる（にげる）— plain, general verb "to run away/flee," broader and more casual than the formal noun 脱出.
- 脱走（だっそう）— "escape (from prison/confinement)," specifically implies breaking out of custody or a controlled facility.
- [避難](vocab-01.md#避難-ひなん)（ひなん）— "evacuation," a planned, organized movement to safety, distinct from an urgent individual escape.

**Opposite words:**
- 閉じ込められる（とじこめられる）— to be trapped/confined

**Example sentences:**
1. 乗客全員が無事に脱出した。— All the passengers escaped safely.
2. 火災現場からの脱出経路を確認した。— We checked the escape route from the fire scene.
3. 彼は危機一髪で脱出に成功した。— He succeeded in escaping by the skin of his teeth.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Implies escaping from a dangerous/confining physical situation (a burning building, a sinking ship); don't confuse with 脱走 (specifically escaping captivity/prison).

## 達する (たっする)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to reach, to attain, to arrive at (a level, amount, or goal)

**Similar words:**
- [着く](vocab-28.md#着く-つく)（つく）— plain "to arrive (at a place)," physical arrival, narrower than the abstract "reach a level" sense of 達する.
- 到達する（とうたつする）— nearly synonymous, slightly more formal/technical, often used for reaching a destination or milestone.
- 至る（いたる）— "to lead to/result in," more literary, often used for processes culminating in an outcome.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 売り上げが100億円に達した。— Sales reached 10 billion yen.
2. 彼はついに目標に達した。— He finally reached his goal.
3. 気温が40度に達する日もある。— There are days when the temperature reaches 40 degrees.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** A formal, often written-register verb for reaching a numerical level or abstract goal (達する often follows a quantity + に); in casual speech, 届く or なる is more natural.

## 脱する (だっする)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to escape, to get out of (a difficult situation or state)

**Similar words:**
- 脱出する（だっしゅつする）— nearly synonymous, often used for more dramatic/physical escapes, while 脱する can be more abstract (危機を脱する).
- 抜け出す（ぬけだす）— casual "to slip out/get out of," usable for both literal and figurative escapes.
- [克服する](vocab-03.md#克服する-こくふくする)（こくふくする）— "to overcome," implies conquering a difficulty rather than simply exiting the state.

**Opposite words:**
- 陥る（おちいる）— to fall into (a bad situation)

**Example sentences:**
1. 会社はようやく赤字を脱することができた。— The company was finally able to get out of the red (financial loss).
2. 危機を脱するための対策を講じた。— We took measures to get out of the crisis.
3. 彼は不況から脱しようと必死だった。— He was desperate to escape the recession.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Often used abstractly for escaping a negative state/condition (危機, 不況, スランプ) rather than a physical location — complements 脱出する, which leans more toward literal physical escape.

## 達成 (たっせい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** achievement, attainment, accomplishment

**Similar words:**
- [成功](vocab-22.md#成功-せいこう)（せいこう）— "success," broader and more general, doesn't necessarily imply a specific pre-set goal was met.
- [実現](vocab-21.md#実現-じつげん)（じつげん）— "realization," emphasizes making something (a dream, plan) become real/actual.
- 成し遂げる（なしとげる）— verb form "to accomplish/carry through," more active/effortful connotation.

**Opposite words:**
- 失敗（しっぱい）— failure

**Example sentences:**
1. 目標を達成するために努力を続けた。— I kept working hard in order to achieve my goal.
2. 彼はついに夢を達成した。— He finally achieved his dream.
3. 売上目標の達成率は80%だった。— The achievement rate for the sales target was 80%.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Specifically implies meeting a pre-defined goal/target (目標達成), unlike 成功 which can apply to any favorable outcome without a prior specific target.

## 脱線 (だっせん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** derailment (of a train); (figuratively) digression, going off-topic

**Similar words:**
- 横道にそれる（よこみちにそれる）— idiom "to go off on a side road," specifically figurative for digressing in conversation.
- 話がそれる（はなしがそれる）— "the conversation strays," a plain descriptive phrase for the figurative sense.
- [事故](vocab-19.md#事故-じこ)（じこ）— general "accident," broader than the specific train-related 脱線.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 大雨で電車が脱線する事故が起きた。— An accident occurred where a train derailed due to heavy rain.
2. 話が脱線してしまい、本題に戻れなくなった。— The conversation went off-topic and we couldn't get back to the main point.
3. 彼の講義はよく脱線するが面白い。— His lectures often digress, but they're interesting.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Has both a literal railway meaning ("derailment") and a very common figurative meaning ("digressing from the topic in conversation/writing") — both senses are frequently used in daily Japanese.

## 唯 (たった)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** only, just, merely (emphasizing a small amount)

**Similar words:**
- ただ（ただ）— "only/just," but also means "free (of charge)"; たった is used almost exclusively before numbers/amounts.
- [わずか](vocab-36.md#わずか)（わずか）— "a mere/slight amount"; more formal and written, often used with precise quantities in reports.
- ほんの（ほんの）— "a mere..." modifying nouns directly; たった modifies numbers and time spans more naturally.

**Opposite words:**
- 大勢（おおぜい）— a large number (of people)
- 多数（たすう）— a large number, majority

**Example sentences:**
1. たった一人でその仕事を終わらせた。— He finished the job all by himself, with just one person.
2. たった三日で風邪が治った。— My cold cleared up in just three days.
3. 財布にはたった千円しか入っていなかった。— There was only a thousand yen in my wallet.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** たった almost always precedes a number, quantity, or time duration (たった一つ, たった五分) to stress how small it is — it isn't a general-purpose "only" like だけ, which attaches after nouns to mean restriction rather than smallness.

## 脱退 (だったい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** withdrawal, secession (from an organization, group, or treaty)

**Similar words:**
- 退会（たいかい）— withdrawing membership from a club/association specifically; 脱退 is broader and can apply to alliances, unions, or political blocs.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 脱退 is a formal, often political/organizational term — used for leaving unions, alliances, bands, or clubs — and sounds too stiff for casually "quitting" something small like a hobby group, where やめる would be more natural.

## 縦 (たて)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** vertical direction, length, height (as opposed to width)

**Similar words:**
- 高さ（たかさ）— "height" as a measured dimension; 縦 refers more to orientation/axis than a specific measurement.
- 垂直（すいちょく）— "perpendicular/vertical" in a technical, geometric sense; 縦 is the everyday word for the vertical axis of an object.

3. 彼は縦書きの小説を好んで読む。— He prefers reading novels written in vertical text.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 縦 and 横 are almost always paired in Japanese (縦横, 縦書き/横書き) — learners often translate only "vertical" and forget that 縦 also covers "length" in contexts like measuring a box (縦・横・高さ), where it specifically means the front-to-back or up-down dimension depending on context.

## 建て前 (たてまえ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** one's public stance or official position, as opposed to true feelings

**Similar words:**
- 本音（ほんね）— one's true feelings/intentions; 建て前 is the paired opposite concept, together forming a key cultural contrast in Japanese communication.
- 表向き（おもてむき）— "on the surface/ostensibly," describing an outward appearance that may not match reality, similar but more adverbial in use.
- 名目（めいもく）— "nominal reason/pretext," often used for official/bureaucratic justifications rather than personal social stance.

- 本音（ほんね）— one's genuine feelings/intentions
