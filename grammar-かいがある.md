# ～かいがある／かいがない {#かいがある}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～甲斐がある／甲斐がない

**Furigana form:** ～かいがある／かいがない

**Meaning:** "It's worth/not worth doing X" — expresses whether an effort or action yields a rewarding/meaningful result.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞た形 + 甲斐がある／ない (した甲斐がある, common)
- 動詞ます形 + がい (compound noun form: 生きがい "reason to live," やりがい "worth doing")
- 名詞 + の甲斐があって (…の甲斐があって、〜, "thanks to the worthwhile effort of...")

**Typical context:** Neutral, usable in both speech and writing; a common, everyday expression not restricted to formal registers, though 甲斐 itself has a slightly literary flavor compared to plain colloquial alternatives.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**がい**](grammar-がい.md) (as a suffix, e.g., やりがい, 生きがい) — a related nominalized form meaning "worth of doing X," used as a standalone noun rather than in the ～があるない predicate form.
- **かいもなく** — "in vain, without any reward"; a set negative adverbial form meaning an effort produced no result, more fixed/idiomatic than 甲斐がない.
- **だけのことはある** — "it lives up to expectations / is worthy of X"; shares the "worth" semantic field but evaluates whether a result matches a reputation or effort, rather than whether an action itself was rewarding.

**Opposite grammar structures:** 甲斐がない directly serves as its own built-in opposite ("not worth it"); no separate opposite construction needed.

**Example sentences:**
1. 努力した甲斐があって、見事合格した。 (Thanks to the worthwhile effort, I passed brilliantly.)
2. 長時間並んだ甲斐があって、人気商品を手に入れた。 (Thanks to waiting in line for so long, I got the popular item.)
3. せっかく手伝ったのに、感謝もされず甲斐がなかった。 (I went out of my way to help, but with no thanks given, it wasn't worth it.)
4. 毎日練習した甲斐があり、大会で優勝できた。 (Thanks to practicing every day, I was able to win the competition.)
5. 彼のために尽くしてきたが、報われず甲斐がないと感じることもある。 (I've devoted myself to him, but sometimes I feel it isn't worth it when it goes unrewarded.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 甲斐がある almost always follows a past-tense verb (した甲斐がある) describing an effort already made, and typically appears in the pattern 〜た甲斐があって、[positive result] — learners sometimes misplace it as a future-looking "it will be worth it," but its natural use looks back at an effort and confirms it paid off. Don't confuse 甲斐 (worth/reward, read かい) with 貝 (shellfish) or 回 (times/occurrences) — all pronounced similarly but unrelated in meaning.
