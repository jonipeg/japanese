# ～もの／～もん {#もの・もん}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～もの／～もん

**Furigana form:** ～もの／～もん (no kanji; sentence-final particle)

**Meaning:** "Because, you know..." — a casual, explanatory/justifying sentence-final particle used to give a reason, often with a slightly whiny, defensive, or childish emotional coloring. もん is the more casual/colloquial variant of もの.

**Conjugation:**
- 普通形 + もの／もん (女性や子供がよく使う、会話で頻出)
- だって疲れたんだもん。(Because I'm tired, that's why!)
- 行きたくない。だって彼がいるんだもの。(I don't want to go. Because he'll be there, you know.)

**Typical context:** Casual spoken Japanese only. Frequently used by children and women in informal conversation, often preceded by だって ("but, because"). Sounds childish or petulant if used in formal contexts.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **から** — the neutral, standard way to give a reason, usable in any register.
- **んだもん** — the colloquial contracted combination with のだ, very common in casual speech.
- [**ものだから**](grammar-ものだから.md) — a related but distinct structure used for giving an excuse/explanation in a fuller sentence (more neutral register than もの alone).

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; this is a reason-giving particle, not part of a contrastive pair.

**Example sentences:**
1. 「どうして食べないの？」「だってお腹がいっぱいなんだもん。」
   ("Why aren't you eating?" "Because my stomach's full, you know.")
2. 行きたくない。だって雨が降ってるんだもの。
   (I don't want to go. Because it's raining, you know.)
3. 「また遅刻したの？」「だって電車が遅れたんだもん。」
   ("You're late again?" "Because the train was delayed, that's why.")
4. 仕方ないよ、まだ子供なんだもの。
   (It can't be helped — he's still a child, after all.)
5. 怒らないでよ、わざとじゃないんだもん。
   (Don't get mad — I didn't do it on purpose, you know.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** もの/もん should be reserved strictly for casual conversation — using it in formal writing or polite speech sounds immature. It's almost always paired with だって at the start of the sentence to set up the excuse. Don't confuse this with ものだ (general truth/nostalgic habit) or ものだから (giving a reason in a longer explanatory sentence) — these are related but functionally distinct patterns.
