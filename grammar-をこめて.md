# ～を込めて {#をこめて}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～を込めて

**Furigana form:** ～をこめて

**Meaning:** "Putting (feeling/effort) into, with (heart/soul) put into" — expresses that something is done with concentrated emotion, effort, or sincerity poured into it.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 (feeling/effort word) + を込めて: 心を込めて (putting one's heart into it), 愛を込めて (with love), 感謝を込めて (with gratitude)
- Often used adverbially before a verb describing the action: 心を込めて手紙を書く (write a letter putting one's heart into it)
- Can modify a noun with 込めた: 心を込めた手紙 (a letter written with heart)

**Typical context:** Spoken and written, neutral-to-warm register. Common in personal messages, speeches, gifts, and descriptions of heartfelt actions (cooking, writing, crafting).

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **〜に思いを込めて** — variant phrasing emphasizing "thoughts/feelings," functionally similar.
- **心から** — "from the heart," adverbial alternative without the "putting into" imagery.
- **真心を込めて** — intensified fixed expression, "with utmost sincerity."

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though 適当に/いい加減に ("carelessly/half-heartedly") contrasts in spirit.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女は心を込めて手作りのクッキーを焼いた。
   (She baked handmade cookies with her whole heart put into it.)
2. 感謝の気持ちを込めて、両親に手紙を書いた。
   (I wrote a letter to my parents, putting my gratitude into it.)
3. 祈りを込めて、千羽鶴を折った。
   (We folded a thousand paper cranes, putting our prayers into them.)
4. 彼は愛情を込めて子供たちを育てた。
   (He raised his children with love poured into every moment.)
5. 一音一音に魂を込めて、ピアノを弾いた。
   (She played the piano, putting her soul into every single note.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** を込めて requires an abstract noun representing a feeling or intention (心, 愛, 感謝, 祈り, 魂) — it cannot be used with concrete objects or physical actions without this emotional framing. Learners sometimes misuse it as a general-purpose "with" (as in using a tool), which is incorrect; で or を使って should be used for instrumental "with."
