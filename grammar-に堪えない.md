# ～に堪えない {#に堪えない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～に堪えない

**Furigana form:** ～にたえない

**Meaning:** Attached to a limited set of emotion nouns, expresses that a feeling is so strong it cannot be suppressed — "cannot help but feel ~," "overwhelmed with ~." Formal register.

**Conjugation:**
- 名 + に堪えない
- 感激に堪えません — "I am overwhelmed with gratitude"

**Typical context:** Written and formal spoken register — common in speeches, congratulatory or condolence remarks, and formal written reflections where the speaker expresses a powerful emotional reaction.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**～を禁じ得ない**](grammar-を禁じ得ない.md) — very similar meaning, "cannot suppress the feeling of ~," but tends to describe an emotion welling up in reaction to witnessing something, whereas に堪えない describes an internal emotional state being too strong to contain.
- [**～てやまない**](grammar-てやまない.md) — formal, "never ceasing to feel ~," typically used with wish/hope verbs like 願う・祈る rather than emotion nouns directly.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite pattern; a mild or neutral statement of feeling would simply use the emotion noun with a plain verb (感謝しています, etc.) without に堪えない.

**Example sentences:**
1. お忙しいところを多くの方にお集まりいただき、感激に堪えません。
   (I am overwhelmed with gratitude that so many of you have gathered here despite your busy schedules.)
2. 田中君がこのような賞を受けたことは、友人である私も喜びに堪えません。
   (That Tanaka received such an award fills me, as his friend, with uncontainable joy.)
3. きちんと確認しておけばこのような事故は起きなかったかもしれないと、後悔の念に堪えない。
   (I am overcome with regret, thinking this accident might not have happened if we had checked things properly.)
4. 突然の訃報に、悲しみに堪えない。
   (At the sudden news of his death, I am overwhelmed with grief.)
5. 被災地の様子を見て、同情に堪えない。
   (Seeing the state of the disaster-stricken area, I couldn't help but feel overwhelming sympathy.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern attaches only to a limited set of emotion nouns (感謝・感激・喜び・後悔・同情・悲しみなど) — it can't be used freely with any noun. The subject is almost always the speaker (first person), even though it's frequently omitted from the sentence, since this expresses the speaker's own uncontainable feeling. Don't confuse this with the homophonous に堪えない that attaches to verbs (見るに堪えない) to mean "unbearable/not worth ~ing" — that is a separate, distinct pattern.
