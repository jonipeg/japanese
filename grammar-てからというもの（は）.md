# ～てからというもの（は） {#てからというもの（は）}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～てからというもの（は）

**Furigana form:** ～てからというもの（は）

**Meaning:** "Ever since X..." — describes how, following a particular turning-point event, some new change or condition set in and has continued steadily ever since, often looking back reflectively over a span of time.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞て形 + からというもの（は）
- 結婚してからというもの、料理が上手になった — "ever since I got married, I've become good at cooking"

**Typical context:** Written and spoken narrative, slightly literary/reflective register — common in personal essays, memoirs, and anecdotes looking back on how one's life changed after a turning point.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**～て以来**](grammar-て以来.md) — also means "ever since X," but more neutral and factual, without the added emphasis on a noticeable, continuing change.
- [**～を境に**](grammar-を境に.md) — "with X as a turning point," similar idea of before/after contrast, but more neutral/formal and doesn't require the continuation to be ongoing at speech time.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a phrase like 元の状態に戻った ("it returned to how it was before") would instead describe reverting rather than a lasting change.

**Example sentences:**
1. 娘が大学に入り家を出ていってからというもの、家の中が寂しくなった。
   (Ever since my daughter entered university and left home, the house has felt lonely.)
2. 子供が生まれてからというものは、子供のおもちゃばかり見て歩いています。
   (Ever since the child was born, I find myself looking at nothing but children's toys wherever I go.)
3. 日本に来てからというもの、国の家族のことを思わない日はない。
   (Ever since I came to Japan, there hasn't been a single day I haven't thought of my family back home.)
4. あの事故に遭ってからというもの、車の運転が怖くて仕方がない。
   (Ever since that accident, I've been terrified of driving.)
5. 彼は昇進してからというもの、以前にも増して仕事に打ち込んでいる。
   (Ever since he got promoted, he's thrown himself into his work even more than before.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Avoid using this for something that just happened moments ago — it requires a meaningful span of time to have elapsed, over which a noticeably changed state has persisted. The clause before it marks a turning-point event; what follows must describe an ongoing state or habit, not a single completed, one-time event.
