# ～たりとも…ない {#たりとも…ない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～たりとも…ない

**Furigana form:** ～たりとも…ない

**Meaning:** "Not even one [minimal unit]..." / "not at all" — cites the smallest possible unit of a quantity to emphatically stress that something does not happen or is not allowed in the slightest.

**Conjugation:**
- 1 + 助数詞 + たりとも + …ない
- 1日たりとも忘れたことはありません — "have not forgotten even for a single day"

**Typical context:** Formal/written, emphatic register — used in solemn declarations, vows, or rules; notably stronger and more literary than ordinary negation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～一つも…ない** — plain equivalent of "not even one," more casual and everyday.
- **片時も…ない** — "not even for a moment" — similarly emphatic and literary, often interchangeable with time-unit uses of たりとも.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; an affirmative universal statement like いつでも／どんな時も～ ("always, at any time") expresses the positive counterpart instead of total negation.

**Example sentences:**
1. あなたのことは1日たりとも忘れたことはありません。
   (I have not forgotten you for even a single day.)
2. 試合中は一瞬たりとも気を抜いてはいけない。
   (You must not let your guard down for even a single moment during the match.)
3. この部分の設計は1ミリたりとも間違いがないようにお願いします。
   (Please make sure the design for this part doesn't have even a single millimeter of error.)
4. 何人たりともこの神聖な場所に立ち入ることは許されていない。
   (No one whatsoever is permitted to enter this sacred place.)
5. 会社のお金は一円たりとも無駄にはできない。
   (Not even a single yen of the company's money can be wasted.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Attaches to the smallest unit of a quantity (1+counter: 1日, 1ミリ, 一瞬, 一円, etc.) paired with a negative predicate to stress that not even the tiniest amount occurs; it does not normally attach to large units (1年, 1トン, etc.). The set phrase 何人たりとも ("no one whatsoever") is an idiomatic exception that doesn't use "1."
