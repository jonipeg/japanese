# ～がち {#がち}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～がち

**Furigana form:** ～がち

**Meaning:** "Tends to ~ / prone to ~ (often with a negative nuance)" — describes a recurring tendency, often an undesirable habit or frequent occurrence.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ます形 (verb stem) + がち — 例: 忘れがち (tends to forget)
- 名詞 + がち — 例: 病気がち (sickly, prone to illness), 遠慮がち (tends to be reserved/hesitant)
- ～がちな + 名詞 — 例: 曇りがちな天気 (weather that tends to be cloudy)
- ～がちだ / ～がちになる

**Typical context:** Both written and spoken, neutral register — common in daily conversation, health discussions, and descriptive writing about patterns of behavior or conditions.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **やすい** — "easy to ~," more neutral, can apply to positive or negative tendencies without the "regrettably often" nuance.
- [**っぽい**](grammar-っぽい.md) — "seems like / has the quality of," more casual and broader, not specifically about frequency/tendency.
- **傾向がある** — "has a tendency to," more formal/analytical phrasing of the same idea.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though めったに～ない ("rarely ~") contrasts in frequency.

**Example sentences:**
1. 最近、忙しくて朝食を抜きがちだ。
   (Lately I've been busy and tend to skip breakfast.)
2. 彼は子供の頃から病気がちだった。
   (He was sickly ever since he was a child.)
3. 約束の時間に遅れがちな人は、信用されにくい。
   (People who tend to be late for appointments are hard to trust.)
4. 冬は運動不足になりがちなので、注意が必要だ。
   (In winter, people tend to lack exercise, so caution is needed.)
5. 彼女は遠慮がちに意見を述べた。
   (She stated her opinion hesitantly/reservedly.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** がち almost always carries a negative or undesirable connotation (forgetting, being late, getting sick), unlike やすい which is neutral — saying 成功しがち ("tends to succeed") sounds odd because success isn't typically framed as an unwanted recurring tendency.
