# ～にはおよばない {#にはおよばない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～には及ばない

**Furigana form:** ～にはおよばない

**Meaning:** "There's no need to X / it's not necessary to go that far" — politely tells someone that an action, often one done out of courtesy, isn't necessary.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形 + には及ばない
- 名詞 + には及ばない
- Example base: 送る → 送るには及ばない

**Typical context:** Polite, formal-to-neutral spoken and written register; often used to graciously decline an offer of help or reassure someone that they needn't trouble themselves. Courteous, not rude — common in business and polite social settings.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **なくてもいい** — plain "you don't have to do X"; more casual and general-purpose, lacking the polite, almost reassuring tone of には及ばない.
- [**には当たらない**](grammar-には当たらない.md) — "it doesn't warrant X / it's not appropriate to X" (e.g., 驚くには当たらない "no need to be surprised"); very close in function, often interchangeable, though 当たらない leans toward "doesn't merit a reaction" while 及ばない leans toward "doesn't require an action."
- [**までもない**](grammar-までもない.md) — "there's no need to go as far as X"; nearly synonymous, emphasizing that a lesser effort suffices, whereas には及ばない more directly reassures that the listener's planned action is unnecessary.

**Opposite grammar structures:** なければならない ("must do X") expresses the reverse — that an action is in fact necessary.

**Example sentences:**
1. 大丈夫です。すぐ近くですから、送っていただくには及びません。 (It's fine. It's very close by, so there's no need for you to walk me there.)
2. わざわざ謝罪に来るには及びませんよ。 (There's no need for you to come all this way to apologize.)
3. この程度のミスなら、報告するには及ばない。 (For a mistake this minor, there's no need to report it.)
4. ご心配には及びません。もう解決しましたから。 (There's no need to worry. It's already been resolved.)
5. わざわざお越しいただくには及びませんので、電話でけっこうです。 (There's no need for you to go out of your way to come; a phone call will do just fine.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** には及ばない is specifically used to reassure or politely decline, usually in response to someone's intended considerate action — it is not a blunt "unnecessary" like 不要だ, and using it about oneself ("I don't need to do X") sounds odd; it almost always addresses what someone else need not do for the speaker's sake. Don't confuse it with 及ばない used in comparisons ("falls short of," e.g., 彼には及ばない "can't match him") — same verb 及ぶ, but a different construction when it follows a comparison target rather than には before a verb.
