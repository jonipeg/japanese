## 勤/務/努める (つとめる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to work, to serve, to try, to endeavor

**Similar words:**
- 勤める（つとめる）— specifically "to work/be employed at" (a company, organization) — 会社に勤める.
- 務める（つとめる）— specifically "to serve in a role/function" (as chairman, as an actor's part) — 議長を務める.
- 努める（つとめる）— specifically "to endeavor/make an effort to" (achieve something) — 解決に努める.

**Opposite words:**
- [怠る](vocab-03.md#怠る-おこたる)（おこたる）— to neglect, be negligent

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は銀行に勤めている。— He works at a bank. (勤める)
2. 彼女が司会を務めた。— She served as the emcee. (務める)
3. 問題解決に努めている。— We are making efforts to solve the problem. (努める)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This is a classic N1/N2 three-kanji homophone set: 勤める (be employed), 務める (serve in a role), 努める (endeavor/try) — all read つとめる but used in distinct grammatical contexts; exams frequently test correct kanji selection for a given sentence.

## 綱 (つな)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a rope (thick, sturdy)

**Similar words:**
- [ロープ](vocab-36.md#ロープ) — the loanword for "rope," generally interchangeable with 綱 but slightly more modern/casual in usage.
- 紐（ひも）— a thinner cord/string, much smaller in scale than the thick, sturdy 綱.
- 命綱（いのちづな）— a lifeline (literally "life rope"), a compound emphasizing the critical, safety-related use of a rope.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 船を綱でしっかりと固定した。— We securely fastened the boat with a rope.
2. 綱引きで赤組が勝った。— The red team won the tug-of-war.
3. 彼にとって家族は最後の綱だった。— For him, his family was his last lifeline.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Thicker/sturdier than 紐 (string/thin cord) — context clue for distinguishing scale. Also used figuratively as "last lifeline/hope" (頼みの綱, "one's last resort/hope") — a common idiomatic extension.

## 繋がり (つながり)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a connection, a relation, a tie, a bond

**Similar words:**
- [繋がる](#繋がる-つながる)（つながる）— the verb form, "to be connected," from which 繋がり is derived as a noun.
- [関係](vocab-13.md#関係-かんけい)（かんけい）— relationship/relation, a more general and common word, without 繋がり's slight nuance of a physical or network-like link.
- 絆（きずな）— a bond (emotional, often between people), implying deeper emotional connection than the more neutral 繋がり.

**Opposite words:**
- 断絶（だんぜつ）— a break, severance (of ties/relations)

**Example sentences:**
1. この二つの事件には何か繋がりがある。— There's some connection between these two incidents.
2. 彼とは仕事上の繋がりがある。— I have a professional connection with him.
3. SNSを通じて人との繋がりが広がった。— My connections with people have expanded through social media.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Neutral and broad — covers everything from abstract logical connections (between events/facts) to personal/social networks — unlike 絆 which specifically implies emotional closeness/bonding.

## 繋がる (つながる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to connect, to be tied, to be linked, to be related

**Similar words:**
- [繋ぐ](#繋ぐ-つなぐ)（つなぐ）— the transitive pair: someone connects/ties two things (繋ぐ) vs things are connected/linked (繋がる) on its own.
- 通じる（つうじる）— to lead to/communicate, overlapping sense of "connection" but more about paths leading somewhere or being understood.
- 結びつく（むすびつく）— to be linked/tied together, often for more abstract relationships (cause and effect, ideas).

**Opposite words:**
- [切れる](vocab-16.md#切れる-きれる)（きれる）— to be cut off, disconnected

**Example sentences:**
1. このケーブルはパソコンに繋がっている。— This cable is connected to the computer.
2. 努力が成功に繋がった。— The effort led to (was connected to) success.
3. 彼女とはSNSで繋がっている。— I'm connected with her on social media.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Intransitive — contrast with transitive 繋ぐ (to actively tie/connect something); frequently used for internet/phone "connection" (電話が繋がらない "the call won't connect") as well as abstract causal links (努力が成功に繋がる).

## 繋ぐ (つなぐ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to tie (to), to fasten, to connect

**Similar words:**
- [繋がる](#繋がる-つながる)（つながる）— the intransitive pair: someone ties/connects something (繋ぐ) vs things become connected (繋がる).
- [繋げる](#繋げる-つなげる)（つなげる）— very close synonym/near-duplicate of 繋ぐ, sometimes used interchangeably, though 繋ぐ is more established/traditional.
- 結ぶ（むすぶ）— to tie/bind (a knot, a relationship), broader and can be more about binding/knotting than connecting two separate things.

**Opposite words:**
- 放す（はなす）— to let go, release
- 切る（きる）— to cut, disconnect

**Example sentences:**
1. 犬をリードで繋いだ。— I tied the dog with a leash.
2. 手を繋いで歩いた。— We walked holding hands.
3. 電話を繋いでおいてください。— Please keep the call connected (on hold).

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Transitive — needs an object (を); commonly used for literal tying (a leash, a boat) and for holding hands (手を繋ぐ), as well as technical/phone connections — compare with the near-synonym 繋げる, which is largely interchangeable but slightly more modern/casual.

## 繋げる (つなげる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to tie (to), to fasten, to connect

**Similar words:**
- [繋ぐ](#繋ぐ-つなぐ)（つなぐ）— essentially a near-synonym; 繋ぐ is the more traditional/established transitive verb, while 繋げる is a newer, increasingly common alternative with largely the same meaning.
- 接続する（せつぞくする）— to connect (especially electronic/technical devices), a more formal, technical register than the everyday 繋げる.
- 結びつける（むすびつける）— to link/tie together (often ideas or causes), more abstract than the typically concrete 繋げる.

**Opposite words:**
- 切り離す（きりはなす）— to cut off, separate

**Example sentences:**
1. パソコンをインターネットに繋げた。— I connected the computer to the internet.
2. この経験を次の挑戦に繋げたい。— I want to carry this experience forward into my next challenge.
3. 二つの部屋を繋げてリビングにした。— We connected the two rooms to make a living room.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Largely interchangeable with 繋ぐ in modern usage, though some purists consider 繋ぐ more "correct" traditionally — both are acceptable in contemporary Japanese; 繋げる is especially common in casual/spoken contexts and for abstract "carrying forward" (経験を次に繋げる).

## 津波 (つなみ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a tsunami, a tidal wave

**Similar words:**
- 高潮（たかしお）— a storm surge, caused by weather/storms rather than seismic activity, a different oceanographic phenomenon.
- 波（なみ）— a general wave, much smaller scale and not catastrophic like 津波.
- [洪水](vocab-18.md#洪水-こうずい)（こうずい）— a flood, broader term for water overflowing, not specific to ocean-generated waves.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 大地震の後、津波警報が発令された。— A tsunami warning was issued after the major earthquake.
2. 津波で多くの家が流された。— Many houses were swept away by the tsunami.
3. 津波の被害を防ぐための防波堤が建てられた。— A breakwater was built to prevent tsunami damage.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This Japanese word has been adopted directly into English and many other languages as "tsunami" — note it's specifically earthquake/seismic-triggered, unlike 高潮 which is storm-related; also used metaphorically (例: 情報の津波, "a tsunami of information").

## 常に (つねに)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** always, at all times, whenever, constantly

**Similar words:**
- いつも — the everyday, casual equivalent of "always," more common in spoken Japanese than the more formal/written 常に.
- [絶えず](vocab-25.md#絶えず-たえず)（たえず）— constantly/incessantly, emphasizing continuity without interruption, slightly more literary.
- 常日頃（つねひごろ）— "day in, day out, ordinarily," emphasizing habitual regularity over time.

**Opposite words:**
- たまに — occasionally, once in a while
- めったに〜ない — rarely, seldom

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は常に冷静さを保っている。— He always maintains his composure.
2. 安全第一を常に心がけている。— I always keep safety first in mind.
3. 常に変化し続ける市場に対応する必要がある。— We need to respond to a market that is constantly changing.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** More formal/written register than いつも — appropriate in business writing, instructions, and formal speech; using 常に in very casual conversation can sound slightly stiff, where いつも is more natural.

## 捻る (つねる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to pinch

**Similar words:**
- つまむ — to pick up/pinch with the fingertips (e.g. picking up a small object), different from つねる which specifically means to pinch skin, often to cause pain.
- ひねる（捻る, same kanji different reading!）— to twist (a cap, one's body, an idea); note the kanji is identical but the reading and meaning differ based on context — a key trap.
- かむ — to bite, a different kind of pain-inducing physical action.

**Opposite words:**
- なでる — to stroke, pet gently

**Example sentences:**
1. 痛くて思わず自分の腕をつねった。— It hurt so much that I unconsciously pinched my own arm.
2. 彼は夢かどうか確かめるために頬をつねった。— He pinched his cheek to check whether it was a dream.
3. いたずらで妹の腕をつねってしまった。— I pinched my little sister's arm as a prank.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Shares the kanji 捻る with ひねる (to twist), but つねる specifically means "to pinch" (skin, usually to cause slight pain), while ひねる means "to twist" (a knob, one's body, or figuratively "to rack one's brain," 頭をひねる) — reading context determines which pronunciation/meaning applies.

## 角 (つの)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a horn, an antler

**Similar words:**
- 牙（きば）— a fang/tusk, a different type of animal projection (teeth-based rather than head-growth).
- [角](#角-つの)（かど）— homograph! Same kanji, different reading (かど), meaning "a corner" — unrelated meaning, context/reading distinguishes them.
- つの（鹿の角）— specifically deer antlers are often called 鹿の角, a common natural compound.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 鹿の角は毎年生え変わる。— A deer's antlers regrow every year.
2. 牛には大きな角がある。— Cows have large horns.
3. 彼女は怒って「角を出す」ような顔をした。— She made a face as if "sprouting horns" in anger (idiom for jealousy/anger).

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Critical homograph: 角 read as つの means "horn/antler," but the same kanji read as かど means "corner" — always confirm via furigana or context which reading is intended, as this is a common point of confusion in reading practice.

## 募る (つのる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to make an appeal for, to recruit, to solicit; to grow stronger/intensify (feelings)

**Similar words:**
- 募集する（ぼしゅうする）— to recruit/call for applicants, a more formal, noun-based equivalent commonly seen in job/volunteer ads.
- [呼びかける](vocab-33.md#呼びかける-よびかける)（よびかける）— to call out to/appeal to (people), broader than 募る's specific sense of soliciting participation or donations.
- [高まる](vocab-25.md#高まる-たかまる)（たかまる）— to rise/intensify (emotions, tension), overlapping with 募る's "intensifying feelings" sense but more general.

**Opposite words:**
- 鎮まる（しずまる）— to calm down, subside

**Example sentences:**
1. ボランティアを募ることにした。— We decided to recruit volunteers.
2. 彼への不信感が募るばかりだった。— My distrust toward him just kept growing.
3. 寄付を募るキャンペーンを始めた。— We launched a campaign to solicit donations.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Has two distinct senses: (1) "to recruit/solicit" (people, donations, applications) and (2) "to intensify" (of feelings like anxiety, longing, distrust, usually negative or emotionally charged) — don't assume it only means recruitment.

## 唾 (つば)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** saliva, spittle, spit

**Similar words:**
- 唾液（だえき）— the more formal/medical term for "saliva," used in scientific or health contexts, versus the everyday/blunt 唾.
- つばを吐く（つばをはく）— "to spit," the common verb collocation with this noun.
- よだれ — drool, specifically saliva dribbling out (often associated with babies or eager anticipation), a related but distinct image.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は道に唾を吐いた。— He spat on the road.
2. 緊張のあまり、唾を飲み込んだ。— Out of nervousness, I swallowed (my spit).
3. 唾をつけて封筒を閉じた。— I licked (moistened with saliva) and sealed the envelope.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Note the idiom 唾をつける meaning "to stake a claim on" something (literally "put spit on it"), and 眉唾（まゆつば）meaning "dubious/suspect" — both idiomatic extensions worth recognizing beyond the literal meaning.

## 粒 (つぶ)
<br>
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a grain, a grain of rice, a drop (of rain), a particle

**Similar words:**
- 一粒（ひとつぶ）— "one grain/drop," the common counter-attached form used in everyday speech.
- 滴（しずく）— a drop (of liquid specifically), used for water/rain drops rather than solid grains like rice.
- 塊（かたまり）— a lump/mass, much larger and less uniform than the small, discrete unit implied by 粒.

**Opposite words:**
- 塊（かたまり）— a large lump/mass (as a contrast in scale)

**Example sentences:**
1. お米の粒がこぼれた。— Grains of rice spilled.
2. 大粒の雨が降ってきた。— Large drops of rain began to fall.
3. 彼の涙が一粒こぼれた。— A single tear fell from his eye.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Functions both as a noun and as a counter suffix (一粒、二粒) for small round/granular things — used not only for rice and rain but figuratively for tears (涙の粒) and even "small/elite in number but high quality" in the idiom 粒ぞろい (all of excellent quality).

## 潰す (つぶす)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to crush, to smash, to kill time, to ruin/bankrupt (a business)

**Similar words:**
- [潰れる](#潰れる-つぶれる)（つぶれる）— the intransitive pair: 潰す "to crush" (someone crushes it) vs 潰れる "to be crushed/collapse" (it happens on its own).
- 壊す（こわす）— to break/destroy, more general destruction, without 潰す's specific nuance of flattening/squashing.
- 時間を潰す（じかんをつぶす）— a set idiom meaning "to kill time," a very common usage of this verb beyond physical crushing.

**Opposite words:**
- 膨らませる（ふくらませる）— to inflate, puff up

**Example sentences:**
1. 空き缶を踏んで潰した。— I stepped on the empty can and crushed it.
2. 電車を待つ間、時間を潰した。— I killed time while waiting for the train.
3. 彼は会社を潰してしまった。— He ended up bankrupting the company.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Beyond literal crushing, two idiomatic uses are essential: 時間を潰す (to kill time) and 会社を潰す (to bankrupt/ruin a business) — recognize these set collocations rather than always translating literally as "crush."

## 潰れる (つぶれる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to go under, to be crushed, to collapse, to be ruined (of a business)

**Similar words:**
- [潰す](#潰す-つぶす)（つぶす）— the transitive pair: 潰れる "to collapse/be crushed" happens on its own, while 潰す is someone actively crushing/ruining it.
- 倒産する（とうさんする）— to go bankrupt, a more formal/specific business term overlapping with 潰れる's "business ruined" sense.
- 壊れる（こわれる）— to break/be broken, more general, without 潰れる's specific nuance of being flattened or collapsing inward.

**Opposite words:**
- 成功する（せいこうする）— to succeed
- 復活する（ふっかつする）— to revive, make a comeback

**Example sentences:**
1. 空き箱が荷物の重みで潰れた。— The empty box got crushed under the weight of the luggage.
2. 不況で多くの店が潰れた。— Many stores went under due to the recession.
3. 彼は酔っ払って潰れてしまった。— He got drunk and passed out completely.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Like 沈没する, 潰れる has a casual slang sense of "to pass out drunk" (酔って潰れる) in addition to its literal "to be crushed" and figurative "to go bankrupt/fail" senses — context determines which applies.

## デート (でーと)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a date, to go on a date

**Similar words:**
- [付き合う](vocab-28.md#付き合う-つきあう)（つきあう）— to date/be in a relationship with someone on an ongoing basis, versus デート which refers to a single outing/occasion.
- [交際](vocab-18.md#交際する-こうさい)（こうさい）— a more formal term for a romantic relationship overall, broader than a single デート event.
- 出会い（であい）— an encounter/meeting (often the first meeting that could lead to dating), a different stage from an established デート.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 週末に彼女とデートする予定だ。— I'm planning to go on a date with my girlfriend this weekend.
2. 初デートでどこに行くか迷っている。— I'm undecided about where to go on our first date.
3. 二人は映画館でデートした。— The two of them went on a date at the movie theater.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** A loanword specifically for romantic dates — does not extend to "appointment date" in the calendar sense (that's 日付 ひづけ) or a business meeting date; context is purely romantic/social outings.

## テーマ (てーま)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a theme, a subject, a topic

**Similar words:**
- 主題（しゅだい）— the more formal/literary Japanese equivalent of "theme," often used in academic or artistic analysis.
- トピック — a loanword closer to "topic" in the sense of a specific subject of discussion, sometimes narrower than テーマ's broader "overarching theme."
- 題材（だいざい）— subject matter/material (for a work of art or writing), the raw content a theme is built from.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. このレポートのテーマは環境問題です。— The theme of this report is environmental issues.
2. 今日の会議のテーマについて話し合いましょう。— Let's discuss today's meeting theme.
3. 彼の小説のテーマは「愛と喪失」だ。— The theme of his novel is "love and loss."

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Widely used across academic, business, and artistic contexts as the overarching "theme/subject" of a piece of work or discussion — more common in everyday Japanese than the native equivalent 主題, which sounds more formal/literary.

## 手配 (てはい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** arrangement, preparation, disposition (also: an arrest warrant/manhunt arrangement)

**Similar words:**
- 準備（じゅんび）— preparation in general, broader and more common than 手配, which specifically implies arranging logistics (tickets, accommodations, personnel).
- 手はずを整える（てはずをととのえる）— to make arrangements/set things up, a slightly more formal phrase overlapping in meaning.
- 指名手配（しめいてはい）— a specific compound meaning "wanted (by police, by name)," the criminal-justice sense of 手配.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. ホテルの手配はもう済んでいる。— The hotel arrangements are already done.
2. 会議に必要な資料の手配をお願いします。— Please arrange the materials needed for the meeting.
3. 逃走中の容疑者は指名手配された。— The fleeing suspect was put on a wanted list.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Has two quite different senses depending on context: everyday logistical "arrangements" (手配する for tickets, cars, meetings) versus the criminal justice sense of being "wanted" (指名手配) — don't confuse the two meanings when reading news versus business Japanese.

## 手芸 (てげい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** handicraft, needlework/craftwork

**Similar words:**
- [工芸](vocab-18.md#工芸-こうげい)（こうげい）— craft/industrial art in general (pottery, woodwork); 手芸 specifically means hand crafts like sewing, knitting, embroidery.
- 手作り（てづくり）— "handmade," a broader adjective-like noun for anything made by hand, not limited to needlework/craft hobbies.
- 裁縫（さいほう）— specifically sewing/needlework, a subset of 手芸.

**Opposite words:**
- 機械生産（きかいせいさん）— machine production

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女は趣味で手芸をしている。— She does handicrafts as a hobby.
2. 手芸店でかわいいボタンを買った。— I bought cute buttons at the craft store.
3. 手芸が得意で、自分でバッグを作った。— She's good at handicrafts and made her own bag.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 手芸 refers to hobby-level handmade crafts (knitting, embroidery, sewing), not fine art or industrial craftsmanship (工芸), which carries more artisanal/professional weight.

## 手本 (てほん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a model, example to follow, copybook

**Similar words:**
- 模範（もはん）— "model/exemplar," more formal and often applied to people's conduct (模範的な行動); 手本 is more concrete, often literally something to copy (handwriting, behavior demonstrated to a child).
- 見本（みほん）— "sample," used for product samples, not a moral/behavioral model.
- お手本（おてほん）— the polite form, very commonly used in teaching/calligraphy contexts.

**Opposite words:**
- 悪例（あくれい）— a bad example

**Example sentences:**
1. 先生がお手本を見せてくれた。— The teacher showed us an example.
2. 彼の生き方は若者の手本になる。— His way of life serves as a model for young people.
3. 手本通りに字を書く練習をした。— I practiced writing characters exactly like the model.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Often used with 〜になる (to become a model/example) in a moral sense, distinct from 見本, which is purely a physical sample of a product.

## 手袋 (てぶくろ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** gloves

#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** degree, point, times/occasions

**Similar words:**
- 回（かい）— "times/occurrences," used for counting repeated events, similar to 度 in counting occurrences (今度 vs 今回 nuance differs slightly).
- 程度（ていど）— "degree/extent," a compound emphasizing the extent/level of something.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 気温は30度まで上がった。— The temperature rose to 30 degrees.
2. この度はありがとうございました。— Thank you very much on this occasion.
3. 彼の度を越した態度に驚いた。— I was surprised by his attitude that went beyond acceptable limits.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 度 is highly polysemous: temperature/angle degrees, a counter for occasions (この度, 今度), and in 度を越す ("to go too far/overstep limits") — learners often only know the temperature sense.

**Similar words:**
- [独裁](#独裁-どくさい)（どくさい）— "dictatorship," political autocratic rule, not to be confused despite sharing the 独 kanji — 独占 is about economic/exclusive control, not governance.
- 占有（せんゆう）— "occupation/possession," a more neutral legal term for holding/possessing something, without the exclusivity implication of 独占.

**Opposite words:**
- 共有（きょうゆう）— shared ownership

**Example sentences:**
1. その会社は市場を独占している。— That company monopolizes the market.
2. 彼は独占インタビューに応じた。— He agreed to an exclusive interview.
3. 独占禁止法という法律がある。— There's a law called the Antimonopoly Act.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 独占 is commonly used in both business contexts (market monopoly) and media contexts (独占インタビュー, exclusive interview) — a dual usage worth remembering since it's not purely economic.
