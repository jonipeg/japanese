# ～(が)ゆえ(に)／(が)ゆえのN {#がゆえに}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～（が）故（に）／（が）故のN

**Furigana form:** ～（が）ゆえ（に）／（が）ゆえのN

**Meaning:** "Because of X / owing to X" — a literary causal connector explaining a reason, often for an unusual or noteworthy result.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞（＋である）＋（が）ゆえに
- い形容詞／な形容詞語幹＋（である）＋（が）ゆえに
- 名詞＋（が）ゆえのN（attributive form before a noun）
- Example base: 若さ → 若さゆえに（若いがゆえに）

**Typical context:** Formal, written/literary register; appears in essays, formal speeches, and poetic or elevated prose to state a cause, especially for an outcome that's slightly unexpected or dramatic. Not used in casual speech.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **から／ので** — the everyday, neutral causal connectors; ゆえに is a far more formal/literary equivalent, essentially interchangeable in meaning but drastically different in register.
- [**だけに**](grammar-だけに.md) — "precisely because X"; shares some of ゆえに's "given this cause, an intensified/notable result follows" flavor, but だけに is less archaic and more usable in neutral writing.
- [**とあって**](grammar-とあって・とあっては・あれば.md) — "given that it's X (a special circumstance)"; a related formal causal connector, typically used for circumstantial/situational causes rather than ゆえに's broader, more philosophical "because of this very nature/quality" causal framing.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a result stated without any causal link (にもかかわらず、"despite that") would contrast with ゆえに's explicit cause-effect framing.

**Example sentences:**
1. 若さゆえの過ちだったと、彼は反省していた。 (He reflected that it had been a mistake born of youth.)
2. 完璧を求めるがゆえに、彼はいつもストレスを抱えていた。 (Precisely because he sought perfection, he was always carrying stress.)
3. 貧しさゆえに、多くの子どもが学校に通えずにいる。 (Because of poverty, many children are unable to attend school.)
4. 彼女は正直であるがゆえに、時に人を傷つけてしまう。 (Precisely because she is honest, she sometimes ends up hurting people.)
5. 愛するがゆえの厳しさだったのだろう。 (It was probably strictness born out of love.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ゆえに is strongly literary and often used to frame a cause-effect relationship as almost philosophically inevitable or poignant (e.g., "precisely BECAUSE of this admirable/notable trait, this result followed") — using it for mundane, everyday causes sounds overly dramatic; から or ので would be more natural there. Note the attributive form ゆえのN (as in 若さゆえの過ち) directly modifies a following noun, distinct from the adverbial ゆえに used before a clause.
