# ～ときたら {#ときたら}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ときたら

**Furigana form:** ～ときたら

**Meaning:** "Speaking of X..." — introduces a topic to make a critical or exasperated remark about it (occasionally also to praise it as the best example), setting up a judgment that follows.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + ときたら
- Example base: うちの猫 → うちの猫ときたら

**Typical context:** Spoken, casual-to-neutral register; conveys a familiar, often complaining or mock-exasperated tone about someone or something well known to the speaker. Common in everyday conversation, not formal writing.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **ときた日には** — an intensified variant culminating a list of complaints with ときたら's topic-introducing function, used for a more dramatic, cascading complaint.
- **といったら（ありはしない）** — "indescribably X"; shares the complaining/exclamatory flavor but intensifies a following adjective's degree, rather than introducing a topic for subsequent commentary.
- **は** (plain topic particle) — the neutral, unmarked way to introduce any topic; ときたら specifically signals an upcoming critical or strongly opinionated remark, which plain は does not inherently imply.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a neutral topic introduction without implied judgment (について言えば, "speaking of X" neutrally) contrasts with ときたら's built-in critical/exasperated framing.

**Example sentences:**
1. うちの子ときたら、全然勉強しようとしない。 (Speaking of my kid, he just won't study at all.)
2. この店の店員ときたら、いつも態度が悪い。 (Speaking of the staff at this shop, their attitude is always bad.)
3. あの政治家ときたら、口先だけで何も実行しない。 (Speaking of that politician, it's all talk and no action.)
4. 彼女の料理の腕ときたら、プロ顔負けだ。 (Speaking of her cooking skills, they put professionals to shame — in a good way.)
5. 最近の天気ときたら、本当に予測がつかない。 (Speaking of the weather lately, it's really unpredictable.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ときたら most commonly introduces a complaint or exasperated observation about a familiar topic (a family member, a regular annoyance), but as example 4 shows, it can occasionally introduce enthusiastic praise about something impressively notable — context determines whether the tone is negative or admiring. It is distinctly casual and conversational, with a personal, "let me tell you about this" flavor, so it shouldn't appear in formal writing.
