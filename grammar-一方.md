# 一方 {#一方}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** 一方

**Furigana form:** いっぽう

**Meaning:** "On the other hand, meanwhile" — a conjunction contrasting two parallel facts or perspectives, more neutral and structural than emotional.

**Conjugation:** Sentence-initial conjunction, no conjugation needed — attaches at the sentence boundary, connecting the previous sentence to the next. Can also attach directly after a plain-form verb/clause mid-sentence as 〜一方(で) to contrast two aspects of the same topic within one sentence (e.g. 便利である一方、危険も伴う).

**Typical context:** Written/formal-leaning register, common in news reports, essays, and analytical writing that lays out two sides of an issue (e.g. one group's situation vs. another's, or one aspect of a topic vs. another). Usable in neutral spoken contexts like presentations or the news, but less common in casual daily conversation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**しかし**](grammar-しかし.md) — standard written "however"; negates/counters the preceding statement, whereas 一方 presents a parallel, complementary perspective rather than negating it.
- [**だが**](grammar-だが.md) — literary/terse "but"; also a direct contrast/negation, unlike 一方's side-by-side framing.
- [**それなのに**](grammar-それなのに.md) — emotional "and yet"; 一方 is neutral and structural, without the frustration nuance.
- **けれども/でも/だけど** — direct contrastive "but" conjunctions across formality levels; all negate/contrast the prior clause, whereas 一方 juxtaposes two separate facts without necessarily implying one is wrong or disappointing.
- **その反面** — similar nuance to 一方, highlighting a contrasting side/drawback of the same subject, often slightly more negative-leaning than neutral 一方.

**Opposite grammar structures:** しかし/だが (direct negating contrast) are the functional opposite in that they counter the first statement, while 一方 merely presents a parallel counterpart without negation. そして/また (pure addition) is the opposite in that it doesn't contrast at all.

**Example sentences:**
1. 都市部では人口が増えている。一方、地方では過疎化が進んでいる。
   (In urban areas, the population is increasing. On the other hand, depopulation is progressing in rural areas.)
2. 兄は医者になった。一方、弟は画家の道を選んだ。
   (The older brother became a doctor. Meanwhile, the younger brother chose the path of a painter.)
3. この技術は便利である一方、プライバシーの問題も抱えている。
   (This technology is convenient, but at the same time it also carries privacy concerns.)
4. 売上は好調だった。一方、コストも大きく増加していた。
   (Sales were strong. On the other hand, costs had also increased significantly.)
5. 彼女は仕事に厳しい。一方で、部下への気配りも忘れない。
   (She is strict about work. At the same time, she never forgets to be considerate toward her subordinates.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often use 一方 as a direct substitute for しかし, but 一方 doesn't necessarily imply the second fact is negative or contradicts the first — it simply presents a parallel or complementary fact/perspective, often about a different subject (brother vs. brother, urban vs. rural) rather than negating the first clause's claim. Confusing them can make a sentence sound like it's claiming a contradiction where none is intended.
