# ～ことこのうえない {#ことこのうえない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ことこの上ない

**Furigana form:** ～ことこのうえない

**Meaning:** "Extremely X, beyond compare" — a literary intensifier stating that a quality reaches its utmost extreme, with nothing surpassing it.

**Conjugation:**
- な形容詞語幹 + な + こと + この上ない
- い形容詞 + こと + この上ない
- Example base: 見苦しい → 見苦しいことこの上ない

**Typical context:** Formal, written/literary register; used in elevated prose, editorials, and formal criticism to intensify a (usually negative) evaluative adjective. Rare in casual speech.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **きわまりない／きわまる** — "extremely X, in the extreme" (e.g., 失礼きわまりない); nearly synonymous literary intensifier, often interchangeable, though きわまりない attaches directly to the adjective stem without need for こと.
- **このうえもなく** — an adverbial variant meaning "to the utmost degree," used before a verb/adjective rather than as this sentence-final nominal-based pattern.
- **非常に／極めて** — plain adverbial intensifiers ("extremely"); much more neutral and usable in any register, lacking the literary, almost rhetorical flourish of ことこの上ない.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; conceptually 普通だ／ありふれている ("ordinary/commonplace") expresses the reverse — an unremarkable degree.

**Example sentences:**
1. 遺産を巡る肉親同士の言い争いは見苦しいことこの上ない。 (The dispute among family members over the inheritance is unsightly in the extreme.)
2. 彼の無礼な態度は不愉快なことこの上なかった。 (His rude attitude was utterly displeasing.)
3. その演説は感動的なことこの上なかった。 (That speech was moving beyond compare.)
4. 突然の裏切りに、彼女の怒りは激しいことこの上なかった。 (At the sudden betrayal, her anger was fierce beyond measure.)
5. この景色の美しさは言葉にできないことこの上ない。 (The beauty of this scenery is indescribable in the extreme.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ことこの上ない is a literary, almost old-fashioned construction, so overusing it in casual speech sounds stilted — reserve it for formal writing or dramatic rhetorical effect. It is most frequently paired with negative evaluative adjectives (見苦しい, 不愉快, 無礼) but, as shown above, can also intensify positive ones (感動的, 美しい) — the key constant is "to an extreme, unsurpassed degree," not inherent negativity.
