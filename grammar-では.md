# では {#では}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** では（漢字なし／平仮名のみ）

**Furigana form:** では

**Meaning:** "Well then, in that case" — a transitional conjunction used to move from the preceding topic/statement to a new action, topic, or next step.

**Conjugation:** Sentence-initial conjunction, no conjugation needed — attaches at the sentence boundary, connecting the previous sentence/situation to the next action or topic being introduced. It is also commonly used as a short stand-alone transitional phrase before a command/invitation (では、始めましょう "well then, let's begin") rather than linking two clauses within one sentence.

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal, usable in both written and spoken registers — natural in polite conversation, presentations, meetings, classroom instructions, and written transitions in formal documents. It is the neutral/written-leaning form of this "well then" function, less casual than its spoken contraction じゃ, and less performative/polished than それでは, which is often used by hosts/announcers.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**じゃ**](grammar-じゃ.md) — casual spoken contraction of では; used identically but far more informally, common in relaxed conversation among friends/family.
- [**それでは**](grammar-それでは.md) — more formal/polite spoken version, often used by hosts, announcers, teachers, or at the start of formal meetings/events to transition ("それでは、始めましょう" = "well then, let's begin").
- [**それから**](grammar-それから.md) — purely sequential "after that, next"; lacks では's function of actively transitioning to a new topic/action/decision point.
- [**そこで**](grammar-そこで.md) — narrates a decision/action taken in response to a situation; では instead marks a transition to the next step/topic, not necessarily in response to a problem.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; it functions as a topic/action transition marker rather than a logical or causal connector, so it has no natural contrastive counterpart.

**Example sentences:**
1. では、次の議題に移りましょう。
   (Well then, let's move on to the next agenda item.)
2. では、お先に失礼します。
   (Well then, I'll be heading out ahead of you / excuse me for leaving first.)
3. A: 準備はできましたか? B: はい。 A: では、始めます。
   (A: Are you ready? B: Yes. A: Well then, let's begin.)
4. では、ここからは質疑応答の時間にしたいと思います。
   (Now then, let's move to the Q&A session from here.)
5. では、具体的な数字を見ていきましょう。
   (Well then, let's take a look at the specific figures.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The most common register mistake is using では in very casual conversation where じゃ would sound more natural (e.g. saying では、行こう to a close friend sounds stiff compared to じゃ、行こう), and conversely using じゃ in a formal meeting or presentation where では or それでは is expected. Learners should also distinguish では as a transitional conjunction from では as the contracted form of “だ + は” used in negation/topic-marking constructions (e.g. 学生ではない); the conjunction では discussed here always appears sentence-initially as a standalone transition word, not attached to a preceding noun/adjective.
