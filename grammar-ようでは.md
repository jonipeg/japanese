# ～ようでは {#ようでは}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～ようでは

**Furigana form:** ～ようでは

**Meaning:** "If things continue to be like ~ (an undesirable state), the result will be bad" — points out an undesirable current state or habit and predicts that it will lead to an unfavorable result if it continues.

**Conjugation:**
- 普通形（ナ形幹-な／-である・名詞幹-である）＋ ようでは
- 小さな失敗をいちいち気にするようでは、この会社ではやっていけないよ — "if you're going to worry about every little mistake like this, you won't be able to make it at this company"

**Typical context:** Spoken and written register, mildly admonishing or worried tone — used when warning, scolding, or expressing concern about someone's (or one's own) undesirable habit or situation and its likely bad consequences.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～ようじゃ** — a contracted, more casual/colloquial version of ～ようでは, used in spoken Japanese, especially by older or more blunt speakers.
- **～のでは** — "if it's the case that ~," a broader conditional that can carry a similar "given this, there's a problem" nuance, but without the built-in implication that the preceding fact is specifically something undesirable.
- [**～ては**](grammar-ては.md) — a more general conditional expressing a negative cycle or repeated bad outcome (e.g. 食べては寝て, "eating and then sleeping, over and over"), sometimes overlapping with ようでは's cautionary tone.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a structure complimenting a positive habit and predicting a good outcome, like ～ようなら安心だ ("if it's like ~, that's reassuring"), would express the reverse sentiment.

**Example sentences:**
1. 小さな失敗をいちいち気にするようでは、この会社ではやっていけないよ。
   (If you're going to fuss over every little mistake like this, you won't last at this company.)
2. ああ、僕は忘れっぽくて困る。こんなにすぐ忘れるようでは、この先のことが心配だ。
   (Ugh, I'm so forgetful it's a problem. If I keep forgetting things this quickly, I'm worried about the future.)
3. 報告書にこんなにミスが多いようでは、安心して仕事を任せられない。
   (If there are this many mistakes in the report, I can't comfortably entrust this work to you.)
4. 毎日こんなに残業しているようでは、体を壊してしまうよ。
   (If you keep working overtime like this every day, you're going to ruin your health.)
5. 約束の時間に毎回遅れるようでは、信用をなくしてしまう。
   (If you keep showing up late every time like this, you'll lose people's trust.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The clause before ようでは must describe a fact the speaker views as undesirable or problematic — this pattern cannot be used to introduce a neutral or good state of affairs. The clause that follows almost always predicts or warns of a negative consequence (inability to continue, loss of trust, health problems, etc.), making the whole sentence function as a warning or expression of concern rather than a simple observation.
