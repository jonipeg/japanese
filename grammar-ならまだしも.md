# ～ならまだしも {#ならまだしも}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ならまだしも

**Furigana form:** ～ならまだしも *(no kanji; written in kana)*

**Meaning:** "If it were just X, that would be bearable/acceptable, but (Y, the actual case, is not)" — sets a threshold of tolerance at X and indicates the real situation (Y) exceeds or fails to meet it, triggering complaint or disapproval.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 (noun) + はまだしも／ならまだしも
- Both はまだしも and ならまだしも are attested, following a noun phrase representing the "bearable" hypothetical scenario, followed by a clause describing the actual, less tolerable situation.

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal, usable in both spoken complaints and formal written criticism. Carries a mildly disapproving or exasperated tone — commonly used when voicing a complaint about something exceeding a reasonable limit (e.g., being asked to work on a busy weekend vs. a quiet weekday).

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**としたことが**](grammar-としたことが.md) — means "X, of all people" (surprise/self-reproach at an uncharacteristic mistake); lacks the explicit tolerance-threshold comparison structure of ならまだしも.
- [**におかれましては**](grammar-におかれましては.md) — an ultra-formal honorific topic marker; entirely unrelated in function (no tolerance/complaint nuance).
- **ときた日には** — means "when it comes to X" (introducing a negative characteristic complaint); differs because ならまだしも specifically compares a bearable hypothetical case against a worse actual case, whereas ときた日には is a general complaint-introducer without that explicit two-case comparison.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct single opposite, but はもとより ("not to mention X, [Y too]," additive-affirmative) moves in a conceptually opposite direction, affirming inclusion rather than drawing a tolerance line that gets crossed. No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 平日はまだしも、忙しい週末にアルバイトに休まれると、困るんだが。
   (If it were a weekday, that'd be one thing, but having my part-timer take the day off during a busy weekend is a real problem.)
2. 一回ならまだしも、同じ失敗を三回も繰り返すとは呆れる。
   (If it happened once, that'd be understandable, but repeating the same mistake three times is exasperating.)
3. 遅刻一回ならまだしも、毎日遅れてくるのは困る。
   (Being late once would be tolerable, but coming late every single day is a problem.)
4. 子供の発言ならまだしも、大人がそんなことを言うのは許されない。
   (If it were a child's remark, that'd be one thing, but it's unacceptable for an adult to say such a thing.)
5. 少額ならまだしも、これだけの金額を無断で使うのは問題だ。
   (If it were a small amount, that'd be tolerable, but using this much money without permission is a problem.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Confused on graded tests with としたことが, におかれましては, and ときた日には. The key nuance for students: ならまだしも always sets up an explicit two-part "bearable hypothetical vs. actual worse case" comparison, and the sentence's second half inevitably expresses complaint, disapproval, or disbelief about exceeding that threshold. This differs from としたことが (surprise at a single lapse, no threshold comparison) and におかれましては (honorific, no complaint at all).
