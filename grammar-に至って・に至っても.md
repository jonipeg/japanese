# ～に至って・～に至っても {#に至って・に至っても}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～に至って・～に至っても

**Furigana form:** ～にいたって・～にいたっても

**Meaning:** ～に至って: only once a situation had progressed as far as X did a certain state finally come about (often implying "too late"). ～に至っても: even though the situation progressed as far as X, a certain state still failed to come about.

**Conjugation:**
- 名・動辞書形 + に至って・に至っても
- 死者が出るに至って — "only once deaths occurred"

**Typical context:** Formal written register — common in news reports and commentary describing serious situations that escalated to an extreme stage before (or despite) producing a response.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～てはじめて** — "only after X did..." — a more neutral, everyday way to express the same "too late" realization.
- **～にようやく** — "finally, at last" — shares the sense of a long-delayed outcome, but without に至って's implication that the situation had to reach an extreme stage first.

**Opposite grammar structures:** ～に至っても itself supplies the contrasting nuance (reaching an extreme point yet nothing changes); more generally, ～する前に ("before reaching X") implies acting proactively rather than too late.

**Example sentences:**
1. 死者が出るに至って、国は初めて病気の感染拡大の深刻さに気がついたのだ。
   (It was only once deaths occurred that the government finally recognized the seriousness of the disease's spread.)
2. 病気の牛20万頭が処分されるに至って、経済的混乱がマスコミに取り上げられるようになった。
   (Only once 200,000 sick cattle had to be culled did the media begin covering the resulting economic turmoil.)
3. ひどい症状が出るに至っても、彼は病院へ行こうとしなかった。
   (Even once severe symptoms appeared, he still refused to go to the hospital.)
4. 大切な情報がインターネットで流れるという事態に至っても、ことの重大さを認識できない人たちがいる。
   (Even once a situation arose where important information leaked onto the internet, there are still people who fail to grasp the gravity of it.)
5. 会社の業績が赤字に転落するに至って、経営陣はようやく改革に乗り出した。
   (It was only once the company's earnings fell into the red that management finally set out to reform.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Attaches to an extreme or unusual state of affairs that goes beyond what would normally be expected. The clause after に至って describes what finally happened once things reached that point (often "too late"); the clause after に至っても describes what still failed to happen despite reaching that point — don't confuse the two forms' opposite implications.
