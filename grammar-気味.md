# ～気味 {#気味}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～気味

**Furigana form:** ～ぎみ

**Meaning:** "A bit, slight tendency toward (usually negative)" — indicates a mild degree or leaning toward an undesirable state, symptom, or trend.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + 気味
- 動詞ます形（stem）+ 気味
- 風邪気味, 疲れ気味, 太り気味

**Typical context:** Spoken and written, neutral-casual register — very common in daily conversation, especially regarding health, mood, and trends.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**っぽい**](grammar-っぽい.md) — "-ish, tends to be," a broader expression for characteristics/appearance, not limited to negative physical or mental states.
- [**がち**](grammar-がち.md) — "tends to (do), prone to," a similarly mild-tendency nuance but used for recurring actions/habits rather than a current state.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite — 気味 is inherently about a slight degree, so there's no natural antonym construction.

**Example sentences:**
1. 最近風邪気味なので、早く寝ます。
   (I've had a bit of a cold feeling lately, so I'll go to bed early.)
2. ここのところ疲れ気味で、集中できない。
   (I've been a bit tired lately and can't concentrate.)
3. 彼は最近太り気味だと気にしている。
   (He's worried that he's been gaining a bit of weight lately.)
4. 最近残業が増え気味で、体調が心配だ。
   (Overtime has been increasing a bit lately, and I'm worried about my health.)
5. 会議の議論は少し脱線気味だった。
   (The meeting's discussion drifted a bit off-topic.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 気味 almost always implies a negative or undesirable leaning — avoid using it for clearly positive tendencies (そのチームは勝ち気味 sounds odd). It attaches directly to a noun or masu-stem with no の needed before 気味.
