# ～んばかりだ {#んばかりだ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～んばかりだ

**Furigana form:** ～んばかりだ

**Meaning:** "As if about to ~" / "Looking as though it would ~ at any moment" — describes a state or action that is so extreme it looks almost like something else is about to happen, even though it doesn't actually reach that point.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ない形 ＋ んばかりだ （例外：する→せんばかりだ）
- 頭を畳につけんばかりにしてわびた — "apologized as if about to press his head right into the tatami"

**Typical context:** Written, literary/descriptive register — common in narrative prose, novels, and vivid descriptions of a scene or someone's intense emotion or action. Rare in everyday conversation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**～ばかりに**](grammar-ばかりに.md) — "precisely because ~," a different grammar point built on the same ばかり but meaning "solely due to (an unfortunate cause)"; easy to confuse by appearance but unrelated in meaning.
- **～そうだ (様態)** — "looks like it's about to ~," a more basic and common way to express an imminent-seeming state, but んばかりだ is more literary and emphasizes an exaggerated, almost-but-not-quite degree.
- [**～とばかりに**](grammar-とばかりに.md) — related in form (both use ばかり) and also describes an implied, unspoken attitude, but とばかりに attaches to a quoted/implied utterance rather than a verb's ない形.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a plain descriptive statement without ばかり would simply state the fact without the "as if on the verge of" exaggeration.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は力強くうなずいた。任せろと言わんばかりだった。
   (He nodded firmly, as if to say "leave it to me.")
2. 頭を畳につけんばかりにしてわびたのに、父は許してくれなかった。
   (She apologized so deeply she almost pressed her head to the tatami, but my father still wouldn't forgive her.)
3. 演奏が終わったとき、会場には割れんばかりの拍手が起こった。
   (When the performance ended, applause broke out so loud it seemed like it would bring the hall down.)
4. かごいっぱい、あふれんばかりのさくらんぼをいただいた。
   (I was given a basket of cherries so full it looked ready to overflow.)
5. 子供は今にも泣き出さんばかりの顔でこちらを見ていた。
   (The child looked at me with an expression as if he were about to burst into tears.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** んばかりだ describes a state that is close to, but doesn't actually reach, the extreme it resembles (the tatami wasn't literally touched, the hall didn't literally break) — using it for something that genuinely did happen is incorrect. Remember the irregular conjugation of する, which becomes せんばかりだ rather than しんばかりだ.
