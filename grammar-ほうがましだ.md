# ～ほうが（まだ）ましだ／だけ（まだ）ましだ {#ほうがましだ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～方が（まだ）増しだ／だけ（まだ）増しだ

**Furigana form:** ～ほうが（まだ）ましだ／だけ（まだ）ましだ

**Meaning:** "X would be (relatively) better / at least glad it's not worse" — compares two undesirable options and marks one as the lesser evil, or acknowledges a bad situation could have been worse.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形／た形 + ほうが（まだ）ましだ (comparing two options)
- 動詞・名詞 + だけ（まだ）ましだ (acknowledging a silver lining: もらえるだけましだ)

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-casual spoken register; conveys a resigned, somewhat bitter or self-consoling tone. Common in everyday complaints and consolations.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **よりはいい** — "is better than X"; a simpler, more neutral comparison, lacking ましだ's resigned "lesser of two evils" flavor.
- **たほうがいい** — plain advice "you should do X"; a recommendation construction, distinct from ほうがましだ's comparison between two already-bad options.
- [**とはいえ**](grammar-とはいえ.md) — "although X, still Y"; a general concessive connector, whereas だけましだ specifically frames a situation as having one redeeming, consoling aspect.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; conceptually もっと悪い ("even worse") would reverse the consoling framing into a more negative comparison.

**Example sentences:**
1. こんなに苦しいなら、死んだほうがましです。 (If it's this painful, I'd rather be dead.)
2. もらえるだけまだましですよ。 (At least you're getting something — be glad for that much.)
3. 遅刻するくらいなら、休むほうがまだましだ。 (Rather than being late, it's better to just take the day off.)
4. 給料は減ったが、クビになるよりはましだ。 (My salary was cut, but it's better than being fired.)
5. 雨が降っただけで済んだだけまだましだった。台風が直撃しなくてよかった。 (It was a relief it only rained — at least that's better; good thing the typhoon didn't hit directly.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ましだ always implies a comparison against something worse, even when that worse alternative isn't explicitly stated — learners should recognize the resigned, "at least it's not as bad as it could be" undertone rather than reading it as genuine praise or satisfaction. Don't confuse 増し/まし (ましだ, "relatively better") with the humble/polite auxiliary ます — they are unrelated despite similar sound.
