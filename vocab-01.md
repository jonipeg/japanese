## 鬱々 (うつうつ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** gloomy, melancholy, pessimistic

**Similar words:**
- 憂鬱（ゆううつ）— the more common noun/na-adjective for "depressed/gloomy"; 鬱々 is more literary and emphasizes a drawn-out, stagnant low mood.
- [陰気](vocab-07.md#陰気-いんき)（いんき）— describes a gloomy atmosphere or personality trait, more about outward impression than inner feeling.
- [落ち込む](vocab-10.md#落ち込む-おちこむ)（おちこむ）— a verb for "to feel down"; more casual and event-triggered, whereas 鬱々 describes a lingering state.

**Opposite words:**
- 朗らか（ほがらか）— cheerful, bright
- 晴れやか（はればれ）— clear, cheerful (lit. and fig.)

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は最近、鬱々とした顔をしている。— He's had a gloomy look on his face lately.
2. 雨が続いて気分が鬱々としてくる。— With the rain continuing, I'm starting to feel melancholy.
3. 鬱々とした毎日を送っていた彼女が、ようやく笑顔を見せた。— She, who had been living gloomy day after day, finally smiled.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often use 鬱々 as a casual everyday word like 憂鬱, but it's a somewhat literary, descriptive word (often 鬱々とした/鬱々と) rather than something you'd say about a one-off bad mood — it implies a prolonged, heavy atmosphere of gloom.

## 患者 (かんじゃ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** patient

**Similar words:**
- 病人（びょうにん）— "sick person" in general, not necessarily someone under a doctor's care; 患者 specifically denotes someone being treated/seen by medical staff.
- 入院患者（にゅういんかんじゃ）— specifically an inpatient, a subset of 患者.
- 被験者（ひけんしゃ）— a test subject/research participant, different context (clinical trials, not general treatment).

**Opposite words:** no direct opposite (closest conceptual contrast is 医者/いしゃ "doctor," but that's a different role, not an antonym)

**Example sentences:**
1. この病院には多くの患者が通っている。— Many patients come to this hospital.
2. 患者の容体が急変した。— The patient's condition suddenly changed.
3. 医者は患者一人一人に丁寧に説明した。— The doctor carefully explained things to each patient.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes confuse 患者 (the person being treated) with 病人 (just means "a sick person," no clinical relationship implied). Also note 患者 is always a noun referring to a person, not a symptom or illness itself.

## 物騒 (ぶっそう)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** dangerous, unsettled, troubled, disturbed, insecure

**Similar words:**
- 危険（きけん）— plain "dangerous," more direct and about a specific hazard; 物騒 implies a general uneasy social atmosphere (e.g. a neighborhood feeling unsafe).
- 不穏（ふおん）— "unsettled/ominous," often used for political/social tension; overlaps with 物騒 but is more formal/written.
- 治安が悪い（ちあんがわるい）— "public order is bad," a descriptive phrase covering the same idea as 物騒 but more explicit.

**Opposite words:**
- 平和（へいわ）— peaceful
- 安全（あんぜん）— safe

**Example sentences:**
1. 最近この辺りは物騒になった。— This area has gotten dangerous/unsettled lately.
2. 物騒な世の中になったものだ。— The world has become such a dangerous place.
3. 夜一人で歩くのは物騒だからやめなさい。— Walking alone at night is risky, so don't do it.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often translate 物騒 too literally as "dangerous" like 危険, missing that it specifically describes a general atmosphere of unease/crime risk in a place or era, rather than a concrete danger like a cliff or fire.

## 寸前 (すんぜん)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** just before, on the verge of, on the brink of, just in front of

**Similar words:**
- [直前](vocab-28.md#直前-ちょくぜん)（ちょくぜん）— "immediately before," more neutral/temporal; 寸前 often carries a dramatic nuance of narrowly avoiding something.
- 間際（まぎわ）— "right at the point of," similar nuance to 寸前, slightly more about timing than danger.
- あわや — an adverb expressing "narrowly escaped," often paired with 寸前 situations (あわや〜するところだった).

**Opposite words:** no direct opposite (the natural contrast is simply "long before," but there's no fixed antonym word)

**Example sentences:**
1. 電車に乗り遅れる寸前で間に合った。— I made it just before missing the train.
2. 彼は倒産寸前の会社を立て直した。— He turned around a company on the verge of bankruptcy.
3. 事故寸前で車を止めた。— I stopped the car just before an accident.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes use 寸前 for calm, neutral "before" situations (like "just before lunch"), but it's best reserved for moments of tension, danger, or a dramatic brink — for neutral timing, 直前 is more natural.

## 予め (あらかじめ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** beforehand, in advance, ahead of time, previously

**Similar words:**
- 前もって（まえもって）— near-synonymous and very common in speech; 予め is slightly more formal/written.
- 事前に（じぜんに）— "in advance," very common in business/formal contexts, largely interchangeable with 予め.
- 先に（さきに）— "ahead/first," broader meaning (can mean "earlier in a sequence") not always about advance preparation.

**Opposite words:**
- 後で（あとで）— afterward, later
- 事後に（じごに）— after the fact

**Example sentences:**
1. 予め予約しておいてください。— Please make a reservation in advance.
2. 会議の資料は予め配っておいた。— I distributed the meeting materials beforehand.
3. 予めご了承ください。— Please understand this in advance (common polite set phrase).

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often forget 予め is used with 〜ておく constructions and formal/written register; in casual speech 前もって is more natural. Also, the kanji reading あらかじめ is irregular (not a simple on/kun reading learners can guess), so it's often misread.

## 熟 (つくづく)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** deeply, keenly, severely, completely, utterly, intently, carefully

**Similar words:**
- しみじみ — "deeply, with quiet emotion," overlaps closely with つくづく when expressing heartfelt realization; しみじみ leans more sentimental/nostalgic.
- つくつく — a reading variant/onomatopoeic-adjacent form, essentially the same core meaning but less common in writing.
- 本当に（ほんとうに）— "truly, really," a much more generic intensifier without the reflective, introspective nuance of つくづく.

**Opposite words:** no direct opposite (it's an intensifying adverb, not something with a natural antonym)

**Example sentences:**
1. 人生について熟（つくづく）考えさせられた。— I was made to think deeply about life.
2. 自分の運のなさを熟感じる。— I keenly feel my own bad luck.
3. 熟いやになった。— I've become completely fed up (with something).

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This word is rarely written in kanji (熟) in modern usage — it's almost always written つくづく in hiragana, so learners who only know the kanji form may fail to recognize it in context. It's also an adverb expressing deep, often resigned reflection, not a plain intensity marker like とても.

## 筆記 (ひっき)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** (taking) notes, copying

**Similar words:**
- [メモ](vocab-19.md#メモ) — a casual loanword for "note/memo," used for quick jottings; 筆記 is more formal and associated with structured writing (e.g. exams, lectures).
- 記録（きろく）— "record," broader in scope (can be data, history, not just handwritten notes).
- ノート — the physical notebook or the act of note-taking in a casual/student context; overlaps informally with 筆記.

**Opposite words:** no direct opposite (closest contrast would be 口述/こうじゅつ "oral/dictation," since 筆記 implies written transcription)

**Example sentences:**
1. 筆記試験は来週行われます。— The written exam will be held next week.
2. 講義の内容を筆記した。— I took notes on the lecture content.
3. 筆記用具を忘れずに持ってきてください。— Please don't forget to bring writing materials.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes confuse 筆記 (the act/mode of writing by hand, as in 筆記試験 "written exam" vs 実技試験 "practical exam") with simply "notes" in general — 筆記 specifically emphasizes the written/handwritten mode, not just information recorded.

## 打ち解ける (うちとける)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** to open up (to someone), to become unreserved, to lower one's guard

**Similar words:**
- 馴染む（なじむ）— "to get used to/fit in," about adapting to an environment or group, not necessarily emotional openness with a person.
- 心を開く（こころをひらく）— "to open one's heart," very close in meaning but more literal/explicit phrasing; 打ち解ける is the more natural single-verb equivalent.
- 気を許す（きをゆるす）— "to let one's guard down" toward a specific person, emphasizing trust rather than general sociability.

**Opposite words:**
- 警戒する（けいかいする）— to be wary, on guard
- よそよそしい — distant, aloof (adjective, not verb, but the natural behavioral opposite)

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女とはすぐに打ち解けることができた。— I was able to open up to her quickly.
2. 新しいクラスでなかなか打ち解けられない。— I can't seem to open up in my new class.
3. 時間が経つにつれて、みんな打ち解けていった。— As time passed, everyone gradually opened up to each other.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes treat 打ち解ける as transitive ("to open someone up"), but it's intransitive — it describes the subject's own change in attitude/relaxation, always used with と (人と打ち解ける) rather than taking a direct object.

## おっとり (おっとり)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** gently, quietly, calmly (often describing a calm, easygoing personality)

**Similar words:**
- のんびり — "leisurely, relaxed," more about pace/activity level; おっとり is specifically about personality/demeanor (gentle, unhurried nature).
- [穏やか](vocab-02.md#穏やか-おだやか)（おだやか）— "calm, mild," overlaps closely but 穏やか can describe weather/situations too, while おっとり is almost always about a person's character.
- ゆったり — "at ease, roomy," often about physical space or relaxed movement rather than personality.

**Opposite words:**
- せかせか — fidgety, restless
- 気が短い（きがみじかい）— short-tempered

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女はおっとりした性格で、周りを安心させる。— She has a gentle, easygoing personality that puts people at ease.
2. おっとり構えていては、このビジネスでは勝てない。— You can't win in this business if you stay so laid-back.
3. 彼はおっとりしていて、めったに怒らない。— He's easygoing and rarely gets angry.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** おっとり can carry a slightly negative nuance of being "too slow/naive" depending on context, not purely positive "gentle" — learners should be careful that in some sentences it implies someone is a bit oblivious or impractically laid-back.

## ごっそり (ごっそり)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** completely, entirely, all, in large amounts, a lot (often of something being removed/taken away)

**Similar words:**
- [そっくり](vocab-24.md#そっくり) — "entirely, as it is," similar "whole amount" nuance, but also means "resembling closely" in other contexts (ambiguous); ごっそり is unambiguously about quantity/completeness of removal or gain.
- [すっかり](vocab-22.md#すっかり-すっかり) — "completely," broader usage (completely finished, completely forgot), while ごっそり is specifically about a large mass/amount moving or disappearing.
- たっぷり — "plenty, abundantly," positive/neutral connotation of abundance, without ごっそり's frequent nuance of something being taken or lost.

**Opposite words:** no direct opposite (it's a quantity adverb; the contrast would just be 少し "a little")

**Example sentences:**
1. 財布からお金をごっそり盗まれた。— A huge amount of money was stolen from my wallet.
2. 冷蔵庫の食べ物をごっそり食べられてしまった。— All the food in the fridge got completely eaten.
3. 台風で畑の作物がごっそりなくなった。— The typhoon wiped out the entire crop in the field.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often use ごっそり for any "a lot," but it specifically implies a large chunk being removed, lost, or taken all at once — it's rarely used for things simply being abundant and staying put (for that, たっぷり or 大量に are more natural).

## じっくり (じっくり)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** (slowly and) carefully, without haste, thoroughly, deliberately

**Similar words:**
- ゆっくり — "slowly," primarily about pace/speed; じっくり emphasizes careful, thorough engagement, not just slowness.
- 丁寧に（ていねいに）— "carefully, politely," about the manner/quality of doing something, which can overlap but doesn't inherently include the "taking time" aspect.
- 念入りに（ねんいりに）— "meticulously," very close in meaning, slightly more formal/written than じっくり.

**Opposite words:**
- 急いで（いそいで）— hurriedly
- 慌てて（あわてて）— in a fluster, hastily

**Example sentences:**
1. この問題はじっくり考えたほうがいい。— You should think this problem through carefully.
2. 彼とはじっくり話し合う必要がある。— I need to have a thorough discussion with him.
3. 休みの日はじっくり本を読みたい。— On my day off, I want to read books slowly and attentively.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes confuse じっくり with ゆっくり — ゆっくり is purely about reduced speed (e.g. ゆっくり歩く "walk slowly"), while じっくり implies deliberate, thorough attention to a task (thinking, discussing, examining), and sounds odd applied to simple physical actions like walking.

## 整然 (せいぜん)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** orderly, regular, systematic, well-organized

**Similar words:**
- [秩序](#秩序-ちつじょ)（ちつじょ）— "order" as a noun concept (the state of order itself), while 整然 is an adjectival/adverbial description of something being arranged orderly (整然とした).
- きちんと — "neatly, properly," more casual/everyday; 整然 is more formal/written, often describing rows, systems, or logical structure.
- 規則正しい（きそくただしい）— "regular, disciplined" (e.g. a regular lifestyle), focuses on routine/regularity rather than visual/structural order.

**Opposite words:**
- 乱雑（らんざつ）— disorderly, messy
- 雑然（ざつぜん）— cluttered, in disarray

**Example sentences:**
1. 本棚には本が整然と並んでいた。— The books were arranged neatly and orderly on the shelf.
2. デモ隊は整然と行進した。— The demonstrators marched in an orderly fashion.
3. 彼の論理は整然としていてわかりやすい。— His logic is systematic and easy to understand.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 整然 is almost always used as 整然と(した) — learners sometimes try to use it as a plain na-adjective without と, which sounds unnatural; it's an adverbial/descriptive word, closer to a fixed phrase than a flexible adjective.

## 敢然 (かんぜん)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** boldly, bravely, resolutely, determinedly

**Similar words:**
- 果敢（かかん）— "bold, daring," very close in meaning, often used as 果敢に; 敢然 is slightly more literary/formal, often paired with 立ち向かう (to confront).
- 勇敢（ゆうかん）— "brave, courageous," broader and more common; describes a trait, while 敢然 describes the manner of an action.
- 毅然（きぜん）— "resolute, firm," emphasizes unwavering composure rather than boldness in the face of danger.

**Opposite words:**
- [臆病](vocab-03.md#臆病-おくびょう)（おくびょう）— cowardly
- 弱腰（よわごし）— weak-kneed, timid (attitude)

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は困難に敢然と立ち向かった。— He boldly confronted the difficulty.
2. 敢然として不正に抗議した。— He resolutely protested against the injustice.
3. 兵士たちは敢然と戦った。— The soldiers fought bravely.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Easily confused with 完全（かんぜん, "complete/perfect”) due to identical pronunciation — learners must rely on kanji/context, since 敢然 (boldly) and 完全 (completely) are totally unrelated meanings that sound identical.

## 漠然 (ばくぜん)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** vague, obscure, indistinct, hazy, ambiguous

**Similar words:**
- [曖昧](vocab-06.md#曖昧-あいまい)（あいまい）— "ambiguous, unclear," very close, but 曖昧 often implies something deliberately or problematically unclear (e.g. an ambiguous answer), while 漠然 is more about a naturally vague, unformed idea or feeling.
- 不明瞭（ふめいりょう）— "unclear, indistinct," more formal/technical, often about clarity of expression or signals.
- ぼんやり — "vague, hazy," more casual, can describe a hazy mental state (absent-mindedness) as well as vague ideas.

**Opposite words:**
- 明確（めいかく）— clear, definite
- [はっきり](vocab-31.md#はっきり-はっきり) — clear, distinct

**Example sentences:**
1. 将来について漠然とした不安がある。— I have a vague anxiety about the future.
2. 彼の説明は漠然としていてよくわからない。— His explanation was vague and hard to understand.
3. 漠然としたイメージしか浮かばない。— Only a hazy image comes to mind.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes mix up 漠然 and 曖昧 — 漠然 describes something inherently unformed/unspecific (like a vague feeling or plan), whereas 曖昧 often implies ambiguity that could be clarified but isn't (like an evasive answer). Also always used as 漠然とした/漠然と, rarely as a plain predicate.

## 横たえる (よこたえる)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** to lay down, to wear (a sword, etc.) at one's side

**Similar words:**
- 寝かせる（ねかせる）— "to lay (someone) down to sleep/rest," more common and casual; 横たえる is more literary and can apply to objects, not just people.
- 横たわる（よこたわる）— the intransitive counterpart ("to lie down"), describing the subject itself reclining, vs 横たえる which takes an object (to lay something/someone down).
- 置く（おく）— generic "to place," lacks the specific horizontal/reclining nuance of 横たえる.

**Opposite words:**
- 起こす（おこす）— to raise up, to wake
- 立てる（たてる）— to stand (something) up

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は負傷した仲間を地面に横たえた。— He laid his wounded comrade down on the ground.
2. 侍は刀を腰に横たえていた。— The samurai wore his sword at his side.
3. 疲れた体をベッドに横たえた。— I laid my tired body down on the bed.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often confuse the transitive 横たえる (to lay something down) with the intransitive 横たわる (to lie down oneself) — 「体が横たえる」is incorrect; it should be 「体を横たえる」(transitive, with を) or 「体が横たわる」(intransitive, with が).

## 傾げる (かしげる／かたげる)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** to tilt (esp. head), to lean, to incline, to slant

**Similar words:**
- [傾ける](#傾ける-かたむける)（かたむける）— "to tilt/incline," broader usage (can tilt a cup, tilt effort toward something); 傾げる is narrower, almost always about tilting one's head (首を傾げる).
- かしぐ — "to lean, list (e.g. a structure)," more about physical objects losing balance than a deliberate head-tilt gesture.
- [うなずく](vocab-08.md#うなずく) — "to nod," a different, more specific gesture (vertical nod of agreement) vs the quizzical sideways tilt of 傾げる.

**Opposite words:** no direct opposite (it's a specific gesture verb without a natural antonym)

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は不思議そうに首を傾げた。— He tilted his head, looking puzzled.
2. 猫が音のする方に首を傾げた。— The cat tilted its head toward the sound.
3. その答えに先生は首を傾げた。— The teacher tilted her head at that answer, looking doubtful.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Almost exclusively used in the fixed expression 首を傾げる meaning "to tilt one's head in puzzlement/doubt" — learners should not confuse this with 傾ける (かたむける), which is the more general verb for tilting objects or devoting effort (耳を傾ける "to lend an ear").

## 穏便 (おんびん)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** gentle, peaceable, amicable, quiet, without fuss, simply

**Similar words:**
- [円満](vocab-03.md#円満-えんまん)（えんまん）— "harmonious, amicable" (often of relationships/settlements); 穏便 focuses more on handling a matter quietly/without escalation, while 円満 emphasizes a satisfying, harmonious outcome for all parties.
- 平和的（へいわてき）— "peaceful (in approach)," broader and more formal/political register.
- [穏やか](vocab-02.md#穏やか-おだやか)（おだやか）— "calm, mild," describes a general disposition or state, while 穏便 specifically describes how a matter/dispute is resolved (quietly, without conflict).

**Opposite words:**
- [強硬](vocab-02.md#強硬-きょうこう)（きょうこう）— hardline, firm/forceful
- 荒立てる（あらだてる）— to stir up trouble, make a scene

**Example sentences:**
1. この件は穏便に済ませたい。— I want to settle this matter quietly, without fuss.
2. 上司は穏便な解決を求めた。— The boss sought an amicable resolution.
3. できれば穏便にお願いします。— If possible, please handle this gently/without escalation.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 穏便 is almost always used in the context of resolving disputes or handling delicate situations (穏便に済ます/穏便な処置) — learners sometimes misuse it as a general personality adjective like "calm person," but for that, 穏やか is the correct word.

## 強情 (ごうじょう)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** obstinate, stubborn, headstrong

**Similar words:**
- [頑固](vocab-06.md#頑固-がんこ)（がんこ）— "stubborn, obstinate," very close in meaning and highly common; 頑固 often implies set-in-one's-ways habits/beliefs, while 強情 emphasizes refusing to yield in an argument or conflict.
- 意地っ張り（いじっぱり）— "stubborn, unyielding" due to pride/ego, often with a slightly childish or petty nuance.
- 頑な（かたくな）— "obstinate, inflexible," more literary, often describing a rigid refusal to change one's heart/attitude.

**Opposite words:**
- [素直](vocab-22.md#素直-すなお)（すなお）— obedient, meek, straightforward
- [柔軟](vocab-20.md#柔軟-じゅうなん)（じゅうなん）— flexible, adaptable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は強情で、人の意見を聞かない。— He's stubborn and won't listen to others' opinions.
2. 子供が強情を張って泣き止まない。— The child is being stubborn and won't stop crying.
3. そんなに強情を張らずに、謝ったらどうだ。— Don't be so stubborn — why don't you apologize?

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes treat 強情 and 頑固 as fully interchangeable, but 強情 more often appears in the set phrase 強情を張る ("to dig in one's heels/insist stubbornly in a dispute"), whereas 頑固 more naturally describes a lasting personality trait (頑固な性格/頑固おやじ).

## 出没 (しゅつぼつ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** making frequent appearances, appearing often (and disappearing), especially of animals/suspicious figures

**Similar words:**
- 頻出（ひんしゅつ）— "frequent occurrence," used more for abstract things like exam questions or patterns, not physical appearances of creatures/people.
- [現れる](vocab-07.md#現れる-あらわれる)（あらわれる）— plain "to appear," a basic verb without the repeated appear-and-vanish nuance that 出没 carries.
- うろつく — "to loiter, wander around," describes suspicious lingering in an area, overlapping with the "appearing around" sense but without the formal, reportorial tone of 出没.

**Opposite words:** no direct opposite (closest conceptual contrast would be 姿を消す "to disappear for good," but it's not a true antonym pair)

**Example sentences:**
1. この山ではクマが出没することがある。— Bears sometimes appear in this mountain area.
2. 最近、不審者が出没しているらしい。— Apparently a suspicious person has been appearing around here recently.
3. 夜になるとイノシシが畑に出没する。— At night, wild boars show up in the fields.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 出没 is specifically used for things that appear unpredictably and repeatedly (animals, suspicious people, phenomena) — it sounds unnatural applied to a scheduled or singular appearance (e.g. you wouldn't use it for "my friend showed up at the party").

## 執着 (しゅうちゃく／しゅうじゃく)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** attachment, adhesion, insistence, tenacity, fixation, obsession

**Similar words:**
- こだわり — "particularity, strong preference," often has a positive/neutral nuance (a chef's こだわり for ingredients), while 執着 often carries a negative connotation of unhealthy clinging.
- 固執（こしつ）— "persistence, sticking to (an opinion)," very close in meaning, often used for insisting on an idea/method rather than an emotional attachment to a person or thing.
- 未練（みれん）— "lingering regret/attachment," specifically about not being able to let go of a past relationship or lost opportunity.

**Opposite words:**
- 執着しない — not attached, unattached (no single-word antonym)
- 無欲（むよく）— free of desire/attachment

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼はお金に執着している。— He's obsessed with/fixated on money.
2. 過去の失敗に執着してはいけない。— You shouldn't cling to past failures.
3. 執着を捨てることで心が楽になった。— Letting go of my attachment made my heart feel lighter.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes use 執着 as a neutral word for "interest" or "preference," but it almost always carries a negative or at least heavy, obsessive connotation in modern usage — for a positive nuance of "particular about quality," こだわり is the natural choice instead.

## 思慮 (しりょ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** prudence, discretion, thought, consideration

**Similar words:**
- [配慮](vocab-31.md#配慮-はいりょ)（はいりょ）— "consideration (for others)," more about being thoughtful toward other people's feelings/circumstances, while 思慮 is more about careful, prudent thinking in general (including for oneself).
- 分別（ふんべつ）— "good sense, discretion," close in meaning, often used in 分別がある "to have good sense/be sensible."
- 熟考（じゅっこう）— "careful consideration," emphasizes the act of deep thinking on a specific matter, while 思慮 is more about a general quality/capacity for prudent thought.

**Opposite words:**
- [軽率](vocab-06.md#軽率-けいそつ)（けいそつ）— rash, thoughtless
- 無分別（むふんべつ）— imprudent, indiscreet

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は思慮深い人だ。— He's a thoughtful, prudent person.
2. もっと思慮を巡らせてから行動すべきだ。— You should think things through more carefully before acting.
3. 若気の至りで思慮に欠けた行動をした。— Out of youthful impulsiveness, I acted without due thought.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Most commonly seen in the compound 思慮深い (しりょぶかい, "prudent, thoughtful") — learners sometimes use 思慮 alone as a casual word for "thinking," but it's a somewhat formal/written noun usually paired with 深い or 欠ける, not used loosely like 考え.

## 密着 (みっちゃく)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** close adhesion, sticking firmly (to), being glued (to)

**Similar words:**
- 接着（せっちゃく）— "adhesion (bonding)," more technical/physical (glue, adhesives), while 密着 can also describe close relationships or media "sticking close" to a subject (密着取材 "close-up coverage").
- [くっつく](vocab-16.md#くっつく-くっつく) — casual verb for "to stick/cling to," used for both physical and emotional closeness, less formal than 密着.
- 癒着（ゆちゃく）— "collusion" (medically: abnormal tissue adhesion), used figuratively for improper, too-close ties (e.g. between business and government) — a negative counterpart to neutral 密着.

**Opposite words:**
- 乖離（かいり）— separation, divergence
- 離れる（はなれる）— to separate, move apart

**Example sentences:**
1. シャツが汗で肌に密着している。— The shirt is sticking tightly to my skin with sweat.
2. その記者は政治家に密着取材した。— The reporter did close-up, embedded coverage of the politician.
3. 二人は密着して歩いていた。— The two were walking very close together.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes confuse 密着 (close physical/figurative sticking, generally neutral) with 癒着 (collusion/improper closeness, negative) — 癒着 specifically implies an unhealthy or corrupt closeness (e.g. 政治と企業の癒着), while 密着 is neutral or even positive (close contact, detailed coverage).

## 体裁 (ていさい／たいさい)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** (outward) appearance, (proper) format (e.g. of an essay)

**Similar words:**
- 見栄（みえ）— "vanity, putting on appearances," more about pride/ego-driven image management, while 体裁 is broader (can refer to format/structure as well as social appearance).
- 格好（かっこう）— "appearance, shape," more casual and often about physical look/style rather than formal propriety.
- [面目](vocab-19.md#面目-めんぼくめんもく)（めんぼく）— "face, reputation," about social standing/honor, overlapping with 体裁 in the sense of "saving face" (体裁を保つ vs 面目を保つ).

**Opposite words:** no direct opposite (closest contrast is 本質/ほんしつ "substance/essence," as in the classic 体裁 vs 中身 contrast of "form vs. content")

**Example sentences:**
1. レポートは体裁を整えてから提出しなさい。— Fix the format properly before submitting the report.
2. 彼は体裁ばかり気にして中身がない。— He only cares about appearances and has no substance.
3. 世間体裁を気にして本音を言えない。— Worried about appearances/social image, I can't say what I really think.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 体裁 has two distinct senses — "outward appearance/image" (体裁を気にする) and "format/form of a document" (体裁を整える) — learners sometimes only learn one sense and are confused encountering the other; also note the reading たいさい is rare/archaic, ていさい is standard modern reading.

## 辛抱 (しんぼう)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** patience, endurance, perseverance

**Similar words:**
- 我慢（がまん）— "patience, endurance (of discomfort)," extremely common and close in meaning; 我慢 often implies suppressing a desire or discomfort in the moment, while 辛抱 implies enduring a difficult situation over a longer period.
- 忍耐（にんたい）— "perseverance, endurance," more formal/written, often used in abstract or moral contexts (忍耐力 "capacity for perseverance").

- 投げ出す（なげだす）— to give up, throw in the towel

3. 辛抱が足りなくてすぐに諦めてしまう。— I lack patience and give up too quickly.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Often confused with 我慢 — 我慢 is about enduring something unpleasant in the moment (我慢できない "I can't stand it"), while 辛抱 implies enduring a prolonged hardship toward an eventual goal (辛抱強い "patient/persevering" describes a long-term virtue, not momentary restraint).

#vocabulary

**Meaning:** impudent, shameless, brazen, forward, audacious, cheeky

**Similar words:**
- 厚かましい（あつかましい）— nearly identical meaning and very commonly paired/interchangeable; 厚かましい may feel slightly more old-fashioned/colloquial.
- 生意気（なまいき）— "cheeky, impertinent," focuses more on disrespectful attitude from someone perceived as inferior (e.g. a junior being cheeky to a senior), rather than general shamelessness.

## 只管 (ひたすら)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** intently, single-mindedly, devotedly, solely, earnestly

**Similar words:**
- 一心に（いっしんに）— "with one's whole heart/single-mindedly," very close in meaning, slightly more formal/literary.
- 専念する（せんねんする）— "to devote oneself (to)," a verb expressing the same single-minded focus, used differently grammatically (as a verb rather than an adverb).
- がむしゃらに — "recklessly/single-mindedly (without regard for consequences)," similar intensity but with a connotation of blind, unrestrained effort rather than calm devotion.

## 一向 (いっこう)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** completely, absolutely, totally, (not) at all, (not) a bit

**Similar words:**
- 全然（ぜんぜん）— "(not) at all," extremely common and largely interchangeable with 一向に in negative sentences, though 一向に is more formal/written.
- まったく — "completely, totally," overlaps closely, usable in both positive and negative contexts, whereas 一向 is almost always paired with negative predicates.
- さっぱり — "(not) at all, completely," colloquial equivalent often used for "I have no idea at all" (さっぱりわからない), similar register-shift to 一向.

1. 彼の態度は一向に変わらない。— His attitude hasn't changed at all.
2. 雨が一向にやまない。— The rain shows no sign of stopping at all.
3. 景気は一向に回復しない。— The economy isn't recovering in the slightest.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Almost exclusively used with negative verb forms (一向に〜ない) in modern Japanese — learners sometimes try to use it in positive sentences meaning "very much," which is an archaic usage now largely obsolete; modern 一向 functions essentially as an intensifier for negation, similar to 全然.

## 屈指 (くっし)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** leading, foremost, preeminent, outstanding, one of the best

**Similar words:**
- 有数（ゆうすう）— "one of the few notable," nearly synonymous, both used as 屈指の/有数の + noun to mean "among the best."
- トップクラス — loanword for "top-class," more casual/modern, used similarly but without the formal, written-register feel of 屈指.
- 随一（ずいいち）— "the foremost, the number one," stronger claim of being THE best, while 屈指 means "among the best" (one of a handful), not necessarily the single top.

**Opposite words:** no direct opposite (the natural contrast would just be 平凡 "ordinary/mediocre," but there's no fixed antonym)

**Example sentences:**
1. この店は街でも屈指の人気店だ。— This shop is one of the most popular in the whole town.
2. 彼は日本でも屈指のピアニストだ。— He's one of the leading pianists in Japan.
3. 屈指の難関大学に合格した。— He got into one of the most elite, hard-to-enter universities.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 屈指 means "one of the top few" (counted on one's fingers, lit. "bending fingers to count"), not "the single best" — learners sometimes use it interchangeably with 最高 ("the best"), but 屈指の〜 should be understood as "among the top-ranked," allowing for other equally ranked things.

## 還暦 (かんれき)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** 60th birthday (traditional milestone)

**Similar words:**
- 古希（こき）— the 70th birthday milestone, part of the same traditional milestone-birthday system (賀寿) as 還暦, just a different age.
- 喜寿（きじゅ）— the 77th birthday milestone, another member of the same set of traditional celebratory ages.
- 誕生日（たんじょうび）— plain "birthday," generic and age-neutral, lacking the specific cultural/ceremonial weight of 還暦.

**Opposite words:** no direct opposite (it's a specific cultural milestone, not a word with an antonym)

**Example sentences:**
1. 父が還暦を迎えたので、家族でお祝いをした。— My father turned 60, so the family celebrated together.
2. 還暦のお祝いには赤いちゃんちゃんこを贈る習慣がある。— There's a custom of giving a red vest as a gift for the 60th birthday celebration.
3. 還暦を過ぎても元気に働いている。— He's still working energetically even past his 60th birthday.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 還暦 isn't just "being 60 years old" generically — it specifically refers to completing the 60-year cycle of the Chinese zodiac calendar and "returning" to one's birth zodiac sign, carrying strong traditional/ceremonial significance (often marked with the gift of a red vest/cap), which learners may miss if they treat it as a plain age descriptor.

## 顧みる (かえりみる)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** to look back on, reflect on

**Similar words:**
- 省みる（かえりみる）— same reading, different kanji, meaning "to reflect on (oneself), examine one's conscience" — these are the core confusion pair, both pronounced かえりみる but with subtly different kanji-implied focus (顧みる = looking back/considering something external like the past or others; 省みる = introspective self-reflection).
- 慮る（おもんぱかる）— "to consider, be thoughtful of" (others' feelings/circumstances), a different reading, emphasizing careful consideration of someone else's situation rather than looking back at the past.
- 鑑みる（かんがみる）— "to consider in light of (precedent/a model)," used when judging a situation based on reference to past examples or standards, a more formal/legalistic nuance.
- 観る（みる）— simply "to view, watch," unrelated in nuance — just shares a visual-perception root, not part of the reflective-thought cluster.

**Opposite words:** no direct opposite (reflective/contemplative verb without a clean antonym)

**Example sentences:**
1. 自分の半生を顧みて、涙がこぼれた。— Looking back on half my life, tears welled up.
2. 彼は家庭を顧みずに仕事に没頭した。— He immersed himself in work without any regard for his family.
3. 過去の過ちを顧みることも大切だ。— It's important to look back and reflect on past mistakes.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This is a classic exam confusion cluster: 顧みる (look back on/consider, often with a nuance of "paying heed to," as in 家庭を顧みない "neglecting one's family") vs 省みる (introspective self-reflection, as in 自らを省みる "reflect on oneself") — both read かえりみる, so context and the specific kanji matter; also distinct from 慮る (おもんぱかる, consider others' feelings) and 鑑みる (かんがみる, judge based on precedent), which have different readings entirely despite similar meanings.

## 築く (きずく)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** to build, construct (abstract: build a relationship/fortune)

**Similar words:**
- [建てる](vocab-26.md#建てる-たてる)（たてる）— "to build (a physical structure, e.g. a house)," the key confusion pair: 建てる is strictly for physical construction (建物を建てる), while 築く is used for abstract things (信頼を築く "build trust," 財を築く "build a fortune") and sometimes larger-scale physical structures like fortresses/embankments.
- 構築する（こうちくする）— "to construct, build (a system)," more formal/technical, often used for abstract systems (関係を構築する), closely overlapping with abstract uses of 築く.
- 作り上げる（つくりあげる）— "to create, build up," more general-purpose verb for creating something through effort, without 築く's connotation of a solid, lasting foundation.

**Opposite words:**
- 壊す（こわす）— to destroy, break down
- [崩す](vocab-16.md#崩す-くずす)（くずす）— to collapse, tear down

**Example sentences:**
1. 二人は長年かけて信頼関係を築いた。— The two built a relationship of trust over many years.
2. 彼は一代で巨万の富を築いた。— He built a vast fortune in one generation.
3. 古代の人々は石で城壁を築いた。— Ancient people built stone city walls.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The key distinction tested is 築く vs 建てる — 建てる is reserved for erecting a specific physical building (家を建てる, ビルを建てる), while 築く is used for abstract construction (relationships, fortune, reputation) or larger structural/foundational works (城を築く, 堤防を築く); using 建てる for "build a relationship" is a common learner error.

## 寛容さ (かんようさ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** tolerance, magnanimity

**Similar words:**
- [寛容](#寛容-かんよう)（かんよう）— the base na-adjective/noun form ("tolerant" / "tolerance"); 寛容さ is the nominalized form emphasizing "the quality/degree of tolerance" (e.g. 寛容さを示す "show tolerance").
- 寛大さ（かんだいさ）— "the quality of being generous/magnanimous," from 寛大, which focuses more on generosity in forgiving or being lenient toward someone's faults, slightly more about leniency in judgment than open-mindedness toward differences.
- 包容力（ほうようりょく）— "capacity to accept/embrace (others)," emphasizes an emotionally embracing, accepting capacity, often used for personality warmth rather than philosophical/social tolerance.

3. 社会全体の寛容さが失われつつある。— Society's overall tolerance is gradually being lost.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** See the 寛容 vs 寛大 distinction below — 寛容さ (from 寛容) is about tolerating differing views/beliefs/people without judgment, while 寛大さ (from 寛大) is about generosity in forgiving faults or being lenient in punishment; learners often blur these since both translate loosely as "generous/tolerant" in English.

## 傾ける (かたむける)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** to tilt, incline; to devote (effort)

**Similar words:**
- [傾げる](#傾げる-かしげるかたげる)（かしげる）— "to tilt (one's head)," a narrower verb almost exclusively used for the head-tilting gesture of puzzlement, while 傾ける is the general-purpose verb for tilting any object plus figurative uses.
- [注ぐ](vocab-24.md#注ぐ-そそぐ)（そそぐ）— "to pour (into), devote (attention/effort)," overlaps in the figurative sense (全力を注ぐ vs 全力を傾ける, both "pour/devote all one's effort"), but 注ぐ is also literally "to pour liquid."
- 傾倒する（けいとうする）— "to be devoted to, enthralled by (an idea/person)," a more formal/intellectual devotion, often to ideologies or admired figures, vs 傾ける's broader "devote effort to a task."

**Opposite words:**
- 起こす（おこす）— to raise, set upright
- 立て直す（たてなおす）— to set back upright, rebuild

**Example sentences:**

#vocabulary

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 貫禄 implies a weighty, commanding presence typically built up through age, experience, or achievement — learners sometimes use it for someone merely "confident" or "attractive," but 貫禄 specifically connotes an earned seniority/gravitas (a rookie wouldn't typically be described as having 貫禄 unless noted as surprising, as in 若いのに貫禄がある).

**Similar words:**

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The reading is しきさい, not the invented しょくさい that some students mistakenly guess (perhaps by analogy with 食材/しょくざい "ingredients" or a misapplied reading of 色 as しょく) — 色 is read しき in this compound (as in 色彩, 色調), a reading worth memorizing specifically since it's less predictable than いろ or しょく.

## 衝撃 (しょうげき)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** shock, impact

**Similar words:**

- 退避（たいひ）— "taking shelter, withdrawing (to safety)," close in meaning to 避難, often used in more immediate/tactical contexts (e.g. taking cover during an attack).

**Meaning:** wing

**Similar words:**

- 及ばない（およばない）— to fall short of

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼の実力はプロに匹敵する。— His skill is comparable to that of a professional.
2. このワインは高級品に匹敵する味わいだ。— This wine has a taste comparable to high-end products.
3. 彼女の人気は国民的スターに匹敵する。— Her popularity rivals that of a national star.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The critical distinction is 匹敵 (being equal/comparable in strength, quality, or status — a qualitative comparison) vs 比例 (ひれい, "proportion," a mathematical/logical relationship describing how one variable changes in relation to another) — these are conceptually unrelated despite superficial reading similarity, and a common wrong-answer pattern is picking 比例 when the sentence actually calls for a comparison of equivalence/rivalry, not mathematical proportionality.

## 励む (はげむ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** to strive, work hard at

**Similar words:**
- [挑む](vocab-04.md#挑む-いどむ)（いどむ）— "to challenge, take on (a difficult task)," emphasizes confronting a specific difficulty or opponent, while 励む implies sustained, diligent effort toward improvement over time (practice, study) rather than a single confrontation.
- [望む](vocab-31.md#望む-のぞむ)（のぞむ）— "to wish for, hope for," about desiring an outcome, unrelated to the effort/diligence nuance of 励む — grouped here only as a surface-level nuance-cluster distractor.
- 精を出す（せいをだす）— "to work hard, put effort into," a near-synonymous idiomatic phrase, similar register to 励む.

**Opposite words:**
- 怠ける（なまける）— to be lazy, slack off
- サボる — to skip/shirk (casual)

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は日々の練習に励んでいる。— He's diligently working at his daily practice.
2. 学業に励んで奨学金を得た。— I worked hard at my studies and earned a scholarship.
3. 仕事に励む姿勢が評価された。— His diligent attitude toward work was appreciated.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 励む specifically describes putting sustained, diligent effort into an ongoing activity (practice, work, study) — learners sometimes confuse it with 挑む (which is about confronting/challenging a specific difficulty, often with an element of risk or competition) or 望む (which is about hoping/wishing, not effort at all); 励む always needs an object marked with に (〜に励む).

## ばてる (ばてる)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** to be exhausted, worn out

**Similar words:**
- 化ける（ばける）— "to transform, turn into (something else, e.g. a ghost/fox in folklore)" — the key confusion pair (see nuance note below), an entirely different and unrelated meaning despite a superficially similar sound shape.
- [疲れる](vocab-28.md#疲れる-つかれる)（つかれる）— "to get tired," the general everyday word for fatigue, while ばてる implies a more complete, often heat- or exertion-induced exhaustion (バテる from summer heat, after a hard workout).
- へとへとになる — "to become utterly worn out/exhausted," a common idiomatic phrase expressing a similar degree of complete exhaustion as ばてる.

**Opposite words:**
- 元気になる（げんきになる）— to regain energy, become lively
- 回復する（かいふくする）— to recover

**Example sentences:**
1. 夏の暑さでばててしまった。— I got completely worn out from the summer heat.
2. 長時間の運動でばてた。— I was exhausted from exercising for a long time.
3. 連日の残業でもうばてている。— I'm already worn out from days of overtime work.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The critical distinction is ばてる (to become exhausted/worn out, often from heat or exertion) vs 化ける (ばける, to transform/shapeshift into something else, e.g. a supernatural creature) — despite the superficially similar sound, these are completely unrelated; a common wrong-answer pattern is picking 化ける when the sentence is clearly describing physical exhaustion, not transformation. Note ばてる is usually written in kana, not with kanji.

## ふさわしい (ふさわしい)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** suitable, befitting

**Similar words:**
- 相応しい — the kanji form of the same word, used identically; mostly a matter of orthographic choice (kana vs kanji), not a separate near-synonym.
- [適切](vocab-29.md#適切-てきせつ)（てきせつ）— "appropriate, suitable," more neutral/formal, often used for actions/methods/decisions being correct or fitting, while ふさわしい often emphasizes matching someone's status, dignity, or the gravity of an occasion.
- 似合う（にあう）— "to suit, look good on (someone)," specifically about physical appearance/style matching a person (clothes, hairstyle), narrower than ふさわしい's broader sense of befitting dignity or circumstance.

**Opposite words:**
- 不相応（ふそうおう）— unsuitable, ill-matched
- 不釣り合い（ふつりあい）— mismatched, disproportionate

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼にふさわしい役職が与えられた。— He was given a position befitting him.
2. その服装はこの場にふさわしくない。— That outfit isn't suitable for this occasion.
3. 彼女は会長にふさわしい人物だ。— She's a person befitting the role of chairperson.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ふさわしい often carries a connotation of matching someone's dignity, status, or the solemnity of an occasion (ふさわしい服装, ふさわしい人物), distinct from more neutral/mechanical "appropriate" (適切) which is used for correct methods or procedures — learners sometimes use 適切 where ふさわしい's dignity-matching nuance would be more natural, and vice versa.

## 判決 (はんけつ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** (court) verdict, judgment

**Similar words:**
- 晩年（ばんねん）— "one's later years (of life)" — a completely unrelated word, included here only because it was the wrong answer students mistakenly picked on an exam, presumably due to some surface-level sound/kanji-shape similarity (判 vs 晩); learners should note these share no semantic connection.
- 裁判（さいばん）— "trial, court case," the broader legal process/proceeding, of which 判決 (the verdict) is the outcome/conclusion.
- 判定（はんてい）— "decision, ruling (judgment, often in sports or less formal contexts)," close in structure (shares 判) but used more broadly outside strict legal verdicts (e.g. a judge's ruling in a competition).

**Opposite words:** no direct opposite (it's a legal outcome noun without an antonym)

**Example sentences:**
1. 裁判所は有罪の判決を下した。— The court handed down a guilty verdict.
2. 判決を聞いて被告人は泣き崩れた。— Upon hearing the verdict, the defendant broke down in tears.
3. 最高裁の判決が注目されている。— The Supreme Court's ruling is drawing attention.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The wrong-answer pattern noted in source material (students picking 晩年 "one's later years" instead of 判決 "verdict") suggests a listening/reading mix-up based on superficial shape/sound rather than meaning — these words are entirely unrelated, so learners should anchor 判決 firmly to legal/courtroom contexts (裁判所, 有罪, 無罪) to avoid this confusion.

## 非行 (ひこう)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** delinquency, misconduct

**Similar words:**
- 飛行（ひこう）— "flight (flying), aviation" — an unrelated homophone, a classic same-reading confusion pair; context (crime/youth vs. aircraft/flying) is essential to distinguish them.
- 不良（ふりょう）— "delinquent (person), bad (quality)," often used as a noun for a delinquent youth (不良少年), closely related in the social-deviance sense but 不良 describes the person/type, while 非行 describes the acts/behavior itself.
- 犯罪（はんざい）— "crime," a more serious/formal legal term, while 非行 is often specifically used for youth misconduct/juvenile delinquency, not necessarily full-blown criminal acts.

**Opposite words:**
- 善行（ぜんこう）— good deed, virtuous conduct
- 模範的行動（もはんてきこうどう）— exemplary behavior

**Example sentences:**
1. 非行に走る若者が社会問題になっている。— Youths turning to delinquency have become a social problem.
2. 彼は中学時代、非行を繰り返していた。— He repeatedly engaged in delinquent behavior during junior high.
3. 非行少年の更生support を行う施設がある。— There are facilities that support the rehabilitation of delinquent youths.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The homophone 飛行 (ひこう, "flight/aviation") is a classic same-reading confusion pair with 非行 (ひこう, "delinquency") — identical pronunciation, unrelated meanings, distinguished only by kanji and context (非行少年 "delinquent youth" vs 飛行機 "airplane").

## 侮辱 (ぶじょく)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** insult, humiliation

**Similar words:**
- 負傷（ふじょう）— "injury, wound" — the key confusion pair (see nuance note below), an entirely unrelated meaning despite some surface similarity, and the wrong answer students commonly picked.
- 屈辱（くつじょく）— "humiliation, disgrace," very close in meaning, often describing the feeling of being humiliated (屈辱を味わう "to taste humiliation"), while 侮辱 emphasizes the act of insulting someone else.
- [軽蔑](vocab-17.md#軽蔑する-けいべつ)（けいべつ）— "contempt, scorn," about looking down on someone with disdain, related but distinct from 侮辱's active "insulting" action — 軽蔑 can be a silent attitude, while 侮辱 typically involves an overt insulting act or words.

**Opposite words:**
- 敬意（けいい）— respect
- 称賛（しょうさん）— praise, admiration

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼の発言は私を侮辱するものだった。— His remark was an insult to me.
2. そんな侮辱は許せない。— I can't forgive such an insult.
3. 彼女は人前で侮辱されて涙を流した。— She was insulted in front of others and shed tears.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The critical distinction is 侮辱 (ぶじょく, "insult/humiliation," an intentional act of disrespect) vs 負傷 (ふじょう, "injury/wound," a physical harm) — these are completely unrelated in meaning, but students have mistakenly picked 負傷 when the correct answer was 侮辱, likely due to surface reading/kanji-shape similarity rather than any semantic connection; always verify via kanji and context (verbal/social insult vs. physical injury).

## はかどる (捗る) (はかどる)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** to make good progress

**Similar words:**
- 進む（すすむ）— "to progress, move forward," the general-purpose word for progress of any kind, while はかどる specifically emphasizes things proceeding smoothly and efficiently (work, tasks getting done quickly).
- 順調に進む（じゅんちょうにすすむ）— "to proceed smoothly," a descriptive phrase conveying a similar sense of smooth progress, slightly more formal/explicit than the single verb はかどる.
- 捗（はかどり）— nominalized form sometimes seen in compound expressions, referring to "the degree of progress," derived from the same verb.

**Opposite words:**
- [滞る](vocab-30.md#滞る-とどこおる)（とどこおる）— to stagnate, be delayed
- 遅れる（おくれる）— to fall behind, be late

**Example sentences:**
1. 今日は仕事がはかどった。— My work made great progress today.
2. 静かな環境だと勉強がはかどる。— Studying goes smoothly in a quiet environment.
3. 体調が悪くて作業がはかどらない。— I'm not feeling well, so my work isn't progressing smoothly.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Almost always written in hiragana (はかどる) rather than the kanji form 捗る, which is rare in everyday writing — learners who only learn the kanji form may fail to recognize the common hiragana spelling in natural text; also note it's intransitive and typically describes tasks/work as the subject (仕事がはかどる), not a person actively "progressing."

## 磁気 (じき)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** magnetism

**Similar words:**
- [磁石](vocab-20.md#磁石-じしゃく)（じしゃく）— "magnet," the physical object that produces magnetism, distinct from 磁気 which is the abstract physical phenomenon/property itself.
- 磁力（じりょく）— "magnetic force," closely related, referring specifically to the force exerted by magnetism, a more specific technical term than general 磁気.
- 電気（でんき）— "electricity," a related but distinct physical phenomenon, often discussed alongside magnetism in physics (electromagnetism, 電磁気).

**Opposite words:** no direct opposite (it's a physics phenomenon noun without an antonym)

**Example sentences:**
1. 磁気を帯びた金属が引き寄せられた。— The magnetized metal was attracted.
2. 磁気カードの読み取りができなくなった。— The magnetic card could no longer be read.
3. 強い磁気が精密機器に影響を与えることがある。— Strong magnetism can sometimes affect precision instruments.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes confuse 磁気 (the abstract phenomenon/property of magnetism) with 磁石 (the concrete object, a magnet) — you'd say 磁石を使う ("use a magnet," object) but 磁気を帯びる ("be magnetized/carry magnetism," abstract property), and mixing up which noun fits which grammatical role is a common error.

## 消去 (しょうきょ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** deletion, elimination

**Similar words:**
- 削除（さくじょ）— "deletion, removal," very close in meaning and highly common, especially in digital/text contexts (データを削除する), largely interchangeable with 消去 in many IT contexts.
- 抹消（まっしょう）— "erasure, deletion (esp. from an official record)," more formal/legal, used for striking something from a registry or record (戸籍から抹消する).
- [取り消す](vocab-30.md#取り消す-とりけす)（とりけす）— "to cancel, retract," about nullifying an action, decision, or statement, rather than physically/digitally erasing data or marks.

**Opposite words:**
- 作成（さくせい）— creation (of a document/data)
- [追加](vocab-28.md#追加-ついか)（ついか）— addition

**Example sentences:**
1. 間違えたデータを消去した。— I deleted the incorrect data.
2. 黒板の文字を消去してください。— Please erase the characters on the blackboard.
3. 消去法で正解を導き出した。— I arrived at the correct answer by process of elimination.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 消去 is used both for literal erasing (blackboard, digital data) and the common idiomatic phrase 消去法 ("process of elimination," a method of deduction by ruling out options) — learners sometimes only learn the literal "delete" sense and miss this common figurative/methodological usage.

## 障害 (しょうがい)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** obstacle, disability, disorder

**Similar words:**
- 障がい／障碍 — alternative orthographic variants sometimes used (especially 障がい in official/sensitive contexts) to soften or avoid the kanji 害 ("harm") when referring to disability, reflecting social sensitivity around the term, though pronounced identically.
- 妨げ（さまたげ）— "hindrance, obstruction," closer to the "obstacle" sense specifically (something that gets in the way of progress), without 障害's additional medical/disability sense.
- 支障（ししょう）— "hindrance, impediment (to proceeding smoothly)," overlaps with the "obstacle" sense, often used in business/practical contexts (支障をきたす "to cause a hindrance"), without the medical connotation.

**Opposite words:** no direct opposite (it's a multi-sense noun — obstacle/disability/disorder — without a clean single antonym)

**Example sentences:**
1. 計画の実行にはいくつかの障害がある。— There are several obstacles to carrying out the plan.
2. 彼は視覚障害を持ちながら働いている。— He works despite having a visual impairment.
3. 睡眠障害に悩まされている。— I'm troubled by a sleep disorder.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 障害 covers three related but distinct senses — a physical/abstract obstacle (障害物, 障害を乗り越える), a disability (視覚障害, 身体障害者), and a medical disorder (睡眠障害, 発達障害) — learners should note the register sensitivity around the disability sense, where 障がい (with hiragana が) is increasingly used in official/polite contexts in place of 障害 to avoid the connotation of the kanji 害 ("harm").
