# ～わけない {#わけない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～訳ない

**Furigana form:** ～わけない

**Meaning:** "There's no way that... / it can't possibly be that..." — a casual, spoken contraction of わけがない (see **わけ（が）ない**) that strongly denies a possibility. Note: despite the surface components ("わけ" = reason/circumstance + "ない" = isn't), this is NOT a statement that something is "easy" or "no big deal" — it is an emphatic denial, just like its more formal counterpart わけがない, with the が simply dropped in casual speech.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞普通形 (plain-form verb) + わけない
- い形容詞普通形 + わけない
- な形容詞 + な/である + わけない
- 名詞 + の/である + わけない
- Example: そんなの簡単にできるわけない (There's no way that can be done easily)

**Typical context:** Spoken, casual/colloquial register — essentially a clipped version of わけがない used among friends or in informal conversation. Carries an emphatic, sometimes slightly exasperated or incredulous tone, as if the speaker finds the suggested possibility obviously absurd.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**わけ（が）ない**](grammar-わけがない.md) — the full, slightly more neutral/formal form of the same strong denial; わけない is simply its casual contraction.
- **はずがない** — also denies likelihood ("there's no way it should be..."), but is based more on logical expectation/deduction than on obvious common-sense denial.
- [**わけじゃない**](grammar-わけじゃない.md) — a much softer, partial denial ("it's not that..."), completely different force from わけない's flat denial.
- [**わけにはいかない**](grammar-わけにはいかない.md) — denies permissibility/feasibility due to circumstances, not denial of a fact or possibility.

**Opposite grammar structures:** 〜に違いない ("must be / surely is") or 〜はずだ ("should be the case") — both affirm a strong expectation, the reverse of denying one.

**Example sentences:**
1. そんな難しい問題、一日で解けるわけない。
   (There's no way you can solve such a difficult problem in a single day.)
2. 彼が約束を忘れるわけない、いつも几帳面なんだから。
   (There's no way he'd forget a promise — he's always so meticulous.)
3. こんな安い値段で本物のブランド品なわけない。
   (At such a cheap price, there's no way it's a genuine brand-name item.)
4. 誰もいない部屋から物音が聞こえるわけないよ、気のせいだって。
   (There's no way a sound could come from an empty room — you're just imagining it.)
5. 彼女があんなひどいことを言うわけないでしょう。
   (There's no way she would say something that awful, right?)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes parse わけない literally as "no reason" and guess it means "it's fine / no problem / easy," especially because colloquial example sentences look casual and unassuming. In fact, わけない has exactly the same emphatic denying force as わけがない — it is simply が being dropped in speech, the same way other casual contractions drop particles. Always translate it as "there's no way..." rather than "it's easy" or "no big deal."
