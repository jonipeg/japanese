# ～はもちろん {#はもちろん}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～は勿論

**Furigana form:** ～はもちろん (勿論 is almost always written in kana as もちろん in modern Japanese)

**Meaning:** "Needless to say X," "not to mention X," "of course X (and Y too)" — presents an obvious baseline case and then adds that something further/additional also applies.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + はもちろん(のこと)、〜も
- 彼は日本語はもちろん、中国語も話せる。(Needless to say Japanese, he can speak Chinese too.)
- Often followed by も, さえ, まで in the second half for emphasis.

**Typical context:** Spoken and written, neutral-to-formal. Extremely common in both daily conversation and business/formal writing.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **はもとより** — near-synonym, more literary/formal register, common in written Japanese.
- [**ばかりか**](grammar-ばかりか.md) — "not only X but also Y," similar additive meaning but with more emphasis on surprise.
- **だけでなく** — more casual equivalent of the overall "not only X" structure.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; しか～ない expresses the restrictive opposite ("only X, nothing else").

**Example sentences:**
1. この店はランチはもちろん、ディナーも人気がある。
   (Needless to say lunch, this restaurant's dinner is popular too.)
2. 彼女は仕事はもちろん、家事も完璧にこなす。
   (Not to mention work, she handles housework perfectly too.)
3. 子供はもちろん、大人もこのアニメを楽しめる。
   (Of course children, but adults can enjoy this anime too.)
4. 平日はもちろん、休日も彼はジムに通っている。
   (Needless to say weekdays, he goes to the gym on holidays too.)
5. 彼は英語はもちろん、スペイン語やドイツ語も堪能だ。
   (Not to mention English, he's also fluent in Spanish and German.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** はもちろん presents the first item as an obvious/expected baseline, then adds something that might be more surprising — the focus of new information is on the second clause, not the first. Learners sometimes put the more surprising fact first by mistake, which reverses the intended emphasis.
