# ～ないではすまない・～ずにはすまない {#ないではすまない・ずにはすまない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～ないではすまない・～ずにはすまない

**Furigana form:** ～ないではすまない・～ずにはすまない

**Meaning:** Given the circumstances or social/moral norms, it's unthinkable not to do ~, or one cannot get away without doing ~. ～ずにはすまない is the more formal/literary variant.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ない形 + ではすまない
- 動詞ない形 + ずにはすまない（例外：する → せず）
- 謝らないではすまない／謝らずにはすまない — "there's no getting away without apologizing"

**Typical context:** Written and formal spoken register — used when discussing socially expected obligations, consequences of wrongdoing, or situations where avoiding an action would be socially unacceptable.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～ないわけにはいかない** — very similar meaning, "cannot avoid doing ~," but more neutral and far more common in everyday speech.
- [**～ざるを得ない**](grammar-ざるを得ない.md) — expresses being forced to do something by circumstances, but focuses more on the speaker's own compulsion/judgment rather than social obligation or consequence.

**Opposite grammar structures:** **～なくてもすむ** — "it's fine not to do ~, one can get away without doing ~" — the direct structural and semantic opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 人の心を傷つけてしまったなら、謝らないではすまない。
   (If you've hurt someone's feelings, you can't get away without apologizing.)
2. 家のお金を黙って持ち出したなんて、親に知られたらしかられないではすまないぞ。
   (Taking money from home without telling anyone — if your parents find out, you definitely won't get away without being scolded.)
3. アパートで犬を飼えば、こっそり飼っているつもりでも隣の人に知られずにはすまないだろう。
   (If you keep a dog in your apartment, even if you think you're hiding it, the neighbors are bound to find out eventually.)
4. このままの経営状態が続けば、借金をせずにはすむまい。
   (If the business keeps running like this, there's no way to avoid taking on debt.)
5. 彼の嘘がばれた以上、会社を辞めないではすまないだろう。
   (Now that his lie has been exposed, there's no way he'll avoid having to quit the company.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern fits poorly when the motivation to act comes purely from the speaker's personal feelings rather than from external social pressure or consequence — in those cases, other forms like ～ないわけにはいかない are more natural. Remember する becomes せず (not しず) before ずにはすまない. The negative verb form (ない/ず) plus すまない is a double negative structurally, reinforcing the sense that avoiding the action is simply not an option.
