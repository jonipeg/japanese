# ～わけにはいかない {#わけにはいかない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～訳にはいかない

**Furigana form:** ～わけにはいかない (訳 read as わけ)

**Meaning:** "Cannot (afford to)..., can't just..." — expresses that an action is impossible due to social, moral, or situational obligations/common sense, even if one wanted to do it.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形 + わけにはいかない: 休むわけにはいかない (I can't just take a day off)
- 動詞ない形 + わけにはいかない: 行かないわけにはいかない (I can't NOT go / I have to go)

**Typical context:** Spoken and written, neutral-to-formal register. Common when explaining why one must act against personal preference due to duty, responsibility, or social norms.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **なければならない** — "must do," a more direct/plain obligation statement without the "can't afford to do otherwise" social nuance.
- [**ざるを得ない**](grammar-ざるを得ない.md) — "have no choice but to," more formal/written, emphasizes compulsion from circumstance rather than social obligation.
- **てはいけない** — simple prohibition, lacks the nuance of weighing obligation against personal will.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No single fixed opposite, though ～してもいい/構わない ("it's fine to do X") contrasts by allowing the action freely.

**Example sentences:**
1. 大事な会議があるので、今日は休むわけにはいかない。
   (There's an important meeting today, so I can't just take the day off.)
2. 友達に借りたお金だから、返さないわけにはいかない。
   (It's money I borrowed from a friend, so I have to pay it back.)
3. 皆が頑張っているのに、自分だけ諦めるわけにはいかない。
   (Everyone else is working hard, so I can't just give up on my own.)
4. 子供が熱を出したので、仕事に行かないわけにはいかない、というわけでもなかった。
   (My child had a fever, so it's not as though I absolutely had to go to work. [example of negated nuance])
5. 上司に頼まれた以上、断るわけにはいかない。
   (Since my boss asked me directly, I can't just refuse.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** わけにはいかない emphasizes social/moral obligation or common sense preventing an action — it implies the speaker would personally prefer another option but circumstances don't allow it. Don't confuse with なければならない, which is a flatter statement of necessity without this "going against one's own wish" nuance. Also note the negative form 〜ないわけにはいかない means "can't avoid doing X" (i.e., must do X) — a double negative that trips up learners.
