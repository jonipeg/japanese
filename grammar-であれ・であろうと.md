# ～であれ／～であろうと {#であれ・であろうと}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～であれ／～であろうと

**Furigana form:** ～であれ／～であろうと

**Meaning:** "Even if it's X" / "No matter what X is" — states that even under the given condition or identity, nothing changes; the condition has no bearing on the outcome.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞・疑問詞 + であれ／であろうと
- あらしの夜であれ — "even if it's a stormy night"
- どんな権力者であれ — "no matter what kind of powerful person [one is]"

**Typical context:** Written and formal spoken register — common in speeches, written argumentation, proverbs, and dramatic declarations; often paired with words like たとえ or どんな for emphasis.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～にせよ／～にしろ** — also means "whether it be X," similarly formal, often used for listing contrasting alternatives (例: 賛成にせよ反対にせよ).
- **～だろうと／～だろうが** — a slightly less formal equivalent meaning "whether it's X," common in spoken as well as written Japanese.
- [**～といえども**](grammar-といえども.md) — "even though it is X," very literary/classical in tone, often used in scholarly or rhetorical writing.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a conditional like ～次第だ ("it depends on X") would instead state that the outcome *does* change according to the condition.

**Example sentences:**
1. たとえあらしの夜であれ、わたしは仕事のためなら外出する。
   (Even if it's a stormy night, I'll go out if it's for work.)
2. どんな権力者であれ、いつかは命の終わりが来る。
   (No matter how powerful a person is, death comes to everyone eventually.)
3. 理由が何であれ、無断欠席は許されない。
   (Whatever the reason may be, an unexcused absence is not permitted.)
4. 君に会うためなら、たとえ火の中、水の中であろうと、僕は平気だ。
   (If it means seeing you, I don't care even if I have to walk through fire or water.)
5. 国籍がどこであれ、法の下では誰もが平等だ。
   (No matter what one's nationality is, everyone is equal under the law.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The clause that follows states a fact or judgment unaffected by the stated condition — it's not used to make requests. It frequently teams up with だれ・どんな・たとえ-type words to intensify the "no matter what" nuance, and commonly appears as a set phrase like 何であれ or だれであれ.
