# ～に忍びない {#に忍びない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～に忍びない

**Furigana form:** ～にしのびない

**Meaning:** "I can't bring myself to do X" — describes an action that is so emotionally painful or distressing to the speaker that they cannot bear to carry it out, usually out of compassion, sentimentality, or attachment.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形 + に忍びない
- 処分するに忍びない — "can't bring myself to dispose of it"

**Typical context:** Written, literary/formal register; appears in essays, memoirs, and formal writing describing emotional reluctance tied to sentimentality, compassion, or grief. Rare in casual conversation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～に堪えない／耐えない** — also "cannot bear to," near-synonymous and interchangeable in many contexts, slightly more common in modern writing.
- [**～ざるを得ない**](grammar-ざるを得ない.md) — different meaning ("have no choice but to"), expresses being forced to act rather than being emotionally unable to act — useful contrast to avoid confusion.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a phrase like 平気で～する ("to do X without any hesitation/concern") would describe the absence of the emotional reluctance this pattern conveys.

**Example sentences:**
1. 古い本はもう読まないとは思うが、処分するに忍びない。
   (I don't think I'll ever read these old books again, but I can't bring myself to get rid of them.)
2. 実家の家は今はだれも住んでおらず、見るに忍びないほど荒れてしまった。
   (No one lives in my parents' old house anymore, and it has fallen into such disrepair that it's painful to look at.)
3. 彼女の悲しそうな顔を見るに忍びなくて、本当のことが言えなかった。
   (I couldn't bear to see her sad face, so I couldn't bring myself to tell her the truth.)
4. 長年連れ添った愛犬を手放すに忍びない。
   (I can't bring myself to give up the dog I've had for so many years.)
5. 被災地の惨状はとても見るに忍びないものだった。
   (The devastation in the disaster area was almost too painful to look at.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern attaches to verbs of perception or disposal that would cause emotional pain if carried out — 見る, 聞く, 捨てる, 処分する, 手放す are typical. It is a fixed, somewhat bookish expression; learners should not overuse it in casual speech, where simpler phrasing like かわいそうで～できない is more natural.
