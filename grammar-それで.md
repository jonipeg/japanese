# それで {#それで}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** それで（漢字なし／平仮名のみ）

**Furigana form:** それで

**Meaning:** "So, and so, that's why" — a casual-neutral conjunction that narrates the next event or reason as the natural outcome of what was just said; also used as a short interjection meaning "and then? / so?" prompting someone to continue.

**Conjugation:** Sentence-initial conjunction, no conjugation needed — attaches at the sentence boundary, connecting the previous sentence/clause to the next. It does not typically appear mid-sentence linking two clauses within one sentence the way だから can; それで is almost always its own sentence-initial connector, including as a one-word question (それで？).

**Typical context:** Casual-to-neutral, spoken register — very common in everyday conversation and storytelling, especially when narrating a sequence of events and their natural result ("and so, that's how..."). It can also appear in slightly more neutral spoken contexts (e.g. a casual interview) but is too conversational for formal written reports or academic prose, where そのため or したがって would be used instead.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**だから**](grammar-だから.md) — casual, spoken; states a reason more directly/assertively, sometimes with a slightly insistent "that's why" tone, whereas それで is more neutral and narrative.
- [**そのため**](grammar-そのため.md) — neutral-formal, written; the written step up from それで's spoken narration.
- [**したがって**](grammar-したがって.md) — formal, written/business/academic; far more formal than それで.
- [**それから**](grammar-それから.md) — sequential "after that, and then"; purely about time order, not causation, unlike それで which implies the next event followed as a result.
- [**すると**](grammar-すると.md) — narrates a natural/automatic consequence in written narrative style; similar storytelling function to それで but more literary and less conversational.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; its contrastive counterpart would be a concessive conjunction like それでも ("even so"), which blocks the expected natural result that それで affirms.

**Example sentences:**
1. 電車が遅れたんです。それで、会議に遅刻してしまいました。
   (The train was delayed. So, I ended up being late for the meeting.)
2. 彼女に告白されたんだ。それで、どうしたらいいか分からなくて。
   (She confessed her feelings to me. So, I didn't know what to do.)
3. A: 昨日、財布をなくしちゃって。 B: それで、見つかったの?
   (A: Yesterday I lost my wallet. B: So, did you find it?)
4. 雨が急に降ってきたんです。それで、傘を買いに行きました。
   (It suddenly started raining. So, I went to buy an umbrella.)
5. 彼は体調を崩したらしい。それで、今日の会は欠席だって。
   (Apparently he's not feeling well. That's why he's absent from today's gathering.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often confuse それで with だから: それで leans narrative and neutral, reporting how one thing led to another (similar to "and so" in a story), while だから leans assertive and conversational, often justifying a decision or opinion ("that's why I..."). A common mistake is using それで in formal writing, where it reads as too casual/conversational compared to そのため or したがって. Another nuance to watch for is それで's secondary use as a bare interjection ("so? and then?") prompting more of a story — confusing this with だから, which cannot be used this way, is a common beginner error.
