# ～として(は)/(も) {#として(は)・(も)}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～として(は)/(も)

**Furigana form:** ～として(は)/(も) (no kanji reading needed; として is already kana)

**Meaning:** "As X" — marks the capacity, role, position, qualification, or category in which something acts or is treated. ～としては narrows this to "speaking as X / from X's standpoint," while ～としても adds a concessive nuance, "even as/even in the capacity of X."

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 (noun) + として(は)/(も)
- 専門家として (as an expert)
- 学生としては (speaking as a student)
- 冗談としても (even as/taken as a joke)

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal; extremely common in both speech and writing — business reports, news, essays, and everyday conversation when stating one's role or standpoint.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**にとって**](grammar-にとって.md) — "for X / from X's perspective (on how something affects X)"; としては is about the speaker's own stated role/standpoint while にとって focuses on the effect on X.
- **の立場で** — "from the standpoint/position of," more explicit and slightly more formal than として.
- [**にしては**](grammar-にしては.md) — "for X, surprisingly," carries a contrastive/surprised nuance absent in plain として.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though ～にもかかわらず ("despite/regardless of role or status") can contrast with the concessive としても.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は医者として、多くの患者を救ってきた。
   (As a doctor, he has saved many patients.)
2. この問題は個人としては賛成だが、会社の立場では反対せざるを得ない。
   (Personally [as an individual], I agree with this issue, but from the company's standpoint I have no choice but to oppose it.)
3. 冗談としても、あの発言はひどすぎる。
   (Even taken as a joke, that remark was too much.)
4. 彼女は通訳として会議に参加した。
   (She participated in the meeting as an interpreter.)
5. 一時的な対応としては悪くないが、根本的な解決にはならない。
   (As a temporary measure it's not bad, but it won't be a fundamental solution.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often confuse として (role/capacity) with にとって (perspective on impact) — "日本人として" (as a Japanese person, in that capacity) vs. "日本人にとって" (for/from the viewpoint of Japanese people). Also, としては narrows to a specific standpoint among possible others, while として alone is a flat role statement.
