# ～わりに（は） {#わりには}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～割に（は）

**Furigana form:** ～わりに（は）(割 read as わり)

**Meaning:** "Considering..., for (what one would expect)..., relatively..." — indicates that a result doesn't quite match what would normally be expected given a stated condition (either surprisingly better or worse than expected).

**Conjugation:**
- 普通形 (plain form) + わりに(は)
- 動詞普通形 + わりに: 勉強した割には (considering he studied...)
- い形容詞普通形 + わりに: 高い割には (considering it's expensive...)
- な形容詞 + な + わりに: 簡単なわりに (considering it's simple...)
- 名詞 + の + わりに: 年齢のわりに (considering his age...)

**Typical context:** Spoken and written, neutral register. Used to express a mismatch between expectation and reality, often with a mildly surprised or evaluative tone.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**にしては**](grammar-にしては.md) — very similar meaning ("for X, unexpectedly..."), largely interchangeable with わりに, though にしては often implies the subject is the standard being evaluated against.
- **のに** — concessive "even though," but without the comparative "relative to expectation" nuance.
- **~ながら(も)** — "although," more literary, lacks the comparative-expectation framing.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. このレストランは値段が高いわりに、味はそれほどでもない。
   (This restaurant is expensive, but the taste isn't all that great for the price.)
2. 彼は練習しなかったわりに、試合でよく頑張った。
   (Considering he didn't practice, he did well in the match.)
3. この機械は複雑な割には、操作が簡単だ。
   (Considering how complex this machine is, it's easy to operate.)
4. 彼女は年齢のわりに、とても落ち着いている。
   (For her age, she's very calm and composed.)
5. 狭い部屋のわりには、収納スペースが多い。
   (For such a small room, there's a lot of storage space.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** わりに always implies a comparison against a baseline expectation set by the first clause — it's not simply "but." Learners sometimes substitute のに where わりに's comparative/evaluative nuance is intended, losing the "relative to what's expected" meaning. Also, わりに can describe either a pleasantly or unpleasantly surprising mismatch — context (not the grammar itself) determines the direction.
