# ～得る／～得ない {#得る・得ない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～得る／～得ない

**Furigana form:** ～うる／～える、～えない (得る is read うる in the formal/classical-flavored set phrase style, える in more conversational style; 得ない is read えない)

**Meaning:** Attached to a verb's masu-stem, expresses logical/abstract possibility "can happen / can do" (得る) or impossibility "cannot happen / cannot do" (得ない). Used for conceptual or logical possibility rather than physical skill-based ability.

**Conjugation:**
- Verb ます形 (stem, drop ます) + 得る／得ない
- 起こる → 起こり得る／起こり得ない
- 考える → 考え得る／考え得ない
- Set phrase: あり得る（ありうる／ありえる）— "can happen, is plausible"

**Typical context:** Formal, written register — common in news reports, essays, academic and argumentative writing. Rarely used in casual conversation, where できる is preferred instead.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**かねない**](grammar-かねない.md) — expresses a negative potential/risk ("might well happen"), but specifically implies an undesirable outcome, unlike the neutral possibility of 得る.
- **できる** — the casual, general-ability/possibility verb; 得る is its formal, logical-possibility counterpart.
- [**わけにはいかない**](grammar-わけにはいかない.md) — "cannot do X" due to social or moral constraints, a different kind of impossibility than 得ない's logical impossibility.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite — 得る and 得ない are themselves the affirmative/negative pair covering this meaning.

**Example sentences:**
1. そのようなミスは誰にでもあり得る。
   (Such a mistake could happen to anyone.)
2. 彼が裏切るとは考え得ない。
   (It's inconceivable that he would betray us.)
3. この事故は防ぎ得たはずだ。
   (This accident could have been prevented.)
4. 人間はすべてを理解し得ない。
   (Humans cannot understand everything.)
5. その結果は十分あり得ることだ。
   (That result is entirely plausible.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often try to attach 得る to the dictionary form instead of the masu-stem — the stem is required. Also, 得る as an independent verb (える, "to obtain/gain") is a different usage; context makes clear whether it's the independent verb or this possibility suffix. Don't substitute 得る for できる in casual speech — it will sound overly stiff.
