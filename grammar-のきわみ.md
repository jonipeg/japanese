# ～のきわみ {#のきわみ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～の極み

**Furigana form:** ～のきわみ

**Meaning:** "The ultimate extreme of X" — describes an emotional or physical state (joy, exhaustion, luxury) as having reached its absolute peak.

**Conjugation:**
- N(感情・状態を表す名詞) + の極み

**Typical context:** Formal, literary register — used in speeches, ceremonial writing, and elevated description of emotional or physical peaks (感動の極み, 疲労の極み, 贅沢の極み).

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**のいたり**](grammar-のいたり.md) — also "the utmost extreme," but いたり carries a humble, self-referential nuance and is tied to fixed ceremonial phrases (光栄の至り), whereas きわみ more objectively describes the state reached and can apply to physical or negative states too (疲労の極み).
- [**かぎりだ**](grammar-かぎりだ.md) — attaches to adjectives for a personal, subjective feeling exclamation, whereas のきわみ attaches to nouns to objectively describe a peak state reached.
- [**てやまない**](grammar-てやまない.md) — describes a continuous, ongoing feeling via a verb, in contrast to のきわみ's single-moment description of having reached a peak.
- **きわまる/きわまりない** — attaches to na-adjective stems to intensify a quality (失礼極まる), while のきわみ attaches to emotion/state nouns to name the peak itself.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. あの有名なスポーツ選手と握手できたなんて、感動の極みだ。
   (Getting to shake hands with that famous athlete is the height of excitement.)
2. 連日の残業で、疲労の極みに達している。
   (After days of overtime, I've reached the peak of exhaustion.)
3. あのホテルの内装はまさに贅沢の極みだった。
   (That hotel's interior was truly the height of luxury.)
4. 子どもの合格の知らせを聞いて、喜びの極みだった。
   (Hearing the news of my child's acceptance was the height of joy.)
5. 彼のわがままな態度は失礼の極みだと感じた。
   (I felt his selfish attitude was the height of rudeness.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** のきわみ sits in the N1 "extreme degree" confusable cluster and is most often mixed up with のいたり because both translate as "the utmost X." The distinguishing test: のいたり is reserved for humble, self-referential ceremonial expressions (光栄の至り, "it is my great honor") almost exclusively about the speaker's own feeling, while のきわみ is more flexible and can describe anyone's or anything's peak state, including physical ones like exhaustion or luxury.
