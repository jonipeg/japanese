# ～(よ)うが／(よ)うと～まいと／(よ)うが～まいが {#うがまいが}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～（よ）うが／（よ）うと～まいと／（よ）うが～まいが

**Furigana form:** ～（よ）うが／（よ）うと～まいと／（よ）うが～まいが

**Meaning:** "Whether X happens or not, (it doesn't matter)" — expresses that regardless of whether an action occurs or not, the outcome or the speaker's stance remains unaffected.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞意向形（よう／おう）+ が／と + 動詞まい形 + と／が
- Example base: 行く → 行こうが行くまいが

**Typical context:** Formal-to-neutral, usable in both speech and writing; conveys a resolute, dismissive, or indifferent stance toward an alternative possibility. Slightly literary in flavor but also used in assertive spoken statements.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **ても～なくても** — "whether X or not"; the everyday, more accessible equivalent (e.g., 行っても行かなくても), usable in casual speech, while （よ）うが～まいが is more formal/literary.
- **にせよ～にせよ** — "whether it be X or Y"; similarly presents two alternatives as equally inconsequential, but structured around two named alternatives rather than specifically an affirmative/negative pair of the same verb.
- **であれ～であれ** — "whatever A or B"; a related formal concessive listing alternatives, broader in scope (can cover more than two options) compared to the specifically affirmative-vs-negative framing of （よ）うが～まいが.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a conditional statement making the outcome clearly depend on X (次第だ、"it depends on X") would contrast with this pattern's insistence that the outcome does NOT depend on X.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼が来ようが来まいが、会議は予定通り行う。 (Whether he comes or not, the meeting will proceed as scheduled.)
2. 雨が降ろうが降るまいが、試合は中止にならない。 (Whether it rains or not, the match won't be canceled.)
3. 誰が反対しようがしまいが、私はこの計画を実行する。 (Whether anyone objects or not, I will carry out this plan.)
4. 周りがどう思おうが思うまいが、自分のやり方を貫くつもりだ。 (Whatever others think or don't think, I intend to stick to my own way.)
5. 結果がどうなろうがなるまいが、挑戦したことに意味がある。 (Whatever the result turns out to be or not, there's meaning in having tried.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern requires the same verb repeated in both volitional (～よう) and negative-volitional/plain-negative-archaic (～まい) forms — learners sometimes mistakenly use two different verbs. The overall tone is resolute and somewhat dismissive of the alternative's relevance ("it makes no difference either way, I'll proceed regardless"), so it fits contexts of firm resolve or defiant independence from outside factors.
