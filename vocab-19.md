## 高度 (こうど)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** altitude, height; a high degree (of something)

**Similar words:**
- 高さ（たかさ）— height in general, a basic, everyday term without the technical/figurative nuance of 高度.
- 標高（ひょうこう）— elevation above sea level, a more precise geographic/technical term than 高度.
- [レベル](vocab-34.md#レベル-れべる)— level (loanword), used more casually for "a degree/level" of skill or quality.

**Opposite words:**
- 低度（ていど）— (rarely used) low degree

**Example sentences:**
1. 飛行機は高度1万メートルを飛行している。— The airplane is flying at an altitude of 10,000 meters.
2. この技術は高度な知識を必要とする。— This technology requires an advanced level of knowledge.
3. 高度経済成長期に日本は急速に発展した。— Japan developed rapidly during the period of high economic growth.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 高度 functions both as a noun for literal altitude (高度1万メートル) and as a na-adjective meaning "advanced/sophisticated" (高度な技術, advanced technology) — learners should be careful not to confuse this abstract "high degree" sense with simple 高さ (physical height).

## 行動 (こうどう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** action, behavior, conduct

**Similar words:**
- [態度](vocab-25.md#態度-たいど)（たいど）— attitude, more about one's manner/demeanor than concrete actions.
- 動作（どうさ）— movement/motion, more about physical gestures than broader patterns of behavior.
- 振る舞い（ふるまい）— conduct/behavior, slightly more formal and focused on manners/etiquette.

**Opposite words:**
None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼の行動には一貫性がない。— His actions lack consistency.
2. もっと早く行動すべきだった。— I should have taken action sooner.
3. 動物の行動を観察する研究をしている。— I'm doing research observing animal behavior.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 行動 emphasizes the taking of concrete action (often paired with する, 行動する, "to take action"), distinct from 態度, which describes one's inner stance or demeanor without necessarily implying any actual movement or deed.

## 強盗 (ごうとう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** robbery, burglary; a robber

**Similar words:**
- 泥棒（どろぼう）— thief/burglar, a broader, more casual term that doesn't necessarily imply violence or direct confrontation.
- 窃盗（せっとう）— theft (legal term), typically non-violent and without direct confrontation with the victim.
- 強奪（ごうだつ）— forcible seizure/robbery, emphasizes the violent taking by force, close synonym.

**Opposite words:**
None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 昨夜、近所のコンビニで強盗事件が発生した。— Last night, a robbery occurred at a nearby convenience store.
2. 強盗はマスクをして犯行に及んだ。— The robber committed the crime wearing a mask.
3. 警察は強盗犯を追跡している。— The police are pursuing the robber.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 強盗 specifically implies robbery involving force, threat, or confrontation with a victim, legally distinct from 窃盗 (theft without confrontation, like pickpocketing or burglary of an empty house) — this distinction matters in legal/news contexts.

## 合同 (ごうどう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** combination, union, joint (as in combined effort)

**Similar words:**
- [統合](vocab-04.md#統合-とうごう)（とうごう）— integration/merger, often implies a more permanent, structural combination (e.g., merging companies).
- [連合](vocab-34.md#連合-れんごう)（れんごう）— alliance/union, emphasizes cooperation between distinct entities that retain their own identity.
- [共同](vocab-15.md#共同-きょうどう)（きょうどう）— joint/collaborative, used for shared actions/projects rather than merging entities.

**Opposite words:**
- 分離（ぶんり）— separation

**Example sentences:**
1. 二つの学校が合同で運動会を開催した。— The two schools jointly held a sports festival.
2. 両社は合同会社を設立した。— The two companies established a joint corporation.
3. 合同訓練が自衛隊と米軍の間で行われた。— Joint training was conducted between the Self-Defense Forces and the U.S. military.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 合同 often describes a temporary or event-based joining of forces (合同練習, joint practice; 合同会社, a specific type of joint company) — it doesn't necessarily imply permanent merging like 統合 does, which is a useful distinction in business contexts.

## 講読 (こうどく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** reading (and studying a text, especially in an academic setting)

**Similar words:**
- 読解（どっかい）— reading comprehension, more about the skill of understanding text than a formal course/subject.
- 購読（こうどく）— subscription (to a newspaper/magazine), a homophone with completely different meaning and kanji.
- 精読（せいどく）— close/intensive reading, emphasizes careful, detailed reading of a text.

**Opposite words:**
None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 大学でドイツ語の文学作品を講読している。— I'm studying (reading and analyzing) German literary works at university.
2. 原典講読の授業は難しいが、ためになる。— The original-text reading course is difficult but worthwhile.
3. 彼は古典の講読に力を入れている。— He is putting effort into the reading/study of classical texts.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 講読 (reading/studying a text academically) is a homophone of 購読 (こうどく, subscribing to a publication) — same pronunciation, totally different meaning and kanji, so listening context is essential to tell them apart.

## 購入(する) (こうにゅう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** purchase, to buy

**Similar words:**
- 買う（かう）— to buy, the basic, everyday verb, much more casual than 購入する.
- [購買](#購買-こうばい)（こうばい）— purchasing (often in a business/corporate context, e.g., a purchasing department).
- [入手](vocab-30.md#入手-にゅうしゅ)（にゅうしゅ）— to obtain/acquire, broader and doesn't necessarily imply paying money.

**Opposite words:**
- 売却（ばいきゃく）— sale, selling off

**Example sentences:**
1. 新しいパソコンを購入した。— I purchased a new computer.
2. このチケットはオンラインでのみ購入可能です。— This ticket can only be purchased online.
3. 会社は新しい設備を購入する予定だ。— The company plans to purchase new equipment.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 購入する is a formal, written-register synonym of 買う — it's common in business documents, receipts, and announcements, but using it in casual conversation (instead of 買う) can sound unnaturally stiff.

## 公認 (こうにん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** official recognition/approval, authorization

**Similar words:**
- 認可（にんか）— authorization/license (often governmental), more bureaucratic/legal in tone.
- 承認（しょうにん）— approval, broader and used in many contexts (business, legal, casual).
- [公式](vocab-18.md#公式-こうしき)（こうしき）— official, describes formality in general rather than the specific act of granting recognition.

**Opposite words:**
None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は政党の公認候補として選挙に出た。— He ran in the election as the official candidate endorsed by the party.
2. この資格は国家公認のものだ。— This qualification is officially recognized by the state.
3. 公認会計士になるのは簡単ではない。— Becoming a certified public accountant is not easy.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 公認 often appears in set terms like 公認会計士 (certified public accountant) and 公認候補 (officially endorsed candidate) — it implies recognition/endorsement by an authoritative body, not just general social acceptance, which distinguishes it from looser terms like 承認.

## 光熱費 (こうねつひ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** utility expenses (electricity, gas, heating/lighting costs)

**Similar words:**
- 公共料金（こうきょうりょうきん）— public utility fees, a broader term that can also include water and other services.
- 水道光熱費（すいどうこうねつひ）— utilities including water, the fuller phrase sometimes used in household budgeting.

**Opposite words:**
None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 冬は光熱費が高くなる。— Utility costs go up in winter.
2. 一人暮らしの光熱費を節約する方法を調べた。— I looked into ways to save on utility costs when living alone.
3. 家賃に光熱費が含まれている物件を探している。— I'm looking for a property where utilities are included in the rent.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 光熱費 covers electricity and gas (lighting and heating/fuel) but technically excludes water, which is covered separately under 水道代 — in casual speech, people sometimes lump everything together as 水道光熱費 to be safe.

## 後輩 (こうはい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** junior (person with less seniority, e.g., in school or workplace)

**Similar words:**
- 先輩（せんぱい）— senior, the direct paired opposite term.
- [新人](vocab-21.md#新人-しんじん)（しんじん）— newcomer/rookie, emphasizes being new rather than relative seniority.
- 部下（ぶか）— subordinate, specifically in a hierarchical work relationship, not just seniority by years.

**Opposite words:**
- 先輩（せんぱい）— senior, superior (by years/experience)

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は大学の後輩だ。— He is my junior from university.
2. 後輩の面倒をよく見ている。— I look after my juniors well.
3. 後輩にアドバイスを求められた。— I was asked for advice by a junior.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 後輩/先輩 describe relative seniority based on when someone joined a school, club, or company — not age — so it's possible for an older person to be someone's 後輩 if they joined later, which can confuse learners used to age-based hierarchies.

**Similar words:**

- 仕入れ（しいれ）— stocking/procurement (for resale), used specifically by businesses buying inventory.

## 公表(する) (こうひょう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** publication, official announcement; to make public

**Similar words:**
- 発表（はっぴょう）— announcement/presentation, broader and used for any kind of announcement, not necessarily to the whole public.
- 公開（こうかい）— to open to the public/release (information, a film), emphasizes accessibility rather than formal announcement.
- 告知（こくち）— notice/announcement, often used for advance notices of events.

**Opposite words:**
- 非公開（ひこうかい）— not made public, private

**Example sentences:**
1. 調査結果が公表された。— The survey results were made public.
2. 政府はまだその情報を公表していない。— The government has not yet made that information public.
3. 新しい政策が正式に公表される予定だ。— The new policy is scheduled to be officially announced.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 公表 specifically implies releasing information to the general public (often by an authority like a government or company), distinct from 発表, which can be a more limited announcement to a specific audience (e.g., a presentation to colleagues).

## 交付(する) (こうふ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** delivering/granting (official documents), issuance

**Similar words:**
- [発行](vocab-31.md#発行-はっこう)（はっこう）— issuance (of documents, currency, publications), very close synonym, slightly more common for things like passports/certificates.
- [支給](#支給-しきゅう)（しきゅう）— payment/provision (of money, goods, especially as a benefit), used more for financial disbursements.
- [配布](vocab-31.md#配布-はいふ)（はいふ）— distribution (of flyers, materials), broader and not necessarily official documents.

**Opposite words:**
None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 市役所でパスポートの交付を受けた。— I received my passport issuance at the city hall.
2. 補助金が交付される予定だ。— The subsidy is scheduled to be granted.
3. 住民票の交付には身分証明書が必要だ。— An ID is required to obtain (be issued) a residence certificate.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 交付 is a formal, bureaucratic term specifically for government agencies issuing official documents or granting funds (交付金, grant money) — it's not used for everyday "giving" something to someone, where 渡す or あげる would be natural.

## 幸福 (こうふく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** happiness, well-being, bliss; happy

**Similar words:**
- 幸せ（しあわせ）— happiness, the more common, everyday spoken equivalent of 幸福.
- 満足（まんぞく）— satisfaction/contentment, focuses on fulfillment of needs/desires rather than overall well-being.
- 喜び（よろこび）— joy/delight, a more immediate, momentary feeling than a sustained state of happiness.

**Opposite words:**
- 不幸（ふこう）— unhappiness, misfortune

**Example sentences:**
1. 幸福な家庭を築きたい。— I want to build a happy family/household.
2. 彼女は今の生活に幸福を感じている。— She feels happiness in her current life.
3. 本当の幸福とは何かを考えさせられた。— It made me think about what true happiness really is.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 幸福 is a more formal/written word than 幸せ, which is the standard, everyday term used in conversation (幸せです vs 幸福です) — 幸福 appears more often in philosophical, literary, or legal contexts (e.g., 幸福追求権, the right to pursue happiness).

## 興奮(する) (こうふん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** excitement, agitation; to get excited

**Similar words:**
- わくわくする— to feel excited/thrilled (positive anticipation), a more casual, purely positive expression.
- 熱狂（ねっきょう）— frenzy/fervor, a more intense, often collective excitement (e.g., sports crowds).
- [動揺](vocab-29.md#動揺-どうよう)（どうよう）— agitation/being shaken (emotionally), closer to anxious or distressed excitement rather than thrilled.

**Opposite words:**
- [冷静](vocab-33.md#冷静-れいせい)（れいせい）— calm, composed

**Example sentences:**
1. 試合の結果に興奮して大声を出してしまった。— I got so excited about the match result that I shouted.
2. 興奮して眠れない夜もある。— There are nights when I can't sleep because I'm too excited/agitated.
3. 彼は興奮した様子で事件について語った。— He talked about the incident in an agitated state.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 興奮 can describe both positive excitement (thrilled about something fun) and negative agitation (worked up, overly emotional, even angry) — unlike わくわく, which is purely positive, context determines the emotional valence of 興奮.

## 公平 (こうへい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** impartiality, fairness; impartial, fair

**Similar words:**
- [公正](vocab-18.md#公正-こうせい)（こうせい）— fairness/justice, very close synonym, leans toward correctness per rules or law.
- [平等](vocab-32.md#平等-びょうどう)（びょうどう）— equality, focuses on equal status/outcome rather than unbiased process.
- [中立](vocab-06.md#中立-ちゅうりつ)（ちゅうりつ）— neutrality, emphasizes not taking sides rather than fair treatment per se.

**Opposite words:**
- 不公平（ふこうへい）— unfair

**Example sentences:**
1. 審判は公平に判定しなければならない。— The referee must judge impartially.
2. 親は子供を公平に扱うべきだ。— Parents should treat their children fairly.
3. 公平な立場から意見を述べた。— I stated my opinion from an impartial standpoint.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 公平 emphasizes even-handed, unbiased treatment between individuals (not favoring one over another), while 公正 leans more toward adherence to proper rules/procedures — in everyday disputes about favoritism, 公平 is the more natural choice.

#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** candidacy, a candidate

**Similar words:**
- 立候補（りっこうほ）— to run as a candidate (verb form), the specific act of declaring candidacy.
- 志望者（しぼうしゃ）— an applicant/aspirant, used for job or school applications rather than elections or selections.
- ノミネート— nomination (loanword), often used for awards (movies, music) rather than elections.

## 公募 (こうぼ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** public recruitment/solicitation, open call

**Similar words:**
- 募集（ぼしゅう）— recruitment/solicitation in general, broader and doesn't necessarily mean "open to the public."
- 公開募集（こうかいぼしゅう）— the fuller phrase sometimes used interchangeably with 公募.

## 公務員 (こうむいん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** civil servant, public service employee

**Similar words:**
- 役人（やくにん）— bureaucrat/government official, sometimes carries a slightly negative, stereotypical connotation (red tape).
- 官僚（かんりょう）— bureaucrat, specifically higher-ranking, elite government officials.
- サラリーマン— company employee (loanword), the private-sector counterpart/contrast to a civil servant.

None notable

## 項目 (こうもく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** heading, item, topic, clause

**Similar words:**
- [事項](#事項-じこう)（じこう）— matter/item (often in a formal document), very close synonym, slightly more formal/legal.
- [カテゴリー](vocab-13.md#カテゴリー-カテゴリー)— category (loanword), groups items together rather than being an individual listed point.
- 条項（じょうこう）— clause/article (in a contract or law), a more specific legal subtype of 項目.

None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. アンケートにはいくつかの項目がある。— The questionnaire has several items.
2. 契約書の各項目を確認してください。— Please check each clause of the contract.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 項目 refers to individual listed entries within a larger document or form (a checklist item, a line in a contract) — it's a structural/organizational term, not to be confused with 題目 (だいもく, a title/subject of a speech or theme).

## 紅葉 (こうよう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** autumn leaves, fall foliage/colors

**Similar words:**
- もみじ— the common word for maple trees/leaves specifically, and also a casual reading alternative for 紅葉.
- 落葉（らくよう）— fallen leaves/leaf fall, emphasizes leaves dropping rather than their color change.
- 黄葉（こうよう）— yellowing leaves (as opposed to reddening), a more specific subset of autumn color change, same pronunciation but different kanji/nuance.

**Opposite words:**
- 新緑（しんりょく）— fresh green (of spring/early summer foliage)

**Example sentences:**
1. 秋になると山々が紅葉で彩られる。— In autumn, the mountains are colored with autumn foliage.
2. 家族で紅葉狩りに出かけた。— We went out as a family to view the autumn leaves.
3. 今年の紅葉は例年より美しいと言われている。— This year's autumn colors are said to be more beautiful than usual.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 紅葉 can be read こうよう (the general term for autumn leaf coloring as a phenomenon) or もみじ (often referring to maple trees/leaves specifically, especially in casual speech and as a noun for the plant itself) — context and register guide which reading is natural.

## 小売り (こうり)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** retailing, retail sales

**Similar words:**
- 卸売り（おろしうり）— wholesale, the opposite stage of distribution, selling in bulk to retailers.
- 販売（はんばい）— sales in general, broader and not specific to the retail stage.
- 直売（ちょくばい）— direct sales (from producer to consumer), bypassing the traditional retail/wholesale chain.

**Opposite words:**
- 卸売り（おろしうり）— wholesale

**Example sentences:**
1. この会社は小売りと卸売りの両方を行っている。— This company does both retail and wholesale.
2. 小売り価格は卸売り価格より高い。— The retail price is higher than the wholesale price.
3. 小売店の経営は年々厳しくなっている。— Running a retail store is becoming tougher year by year.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 小売り specifically refers to selling directly to end consumers (as opposed to 卸売り, selling in bulk to other businesses) — the kanji 小 (small) vs 卸 can help learners remember the contrast between small-scale consumer sales and large-scale business-to-business sales.

## 合理 (ごうり)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** rationality; rational, reasonable

**Similar words:**
- 合理的（ごうりてき）— the more commonly used na-adjective form meaning "rational/logical," since 合理 alone rarely stands as an adjective.
- 論理的（ろんりてき）— logical, emphasizes sound reasoning/argument structure specifically.
- 理性的（りせいてき）— rational/reasonable (of a person's thinking), emphasizes reason over emotion.

**Opposite words:**
- 不合理（ふごうり）— irrational, unreasonable

**Example sentences:**
1. もっと合理的に考えるべきだ。— You should think more rationally.
2. 合理化によってコストが削減された。— Costs were reduced through rationalization/streamlining.
3. 彼の主張には合理性がある。— His argument has rationality (is reasonable).

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 合理 is rarely used as a standalone adjective in modern Japanese; it almost always appears as 合理的 (rational, as a na-adjective) or 合理化 (rationalization/streamlining, a common business term) — learners should default to these compound forms rather than using 合理 alone.

## 公立 (こうりつ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** public, municipal (institution)

**Similar words:**
- 国立（こくりつ）— national (run by the national government), a step up in scale/authority from 公立.
- [私立](vocab-21.md#私立-しりつ)（しりつ）— private (institution), the direct contrasting term.
- 県立（けんりつ）— prefectural (run by a prefecture), a more specific subtype of 公立.

**Opposite words:**

3. 卒業式典で校長が挨拶をした。— The principal gave a speech at the graduation ceremony.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 式典 is a formal, often written/official register word used for commemorative or institutional ceremonies — in everyday speech about a wedding or graduation, the simpler 式 (結婚式, 卒業式) is more commonly used standalone.
