# ～とは限らない {#とは限らない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～とは限らない

**Furigana form:** ～とはかぎらない

**Meaning:** "It's not necessarily the case that ~ / not always true that ~" — used to soften a generalization, acknowledging exceptions to an otherwise commonly held belief.

**Conjugation:**
- 普通形 (plain form) + とは限らない — 例: 高いとは限らない (it's not necessarily expensive)
- 名詞 + だとは限らない — 例: 金持ちだとは限らない

**Typical context:** Both written and spoken, neutral-to-formal — frequently used in essays, debates, and everyday conversation to express nuanced, balanced opinions.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**わけではない**](grammar-わけではない.md) — "it doesn't mean that ~," a broader partial-denial, often used similarly but without the "always/necessarily" nuance tied to generalizations.
- **とは言えない** — "one can't say that ~," focuses more on the speaker's inability to assert something confidently.
- **必ずしも～ない** — frequently paired with とは限らない for emphasis (必ずしも～とは限らない), meaning "not necessarily always."

**Opposite grammar structures:** No single-term opposite, though 必ず～ (always ~) expresses the absolute claim that とは限らない denies.

**Example sentences:**
1. 高い物がいつも質がいいとは限らない。
   (Expensive things aren't always high quality.)
2. 勉強しても、試験に合格するとは限らない。
   (Even if you study, it's not necessarily true that you'll pass the exam.)
3. お金持ちが必ずしも幸せだとは限らない。
   (Rich people aren't necessarily always happy.)
4. 経験が長いからといって、上手だとは限らない。
   (Just because someone has a lot of experience doesn't mean they're necessarily skilled.)
5. 天気予報が当たるとは限らないので、傘を持っていくつもりだ。
   (Weather forecasts aren't always right, so I plan to bring an umbrella.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** とは限らない is often paired with 必ずしも for emphasis, but learners sometimes drop 必ずしも and the sentence still works — the key nuance is that it denies a *universal* claim, not a specific instance, so it shouldn't be used to simply negate a single fact.
