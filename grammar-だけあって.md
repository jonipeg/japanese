# ～だけあって {#だけあって}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～だけあって

**Furigana form:** ～だけあって (no kanji)

**Meaning:** "Being worthy of X, as one would expect from X" — praises a result that matches or justifies a person's reputation, status, or effort.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + だけあって
- な形容詞 + な + だけあって
- い形容詞 (plain form) + だけあって
- 動詞 (plain form) + だけあって

**Typical context:** Spoken and written Japanese, admiring/complimentary tone, almost always positive.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**だけに**](grammar-だけに.md) — broader; can intensify either a positive or negative outcome, not limited to praise.
- **さすが～だけあって** — reinforced version combining さすが ("as expected") with だけあって for stronger admiration.
- **にふさわしく** — "befitting/worthy of," a more formal way to express that something matches its status.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though ～にもかかわらず ("despite X") contrasts by describing an outcome that defies expectation rather than confirming it.

**Example sentences:**
1. さすが一流レストランだけあって、料理の味も盛り付けも素晴らしい。
   (As expected of a top-class restaurant, both the taste and presentation are wonderful.)
2. 彼女は長くアメリカに住んでいただけあって、発音がとても自然だ。
   (Having lived in America for a long time, her pronunciation is very natural.)
3. プロの選手だけあって、動きが全然違う。
   (Being a pro athlete, his movements are completely different.)
4. 毎日練習しているだけあって、ピアノの腕前が上達した。
   (Precisely because she practices every day, her piano skill has improved as expected.)
5. 歴史ある大学だけあって、キャンパスの建物も立派だ。
   (Being a university with a long history, the campus buildings are impressive too.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** だけあって is almost always positive and admiring; using it to describe a disappointing or negative result sounds unnatural. For negative intensification, use だけに instead.
