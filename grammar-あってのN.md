# ～あってのN {#あってのN}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～あってのN

**Furigana form:** ～あってのN

**Meaning:** "N exists because of X / N owes its existence to X" — states that a following noun (often an achievement or relationship) is only possible thanks to the preceding element; without it, the noun wouldn't exist.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + あっての + 名詞
- Example base: お客様 → お客様あっての商売

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal, usable in both speech and writing; often appears in humble or appreciative statements acknowledging the true source of one's success or possibility, common in business and heartfelt speeches.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **のおかげで** — "thanks to X"; a more general and common way to credit something for an outcome, usable in any register, while あってのN is more fixed/idiomatic and specifically frames the second noun as entirely dependent on, almost nonexistent without, the first.
- **なくしては～ない** — "without X, ~ is not possible"; expresses essentially the same dependency relationship but as a negative conditional clause rather than a noun-modifying phrase.
- **に支えられた** — "supported by X"; similarly credits a foundation for a following achievement, though に支えられた frames it passively as "being supported," while あってのN more directly asserts existential dependency ("only exists because of").

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a noun described as independent of any such supporting factor (単独で成り立つN, "N that stands on its own") would conceptually contrast with あってのN's dependency framing.

**Example sentences:**
1. お客様あっての商売だということを忘れてはいけない。 (We must not forget that our business exists only because of our customers.)
2. 健康あっての人生だと、病気になって初めて気づいた。 (It was only after getting sick that I realized life is only meaningful with health.)
3. チームの協力あっての優勝だった。 (The championship was only possible thanks to the team's cooperation.)
4. 家族あっての自分だと、彼は改めて実感した。 (He realized once again that he is who he is only because of his family.)
5. 視聴者あってのテレビ番組だから、常に意見を大切にしている。 (Since TV programs only exist because of viewers, we always value their opinions.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** あってのN is a fixed idiomatic construction specifically emphasizing existential dependency — "without the first element, the second simply would not exist/matter" — it is stronger than a simple "thanks to" statement. Learners should recognize the implicit warning tone often present: it's frequently used to humbly remind someone (or oneself) not to take the supporting factor for granted (お客様あっての商売 reminds a business not to forget its customers).
