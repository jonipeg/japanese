# それなのに {#それなのに}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** それなのに (no kanji; always written in kana)

**Furigana form:** それなのに

**Meaning:** "And yet, despite that" — a contrastive conjunction that adds an emotional nuance of surprise, frustration, or unfairness, stronger than plain "however."

**Conjugation:** Sentence-initial conjunction, no conjugation needed — attaches at the sentence boundary, connecting the previous sentence to the next.

**Typical context:** Usable in both spoken and written Japanese, with a notably emotional/expressive tone. Common in personal narratives, complaints, diary writing, and emotional speech where the speaker wants to highlight that an outcome feels unjust or disappointing given what preceded it. Less neutral than しかし — it signals the speaker's feelings, not just a logical contrast.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**だけど**](grammar-だけど.md) — very casual plain contrast; lacks それなのに's emotional charge of unfairness/surprise.
- [**でも**](grammar-でも.md) — casual spoken "but"; more neutral than それなのに.
- [**けれども**](grammar-けれども.md) — polite-neutral contrast; lacks the emotional complaint nuance.
- [**しかし**](grammar-しかし.md) — standard written/formal "however"; a plain logical contrast, whereas それなのに adds frustration or disbelief.
- [**だが**](grammar-だが.md) — literary/terse "but"; dry and matter-of-fact, unlike それなのに's expressive tone.
- [**一方**](grammar-一方.md) — contrasts two parallel facts/perspectives neutrally and structurally; それなのに instead expresses emotional disappointment about a single unfair outcome.

**Opposite grammar structures:** そして/それで (neutral continuation/result conjunctions) oppose それなのに structurally, since they simply continue the narrative without the emotional contrast.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女は何度も謝った。それなのに、彼は許してくれなかった。
   (She apologized many times. And yet, he wouldn't forgive her.)
2. 一生懸命勉強した。それなのに、テストの点数は散々だった。
   (I studied so hard. And yet, my test score was terrible.)
3. 約束したじゃない。それなのに、どうして来なかったの?
   (You promised, didn't you? And yet, why didn't you come?)
4. 彼はずっと体に気をつけていた。それなのに、病気になってしまった。
   (He had always taken care of his health. And yet, he got sick anyway.)
5. お金も時間もかけた。それなのに、プロジェクトは失敗に終わった。
   (We invested money and time. And yet, the project ended in failure.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often use それなのに interchangeably with しかし, but それなのに always carries an emotional undertone of frustration, disappointment, or a sense of unfairness — using it in a neutral factual report (where しかし or 一方 would fit better) can sound overly dramatic or whiny. Conversely, using しかし where the speaker clearly feels wronged can sound flat and under-expressive. Native speakers reserve それなのに for moments of genuine surprise or complaint.
