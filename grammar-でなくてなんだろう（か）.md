# ～でなくてなんだろう（か） {#でなくてなんだろう（か）}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～でなくてなんだろう（か）

**Furigana form:** ～でなくてなんだろう（か）

**Meaning:** A rhetorical expression meaning "if this isn't X, then what is?" — used to strongly assert that something can only be described as X, typically to convey admiration, emotion, or exasperation. A stiff, literary expression.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + でなくてなんだろう（か）
- これこそ天職でなくてなんだろう — "if this isn't my true calling, then what is?"

**Typical context:** Written and formal spoken Japanese — essays, speeches, and emotionally charged narrative passages. Rarely used in plain everyday conversation due to its dramatic, rhetorical tone.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～以外の何ものでもない** — similarly asserts "this is nothing other than X," with comparable emphatic force, though it reads slightly less rhetorical/questioning and more flatly declarative.
- **～と言わずしてなんと言おう（か）** — another rhetorical-question pattern with the same "what else could you call it" logic, often interchangeable in tone and formality.
- **まさに～だ** — a simpler, more direct way to assert "this is truly X," lacking the rhetorical-question structure but conveying similar conviction.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a hedged, uncertain statement (e.g., ～かもしれない, "it might be X") would instead express doubt rather than the strong conviction this pattern conveys.

**Example sentences:**
1. 毎日仕事が楽しくてしかたがない。これこそ天職でなくてなんだろう。
   (I find my work enjoyable every single day without fail — if this isn't my true calling, then what is?)
2. 一度聞いただけの曲をかんぺきに演奏できるなんて、彼は天才でなくてなんだろうか。
   (Being able to perform a piece perfectly after hearing it just once — if he isn't a genius, then what is he?)
3. このような所に道路を作るのは、税金の無駄遣いでなくてなんであろうか。
   (Building a road in a place like this — if it isn't a waste of taxpayer money, then what is it?)
4. 家族のために毎日働き続ける父の姿、これが愛でなくてなんだろう。
   (My father working tirelessly every day for his family — if this isn't love, then what is?)
5. 一夜にしてすべてを失うとは、悲劇でなくてなんであろうか。
   (To lose everything in a single night — if that isn't a tragedy, then what is?)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This is a rhetorical-question pattern, not a genuine question — it is used to express strong emotion such as admiration, astonishment, or grief, and always attaches to nouns carrying a somewhat grand or dramatic meaning (天職, 天才, 無駄遣い, 悲劇, etc.). Because of its emphatic, almost theatrical tone, it would sound out of place applied to a mundane or trivial noun, and it is uncommon in casual daily speech.
