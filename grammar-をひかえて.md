# ～をひかえて {#をひかえて}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～を控えて

**Furigana form:** ～をひかえて

**Meaning:** "With X coming up / facing X (in time or place)" — describes being in a state of anticipation or preparation because an event is imminent, or being positioned near something.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + を控えて
- Example base: 試験 → 試験を一週間後に控えて

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal, usable in both speech and writing; common in describing the lead-up to an important event (exam, wedding, competition) or physical proximity to a landmark.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **を前に（して）** — "with X ahead / in the face of X"; very close in meaning, both describing anticipation of an upcoming event, largely interchangeable with を控えて.
- **に向けて** — "heading toward X / in preparation for X"; emphasizes forward-looking preparation/effort toward a goal, whereas を控えて more neutrally states the temporal or spatial proximity itself.
- **を目前に** — "with X right before one's eyes / imminent"; emphasizes extreme closeness in time, a more intense variant of を控えて.

**Opposite grammar structures:** ～を終えて／～の後で ("having finished X / after X") expresses the reverse temporal relationship — looking back after an event rather than ahead to one.

**Example sentences:**
1. 試験を一週間後に控えて毎日勉強に追われている。 (With the exam coming up in a week, I'm swamped with studying every day.)
2. この寺は美しい山を背後に控えている。 (This temple has a beautiful mountain situated behind it.)
3. 結婚式を来月に控え、彼女は準備に忙しい。 (With her wedding coming up next month, she's busy with preparations.)
4. 開幕戦を明日に控え、選手たちは最終調整を行った。 (With the season opener coming up tomorrow, the players made their final adjustments.)
5. 退職を来年に控えて、今後の生活設計を考え始めた。 (With retirement coming up next year, I've begun thinking about my future life plans.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** を控えて most commonly describes a time-based anticipation (an event coming up soon), but it can also describe physical/spatial positioning (something situated near/behind something else) — learners should recognize both uses rather than assuming it's purely temporal. It typically implies active preparation or awareness of the approaching event, giving it a slightly more purposeful tone than a neutral "in X days" time expression.
