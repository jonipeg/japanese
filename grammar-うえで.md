# ～うえで {#うえで}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～上で

**Furigana form:** ～うえで

**Meaning:** Two related uses: (1) "after doing ~ (and then)" — sequencing an important step before another action; (2) "in the process of ~ / when it comes to ~" — indicating the context/basis in which something is relevant or necessary.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞た形 + 上で — 例: よく考えた上で (after thinking it through carefully)
- 動詞辞書形/名詞の + 上で — 例: 生きていく上で (in the process of living / when it comes to living)

**Typical context:** Written and formal spoken — common in official procedures, advice-giving, and business contexts (e.g., forms stating "after confirming the details, please sign").

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **てから** — "after doing," simpler and more casual sequencing without the formal "as an important step" nuance.
- [**に際して**](grammar-際に・に際して.md) — "on the occasion of/when doing," more about formal timing than sequential importance.
- [**において**](grammar-において.md) — "in the context of," closer to the second usage of うえで (context/basis) but more formal and abstract.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. よく考えた上で、返事をします。
   (I will reply after thinking it over carefully.)
2. 契約書の内容を確認した上で、サインしてください。
   (Please sign after confirming the contents of the contract.)
3. 外国語を学ぶ上で、継続することが一番大切だ。
   (When it comes to learning a foreign language, continuing consistently is the most important thing.)
4. 仕事をする上で、コミュニケーション能力は欠かせない。
   (When it comes to working, communication skills are indispensable.)
5. 両親と相談した上で、進路を決めるつもりだ。
   (I intend to decide my future path after consulting with my parents.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** うえで implies the first action is a deliberate, often important prerequisite step (confirming, consulting, considering) before the next, unlike てから which is a neutral, simple time sequence — using うえで for trivial sequences (e.g., 顔を洗った上で、朝食を食べた) sounds overly formal/unnatural for an everyday routine.
