# あと {#あと}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** あと (後 is possible but this conjunction use is conventionally written in kana)

**Furigana form:** あと

**Meaning:** "Also, additionally" — a casual conjunction adding a point, often as an afterthought.

**Conjugation:** Sentence-initial conjunction, no conjugation needed — attaches at the sentence boundary, connecting the previous sentence/clause to the next.

**Typical context:** Casual spoken register, extremely common in everyday conversation and informal texting when the speaker suddenly remembers one more thing to add ("oh, and also..."). Not used in formal writing or business contexts, where また, それに, or さらに would be expected.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**それに**](grammar-それに.md) — adds a supplementary point reinforcing an argument; more persuasive in flavor than あと's casual afterthought tone.
- [**また**](grammar-また.md) — neutral listing of parallel items, more formal-friendly and composed than あと.
- [**および**](grammar-および.md) — very formal/written "and," used in official documents; essentially opposite register from あと.
- [**さらに**](grammar-さらに.md) — "furthermore, moreover," written/formal, escalating emphasis; あと is casual and doesn't carry that formal weight.
- **しかも/そのうえ** — add a surprising/notable extra fact; あと is plainer and more casual, just tacking something on rather than amplifying.
- [**それも**](grammar-それも.md) — emphatic/exasperated reinforcement of the prior statement; あと is more neutral, simply remembering to mention something else.

**Opposite grammar structures:** しかし/だけど (casual contrastive "but" conjunctions) are the functional opposite, since they contrast rather than add to the preceding statement.

**Example sentences:**
1. 牛乳買ってきて。あと、卵もお願い。
   (Please buy milk. Oh, and eggs too, please.)
2. 今日は掃除した。あと、洗濯もした。
   (I cleaned today. Also, I did the laundry.)
3. 会議は3時からだよ。あと、資料も忘れないでね。
   (The meeting is from 3. Oh, and don't forget the materials either.)
4. この店、安いし美味しい。あと、店員さんも親切。
   (This shop is cheap and delicious. Also, the staff are kind too.)
5. 明日は早く来てね。あと、筆記用具も持ってきて。
   (Please come early tomorrow. Oh, and bring writing supplies too.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes use あと in formal writing or presentations, where it sounds too casual and unplanned — また or さらに would be the appropriate formal swap. あと's defining nuance is that it reads as spontaneous, as if the speaker just remembered the additional point, whereas また/それに feel more like a planned, structured addition.
