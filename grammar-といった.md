# ～といった {#といった}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～と言った

**Furigana form:** ～といった

**Meaning:** "Such as X (and similar things)" — introduces one or more representative examples of a broader category, similar to English "such as" or "like."

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞（、名詞）+ といった + 名詞
- Example base: 鰯や鯛 → 鰯や鯛といった魚

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal, usable in both speech and writing; common when giving illustrative examples within a category, in both conversational and written explanatory contexts.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**など**](grammar-など・なんて・なんか.md) — plain "etc./such as"; the most common, casual-to-neutral way to give examples, usable in nearly any register, while といった is slightly more formal/descriptive and typically modifies a following noun directly.
- **とか～とか** — casual "things like X and Y"; a more conversational listing construction, lacking といった's more composed, written-style feel.
- **というような** — "like X" (more hedged/approximate); close in meaning, slightly softer/more tentative in framing the examples as approximate illustrations.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; specifying a complete, exhaustive list (すべての～、正確には～のみ, "all of them, precisely only X") would contrast with といった's illustrative, non-exhaustive sampling.

**Example sentences:**
1. この近海では、鰯や鯛といった魚が釣れます。 (In these coastal waters, you can catch fish such as sardines and sea bream.)
2. 京都や奈良といった歴史的な都市を訪れたい。 (I want to visit historic cities such as Kyoto and Nara.)
3. 彼はテニスやゴルフといったスポーツが得意だ。 (He's good at sports such as tennis and golf.)
4. りんごやみかんといった果物をよく食べる。 (I often eat fruits such as apples and mandarins.)
5. 漢字や文法といった分野に苦手意識を持つ学習者は多い。 (Many learners feel they struggle with areas such as kanji and grammar.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** といった directly modifies the following noun (functioning adjectivally), so it must be followed by a noun that names the category being illustrated (魚, 都市, スポーツ) — it cannot simply end a sentence the way など can. Learners should note the examples given are meant to be representative/illustrative, not an exhaustive list — don't read といった as implying only those items exist in the category.
