# でも {#でも}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** でも (no kanji; always written in kana)

**Furigana form:** でも

**Meaning:** "But, however" — the most common everyday casual conjunction for contrast.

**Conjugation:** Sentence-initial conjunction, no conjugation needed — attaches at the sentence boundary, connecting the previous sentence/clause to the next.

**Typical context:** Casual conversational register, the default "but" in everyday spoken Japanese among friends, family, and in informal writing (texts, casual blog posts). Not appropriate in formal writing or business contexts, where しかし or けれども would be used instead.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**だけど**](grammar-だけど.md) — also very casual spoken "but," near-synonym to でも, sometimes feels slightly more hedging/conversational.
- [**けれども**](grammar-けれども.md) — polite-neutral, spoken/written, more polished and softer in formality than でも.
- [**しかし**](grammar-しかし.md) — standard written/formal "however"; far more formal than でも.
- [**だが**](grammar-だが.md) — literary/written, terse; opposite register extreme from でも.
- [**それなのに**](grammar-それなのに.md) — adds an emotional "and yet, despite that" nuance of surprise/unfairness; でも is plainer and more neutral.
- [**一方**](grammar-一方.md) — formal structural contrast between parallel facts; unrelated register to でも.

**Opposite grammar structures:** そして/また (casual additive conjunctions) oppose でも structurally as continuation rather than contrast.

**Example sentences:**
1. 疲れてた。でも、最後まで頑張った。
   (I was tired. But I pushed through to the end.)
2. 美味しいよ。でも、ちょっと辛いかも。
   (It's delicious. But it might be a little spicy.)
3. 行きたかった。でも、仕事があって行けなかった。
   (I wanted to go. But I had work, so I couldn't.)
4. 彼は優しい。でも、たまに厳しいことも言う。
   (He's kind. But sometimes he says harsh things too.)
5. 高かった。でも、買ってよかったと思う。
   (It was expensive. But I think it was worth buying.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often overuse でも in formal writing where しかし would be expected, or underuse it in casual speech by defaulting to the more formal-sounding しかし, which can sound stilted among friends. Also note でも has a separate, unrelated usage as a particle meaning "even" or "or something" (e.g. 誰でも "anyone," お茶でも飲もう "let's have tea or something") — don't confuse that particle usage with the sentence-initial conjunction usage covered here.
