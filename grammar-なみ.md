# ～なみ {#なみ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～並み

**Furigana form:** ～なみ

**Meaning:** "On par with/equivalent to X" — expresses that something matches the standard, level, or quality of X, often used to describe something reaching an average or typical standard.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 (noun) + なみ
- Example from source: 世間並み (on par with society/the average person)

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal, usable in both written and spoken Japanese. No strong politeness/rudeness connotation; often appears in comparisons about living standards, prices, weather, or performance levels ("as good as," "equivalent to"). Common in everyday descriptive speech as well as formal writing.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **を境に (さかいに)** — means "with X as the turning point/boundary," a temporal/sequential division; this was a confirmed confusion pair on a graded test, where a student picked なみ instead of を境に. The two are unrelated in meaning — なみ is a comparison of level/standard, while を境に marks a before/after turning point.
- **並み/のごとく** — "like X," a simile; のごとく is more literary and emphasizes resemblance/manner rather than equivalence in standard or degree.
- **同様に** — "similarly to X / in the same way as X"; a more general similarity marker, without なみ's specific nuance of "reaching the same standard/level."
- **並み外れた (なみはずれた)** — "extraordinary, exceeding the norm" — the adjectival form built from 並み that describes something going beyond the standard, essentially an antonym-like derivative.

**Opposite grammar structures:** 並み外れ(た) ("exceptional, beyond the norm") functions as a near-opposite, describing something that exceeds rather than matches the standard.

**Example sentences:**
1. せめて世間並みの生活ができれば充分だと思っています。
   (I think it would be enough if we could just live an average, ordinary life.)
2. 今年の夏は真夏なみの暑さが続いている。
   (This summer, midsummer-level heat has been continuing.)
3. 彼のテニスの腕前はプロ並みだ。
   (His tennis skill is on par with a professional's.)
4. この店のラーメンは、有名店並みのおいしさだ。
   (The ramen at this shop is as delicious as a famous restaurant's.)
5. 彼女は大人並みの判断力を持っている。
   (She has judgment on par with an adult's.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** On a graded test, a student picked なみで when the correct answer was を境に(さかいに) — the key distinction is that なみ expresses "equivalent in standard/level to X" (a static comparison), while を境に marks "X as the dividing point/turning point between before and after" (a temporal shift). Students should check whether the sentence is comparing a quality/level (→なみ) or marking a before/after boundary in time (→を境に).
