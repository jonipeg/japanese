# ～うちに {#うちに}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～内に

**Furigana form:** ～うちに

**Meaning:** "While ~ / before ~ (changes) / take the opportunity during a limited window" — expresses that an action should be done within a certain state or time period before that state ends or changes.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ている形 + うちに — 例: 温かいうちに (while it's still warm)
- 動詞ない形 + うちに — 例: 暗くならないうちに (before it gets dark)
- い形容詞 + うちに — 例: 若いうちに (while you're young)
- な形容詞＋な + うちに — 例: 元気なうちに
- 名詞＋の + うちに — 例: 学生のうちに

**Typical context:** Both spoken and written, neutral register — very common in everyday advice ("do X while you still can") and casual conversation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **間に** — "during the interval of," more neutral/time-focused, without the nuance of "before this window closes."
- **ないうちに** — the negative form specifically emphasizes "before [something undesirable] happens."
- **うちが花** — idiomatic expression meaning "it's best while it lasts," related in spirit but idiomatic rather than grammatical.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though 後で (afterward) contrasts by describing doing something once the window has passed (often implying it's too late or different).

**Example sentences:**
1. 温かいうちに、どうぞ召し上がってください。
   (Please eat it while it's still warm.)
2. 子供が若いうちに、たくさん旅行に連れて行きたい。
   (I want to take my children on many trips while they're still young.)
3. 雨が降らないうちに、洗濯物を取り込もう。
   (Let's bring in the laundry before it starts raining.)
4. 元気なうちに、やりたいことをやっておくべきだ。
   (You should do what you want to do while you're still healthy.)
5. 学生であるうちに、いろいろな経験をしておいたほうがいい。
   (It's better to have various experiences while you're still a student.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** うちに conveys urgency — "this window won't last, act now" — whereas 間に is purely descriptive of simultaneous time without that urgency; learners sometimes use 間に where うちに's "don't miss this chance" nuance is actually intended, flattening the emotional tone of the sentence.
