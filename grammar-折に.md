# ～折に {#折に}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～折に

**Furigana form:** ～おりに

**Meaning:** "On the occasion of X" — marks a particular time, occasion, or opportunity, often used for polite, somewhat formal references to a chance meeting or event.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞た形 (verb た-form) + 折に
- 名詞 + の + 折に
- Example from source: 何かの折にでも (on some occasion or other)

**Typical context:** Formal, polite written and spoken Japanese — frequently used in letters, greetings, and polite conversation (e.g., when parting ways, expressing hope to meet again). Carries a refined, courteous tone; common in business and formal social correspondence. Not used in casual speech.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **際に (さいに)** — "on the occasion of X," very close synonym; 際に is slightly more neutral/common in both formal writing and spoken Japanese, while 折に feels more elegant/old-fashioned and letter-like.
- [**に際して**](grammar-際に・に際して.md) — similarly "upon the occasion of X," often used for significant formal events (ceremonies, important business matters); slightly weightier/more formal than 折に.
- [**おかげで**](grammar-おかげで.md) — "thanks to X"; attributes a positive result to a cause, unrelated to marking a time/occasion.
- [**たびに**](grammar-たびに.md) — "every time X"; describes a recurring, habitual occasion, whereas 折に typically refers to a single, specific (often future, hoped-for) occasion.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. また何かの折にでも、お会いしたいですね。
   (I'd love to meet again sometime, whenever the occasion arises.)
2. 上京の折には、ぜひ我が家にお立ち寄りください。
   (Whenever you come to Tokyo, please be sure to stop by our house.)
3. お目にかかった折に、詳しいお話をうかがいたいと思います。
   (I'd like to hear the details when we have the chance to meet.)
4. 帰省の折には、昔の友人にも会ってみようと思う。
   (When I go back to my hometown, I think I'll try to meet up with old friends too.)
5. 何かお困りの折には、いつでもご連絡ください。
   (Whenever you're in trouble, please feel free to contact me at any time.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** No confirmed student wrong-answer is recorded for this pattern in the source. A key nuance for learners: 折に is almost always used for pleasant, polite, or hoped-for occasions (meeting someone, visiting a place) rather than negative or routine ones, and it carries a more literary/letter-writing flavor than its common synonym 際に. Students should prefer 折に in polite correspondence and 際に in everyday formal speech.
