# Vます+に+Vた (emphasis) {#に-強調用法}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** 待ちに待った (example); general pattern: Vます形+に+Vた形

**Furigana form:** まちにまった (example); general pattern: Vますけい+に+Vたけい

**Meaning:** "Really/thoroughly ~ed" — an emphatic construction where the same verb's ます-stem is repeated with に before the past-tense form, intensifying the meaning (e.g., 待ちに待った = "waited and waited," i.e., long-awaited).

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ます形 (verb ます-stem) + に + 同じ動詞のた形 (same verb's た-form)
- Example: 待ちに待った帰国の日がついにやってきた。 (The long-awaited day of returning home finally arrived.)

**Typical context:** Spoken and written Japanese, neutral-to-literary register; often used in narration or dramatic storytelling to emphasize the intensity or long duration of waiting/longing for something.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **ずっと～ていた** — "had been ~ing for a long time" — conveys continuous duration but without the rhythmic, idiomatic emphasis of the repeated verb structure.
- **散々～した** — "did ~ thoroughly/to excess" — a different intensifier using an adverb rather than verb repetition, usable with a broader range of verbs.
- **心待ちにしていた** — "had been eagerly looking forward to" — specifically about anticipation, similar in spirit to 待ちに待った but grammatically a compound noun + する construction.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though あっさり～した ("did ~ simply/without much feeling") contrasts in spirit by indicating a lack of intensity.

**Example sentences:**
1. 待ちに待った帰国の日がついにやってきた。
   (The long-awaited day of returning home finally arrived.)
2. 彼は考えに考えた末、ついに結論を出した。
   (After thinking and thinking it over, he finally reached a conclusion.)
3. 泣きに泣いた彼女も、今ではすっかり元気を取り戻した。
   (She, who had cried and cried, has now completely recovered her spirits.)
4. 悩みに悩んだ結果、彼女はその仕事を辞めることに決めた。
   (After agonizing over it again and again, she decided to quit that job.)
5. 探しに探した末、ようやく探していた本を見つけた。
   (After searching and searching, I finally found the book I had been looking for.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern only works by repeating the exact same verb in both positions (待ちに待った, not 待ちに探した) — it is a fixed idiomatic intensifier, not a general connector, and is mostly limited to a set of common verbs like 待つ, 考える, 悩む, 泣く, 探す.
