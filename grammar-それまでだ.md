# ～それまでだ {#それまでだ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～それまでだ

**Furigana form:** ～それまでだ

**Meaning:** "That's the end of it" / "if X happens, everything is over/pointless" — expresses finality or futility, commonly used in the pattern "no matter how much effort is put in, if Y happens, それまでだ (it's all for nothing)."

**Conjugation:**
- Clause (often 〜ば/〜たら/〜ても) + それまでだ

**Typical context:** Formal-leaning written/spoken register, often with a resigned, philosophical, or fatalistic tone.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**しまつだ**](grammar-しまつだ.md) — describes the regrettable final RESULT of a chain of bad events, in contrast to それまでだ's sense of total finality/futility regardless of what effort was made.
- [**きりがない**](grammar-きりがない.md) — "there's no end to X," almost an opposite concept (endlessness) compared to それまでだ's abrupt termination/finality.
- [**といったところだ**](grammar-といったところだ.md) — a neutral, modest approximation of degree, lacking それまでだ's sense of fatalistic finality.

**Opposite grammar structures:** [**きりがない**](grammar-きりがない.md) — "there's no end to it," conceptually contrasting with それまでだ's abrupt finality (though not a strict grammatical antonym pair).

**Example sentences:**
1. いくら貯金ばかりしても、死んでしまえばそれまでのことだよ。
   (No matter how much you save money, if you die, that's the end of it.)
2. どんなに頑張って作っても、審査員の好みに合わなければそれまでだ。
   (No matter how hard you work on it, if it doesn't suit the judges' taste, it's all over.)
3. 証拠がなければ、彼がいくら無実を訴えてもそれまでだ。
   (Without evidence, no matter how much he claims innocence, it's all for nothing.)
4. せっかくの計画も、天候が悪ければそれまでだ。
   (Even a carefully made plan is pointless if the weather is bad.)
5. 一度信用を失えば、それまでだ。
   (Once you lose trust, that's the end of it.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** それまでだ is part of the N1 extreme-degree/finality cluster and is sometimes confused with しまつだ because both can sound like a final, negative conclusion to a sentence. The key difference: しまつだ names a specific accumulated bad RESULT after describing the events that led there (きょうは最悪の日だった…忘れる始末だ), while それまでだ is a more abstract, conditional statement about total futility ("no matter what you do, if Y happens, it's all over") and does not require a preceding list of bad events.
