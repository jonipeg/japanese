## 雑木 (ぞうき)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** miscellaneous small trees, mixed woodland, a copse

**Similar words:**
- 雑木林（ぞうきばやし）— the extremely common compound form meaning "a grove/copse of mixed trees," almost always how 雑木 is encountered.
- 林（はやし）— a general word for "woods/grove," without the "miscellaneous/non-specific species" nuance of 雑木.

**Opposite words:**
- 名木（めいぼく）— a notable/famous tree (prized, named specimen)

**Example sentences:**
1. 近所に雑木林がある。— There is a mixed-tree grove near my house.
2. 雑木を伐採して畑を作った。— They cleared the miscellaneous trees and made a field.
3. 雑木林を散歩するのが好きだ。— I like walking through the mixed woodland.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 雑木 almost always appears as 雑木林 (mixed-species woodland, often secondary/non-cultivated forest) — the bare word 雑木 alone (just "miscellaneous trees") is less commonly used standalone in conversation.

## 増強 (ぞうきょう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** reinforcement, buildup, to strengthen/augment

**Similar words:**
- [強化](vocab-15.md#強化-きょうか)（きょうか）— strengthening/reinforcing in general (quality, systems, measures); 増強 specifically implies adding more quantity/numbers to increase strength (troops, staff, equipment).
- 補強（ほきょう）— reinforcement, often used for physical/structural reinforcement (e.g., building supports) or supplementing a weak point.

**Opposite words:**
- 削減（さくげん）— reduction, cutback

**Example sentences:**
1. 軍備を増強する。— To build up (increase) military armaments.
2. チームは戦力を増強するため新人を獲得した。— The team acquired new recruits to reinforce its strength.
3. 生産体制を増強する必要がある。— There is a need to strengthen the production system.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 増強 specifically implies increasing quantity to boost strength (more troops, more staff, more capacity) rather than improving quality per se — for purely qualitative strengthening (e.g., strengthening a relationship or a rule), 強化 is more appropriate.

## 送金 (そうきん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** money transfer, remittance, to send money

**Similar words:**
- 振込（ふりこみ）— a bank transfer specifically via deposit into an account, a more concrete/operational term than the general 送金.
- 支払い（しはらい）— payment in general, which may or may not involve transferring money remotely; 送金 specifically implies sending money from a distance.

**Opposite words:**
- 入金（にゅうきん）— receiving/depositing money

**Example sentences:**
1. 海外に送金する手続きをした。— I went through the procedure to send money overseas.
2. 毎月、実家に送金している。— I send money to my parents' house every month.
3. 送金手数料が高い。— The money transfer fee is expensive.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 送金 emphasizes the "sending" direction specifically — when talking about simply paying for a product/service (not sending money to a person/account from afar), 支払う is more natural than 送金する.

## 増減 (ぞうげん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** increase and/or decrease, fluctuation

**Similar words:**
- 変動（へんどう）— fluctuation/change, a broader term not limited to up/down movement in quantity (can include qualitative change too).
- 上下（じょうげ）— up and down, can be used figuratively for fluctuation similar to 増減 but more literally spatial in origin.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 体重の増減を記録している。— I'm recording the fluctuation (increase/decrease) in my weight.
2. 売上の増減をグラフにまとめた。— I compiled the sales fluctuations into a graph.
3. 季節によって来客数が増減する。— The number of visitors fluctuates depending on the season.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 増減 packages "increase AND/OR decrease" into one word describing fluctuation — it's not just "increase," so don't use it when you mean a one-directional rise (use 増加 instead).

## 倉庫 (そうこ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a warehouse, a storehouse

**Similar words:**
- [蔵](vocab-16.md#蔵-くら)（くら）— a traditional Japanese storehouse (often for valuables, rice, sake), more historical/cultural in flavor than the modern, industrial 倉庫.
- 物置（ものおき）— a small storage shed/closet for household items, much smaller scale than a 倉庫.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 商品を倉庫に保管している。— The products are stored in the warehouse.
2. 倉庫でアルバイトをしている。— I work part-time at a warehouse.
3. 古い倉庫を改装してカフェにした。— They renovated an old warehouse into a cafe.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 倉庫 refers to a modern, often industrial/commercial storage building — for a traditional Japanese-style storehouse with cultural/historical connotations, 蔵 is the more specific and evocative term.

## 相互 (そうご)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** mutual, reciprocal

**Similar words:**
- [お互い](vocab-10.md#お互いに-おたがいに)（おたがい）— "each other," a more everyday/conversational term; 相互 is more formal and often used in compounds (相互理解, 相互作用) in written/academic Japanese.
- 双方（そうほう）— "both parties/sides," emphasizing two distinct sides rather than the reciprocal relationship nuance of 相互.

**Opposite words:**
- 一方的（いっぽうてき）— one-sided, unilateral

**Example sentences:**
1. 両国は相互に協力することに合意した。— The two countries agreed to cooperate mutually.
2. 相互理解を深めることが大切だ。— Deepening mutual understanding is important.
3. 相互作用によって新しい現象が生まれる。— New phenomena arise through mutual interaction.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 相互 is formal and almost always used as a prefix/modifier in compounds (相互理解, 相互関係, 相互作用) rather than standing alone — in everyday speech, お互いに is far more natural.

## 走行 (そうこう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** running, traveling, driving (of a vehicle)

**Similar words:**
- 運転（うんてん）— driving (operating a vehicle), focuses on the driver's action; 走行 focuses on the vehicle's movement/travel itself (e.g., distance traveled, 走行距離).
- [移動](vocab-07.md#移動-いどう)（いどう）— movement/travel in general, not specific to vehicles and their mechanical running.

**Opposite words:**
- 停止（ていし）— stopping

**Example sentences:**
1. この車は走行距離が少ない。— This car has low mileage (distance traveled).
2. 高速道路を走行中に事故が起きた。— An accident occurred while driving on the highway.
3. 新しいエンジンの走行性能をテストした。— They tested the running performance of the new engine.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 走行 focuses on the vehicle's motion/mileage (走行距離, 走行中) rather than the driver's action of operating it — for "who is driving," 運転 is the correct word, not 走行.

## 総合 (そうごう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** synthesis, comprehensive, overall, total

**Similar words:**
- 総括（そうかつ）— summarizing/wrapping up (e.g., a report, a period), more about concluding and reviewing than combining multiple elements into one whole.
- [統合](vocab-04.md#統合-とうごう)（とうごう）— integration/consolidation (e.g., merging organizations or systems), implies actively merging separate things, whereas 総合 is more about viewing/treating things comprehensively as a whole.

**Opposite words:**
- 個別（こべつ）— individual, separate

**Example sentences:**
1. 総合的に判断する。— To judge comprehensively.
2. この大学には総合学部がある。— This university has a general studies department.
3. 総合病院で検査を受けた。— I got examined at a general hospital.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 総合 commonly appears as a prefix meaning "general/comprehensive" (総合病院 = general hospital, 総合的 = comprehensively) — learners should recognize it as a modifier pattern rather than translating it narrowly as just "synthesis."

## 操作 (そうさ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** operation, handling, manipulation (of a machine/device)

**Similar words:**
- 運転（うんてん）— operating/driving, more specific to vehicles or large machinery (trains, cars) than general device handling.
- [操縦](#操縦-そうじゅう)（そうじゅう）— piloting/steering, implies controlling complex, often moving vehicles (planes, ships) rather than general devices like a remote or computer.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. このリモコンの操作は簡単だ。— Operating this remote control is easy.
2. 機械の操作方法を教えてください。— Please teach me how to operate the machine.
3. データを不正に操作した疑いがある。— There is suspicion that the data was illegally manipulated.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 操作 extends beyond physical device handling to the negative figurative sense of "manipulation" (データ操作 = data manipulation/tampering, 世論操作 = manipulating public opinion) — learners should be aware of this darker connotation alongside the neutral "operating a device" meaning.

## 創作 (そうさく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** creation, to create (artistic/literary work)

**Similar words:**
- 作成（さくせい）— creating/making something practical (a document, a plan), neutral and administrative, lacking 創作's artistic/original connotation.
- 創造（そうぞう）— creation in a more philosophical/grand sense (e.g., creating the universe, pure originality), slightly more abstract/elevated than the hands-on artistic 創作.

**Opposite words:**
- 模倣（もほう）— imitation, copying

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女は小説を創作している。— She is writing (creating) a novel.
2. この話は完全な創作だ。— This story is a complete fabrication (creative invention).
3. 創作活動に専念したい。— I want to devote myself to creative activities.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 創作 can mean both positive "artistic creation" and, in casual usage, "a made-up story/fabrication" (それは創作でしょ = "that's made up, right?") — context determines whether it's praise for creativity or an accusation of fabrication.

## 葬式 (そうしき)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a funeral

**Similar words:**
- 告別式（こくべつしき）— a funeral/farewell ceremony, often used for the more formal public part of the funeral proceedings, as distinct from 葬式 which is the broader overall term.
- 法事（ほうじ）— a Buddhist memorial service held after the funeral (e.g., on anniversaries), distinct from the funeral itself.

**Opposite words:**
- 結婚式（けっこんしき）— a wedding (culturally paired life-event contrast)

**Example sentences:**
1. 祖父の葬式に参列した。— I attended my grandfather's funeral.
2. 葬式では黒い服を着るのがマナーだ。— Wearing black clothing at a funeral is proper etiquette.
3. 葬式の準備で家族は忙しかった。— The family was busy with funeral preparations.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 葬式 is the general everyday term for "funeral," while 告別式 refers more specifically to the ceremonial farewell portion — in formal invitations/notices, 告別式 or お葬式 (with honorific お) may appear instead of the bare 葬式.

## 喪失 (そうしつ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** loss, to lose (something abstract, e.g. memory, confidence)

**Similar words:**
- [失う](vocab-08.md#失う-うしなう)（うしなう）— the everyday verb "to lose," used broadly for both concrete and abstract things; 喪失 is the more formal noun form, typically reserved for abstract/significant losses (identity, memory, confidence).
- 紛失（ふんしつ）— losing a physical object (misplacing it), a concrete counterpart to the abstract 喪失.

**Opposite words:**
- [獲得](vocab-04.md#獲得-かくとく)（かくとく）— acquisition, gaining

**Example sentences:**
1. 事故で記憶を喪失した。— He lost his memory in the accident.
2. 自信喪失に陥ってしまった。— I fell into a loss of confidence.
3. 戦争で多くの命が喪失された。— Many lives were lost in the war.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 喪失 is reserved for abstract, often serious losses (記憶喪失 = amnesia, 自信喪失 = loss of confidence) — for losing a physical item like a wallet, 紛失 or simply なくす is correct, not 喪失.

## 操縦 (そうじゅう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** piloting, operating, to steer/control

**Similar words:**
- [操作](#操作-そうさ)（そうさ）— general operation/handling of a device, broader and less specific to vehicles than 操縦, which implies controlling something in motion (planes, ships, even metaphorically people).
- 運転（うんてん）— driving, typically for ground vehicles (cars, trains); 操縦 is more associated with aircraft, ships, and also figurative "manipulating" people.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. パイロットが飛行機を操縦する。— The pilot flies (pilots) the airplane.
2. 彼は人を操縦するのがうまい。— He is skilled at manipulating people.
3. ドローンの操縦は思ったより難しい。— Piloting a drone is harder than I thought.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 操縦 has a notable figurative extension meaning "to manipulate/control a person" (人を操縦する), similar to how English uses "to pilot someone's emotions" less commonly — this darker, manipulative sense is distinct from the neutral "piloting a vehicle" meaning.

## 蔵相 (そうしょう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** the Minister of Finance (historical/formal title)

**Similar words:**
- 財務大臣（ざいむだいじん）— the current official title for Japan's Finance Minister; 蔵相 is an older, abbreviated term (from 大蔵大臣) still seen in historical texts and news shorthand.
- 大蔵大臣（おおくらだいじん）— the full older title that 蔵相 abbreviates, used before the Ministry of Finance was renamed 財務省.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 当時の蔵相が新しい予算案を発表した。— The Finance Minister of that time announced a new budget proposal.
2. 蔵相は経済政策について記者会見を開いた。— The Finance Minister held a press conference on economic policy.
3. 歴代の蔵相の中でも彼は有名だ。— Among successive Finance Ministers, he is famous.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 蔵相 is largely a historical/journalistic abbreviation predating the 2001 ministry reorganization — in current affairs, 財務大臣 is the standard term, so encountering 蔵相 usually signals older news articles or historical context.

## 装飾 (そうしょく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** decoration, ornamentation, to decorate

**Similar words:**
- 飾り（かざり）— the everyday noun "decoration/ornament," more casual than the formal 装飾, which is often used for interior design, architecture, or formal/artistic contexts.
- デコレーション — the loanword "decoration," commonly used for cakes, parties, and more casual/modern decorative contexts than 装飾.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. クリスマスの装飾を始めた。— We started the Christmas decorations.
2. この建物は美しい装飾が施されている。— This building has beautiful decorations applied to it.
3. 装飾品をたくさん集めている。— I collect a lot of ornamental items.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 装飾 is more formal/architectural than everyday 飾り — it's often used for elaborate, artistic, or structural decoration (building facades, formal events), while 飾り covers simpler everyday decorative items.

## 造船 (ぞうせん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** shipbuilding, to build a ship

**Similar words:**
- 建造（けんぞう）— construction/building (of large structures, including ships: 船を建造する), a more general term for large-scale building that overlaps with shipbuilding contexts.
- [製造](vocab-22.md#製造-せいぞう)（せいぞう）— manufacturing in general, much broader than the specific industry term 造船.

**Opposite words:**
- 解体（かいたい）— dismantling, scrapping (e.g., a ship)

**Example sentences:**
1. この地域は造船業で栄えてきた。— This region has prospered through the shipbuilding industry.
2. 造船所で新しい船が完成した。— A new ship was completed at the shipyard.
3. 日本の造船技術は世界的に評価されている。— Japan's shipbuilding technology is highly regarded worldwide.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 造船 is an industry-specific compound (shipbuilding as a sector, 造船所 = shipyard, 造船業 = shipbuilding industry) — it's a fixed technical term, not something you'd break apart to describe building any ordinary object.

## 想像 (そうぞう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** imagination, to imagine

**Similar words:**
- [空想](vocab-15.md#空想-くうそう)（くうそう）— fantasy/daydreaming, implies imagining something unrealistic or fanciful, whereas 想像 is more neutral and can apply to realistic speculation too.
- 創造（そうぞう）— creation (a homophone!), meaning to bring something new into existence, completely different meaning despite identical pronunciation.

**Opposite words:**
- [現実](vocab-18.md#現実-げんじつ)（げんじつ）— reality

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼の反応は想像以上だった。— His reaction was beyond my imagination (more than expected).
2. 将来のことを想像してみた。— I tried imagining what the future would be like.
3. そんなことは想像もできない。— I can't even imagine such a thing.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 想像 (imagination) and 創造 (creation) are perfect homophones (both そうぞう) with completely different kanji and meanings — a very common point of confusion in listening comprehension and kanji writing tests.

## 騒々しい (そうぞうしい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** noisy, boisterous, turbulent

**Similar words:**
- うるさい — the everyday, very common word for "noisy/loud," used constantly in casual speech; 騒々しい is more literary/descriptive, often describing an atmosphere or scene rather than directly complaining about a specific noise.
- 騒がしい（さわがしい）— very close synonym, almost interchangeable with 騒々しい, describing a bustling, noisy environment.

**Opposite words:**
- [静か](vocab-20.md#静か-しずか)（しずか）— quiet, calm

**Example sentences:**
1. 教室が騒々しくて先生が注意した。— The classroom was so noisy that the teacher gave a warning.
2. 世の中が騒々しい時代になった。— The world has become a turbulent era.
3. 騒々しい街を離れて田舎で暮らしたい。— I want to leave the noisy city and live in the countryside.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 騒々しい can describe not just literal noise but also a chaotic, unsettled social/political atmosphere (世の中が騒々しい) — unlike the purely acoustic うるさい, which is almost always about literal sound/volume.

## 相続 (そうぞく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** inheritance, succession, to inherit

**Similar words:**
- 継承（けいしょう）— succession/inheriting (a position, title, tradition), broader than 相続, which specifically refers to legal inheritance of property/assets upon someone's death.
- 遺産（いさん）— the inheritance/estate itself (the assets), whereas 相続 is the act/process of inheriting it.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 父の財産を相続した。— I inherited my father's assets.
2. 相続税について専門家に相談した。— I consulted an expert about inheritance tax.
3. 兄弟で遺産を相続することになった。— The siblings ended up inheriting the estate.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 相続 is specifically a legal term tied to inheriting assets/property after someone's death (相続税, 相続人) — for inheriting a non-material tradition, skill, or title, 継承 is the more appropriate word.

## 相対的(に) (そうたいてき)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** relative(ly), in relative terms

**Similar words:**
- [比較的](vocab-31.md#比較的-ひかくてき)（ひかくてき）— "relatively/comparatively," used when comparing something to a general standard; 相対的 specifically emphasizes dependence on a point of comparison (as opposed to being absolute).
- 絶対的（ぜったいてき）— "absolute(ly)," the direct conceptual opposite, often explicitly contrasted with 相対的 in philosophical or evaluative discussions.

**Opposite words:**
- 絶対的（ぜったいてき）— absolute

**Example sentences:**
1. 物価は相対的に安定している。— Prices are relatively stable.
2. すべての価値は相対的なものだ。— All value is relative.
3. 彼の実力は相対的に見て高い。— His skill level is high, relatively speaking.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 相対的 is almost always paired/contrasted conceptually with 絶対的 in philosophical, scientific, or evaluative writing — learners should study this pair together rather than memorizing 相対的 in isolation.

## 壮大 (そうだい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** magnificent, grand, grandiose (na-adjective)

**Similar words:**
- 壮観（そうかん）— a magnificent/spectacular sight, more about the visual impression of a scene than the overall scale/grandeur that 壮大 describes (which can apply to plans, music, landscapes).
- 雄大（ゆうだい）— grand/majestic, often used specifically for nature (mountains, landscapes), slightly narrower in application than the broader 壮大.

**Opposite words:**
- ささやか — modest, small-scale

**Example sentences:**
1. 壮大な景色に感動した。— I was moved by the grand scenery.

3. この交響曲は壮大なスケールで書かれている。— This symphony is written on a grand scale.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 壮大 can apply to both physical scale (landscapes, architecture) and abstract scale (plans, ambitions, music) — it sometimes carries a slightly skeptical undertone when describing a "grandiose but possibly unrealistic" plan, depending on context.

## 増大 (ぞうだい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** an increase, to increase (often in scale/magnitude)

**Similar words:**
- [増加](vocab-23.md#増加-ぞうか)（ぞうか）— a very close synonym meaning "increase," often used for countable numbers (population, sales); 増大 tends to emphasize growth in magnitude/scale (risk, pressure, burden) rather than simple count.
- [拡大](vocab-12.md#拡大-かくだい)（かくだい）— expansion/enlargement, emphasizing growing in size/scope rather than quantity per se.

**Opposite words:**
- [減少](vocab-02.md#減少-げんしょう)（げんしょう）— decrease

**Example sentences:**
1. 国民の負担が増大している。— The burden on citizens is increasing.
2. リスクが増大する可能性がある。— There is a possibility that the risk will increase.
3. 需要の増大に対応できていない。— We are not able to keep up with the increase in demand.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 増大 tends to pair with abstract nouns describing scale/intensity (負担, リスク, 圧力) rather than simple countable things (people, items), where 増加 is more typical — the choice subtly signals whether you're discussing scale/magnitude or raw count.

## 相談(する) (そうだん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to discuss, to consult, to talk over

**Similar words:**
- 話し合う（はなしあう）— to discuss/talk things over together, more mutual and conversational; 相談する often implies seeking advice or input from someone more knowledgeable, not just a peer discussion.
- [協議](vocab-02.md#協議-きょうぎ)（きょうぎ）— a formal discussion/deliberation (e.g., between organizations), more official and structured than the everyday, personal nuance of 相談.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 進路について先生に相談した。— I consulted my teacher about my career path.
2. 何か困ったことがあったら相談してください。— If you have any troubles, please consult (talk to) me.
3. 家族と相談して決めることにした。— We decided to decide after discussing it with the family.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 相談する often implies seeking advice/guidance (from a teacher, counselor, expert, or trusted person), not just a neutral "discussion" between equals — ~に相談する (consult with ~) is the key pattern, distinct from 話し合う which is more mutual/equal footing.

## 装置 (そうち)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** equipment, a device, an apparatus

**Similar words:**
- 器具（きぐ）— a smaller, often hand-held tool or implement; 装置 implies a larger, built-in or engineered system.
- 機械（きかい）— a machine in general; 装置 emphasizes a device set up for a specific function within a larger system.
- [設備](vocab-23.md#設備-せつび)（せつび）— facilities/installations in a building; overlaps with 装置 but 設備 is broader (e.g., plumbing, lighting).

**Opposite words:**

- ある — the plain "to exist/have," much more neutral and generic than 備わる.

**Opposite words:**
- 欠ける（かける）— to be lacking

**Example sentences:**
1. この建物には最新の設備が備わっている。— This building is equipped with the latest facilities.
2. 彼女には生まれつきのセンスが備わっている。— She has an innate sense built into her.
3. 人間には学ぶ能力が備わっている。— Human beings are endowed with the ability to learn.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 備わる is the intransitive partner of 備える — use 備わっている (state of being equipped with) rather than 備わる alone in most contexts, since it typically describes an ongoing inherent state.

#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** besides, moreover, in addition

**Similar words:**
- それに — a slightly more casual synonym for adding another point; そのうえ is a bit more formal/written.

**Opposite words:**
- 乱す（みだす）— to disrupt, disarrange

**Example sentences:**
1. 靴を玄関で揃えて脱いだ。— I took off my shoes and arranged them neatly at the entrance.
2. 必要な資料を揃えてから会議に臨んだ。— I gathered all the necessary materials before attending the meeting.
3. チームの意見を揃えることが難しかった。— It was difficult to get the team's opinions aligned.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 揃える is also used figuratively for aligning opinions/schedules (意見を揃える, 足並みを揃える "to be in step/unified") beyond the literal sense of neatly arranging physical objects.

## 損 (そん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a loss; to suffer a loss, be at a disadvantage

**Similar words:**
- [損失](#損失-そんしつ)（そんしつ）— "a loss" in a more formal/financial sense (business, accounting); 損 is more casual and general-purpose.
- [赤字](vocab-06.md#赤字-あかじ)（あかじ）— "being in the red," specifically financial deficit, narrower than general 損.
- 不利（ふり）— "disadvantage," broader than monetary loss, can describe any unfavorable position.

**Opposite words:**
- 得（とく）— gain, profit, benefit

**Example sentences:**
1. その投資で大きな損をした。— I suffered a big loss on that investment.
2. 正直者が損をする世の中だ。— It's a world where honest people end up at a disadvantage.
3. 損な役回りを引き受けてしまった。— I ended up taking on the thankless/disadvantageous role.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 損 pairs naturally with 得 in the common phrase 損得（そんとく）"gains and losses/pros and cons" — and 損な役回り ("thankless role") is a frequent idiomatic collocation.

## 損害 (そんがい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** damage, a loss (esp. financial/material)

**Similar words:**
- 被害（ひがい）— "damage/harm suffered" (often from disasters, crime); overlaps heavily, though 損害 leans toward quantifiable financial/material loss.
- [損失](#損失-そんしつ)（そんしつ）— "a loss," often interchangeable in business contexts, though 損害 implies damage caused by an external event, while 損失 is broader.
- ダメージ — the loanword, casual and broad, used for both physical and emotional "damage."

**Opposite words:**
- [利益](vocab-33.md#利益-りえき)（りえき）— profit, benefit

**Example sentences:**
1. 台風で甚大な損害が出た。— The typhoon caused massive damage.
2. 会社は損害賠償を求めて提訴した。— The company filed a lawsuit seeking damages.
3. 事故による損害は保険で補填された。— The damage from the accident was covered by insurance.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 損害賠償 (damages/compensation) is a key fixed legal/business collocation; 損害 emphasizes damage resulting from an event (disaster, accident, negligence) rather than a self-caused financial loss like in investing (which would more often use 損).

## 尊敬 (そんけい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** respect, esteem, to look up to

**Similar words:**
- 敬意（けいい）— "a sense of respect/reverence," a noun often used in the phrase 敬意を払う; 尊敬 is the broader suru-verb for respecting someone.
- [尊重](#尊重-そんちょう)（そんちょう）— "to respect (an opinion/decision)," focuses on honoring someone's views/rights rather than admiring their character.
- [憧れる](vocab-06.md#憧れる-あこがれる)（あこがれる）— "to admire/long to be like," implies aspiration, whereas 尊敬 is about regard for someone's virtue without necessarily wanting to emulate them.

- [軽蔑](vocab-17.md#軽蔑する-けいべつ)（けいべつ）— contempt, disdain

**Example sentences:**
1. 私は彼の誠実さを尊敬している。— I respect his sincerity.

3. 先生は生徒たちから尊敬されている。— The teacher is respected by the students.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 尊敬 is about admiring someone's character/achievements (respect for a person), while 尊重 is about respecting someone's opinions, rights, or decisions — the two are easily confused by learners.

## 存在 (そんざい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** existence

**Similar words:**
- [有無](vocab-08.md#有無-うむ)（うむ）— "existence or non-existence," used specifically when discussing whether something exists at all (yes/no), narrower than 存在.
- 実在（じつざい）— "actual/real existence," emphasizes something is real (not fictional), a more specific nuance than plain 存在.

**Opposite words:**
- 不在（ふざい）— absence, non-presence

3. その問題の存在にようやく気づいた。— I finally became aware of the existence of that problem.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 存在 is frequently used to describe a person's "presence/significance" within a group (〜な存在, e.g. 頼れる存在 "a reliable presence"), not just abstract philosophical existence.

#vocabulary

#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a loss (financial or otherwise)

**Similar words:**

- [損害](#損害-そんがい)（そんがい）— "damage/loss," often from an external cause (disaster, accident); 損失 is broader and includes any kind of foregone value or missed opportunity.

## 存じる/ずる (ぞんじる/ずる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to know, to think (humble form)

**Similar words:**
- [知る](vocab-21.md#知る-しる)（しる）— plain "to know"; 存じる is the humble form used when speaking about oneself to a superior/customer.
- 思う（おもう）— plain "to think"; 存じる also humbly covers this meaning (存じます "I believe").
- ご存知（ごぞんじ）— the respectful (sonkeigo) counterpart used for the listener's knowledge, not the speaker's — easily confused with 存じる.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. その件については存じております。— I am aware of that matter (humbly).
2. お名前は存じ上げております。— I do know your name (very humbly, about a respected person).
3. 私もそのように存じます。— I think the same way (humbly).

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 存じる (humble, about oneself) is often confused with ご存知 (respectful, about the listener) — using 存じる for the listener's knowledge is a common keigo mistake; "do you know?" should be ご存知ですか, not 存じていますか.

## 存続 (そんぞく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to continue to exist, survive, be retained

**Similar words:**
- [継続](vocab-17.md#継続する-けいぞく)（けいぞく）— "continuation" of an activity/process; 存続 specifically concerns an organization/system's survival/ongoing existence.
- 維持（いじ）— "maintenance," focuses on actively sustaining something rather than simply continuing to exist.
- [存在](#存在-そんざい)（そんざい）— "existence" as a state; 存続 adds the dimension of continuing over time, often against a risk of ending.

**Opposite words:**
- 廃止（はいし）— abolition

**Example sentences:**
1. この制度の存続が危ぶまれている。— The continued existence of this system is in jeopardy.
2. 伝統文化を存続させる努力が続けられている。— Efforts continue to keep the traditional culture alive.
3. 会社の存続をかけた重要な決断だった。— It was an important decision that put the company's survival on the line.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 存続 typically implies that continued existence is at stake or uncertain (a system, tradition, or institution that might end) — it carries more tension than the neutral 継続, which is just "continuation" of an ongoing activity.

## 尊重 (そんちょう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** respect (for), to honor (an opinion, right, decision)

**Similar words:**
- [尊敬](#尊敬-そんけい)（そんけい）— "respect/admiration" for a person's character; 尊重 is about honoring someone's opinions, rights, or wishes, not necessarily admiring them.
- 重んじる（おもんじる）— "to value/attach importance to," close synonym, slightly more literary.
- 配慮する（はいりょする）— "to show consideration for," broader, about being considerate rather than specifically honoring rights/opinions.

**Opposite words:**
- 軽視（けいし）— disregard, slighting

**Example sentences:**
1. 個人の意見を尊重すべきだ。— One should respect individual opinions.
2. 彼女の決定を尊重することにした。— We decided to respect her decision.
3. 人権を尊重する社会を目指す。— We aim for a society that respects human rights.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 尊重 is about respecting rights/opinions/decisions (often abstract), while 尊敬 is about admiring a person — mixing these up is a very common learner error since both are translated as "respect" in English.
