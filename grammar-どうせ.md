# どうせ {#どうせ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** どうせ

**Furigana form:** どうせ

**Meaning:** "Anyway, in the end, no matter what" — expresses a resigned, defeatist attitude that the outcome will be the same regardless of effort, implying it's pointless to try to change it.

**Conjugation:**
- どうせ + [clause], often paired with a negative, futile, or inevitable conclusion
- Frequently combined with ～ても / ～たところで to reinforce the futility nuance: どうせ頑張ったところで無駄だ
- Example: どうせ失敗するんだから、やらなくてもいい (It'll fail anyway, so there's no need to even try)

**Typical context:** Spoken, casual register, with a strongly resigned, cynical, or defeatist emotional tone. Often used self-deprecatingly or dismissively, sometimes bordering on sulky or pessimistic, as the speaker preemptively writes off an effort or outcome as not worth pursuing. Can also carry a slightly petulant or sarcastic edge when directed at someone else's situation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**たところで**](grammar-たところで.md) — "even if X were done, [it wouldn't help]," frequently paired with どうせ to reinforce the futility of an action; どうせ supplies the resigned attitude, while たところで supplies the grammatical "even if" structure.
- **結局** — "in the end, ultimately," focuses on describing the final outcome of a process neutrally, without どうせ's built-in defeatist, pre-judged cynicism about that outcome.
- **仕方がない** — "it can't be helped," expresses resignation about something already decided or unavoidable, but is more accepting/neutral in tone compared to どうせ's often sarcastic or dismissive edge.
- **せっかく〜のに** — "even though [effort/special circumstance], [disappointing result]," expresses disappointment after an effort was made, which contrasts with どうせ's preemptive dismissal before any effort is attempted.

**Opposite grammar structures:** きっと〜はずだ ("surely it will turn out...") or 頑張れば報われる ("if you try hard, it'll pay off") — both express hopeful expectation, the opposite of どうせ's resigned pessimism.

**Example sentences:**
1. どうせ告白しても振られるから、言わないでおく。
   (I'll get rejected anyway even if I confess my feelings, so I won't say anything.)
2. どうせ私の意見なんて誰も聞いてくれない。
   (No one's going to listen to my opinion anyway.)
3. どうせ雨が降るんだから、傘を持っていこう。
   (It's going to rain anyway, so let's bring an umbrella.)
4. どうせ買うなら、質のいいものを買ったほうがいい。
   (If you're going to buy it anyway, you might as well buy a good-quality one.)
5. どうせ無理だと決めつけないで、まずはやってみようよ。
   (Don't just assume it's impossible anyway — let's at least give it a try.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes treat どうせ as a neutral word simply meaning "anyway" in the English sense of "in any case," missing its inherent negative or resigned undertone — in Japanese, どうせ nearly always signals that the speaker has already given up or expects a disappointing/unavoidable outcome. Using it in a neutral, upbeat sentence would sound strange or even a little rude/sulky. Compare example 4 above, where どうせ買うなら softens into "if you're going to do it anyway" without strong negativity — context (especially the presence of a follow-up suggestion rather than a complaint) determines whether the resigned tone is sharp or mild.
