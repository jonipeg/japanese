# ～反面／～半面 {#反面・半面}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～反面／～半面

**Furigana form:** ～はんめん (both 反面 and 半面 are read はんめん; they are interchangeable in this grammar pattern, though 反面 is more common)

**Meaning:** "On the other hand," "while at the same time" — describes two contrasting aspects or qualities that coexist within the same subject/situation (unlike a simple contrast between two different things).

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞普通形 + 反面
- い形容詞 + 反面
- な形容詞 + な + 反面
- 名詞 + である + 反面
- この仕事は給料がいい反面、残業が多い。(This job pays well, while at the same time there's a lot of overtime.)

**Typical context:** Written and formal spoken Japanese — common in essays, news reports, and reviews describing pros/cons of a single subject. Neutral-to-formal register.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **一方（で）** — very close synonym; slightly more versatile, can also mean "meanwhile" for two separate subjects, not just contrasting aspects of one thing.
- [**ものの**](grammar-ものの.md) — "although," concessive but doesn't necessarily present a balanced dual-aspect contrast.
- [**にもかかわらず**](grammar-にかかわらず・にもかかわらず.md) — "despite," stronger concessive contrast, not necessarily about two qualities of the same subject.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite grammatically, though ため/からこそ (straightforward causal, non-contrastive) structures represent the non-contrastive alternative.

**Example sentences:**
1. この薬はよく効く反面、副作用も強い。
   (This medicine is very effective, while at the same time it has strong side effects.)
2. 都会の生活は便利な反面、物価が高い。
   (City life is convenient, while at the same time the cost of living is high.)
3. 彼は厳しい先生である反面、学生思いの優しい一面もある。
   (He's a strict teacher, while at the same time he has a kind, caring side for his students.)
4. 在宅勤務は自由な反面、自己管理が難しい。
   (Working from home gives you freedom, while at the same time self-management is difficult.)
5. 新しいシステムは効率的な反面、導入コストが高い。
   (The new system is efficient, while at the same time the introduction cost is high.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 反面 requires both qualities to belong to the SAME subject/topic (two sides of one coin) — unlike 一方 which can introduce an entirely separate parallel subject. Learners sometimes use 反面 to contrast two different people/things, which sounds unnatural; reserve it for dual aspects of a single thing.
