# ～たことにする {#たことにする}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～た事にする

**Furigana form:** ～たことにする

**Meaning:** "To treat/pretend as if X had (or hadn't) happened" — a deliberate, often contrary-to-fact pretense in which the speaker(s) agree to act as though some event did or did not occur, regardless of reality.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞た形 + ことにする (to decide to treat it as though X happened)
- 動詞なかった形 + ことにする (to decide to treat it as though X did not happen)
- Example: 今日のことは聞かなかったことにする (I'll pretend I never heard about what happened today)

**Typical context:** Used in both spoken and written Japanese, neutral-to-casual register. Carries a nuance of deliberate fabrication or face-saving — often used when covering up a mistake, sparing someone's feelings, maintaining appearances, or simply agreeing by mutual understanding to overlook something. Distinct from こととする/こととなる which describe decisions or natural conclusions rather than contrary-to-fact pretense.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **ことにする** (non-past) — expresses a genuine decision to do something going forward (e.g. 明日から運動することにする, "I've decided to exercise from tomorrow"), whereas たことにする specifically fabricates a past event/non-event.
- **ふりをする** — "to pretend/act as if," focuses on outward behavior and appearance in the moment (e.g. 寝たふりをする, "to pretend to be asleep"), while たことにする is about retroactively treating an event as settled fact in shared understanding, often for records or social agreement.
- **ことになっている** — describes an established rule, arrangement, or convention ("it is set that..."), not a deliberate individual pretense about a specific past event.
- **なかったことにする** — the negative counterpart within this same cluster, specifically meaning "to pretend X never happened," often used to undo an embarrassing or regrettable event.

**Opposite grammar structures:** 事実を認める ("to acknowledge the facts") or ありのままに話す ("to tell things as they actually are") — both represent honestly accepting what really happened, the opposite of fabricating a pretense.

**Example sentences:**
1. さっきの失言は聞かなかったことにしてください。
   (Please pretend you didn't hear that slip of the tongue I just made.)
2. 今回のミスはなかったことにして、次から気をつけよう。
   (Let's treat this mistake as if it never happened and be careful from now on.)
3. 二人の間であったことは、誰にも言わずになかったことにしようと決めた。
   (We decided to tell no one and pretend nothing happened between us.)
4. 遅刻した件は上司に報告せず、来なかったことにするわけにはいかない。
   (I can't very well not report my tardiness to my boss and pretend I never showed up.)
5. 彼女はプロポーズを断られたことを、なかったことにして明るく振る舞っている。
   (She's acting cheerful, pretending the proposal rejection never happened.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes confuse たことにする with ふりをする, but the two differ in focus: ふりをする is about performing a convincing outward appearance in real time (e.g., pretending to be asleep while clearly awake), while たことにする is about a shared or personal decision to treat a past event as settled fact going forward, often for the sake of social harmony or saving face, regardless of whether anyone is "watching." Another common error is misapplying the tense — remember the verb before ことにする must be in the た-form (or なかった-form) to refer to a past event, not the dictionary form, which would instead express a future decision (ことにする alone).
