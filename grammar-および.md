# および {#および}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** 及び

**Furigana form:** および

**Meaning:** "And; as well as" — a very formal conjunction used to connect items in official lists.

**Conjugation:** Sentence-initial conjunction, no conjugation needed — attaches at the sentence boundary, connecting the previous clause to the next. Most frequently, however, it links two nouns/noun phrases mid-sentence (A および B) rather than two full sentences, functioning much like a formal "and."

**Typical context:** Very formal/written register, used almost exclusively in official documents, legal text, contracts, government forms, academic writing, and business notices. Rarely, if ever, spoken in everyday conversation — using it in casual speech would sound stiff and bureaucratic.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**そして**](grammar-そして.md) — neutral sequential "and then," far less formal and more narrative than および.
- [**それに**](grammar-それに.md) — adds a supplementary point reinforcing an argument; conversational in flavor, unlike および's dry official register.
- [**また**](grammar-また.md) — neutral listing of parallel items, formal-friendly but still usable in general writing/speech, whereas および is reserved for official/legal contexts.
- [**あと**](grammar-あと.md) — casual spoken "also"; opposite register extreme from および.
- [**さらに**](grammar-さらに.md) — "furthermore, moreover," written/formal but focused on escalating emphasis rather than simply joining list items like および.
- **並びに** — another formal written "and," often used alongside および in legal/official text to join higher-level groups of items (並びに typically joins larger units while および joins smaller ones within them).

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite — および is purely an additive/listing conjunction within the formal register, without a contrastive counterpart in the same register (しかし's formal contrast counterpart would be the nearest functional opposite).

**Example sentences:**
1. 本制度は正社員およびパート社員に適用されます。
   (This system applies to full-time employees as well as part-time employees.)
2. 会議には部長および課長が出席する予定です。
   (The department head and the section chief are scheduled to attend the meeting.)
3. 提出書類には氏名、住所、および生年月日を明記してください。
   (Please clearly state your name, address, and date of birth on the submitted documents.)
4. 本契約は甲および乙の双方の同意なしに変更できない。
   (This contract cannot be altered without the consent of both Party A and Party B.)
5. 入場料には飲食代および施設利用料が含まれています。
   (The admission fee includes the cost of food and drink as well as the facility usage fee.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes use および in casual conversation or informal writing, where it sounds unnaturally bureaucratic — また, それに, or even そして would be far more natural in everyday contexts. Reserve および for official documents, forms, and legal/business writing. Also distinguish it from 並びに in formal documents: および typically connects items within the same level/group, while 並びに connects larger, higher-level groupings alongside them.
