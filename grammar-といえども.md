# ～といえど(も) {#といえども}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～と言えど（も）

**Furigana form:** ～といえど（も）

**Meaning:** "Even though X / although X" — a literary concessive connector acknowledging a fact or status, then asserting that an exception or qualification still applies despite it.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞／普通形 + と言えど（も）
- Example base: 専門家 → 専門家と言えども

**Typical context:** Formal, written/literary register; classical/old-fashioned flavor, used in formal writing, proverbs, and elevated speech to concede a status or fact before noting an important exception. Not casual.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**とはいえ**](grammar-とはいえ.md) — "although it's true that X, nonetheless Y"; the more commonly used modern equivalent with similar concessive meaning, while といえども carries a more classical, formal register.
- [**にしても**](grammar-にしろ・にしても・にせよ.md) — "even granting X, still Y"; shares the concessive "granting this, nonetheless" logic, in a more neutral/modern register than といえども.
- **であっても** — "even if/even being X"; close in meaning, slightly more modern and flexible than the classical といえども.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; だからこそ ("precisely because of X") would reinforce rather than concede/qualify a preceding statement.

**Example sentences:**
1. 専門家と言えども、間違えることはある。 (Even experts can make mistakes.)
2. 小さな会社と言えども、技術力では大手に劣らない。 (Even though it's a small company, it's not inferior to major firms in technical capability.)
3. 子供と言えども、その指摘は的確だった。 (Even though it was a child who said it, the observation was spot-on.)
4. 冗談と言えども、言ってはいけないことがある。 (Even if it's a joke, there are things that shouldn't be said.)
5. 王と言えども、法の下では平等だ。 (Even a king is equal under the law.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** といえども is a classical, formal construction that concedes a status or category (expert, king, small company) before noting an important qualifying exception — it should not be overused in casual contexts, where とはいえ or でも would sound far more natural. The も in といえども is often optional and doesn't change the meaning, but including it adds a slightly more formal, literary flavor.
