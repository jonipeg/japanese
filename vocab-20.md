## 自己主張 (じこしゅちょう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** self-assertion; to assert oneself, advocate for oneself

**Similar words:**
- 主張（しゅちょう）— "assertion, claim," the general word for stating one's opinion/position, while 自己主張 specifically emphasizes asserting one's own needs/identity (often in a personality context).
- わがまま — "selfishness, willfulness," a more negative term implying inconsiderate self-centeredness, distinct from the often neutral-to-positive 自己主張.
- アサーション — loanword from psychology ("assertiveness"), used in more academic/training contexts (assertiveness training).

**Opposite words:**
- [謙遜](vocab-18.md#謙遜する-けんそん)（けんそん）— modesty, humility
- 控えめ（ひかえめ）— reserved, modest

**Example sentences:**
1. 日本では自己主張が強すぎると嫌われることもある。— In Japan, being too self-assertive can sometimes make you disliked.
2. 彼女は会議でしっかり自己主張した。— She firmly asserted herself at the meeting.
3. 自己主張することは悪いことではない。— Asserting yourself isn't a bad thing.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 自己主張 can carry either a positive (healthy self-advocacy) or a mildly negative (overly pushy/self-centered) connotation depending on context — in Japanese culture, excessive 自己主張 is sometimes viewed critically relative to group harmony, a cultural nuance worth noting for learners.

## 資産 (しさん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** assets, property, wealth

**Similar words:**
- 財産（ざいさん）— "property/fortune," broader everyday term for personal wealth/possessions, while 資産 is more formal/financial, often used in accounting and investment contexts.
- 資本（しほん）— "capital," specifically funds/assets used to generate further wealth in business, narrower than the general 資産.
- 不動産（ふどうさん）— "real estate," a specific subtype of asset (immovable property), distinct from the general term 資産.

**Opposite words:**
- 負債（ふさい）— liabilities, debt

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は不動産や株式などの資産を多く持っている。— He has many assets such as real estate and stocks.
2. 会社の資産状況を確認した。— We checked the company's asset status.
3. 資産運用について専門家に相談した。— I consulted an expert about asset management.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 資産 is the formal/financial term for "assets" used in accounting, investing, and legal contexts (資産運用, 資産家) — in everyday speech about personal wealth or belongings, 財産 is more common and natural.

## 磁石 (じしゃく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a magnet, a compass

**Similar words:**
- マグネット — loanword "magnet," used interchangeably with 磁石 especially for small decorative magnets (fridge magnets), while 磁石 is the more standard/scientific term.
- 方位磁石（ほういじしゃく）— "a compass," a specific compound clarifying the navigational-instrument meaning of 磁石.
- 磁力（じりょく）— "magnetic force," the physical property/force itself, not the object.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 冷蔵庫に磁石でメモを貼った。— I stuck a note on the fridge with a magnet.
2. 磁石のN極とS極は引き合う。— The N and S poles of a magnet attract each other.
3. 山の中では磁石を使って方角を確認した。— In the mountains, I used a compass to check direction.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 磁石 covers both "magnet" (the physical attracting object) and, by extension, "compass" (since a compass needle is magnetized) — context determines which meaning applies; for clarity in navigation contexts, 方位磁石 is often specified.

## 辞退 (じたい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to decline, turn down (an offer, invitation, nomination) politely

**Similar words:**
- 断る（ことわる）— "to refuse/decline," the general everyday verb for refusing, while 辞退 is more formal and polite, often used for declining honors, invitations, or nominations.
- 拒否する（きょひする）— "to reject/refuse," stronger and more forceful, often implying outright rejection rather than a polite decline.
- 謝絶する（しゃぜつする）— "to firmly decline/refuse," very formal, often used in written notices (面会謝絶, no visitors allowed).

**Opposite words:**
- 承諾する（しょうだくする）— to accept, consent
- 受諾する（じゅだくする）— to accept (an offer/request)

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼はその役職を辞退した。— He declined that position.
2. 招待されたが、都合により辞退させていただいた。— I was invited, but due to circumstances, I respectfully declined.
3. 候補者の指名を辞退する意向を示した。— He indicated his intention to decline the candidate nomination.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 辞退 is polite and formal, typically used for declining something prestigious or official (an award, a nomination, an invitation) — using it for mundane everyday refusals (declining a snack) would sound overly stiff; 断る is the natural everyday choice there.

## 刺繍 (ししゅう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** embroidery

**Similar words:**
- 編み物（あみもの）— "knitting," a different textile craft using needles and yarn to create fabric, distinct from embroidery's decorative stitching on existing fabric.
- [レース](vocab-34.md#レース-れーす) — "lace," a different decorative textile technique, often made through weaving/knotting rather than stitching onto fabric.
- [手芸](vocab-29.md#手芸-てげい)（しゅげい）— "handicraft," a broad umbrella term covering embroidery, knitting, sewing, etc.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. このハンカチには美しい刺繍が施されている。— This handkerchief has beautiful embroidery on it.
2. 祖母は刺繍を趣味にしている。— My grandmother has embroidery as a hobby.
3. 彼女は自分の名前を刺繍したタオルを持っている。— She has a towel with her name embroidered on it.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 刺繍 specifically refers to decorative needle-and-thread stitching onto fabric (as a craft or decoration), distinct from other textile crafts like knitting (編み物) or weaving — learners should not conflate these different handicraft terms.

## 自主 (じしゅ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** independence, autonomy, self-initiative

**Similar words:**
- 独立（どくりつ）— "independence," broader and often used for national or financial independence, while 自主 emphasizes acting on one's own initiative/judgment rather than being independent of control per se.
- 自発的（じはつてき）— "spontaneous, voluntary," emphasizes acting without being told to, closely related to 自主的 (the na-adjective form of 自主).
- 主体性（しゅたいせい）— "subjectivity, initiative," emphasizes having one's own agency/identity in decision-making, a related but more abstract psychological concept.

**Opposite words:**
- 従属（じゅうぞく）— dependence, subordination
- 他律（たりつ）— heteronomy, being governed by external rules

**Example sentences:**
1. 自主的に勉強する習慣を身につけるべきだ。— One should develop the habit of studying on one's own initiative.
2. 彼らはテレビ局の自主規制を批判した。— They criticized the TV station's self-imposed regulation.
3. 学生たちは自主的にボランティア活動を行った。— The students carried out volunteer activities on their own initiative.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 自主 almost always appears as 自主的（な）or in compounds like 自主規制, 自主性, 自主練習 — it emphasizes doing something voluntarily/on one's own initiative rather than being independent from authority (独立), a distinction learners should keep separate.

## 支出 (ししゅつ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** expenditure, disbursement, spending

**Similar words:**
- 出費（しゅっぴ）— "expense/outlay," more everyday/casual term for spending money, often for a specific unexpected cost, while 支出 is more formal/accounting-oriented and used for budgets.
- 費用（ひよう）— "cost, expense," refers to the cost of something specific (a service, a project), rather than the general act/category of spending.
- [経費](vocab-17.md#経費-けいひ)（けいひ）— "expenses (business)," specifically refers to costs incurred for business/work purposes, often reimbursable.

**Opposite words:**
- [収入](#収入-しゅうにゅう)（しゅうにゅう）— income, revenue

**Example sentences:**
1. 今月の支出を見直す必要がある。— I need to review this month's expenditures.
2. 政府の支出が増加している。— Government spending is increasing.
3. 家計簿をつけて支出を管理している。— I keep a household ledger to manage expenditures.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 支出 is the formal/accounting term paired with 収入 (income) in budgeting contexts (家計, 政府予算) — for a casual one-off unexpected cost ("that was an expense"), 出費 sounds more natural in conversation.

## 辞書 (じしょ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** dictionary

**Similar words:**
- 辞典（じてん）— near-synonym for "dictionary," often used in more formal or specialized dictionary titles (百科辞典, encyclopedia), while 辞書 is the everyday general term.
- 単語帳（たんごちょう）— "vocabulary notebook/flashcard book," a personal study tool, not a comprehensive reference like a dictionary.
- 用語集（ようごしゅう）— "glossary," a specialized list of terms for a specific field, narrower than a general dictionary.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 分からない単語は辞書で調べてください。— Please look up unfamiliar words in a dictionary.
2. 電子辞書は持ち運びに便利だ。— An electronic dictionary is convenient to carry around.
3. この辞書には例文がたくさん載っている。— This dictionary has many example sentences in it.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 辞書 and 辞典 are largely interchangeable, but 辞書 is the default everyday word (国語辞書/国語辞典 both exist, though 国語辞典 is slightly more common in that specific title) — no major nuance pitfalls, but learners should recognize both forms appear on actual dictionary covers.

## 市場 (しじょう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** market, marketplace (economic/commercial sense)

**Similar words:**
- [市場](#市場-しじょう)（いちば）— same kanji, different reading! いちば refers to a physical marketplace (like a fish market), while しじょう refers to the abstract economic "market" (stock market, labor market).
- マーケット — loanword "market," often used interchangeably with しじょう especially in business/marketing contexts (マーケットリサーチ).
- [相場](vocab-24.md#相場-そうば)（そうば）— "market price/rate," refers to the going price/valuation within a market, narrower than the overall market concept.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 株式市場が大きく変動した。— The stock market fluctuated significantly.
2. この商品は海外市場でも人気がある。— This product is popular in overseas markets too.
3. 市場調査をしてから新商品を開発した。— We developed the new product after conducting market research.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 市場 is a critical homograph: read しじょう it means the abstract economic "market" (株式市場, 労働市場), but read いちば it means a physical marketplace (魚市場 can be either, depending on context) — learners must use context to determine the correct reading.

## 辞職 (じしょく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** resignation (from a job/position)

**Similar words:**
- [退職](vocab-25.md#退職-たいしょく)（たいしょく）— "retirement/leaving one's job," broader and can include retiring at normal age, not necessarily implying a voluntary or abrupt resignation like 辞職 (often used for officials/politicians stepping down).
- 辞任（じにん）— "resignation (from a position of responsibility)," very close to 辞職 but often used specifically for stepping down from an official post/role (会長を辞任する) rather than a regular job.
- クビになる — casual "to get fired," the opposite direction (involuntary), distinct from the voluntary nature of 辞職.

**Opposite words:**
- [就任](#就任-しゅうにん)（しゅうにん）— taking up a post, assuming office

**Example sentences:**
1. 大臣は不祥事の責任を取って辞職した。— The minister resigned, taking responsibility for the scandal.
2. 彼は会社を辞職して起業した。— He resigned from the company and started his own business.
3. 辞職願を上司に提出した。— I submitted a letter of resignation to my boss.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 辞職 and 辞任 are very close; 辞職 tends to be used for leaving a job/employment generally, while 辞任 is reserved more specifically for stepping down from an official responsibility or post (会長, 大臣) — the distinction is subtle and context-dependent.

## 詩人 (しじん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** poet

**Similar words:**
- 作家（さっか）— "writer/author," broader term for any kind of writer (novelist, essayist), not specific to poetry like 詩人.
- 歌人（かじん）— "waka poet," specifically a poet who composes traditional Japanese waka poetry, a specialized subtype distinct from the general 詩人.
- 俳人（はいじん）— "haiku poet," specifically someone who writes haiku, another specialized poetic subtype.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は有名な詩人として知られている。— He is known as a famous poet.
2. この詩人の作品は多くの人に愛されている。— This poet's works are loved by many people.
3. 彼女は詩人としてだけでなく、画家としても活躍した。— She was active not only as a poet but also as a painter.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 詩人 refers broadly to poets writing modern/free-form poetry (詩), while specific traditional poetic forms have their own dedicated terms — 歌人 (waka) and 俳人 (haiku) — learners should use the correct specialized term when discussing traditional Japanese poetry forms.

## 地震 (じしん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** earthquake

**Similar words:**
- 余震（よしん）— "aftershock," a specific type of smaller earthquake following a main one, a subset of 地震.
- 震度（しんど）— "seismic intensity (scale)," refers to the measured intensity of shaking at a location, not the earthquake event itself.
- [津波](vocab-29.md#津波-つなみ)（つなみ）— "tsunami," a related but distinct phenomenon often caused by undersea earthquakes.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 昨夜、大きな地震があった。— There was a big earthquake last night.
2. 日本は地震が多い国だ。— Japan is a country with many earthquakes.
3. 地震に備えて非常食を用意しておいた。— I prepared emergency food in preparation for earthquakes.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 地震 is a common and essential word in Japan given the country's seismic activity — related vocabulary (震度, 余震, 防災) frequently appears together with it in news and daily conversation, so learning this cluster of terms together is practical.

## 静か (しずか)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** quiet, still, calm

**Similar words:**
- [静まる](#静まる-しずまる)（しずまる）— the verb form "to become quiet/calm," describing the process of settling down, whereas 静か is the static adjective describing the resulting state.
- [穏やか](vocab-02.md#穏やか-おだやか)（おだやか）— "calm, gentle, mild," often used for weather, personality, or a peaceful atmosphere, broader than the narrower sound-based "quiet" of 静か.
- ひっそり — "quietly, in hushed stillness," more atmospheric/literary, often implying an eerie or lonely quietness.

**Opposite words:**
- うるさい（うるさい）— noisy, loud
- 騒がしい（さわがしい）— noisy, clamorous

**Example sentences:**
1. この図書館はとても静かだ。— This library is very quiet.
2. 彼女は静かな性格だ。— She has a quiet/calm personality.
3. 静かにしてください、赤ちゃんが寝ています。— Please be quiet, the baby is sleeping.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 静か is a na-adjective describing both literal sound quietness and a person's calm, reserved personality — learners should remember it conjugates as a na-adjective (静かな人, 静かに) not an i-adjective, a common beginner error.

## 静まる (しずまる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to become still/quiet, to calm down, to settle

**Similar words:**
- 静める（しずめる）— the transitive counterpart, "to calm/quiet (something) down," while 静まる is intransitive (something becomes calm on its own).
- [落ち着く](vocab-10.md#落ち着く-おちつく)（おちつく）— "to calm down, settle down," more commonly used for a person's emotional state regaining composure, while 静まる can apply to noise, crowds, or storms as well.
- 収まる（おさまる）— "to subside, come to an end," used for conflicts, chaos, or pain subsiding, overlapping with 静まる in some contexts.

**Opposite words:**
- 騒ぐ（さわぐ）— to make noise, clamor

**Example sentences:**
1. 嵐がようやく静まった。— The storm finally calmed down.
2. 会場が静まり返った。— The venue fell into complete silence.
3. 興奮していた観客も次第に静まった。— The excited audience gradually calmed down too.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 静まる often appears in the intensified compound 静まり返る (to fall into dead silence), a very common collocation — and note the transitive/intransitive pairing with 静める (to calm something down), a pattern learners should track alongside similar verb pairs.

## 沈む (しずむ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to sink, settle, subside; to be depressed

**Similar words:**
- 沈没する（ちんぼつする）— "to sink (a ship)," a more formal/technical term specifically for vessels sinking, narrower than the general 沈む.
- [落ち込む](vocab-10.md#落ち込む-おちこむ)（おちこむ）— "to feel depressed/down," closely overlaps with the figurative use of 沈む (気分が沈む) for emotional states, though 落ち込む is more common in casual speech about feelings.
- 没する（ぼっする）— formal/literary "to sink, set (sun)," used in more elevated or classical writing.

**Opposite words:**
- [浮かぶ](vocab-08.md#浮かぶ-うかぶ)（うかぶ）— to float, surface
- 昇る（のぼる）— to rise (sun)

**Example sentences:**
1. 船が嵐で沈んでしまった。— The ship sank in the storm.
2. 夕日が水平線に沈んでいく。— The setting sun is sinking below the horizon.
3. 悪い知らせを聞いて気分が沈んだ。— Hearing the bad news, my mood sank (I felt depressed).

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 沈む covers literal sinking (ships, the sun) and figurative emotional "sinking" (気分が沈む, feeling down) — learners should note this parallels English "my heart sank," making the figurative use intuitive once recognized.

## 沈める (しずめる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to sink, submerge (something) — transitive

**Similar words:**
- [沈む](#沈む-しずむ)（しずむ）— the intransitive counterpart ("to sink" on its own), while 沈める is transitive (to sink/submerge something deliberately).
- 潜らせる（もぐらせる）— causative form of もぐる, "to make (something/someone) dive/submerge," overlapping in some contexts but with a slightly different nuance of forced submersion vs. deliberate sinking.
- 水没させる（すいぼつさせる）— "to cause to be submerged underwater," more formal/technical, often used for flooding or deliberate submersion of structures.

**Opposite words:**
- [浮かべる](vocab-08.md#浮かべる-うかべる)（うかべる）— to float (something)

**Example sentences:**
1. 敵は船を沈めるために魚雷を発射した。— The enemy fired a torpedo to sink the ship.
2. お風呂に体を沈めてリラックスした。— I sank my body into the bath and relaxed.
3. 古い車を海に沈めて漁礁を作った。— They sank an old car into the sea to create a fish reef.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 沈める is the transitive partner of intransitive 沈む — a classic verb pair learners must keep straight: 船が沈む (the ship sinks, on its own) vs. 船を沈める (someone sinks the ship, deliberately).

## 事前 (じぜん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** beforehand, in advance, prior

**Similar words:**
- 前もって（まえもって）— "in advance, beforehand," an adverb with the same meaning, more commonly used in spoken Japanese, while 事前 is more often used as a noun/prefix (事前に, 事前の) in written/formal contexts.
- あらかじめ — "in advance, beforehand," also common and slightly more formal-sounding than 前もって, similar register to 事前に.
- [予め](vocab-01.md#予め-あらかじめ)（あらかじめ）— kanji form of あらかじめ, same meaning.

**Opposite words:**
- 事後（じご）— after the fact, after the event

**Example sentences:**
1. 会議の資料は事前に配布された。— The meeting materials were distributed in advance.
2. 事前予約が必要です。— Advance reservation is required.
3. 事前に確認しておけば問題なかった。— If I had checked beforehand, there wouldn't have been a problem.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 事前 is typically used as a noun-adjective prefix (事前に, 事前の, 事前準備) rather than standing alone as an adverb — learners should pair it with に or の, and remember its direct opposite 事後 (after the fact) for contrastive contexts (事前・事後).

## 試験 (しけん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** examination, test, trial

**Similar words:**
- テスト — loanword "test," more casual, often used for smaller quizzes or informal tests, while 試験 is more formal, used for official exams (entrance exams, qualification exams).
- [検査](vocab-18.md#検査する-けんさ)（けんさ）— "inspection, examination," used for medical checks or quality inspections, not academic testing.
- 実験（じっけん）— "an experiment," scientific testing of a hypothesis, a different kind of "trial" from an academic 試験.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 来週、日本語能力試験を受ける。— Next week I'm taking the Japanese Language Proficiency Test.
2. 試験の結果が出るまで緊張していた。— I was nervous until the exam results came out.
3. この新薬は臨床試験の段階にある。— This new drug is in the clinical trial stage.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 試験 covers both academic exams and broader "trials/tests" (臨床試験, clinical trials) — unlike the more casual テスト, which is reserved mainly for everyday school quizzes, 試験 carries more formal weight and is used for official, high-stakes examinations.

## 資源 (しげん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** natural (material) resources

**Similar words:**
- 原料（げんりょう）— "raw materials," specifically the unprocessed materials used in manufacturing, narrower than the broad concept of 資源 (which includes energy, water, human resources etc).
- [エネルギー](vocab-09.md#エネルギー-エネルギー) — loanword "energy," a specific subtype of resource (エネルギー資源), not the general term itself.
- 財源（ざいげん）— "financial resources/funding source," specifically about money, a figurative extension of the "resource" concept into finance.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 日本は天然資源に乏しい国だ。— Japan is a country poor in natural resources.
2. 水資源の保護が重要な課題となっている。— The protection of water resources has become an important issue.
3. 石油は貴重なエネルギー資源である。— Oil is a valuable energy resource.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 資源 is broad and extends metaphorically beyond physical materials to concepts like 人的資源（じんてきしげん, human resources）and 観光資源（かんこうしげん, tourism resources) — learners should recognize this figurative extension beyond literal minerals/energy.

## 自主独立 (じしゅどくりつ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** independence, autonomy, self-reliance

**Similar words:**
- 独立（どくりつ）— "independence" alone, the core concept, while 自主独立 is an intensified four-character compound emphasizing both self-initiative (自主) and independence (独立) together.
- [自立](vocab-21.md#自立-じりつ)（じりつ）— "self-reliance, standing on one's own," often used for individuals becoming financially/emotionally independent, slightly more personal than the national/organizational scale often implied by 自主独立.
- 独立自尊（どくりつじそん）— "independence and self-respect," a related classical four-character idiom with a similar spirit.

**Opposite words:**
- 従属（じゅうぞく）— dependence, subordination

**Example sentences:**
1. その国は自主独立の精神を国是としている。— That country holds the spirit of self-reliant independence as a national principle.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 周期 is a scientific/technical term for a repeating interval (used in physics, biology, economics) — 周期的に (adverb, "periodically/cyclically") is the common collocation; don't confuse with 周期 vs. 時期 (じき, "a period of time/season"), which is unrelated and refers to a specific point/span in time, not repetition.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 終業 and 就業 are exact homophones (しゅうぎょう) with near-opposite meanings ("ending work" vs. "being employed/working") — context and kanji are essential, and this is a classic listening-comprehension trap in Japanese.

## 粛々 (しゅくしゅく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** quietly, solemnly, calmly (proceeding without fuss)

**Similar words:**
- 静かに（しずかに）— plain "quietly," everyday and neutral; 粛々と carries a more formal, dignified nuance of proceeding steadily and solemnly despite external pressure or scrutiny.
- 厳粛に（げんしゅくに）— "solemnly/gravely," closely related and often used for ceremonies; 粛々と additionally implies calm perseverance in carrying out a task.
- 淡々と（たんたんと）— "matter-of-factly/calmly," emphasizes emotional detachment, while 粛々と emphasizes dignified, orderly procedure.

- [騒々しい](vocab-24.md#騒々しい-そうぞうしい)（そうぞうしい）— noisy, clamorous

3. 裁判は粛々と進められた。— The trial proceeded solemnly and in an orderly manner.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 粛々と is a formal, almost political/bureaucratic adverb often used by officials to mean "we will proceed as planned, undeterred by controversy" (政治家がよく使う表現) — it's not simply "quietly" in the everyday sense, but implies composed resolve under scrutiny.

## 主観 (しゅかん)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** subjectivity, subjective viewpoint

**Similar words:**

## 従って (したがって)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** therefore, consequently, accordingly

**Similar words:**
- だから — the plain, everyday equivalent of "so/therefore," casual register; 従って is formal/written, common in essays, reports, and academic writing.
- [それゆえ](vocab-24.md#それゆえ)（それゆえ）— similarly formal, literary equivalent to 従って, often interchangeable in written arguments.
- [そのため](vocab-24.md#そのため) — "because of that/for that reason," a neutral-register connector often used in both speech and writing.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 台風が接近している。従って、本日の試合は中止となった。— A typhoon is approaching. Therefore, today's match has been canceled.
2. 彼は経験豊富である。従って、この仕事に適任だ。— He is highly experienced; therefore, he is suited for this job.
3. 売上が減少した。従って、経費の削減が必要だ。— Sales have declined. Consequently, expense reduction is necessary.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Don't confuse this conjunction 従って ("therefore") with the te-form of the verb 従う ("to obey/follow," 従って also meaning "following/in accordance with X") — the same written form serves two different grammatical roles, distinguishable only by context (e.g., 規則に従って "in accordance with the rules" vs. a standalone sentence-initial 従って "therefore").
