# ～ところに／へ／を {#ところに・へ・を}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ところに／へ／を

**Furigana form:** ～ところに／へ／を (no special kanji readings needed)

**Meaning:** "Just as X was happening," "right when X" — describes an ongoing situation or moment that is interrupted or coincided with by another event (often unexpectedly).

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ている／た形 + ところに／へ／を
- 出かけようとしたところに、電話がかかってきた。(Just as I was about to leave, the phone rang.)
- 掃除しているところへ、友達が来た。(Just as I was cleaning, a friend came over.)

**Typical context:** Neutral, spoken and written; commonly used in narrative descriptions where one event interrupts or coincides with another ongoing action.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**ところを**](grammar-ところを.md) — very similar but often used when the interrupting event directly "catches" the subject in an awkward/vulnerable moment (see next entry).
- **最中に** — "in the middle of X," a more neutral/formal way to express an interruption during an ongoing action.
- **時に** — generic "when," lacks the nuance of catching an ongoing moment or coincidental timing.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. お風呂に入ろうとしたところに、宅配便が届いた。
   (Just as I was about to take a bath, a delivery arrived.)
2. 会議が始まろうとしたところへ、部長が急に入ってきた。
   (Just as the meeting was about to start, the manager suddenly walked in.)
3. 道を歩いているところを、昔の同僚に声をかけられた。
   (Just as I was walking down the street, a former colleague called out to me.)
4. 眠っていたところに、地震が起きた。
   (Just as I was sleeping, an earthquake happened.)
5. 手紙を書いているところへ、彼女から電話が来た。
   (Just as I was writing a letter, she called me.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** に／へ／を are mostly interchangeable here, but を often implies the subject is caught in the middle of an action by the interrupting event (closely tied to the next entry's more specific "caught in the act" nuance), while に／へ are more neutral about simultaneity. Note the verb before ところ is typically in ている (ongoing) or ようとした (about to) form, not dictionary form alone.
