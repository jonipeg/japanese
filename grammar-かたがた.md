# ～かたがた {#かたがた}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**1. Kanji version:** ～旁々 (archaic kanji, rarely used; almost always written in hiragana as かたがた)

**2. Furigana version:** ～かたがた

**3. Meaning:** While also doing X — combining two purposes into one single trip or action, often a formal greeting or errand (e.g. お見舞いかたがた = "while also paying a hospital visit, [I did Y]").

**4. Conjugation (connection form):** 名詞(スル動詞の語幹) + かたがた

**5. Usage context:** Written, very formal/polite register, often used in business correspondence or formal greetings (hospital visits, condolence calls, seasonal greetings).

**6. Similar grammar structures:**
- がてら — a near-synonym noted alongside かたがた in the source as a related pattern, but がてら is more casual/everyday (e.g. 散歩がてら買い物する "shopping while out for a walk"), whereas かたがた is formal/ceremonial.
- ながら — "while doing X," much more general and casual simultaneity, lacking かたがた's "dual-purpose formal errand" nuance.
- いかんの — confirmed tested distractor; unrelated in meaning (tied to いかん "depending on/regardless of"), a rote-memorization trap rather than a genuine nuance overlap.
- ながらの — confirmed tested distractor; just ながら + の noun-modifying form, grammatically unrelated to かたがた.

**7. Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**8. Example sentences:**
1. お見舞いかたがた休職中の上司を訪ねた。(I visited my boss, who is on leave, both to check in on him and pay a visit.)
2. ご挨拶かたがた、近況をお伝えいたします。(I'm writing to say hello and, at the same time, let you know how I've been.)
3. お礼かたがた、近くまで来たのでお寄りしました。(I stopped by since I was in the area, both to say thank you and to visit.)
4. ご報告かたがた、改めてお詫びを申し上げます。(I'm writing to report the matter and, at the same time, offer my renewed apologies.)
5. 散歩かたがた、近所の友人の様子を見に行った。(I went to check on my neighbor's friend while also taking a walk.)

**9. Common mistakes / key nuances:** Confirmed student picked からの wrongly for this question. かたがた is often confused with superficially similar but semantically unrelated distractors (いかんの, ながらの) due to surface phonetic/structural resemblance rather than genuine meaning overlap — treat these as memorization traps. Distinguish from がてら: かたがた is formal/ceremonial (greetings, condolence visits), while がてら is casual/everyday (e.g. 散歩がてら買い物する).
