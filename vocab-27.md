## 断面 (だんめん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a section, a cross section, a profile (of something cut through)

**Similar words:**
- 切り口（きりくち）— "a cut surface/cross section," a more everyday, concrete term for where something has been physically cut, overlapping closely with 断面.
- [側面](vocab-24.md#側面-そくめん)（そくめん）— "a side/aspect," used figuratively for one perspective or facet of an issue, similar to 断面's figurative use (社会の断面, "a cross-section of society").

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. リンゴの断面を観察した。— I observed the cross section of the apple.
2. この小説は現代社会の断面を描いている。— This novel depicts a cross-section of modern society.
3. 建物の断面図を見せてもらった。— I was shown the cross-sectional diagram of the building.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 断面 is used both literally (a physical cut surface, as in 断面図, "cross-sectional diagram") and figuratively (a revealing "slice" or facet of a larger social/human situation) — learners should recognize this figurative extension common in literary and journalistic writing.

## 弾力 (だんりょく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** elasticity, springiness, flexibility (literal and figurative)

**Similar words:**
- 柔軟性（じゅうなんせい）— "flexibility," more commonly used for figurative adaptability (of thinking, schedules, policy), while 弾力 is more associated with physical springiness, though both share a figurative "flexible" sense (弾力的な対応, "flexible response").
- 伸縮性（しんしゅくせい）— "stretchability," a more specific textile/material term for fabric or material that stretches and returns to shape.

**Opposite words:**
- 硬直性（こうちょくせい）— rigidity, stiffness

**Example sentences:**
1. このボールは弾力があってよく跳ねる。— This ball has elasticity and bounces well.
2. 若い肌は弾力に富んでいる。— Young skin is rich in elasticity.
3. 弾力的な対応をお願いします。— Please respond with flexibility.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 弾力 extends figuratively to mean "flexibility" in policy or response (弾力的な対応/運用, "flexible handling/operation") beyond its literal physical springiness sense — this figurative business/administrative usage is very common and shouldn't be overlooked.

## 治安 (ちあん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** public order, public peace and security

**Similar words:**
- 安全（あんぜん）— "safety," a broader, more general concept of being free from danger, while 治安 specifically refers to the social order/security maintained by law enforcement against crime.
- セキュリティ（せきゅりてぃ）— loanword "security," often used for physical/digital security systems rather than the broader societal concept of public order.
- [秩序](vocab-01.md#秩序-ちつじょ)（ちつじょ）— "order," a more abstract term for systemic order (social, moral), while 治安 is specifically about safety from crime.

**Opposite words:**
- 混乱（こんらん）— chaos, disorder

**Example sentences:**
1. この地域は治安が良いと評判だ。— This area has a reputation for good public safety.
2. 治安が悪化し、夜間の外出は危険だ。— Public order has worsened, and going out at night is dangerous.
3. 警察は治安維持に努めている。— The police are working to maintain public order.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 治安 specifically concerns crime-related safety/order (治安が良い/悪い, "safe/unsafe area" in terms of crime), distinct from general 安全 which can apply to any kind of safety (e.g., food safety, traffic safety) — learners should use 治安 specifically when discussing crime rates and social order.

## 地位 (ちい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** rank, position, status (social or professional)

**Similar words:**
- 身分（みぶん）— "social status/class," historically tied to one's inherited social rank, more fixed than the achievable, career-related nuance of 地位.
- [立場](vocab-26.md#立場-たちば)（たちば）— "standpoint/position," more about one's situational role or viewpoint in a given context, rather than a fixed hierarchical rank.
- ポジション（ぽじしょん）— loanword "position," casual equivalent often used in business/sports contexts.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は会社で高い地位についている。— He holds a high position at the company.
2. 女性の社会的地位が向上してきた。— Women's social status has improved.
3. 地位が人を変えることもある。— Position/rank can sometimes change a person.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 地位 refers to one's rank or standing within a hierarchy (social, professional, political) and is commonly used in set phrases like 社会的地位 ("social status") — it's distinct from 立場, which is about one's situational role/perspective rather than hierarchical rank.

## 地域 (ちいき)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** an area, a region, a district

**Similar words:**
- [地方](#地方-ちほう)（ちほう）— "a region/the provinces," often contrasted with the capital/urban centers (地方 vs 都会), a broader geographic/administrative concept than the more neutral 地域.
- エリア（えりあ）— loanword "area," casual and often used for specific zones (サービスエリア, 対象エリア) in business/service contexts.
- 地区（ちく）— "a district," often a more officially defined administrative subdivision, narrower than the general 地域.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. この地域は自然が豊かだ。— This area is rich in nature.
2. 地域の人々と交流を深めている。— I'm deepening my connections with the local community.
3. 災害が起きた地域に支援物資が届けられた。— Relief supplies were delivered to the disaster-affected area.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 地域 is a very broad, neutral term for "area/region" usable at almost any scale (a neighborhood, a country's region, a global zone) — unlike 地方, which specifically carries connotations of "provincial/rural" areas outside major cities.

## 知恵 (ちえ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** wisdom, intelligence, wit(s), practical sense

**Similar words:**
- [知識](#知識-ちしき)（ちしき）— "knowledge," factual information one has learned, distinct from 知恵 which is the practical ability to apply understanding and judgment well.
- 賢さ（かしこさ）— "cleverness/smartness," closer to innate intelligence, while 知恵 often implies wisdom gained through experience.
- [知性](#知性-ちせい)（ちせい）— "intellect," a more formal, academic term for cognitive/reasoning ability.

**Opposite words:**
- 愚かさ（おろかさ）— foolishness, stupidity

**Example sentences:**
1. お年寄りの知恵を大切にすべきだ。— We should value the wisdom of the elderly.
2. みんなで知恵を出し合って解決した。— We solved it by pooling everyone's wisdom/ideas together.
3. 彼は知恵を絞って新しい方法を考え出した。— He racked his brains and came up with a new method.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 知恵 implies practical, experience-based wisdom or cleverness in solving problems (知恵を絞る, "to rack one's brains"; 知恵を出し合う, "to pool ideas") rather than simply possessing factual knowledge (知識) — the distinction between the two is frequently tested in Japanese proficiency exams.

## 地下 (ちか)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** underground, basement

**Similar words:**
- 地下室（ちかしつ）— "a basement/cellar room," the specific physical space, a compound built from 地下.
- 地下街（ちかがい）— "an underground shopping district," a specific common application of 地下 found in Japanese train stations and city centers.

**Opposite words:**
- 地上（ちじょう）— above ground, surface

**Example sentences:**
1. このビルには地下に駐車場がある。— This building has a parking lot underground.
2. 地下鉄で通勤している。— I commute by subway.
3. 地下に眠る遺跡が発掘された。— Ruins sleeping underground were excavated.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 地下 forms many common compounds (地下鉄, 地下街, 地下水) referring to underground infrastructure prevalent in Japanese cities — learners should recognize it as a productive prefix rather than only memorizing it as a standalone word.

## 違い (ちがい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a difference

**Similar words:**
- 差（さ）— "a difference/gap," often used for quantifiable, numerical differences (点数の差), while 違い is broader and covers qualitative differences too.
- [相違](vocab-23.md#相違-そうい)（そうい）— "discrepancy/difference," a more formal, written synonym often used in legal or academic contexts.
- ギャップ（ぎゃっぷ）— loanword "gap," often used for a difference in perception, generation, or expectation (世代ギャップ).

**Opposite words:**
- [類似](vocab-06.md#類似-るいじ)（るいじ）— similarity, resemblance

**Example sentences:**
1. この二つの商品の違いは何ですか。— What is the difference between these two products?
2. 意見の違いから喧嘩になった。— A quarrel arose from a difference of opinion.
3. 文化の違いを理解することが大切だ。— It's important to understand cultural differences.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 違いない (certainly, no doubt) is a common fixed expression derived from 違い that means the opposite of its literal components suggest — learners should recognize this idiom (〜に違いない, "must be〜, no doubt") as distinct from the plain noun 違い meaning "a difference."

## 誓う (ちかう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to swear, to make a vow, to promise (solemnly)

**Similar words:**
- 約束する（やくそくする）— "to promise," the everyday, general word for making a commitment; 誓う is more solemn/formal, often used for serious vows (marriage, loyalty, revenge).
- 宣誓する（せんせいする）— "to take an oath," a very formal, ceremonial equivalent used in legal or official settings (法廷で宣誓する).
- 決意する（けついする）— "to resolve/determine," about committing internally to a course of action, without necessarily the outward declarative act of 誓う.

**Opposite words:**
- [裏切る](vocab-08.md#裏切る-うらぎる)（うらぎる）— to betray

**Example sentences:**
1. 二人は永遠の愛を誓った。— The two vowed eternal love.
2. 彼は二度と同じ過ちを犯さないと誓った。— He vowed never to make the same mistake again.
3. 神に誓って本当のことを言っている。— I swear to God, I'm telling the truth.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 誓う carries a solemn, often ceremonial weight (wedding vows, oaths, deep personal resolutions) heavier than casual 約束する — using it for everyday small promises ("I promise I'll call you") would sound overly dramatic.

## 違う (ちがう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to be different, to differ; to be wrong/incorrect

**Similar words:**
- 異なる（ことなる）— a more formal, written synonym for "to differ," common in academic/formal writing, while 違う is the everyday spoken equivalent.
- 間違う（まちがう）— "to make a mistake/err," specifically about being incorrect in an action, a narrower sense than 違う's broader "wrong" usage as a short reply.
- 別（べつ）— "different/separate," often used adjectivally (別の, "a different ~") rather than as a predicate verb like 違う.

**Opposite words:**
- 同じ（おなじ）— the same

**Example sentences:**
1. この二つの味は全然違う。— These two flavors are completely different.
2. 「違う、そうじゃない」と彼は言った。— "No, that's not it," he said.
3. 君の考えは少し違うと思う。— I think your idea is a little off/wrong.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 違う is extremely common as a short, casual way to say "No/that's wrong" in conversation (違う！) — distinct from its grammatical role as a verb meaning "to differ," this interjection-like usage is essential for natural spoken Japanese.

## 違える (ちがえる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to strain (a muscle/joint), to break (a promise), to cause to differ

**Similar words:**
- 間違える（まちがえる）— "to make a mistake/mix up," a more common everyday verb for errors, distinct from 違える's narrower uses (straining a body part, breaking a promise).
- [捻る](vocab-29.md#捻る-つねる)（ひねる）— "to twist/sprain," a more specific physical verb often used alongside or instead of 違える for muscle/joint injuries.
- [破る](vocab-34.md#破る-やぶる)（やぶる）— "to break (a promise/rule)," a common alternative for the "break a promise" sense of 違える.

**Opposite words:**
- 守る（まもる）— to keep (a promise), to protect

**Example sentences:**
1. 重い荷物を持って首を違えた。— I strained my neck carrying heavy luggage.
2. 彼は約束を違えることはしない。— He would never break a promise.
3. 寝違えて肩が痛い。— I slept in a bad position and strained my shoulder.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 違える has two quite distinct uses — physically straining a joint/muscle (often in the set phrase 寝違える, "to sleep wrong and strain one's neck") and the more literary "to break a promise" — learners should not confuse it with the far more common 間違える (to make a mistake/mix up).

## 近頃 (ちかごろ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** recently, lately, these days

**Similar words:**
- 最近（さいきん）— the most common, everyday equivalent for "recently"; 近頃 is slightly more old-fashioned/literary but largely interchangeable.
- このごろ（このごろ）— "these days," a close colloquial synonym often used in casual speech, nearly identical to 近頃.
- 今どき（いまどき）— "nowadays," often carries a slightly judgmental or generational-commentary tone (今どきの若者, "young people these days").

**Opposite words:**
- 昔（むかし）— long ago, in the past

**Example sentences:**
1. 近頃、物価が上がって大変だ。— Prices have been rising lately, and it's tough.
2. 近頃の若者はよく勉強していると思う。— I think young people these days study a lot.
3. 近頃、彼を見かけなくなった。— Lately, I haven't been seeing him around.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 近頃 and 最近 are largely interchangeable for "recently," but 近頃 has a slightly more old-fashioned, narrative tone often seen in essays or spoken by older generations — in casual daily conversation, 最近 is more neutral and common among younger speakers.

## 地下水 (ちかすい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** groundwater

**Similar words:**
- 伏流水（ふくりゅうすい）— "underflow/subsurface stream water," a more technical hydrology term for water flowing underground through a riverbed, a specific subtype distinct from general 地下水.
- 湧き水（わきみず）— "spring water," water that has risen naturally to the surface, related to but distinct from 地下水 still underground.

**Opposite words:**
- 地表水（ちひょうすい）— surface water

**Example sentences:**
1. この地域は地下水が豊富だ。— This area has abundant groundwater.
2. 工場排水が地下水を汚染した。— Factory wastewater contaminated the groundwater.
3. 地下水位が年々低下している。— The groundwater level has been dropping year by year.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 地下水 is a standard environmental/geology term frequently appearing in discussions of water resources, pollution, and agriculture — straightforward in meaning, but learners should recognize common compounds like 地下水位 ("groundwater level") and 地下水汚染 ("groundwater contamination").

## 近々 (ちかぢか)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** soon, before long, shortly

**Similar words:**
- まもなく（まもなく）— "soon/shortly," a common, slightly more formal synonym often used in announcements (まもなく到着します).
- 近いうちに（ちかいうちに）— "sometime soon," a close phrase meaning essentially the same thing, commonly interchangeable with 近々.
- すぐに（すぐに）— "immediately/right away," implies a much shorter, more immediate timeframe than the vaguer, slightly longer-term 近々.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 近々お伺いしたいと思います。— I'd like to visit you sometime soon.
2. 近々引っ越す予定です。— I plan to move before long.
3. 近々また連絡します。— I'll be in touch again soon.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Note the reading ちかぢか uses ぢ (not じ) due to rendaku on a reduplicated word (近 + 近) — a common source of kana-writing mistakes for learners typing it from memory.

## 近付く (ちかづく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to approach, to get close to, to near

**Similar words:**
- [近寄る](#近寄る-ちかよる)（ちかよる）— very close in meaning, "to go near/approach," often more about physically moving toward something specific, while 近付く can also describe time/events approaching (試験が近付く).
- [迫る](vocab-23.md#迫る-せまる)（せまる）— "to draw near/press upon," often with urgency or pressure (締め切りが迫る, "the deadline is closing in"), a more intense nuance than neutral 近付く.
- 接近する（せっきんする）— "to approach," a more formal/technical term often used for vehicles, storms, or military contexts.

**Opposite words:**
- 遠ざかる（とおざかる）— to move away, recede

**Example sentences:**
1. 台風が近付いている。— A typhoon is approaching.
2. 試験の日が近付いてきた。— The exam day has been drawing near.
3. 犬がゆっくりと近付いてきた。— The dog slowly approached.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 近付く works for both physical approach (a person/animal nearing) and temporal approach (an event/deadline nearing) — unlike 近寄る, which is used almost exclusively for physical nearness, not time.

## 近付ける (ちかづける)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to bring close to, to let (someone/something) approach

**Similar words:**
- 近寄らせる（ちかよらせる）— the causative form of 近寄る, similarly meaning "to let approach," though less commonly used than 近付ける.
- 寄せる（よせる）— "to bring/draw near," a more general verb for moving something closer, without 近付ける's specific pairing as the transitive counterpart of 近付く.

**Opposite words:**
- 遠ざける（とおざける）— to keep away, distance

**Example sentences:**
1. 子供を火に近付けないでください。— Please don't let the child get close to the fire.
2. 顔を近付けてよく見てみた。— I brought my face close and looked carefully.
3. 彼を会社の重要な情報に近付けないようにした。— We made sure not to let him get close to important company information.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 近付ける is the transitive counterpart of intransitive 近付く — 近付く describes something approaching on its own, while 近付ける describes an agent actively bringing something/someone close, a transitivity pair learners should keep straight.

## 地下鉄 (ちかてつ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** subway, underground railway

**Similar words:**
- 電車（でんしゃ）— "train" in general, a broader category that includes both above-ground and underground trains, while 地下鉄 specifically refers to the underground system.
- メトロ（めとろ）— loanword "metro," sometimes used as a brand name or casual synonym for subway systems, especially in certain cities.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 地下鉄で会社まで通っている。— I commute to the office by subway.
2. この街には地下鉄が通っていない。— This city doesn't have a subway system.
3. 地下鉄の駅から徒歩5分です。— It's a 5-minute walk from the subway station.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 地下鉄 specifically refers to underground train systems — in cities without true subways (many smaller Japanese cities), people commute via above-ground 電車 instead, so learners shouldn't use 地下鉄 generically for "train" when the system in question runs above ground.

## 近寄る (ちかよる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to go near, to approach, to get close (physically)

**Similar words:**
- [近付く](#近付く-ちかづく)（ちかづく）— very close synonym for physical approach, but 近付く also extends to temporal nearness (events/deadlines), while 近寄る is used almost exclusively for physical proximity.
- 接近する（せっきんする）— a more formal/technical equivalent, often used for vehicles, weather systems, or official/news contexts.

**Opposite words:**
- 遠ざかる（とおざかる）— to move away, recede

**Example sentences:**
1. その犬には近寄らない方がいい。— It's better not to go near that dog.
2. 危険なので、これ以上近寄らないでください。— It's dangerous, so please don't come any closer.
3. 彼女にそっと近寄って声をかけた。— I quietly approached her and spoke to her.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 近寄る is almost always used for physical nearness (often with a warning tone, 近寄るな, "don't come near!") — unlike 近付く, it doesn't extend to describing time/events approaching, so learners shouldn't use it for sentences like "the exam is approaching."

## 畜産 (ちくさん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** stockbreeding, livestock farming/industry

**Similar words:**
- 農業（のうぎょう）— "agriculture," the broader category covering crop farming as well as 畜産 (livestock raising) as a subcategory.
- [酪農](vocab-06.md#酪農-らくのう)（らくのう）— "dairy farming," a more specific subtype of 畜産 focused on milk/dairy products.

## 蓄積 (ちくせき)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** accumulation; to accumulate, to store up

**Similar words:**
- 溜まる（たまる）— the everyday verb for things building up (stress, water, trash); 蓄積 is more formal/technical, often used for data, fatigue, knowledge, or capital in written contexts.
- 積み重ねる（つみかさねる）— "to pile up/build up (through repeated effort)," emphasizing a gradual, effortful process, often used figuratively for experience or achievements.
- ストック（すとっく）— loanword "stock," used for accumulated reserves of goods/resources in business contexts.

**Opposite words:**
- 消費（しょうひ）— consumption, using up

**Example sentences:**
1. 長年の経験が彼の中に蓄積されている。— Years of experience have accumulated within him.
2. 疲労が蓄積すると体調を崩しやすい。— When fatigue accumulates, it's easy to fall ill.
3. データの蓄積によって精度が向上した。— Accuracy improved through the accumulation of data.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 蓄積 is commonly used in formal/technical writing about gradual buildup of intangible things — fatigue, stress, knowledge, data, capital (疲労の蓄積, データの蓄積) — it's more academic/written in register than the everyday verb 溜まる.

## 知事 (ちじ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** (prefecture) governor

**Similar words:**
- 市長（しちょう）— "mayor (of a city)," a related but lower-level administrative position compared to the prefecture-level 知事.
- [大臣](vocab-25.md#大臣-だいじん)（だいじん）— "minister (of the national government)," a higher, national-level official, distinct from the prefectural-level 知事.

**Opposite words:**

## 知識 (ちしき)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** knowledge, information

**Similar words:**
- 情報（じょうほう）— "information," raw facts or data; 知識 is processed understanding built from such information.
- [学問](vocab-12.md#学問-がくもん)（がくもん）— "scholarship/learning" as an academic discipline, broader and more formal than 知識.
- 常識（じょうしき）— "common sense," the baseline knowledge everyone is expected to have, a narrower subset of 知識.

**Opposite words:**
- 無知（むち）— ignorance, lack of knowledge
