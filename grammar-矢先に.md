# ～矢先に {#矢先に}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～矢先に

**Furigana form:** ～やさきに

**Meaning:** "Just as one was about to ~ / right at the moment of ~" — describes something (often unexpected or unwelcome) happening right at the moment someone was about to do something or right after an event occurred.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞意向形+とした (volitional form + とした) + 矢先に
- 動詞た形 (verb past form) + 矢先に
- Example: 出かけようとした矢先に、雨が降り出した。 (Just as I was about to go out, it started raining.)

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal spoken and written Japanese; often used in narrative storytelling to describe an ironic or unlucky coincidence in timing.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **か～ないかのうちに** — "just as/right before ~" — focuses purely on near-simultaneous timing without implying intention (とした) as 矢先に does.
- **とたんに** — "the moment ~" — describes an immediate consequence right after an action, usually a completed action rather than an about-to-do intention.
- **さなかに** — "right in the middle of ~" — describes something happening during an ongoing situation/event, rather than at the exact starting point of an intended action.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though しばらく経ってから ("after some time had passed") contrasts in spirit by indicating a delay rather than an immediate coincidence.

**Example sentences:**
1. 出かけようとした矢先に、雨が降り出した。
   (Just as I was about to go out, it started raining.)
2. 新しい仕事を始めた矢先に、会社が倒産してしまった。
   (Just as I had started my new job, the company went bankrupt.)
3. やっと病気が治った矢先に、また熱を出してしまった。
   (Just when my illness had finally healed, I came down with a fever again.)
4. 結婚しようとした矢先に、彼が海外転勤を命じられた。
   (Just as we were about to get married, he was ordered to transfer overseas.)
5. ようやく貯金ができた矢先に、車が故障して修理代がかかってしまった。
   (Just when I had finally managed to save some money, my car broke down and I had to pay for repairs.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 矢先に almost always describes something unlucky, ironic, or unwanted happening at an inopportune moment — it's rarely used for positive coincidences. Learners sometimes use it like とたんに, but 矢先に specifically emphasizes the frustrating timing of an interruption to a plan or newly begun state.
