# ～をへて {#をへて}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～を経て

**Furigana form:** ～をへて

**Meaning:** "Through/via X (a process, place, or stage)" — describes passing through a stage, location, or experience as part of reaching an eventual outcome.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + を経て
- Example base: 様々な困難 → 様々な困難を経て

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal, usable in both speech and writing; common in biographical narration, historical description, and formal explanations of a process's stages.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **を通じて／を通して** — "through/via X"; overlaps somewhat, but を通じて more often describes a means/channel (through a person, medium, or ongoing period), while を経て specifically emphasizes passing through discrete stages or milestones en route to a result.
- [**の末に**](grammar-の末に.md) — "after much X, finally..."; shares the sense of a process leading to an outcome, but の末に emphasizes the difficulty/length of the process reaching its conclusion, whereas を経て more neutrally states the stages passed through.
- **を経由して** — "via X (a route/waypoint)"; more literal/technical, often used for physical transit routes (e.g., flights), while を経て is more general and can apply to abstract stages.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; 直接～する ("do X directly") expresses the reverse — reaching an outcome without passing through intermediate stages.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は様々な困難を経て成功を手に入れた。 (He achieved success after going through various hardships.)
2. 長い交渉を経て、ようやく契約が成立した。 (After lengthy negotiations, the contract was finally concluded.)
3. この法案は国会での審議を経て可決された。 (This bill was passed after deliberation in the Diet.)
4. 彼女は数年の下積み生活を経て、人気女優になった。 (After years of paying her dues, she became a popular actress.)
5. その技術は長年の研究開発を経て実用化された。 (That technology was put into practical use after years of research and development.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** を経て emphasizes the stages/process a subject passed through before reaching a final state, so it is almost always followed by a result clause describing that eventual outcome — using it without a following result can feel incomplete. Learners should distinguish it from を通じて, which more often describes an ongoing means or channel (e.g., 友人を通じて紹介された, "introduced through a friend") rather than sequential stages of development.
