## 大筋 (おおすじ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** the gist, outline

**Similar words:**
- [概要](vocab-11.md#概要-がいよう)（がいよう）— "a summary/overview," more formal and often written (a document's abstract); 大筋 is more conversational, referring to the general gist or main thrust of a story/plan.
- あらすじ（あらすじ）— specifically "a plot summary" (of a story, movie, novel), narrower than 大筋's broader applicability to plans, agreements, discussions.

**Opposite words:**
- 詳細（しょうさい）— details

**Example sentences:**
1. 交渉は大筋で合意に達した。— The negotiations reached agreement in broad outline.
2. 大筋ではその案に賛成だ。— I generally agree with that proposal.
3. 計画の大筋はすでに決まっている。— The general outline of the plan has already been decided.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 大筋で/大筋は is a common set phrase meaning "broadly/in general terms" often used in news about negotiations or agreements (大筋合意, a broad/tentative agreement) — learners should recognize this journalistic collocation.

## 大空 (おおぞら)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** the sky, the heavens

**Similar words:**
- [空](vocab-14.md#空-から)（そら）— the plain, everyday word for "sky"; 大空 is more poetic/grandiose, emphasizing vastness and is common in songs, poems, and emotionally evocative writing.
- 天（てん）— "heaven/the heavens," more philosophical/religious in connotation than the visually descriptive 大空.

**Opposite words:**
- 大地（だいち）— the earth, the ground

**Example sentences:**
1. 鳥たちが大空を自由に飛んでいる。— The birds are flying freely across the vast sky.
2. 大空に向かって夢を語った。— I spoke of my dreams toward the open sky.
3. 彼は大空を見上げてため息をついた。— He looked up at the vast sky and sighed.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 大空 is a literary/emotive word, common in song lyrics and poetic writing to emphasize the sky's vastness and freedom — using it in plain everyday conversation about the weather would sound unnaturally dramatic; 空 is the default neutral word.

## 大通り (おおどおり)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a main street

**Similar words:**
- 通り（とおり）— the general word for "street/road"; 大通り specifically means a wide, major thoroughfare, often with heavy traffic and shops.
- メインストリート（メインストリート）— the loanword equivalent, sometimes used for a town's central shopping street, especially in casual or tourism contexts.

**Opposite words:**
- 路地（ろじ）— a narrow alley, back street

**Example sentences:**
1. 大通りに面したビルにオフィスがある。— The office is in a building facing the main street.
2. 大通りは車でいつも混んでいる。— The main street is always congested with cars.
3. 大通り沿いにレストランが立ち並んでいる。— Restaurants line the main street.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 大通り specifically implies a wide, prominent road (often with multiple lanes and significant traffic/commerce) — it shouldn't be used interchangeably with any 通り (street), which can also refer to small residential roads.

## オートマチック (オートマチック)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** automatic

**Similar words:**
- 自動（じどう）— the native Japanese word for "automatic," used broadly in compounds (自動ドア, automatic door; 自動販売機, vending machine); オートマチック is often specifically used for automatic transmission cars or mechanisms, closer to English "automatic" in a technical/mechanical sense.
- オートマ（オートマ）— a shortened casual form of オートマチック, very commonly used specifically for automatic-transmission cars.

**Opposite words:**
- [マニュアル](vocab-03.md#マニュアル)（マニュアル）— manual (as in manual transmission)

**Example sentences:**
1. この車はオートマチック車です。— This car is an automatic (transmission) car.
2. オートマチックなドアが開いた。— The automatic door opened.
3. 彼はオートマチックの拳銃を持っていた。— He had an automatic pistol.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** In everyday conversation about cars, オートマチック is usually shortened to オートマ or even AT — using the full word オートマチック in casual speech can sound slightly stiff compared to these common abbreviations.

## オートメーション (オートメーション)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** automation

**Similar words:**
- 自動化（じどうか）— the native Japanese equivalent, "automation" (literally "making automatic"), often used interchangeably, especially in technical/business writing.
- 機械化（きかいか）— "mechanization," a related but distinct concept emphasizing replacing manual labor with machines, not necessarily implying full automation/self-operation.

**Opposite words:**
- 手作業（てさぎょう）— manual work, hand labor

**Example sentences:**
1. 工場はオートメーション化が進んでいる。— The factory is becoming increasingly automated.
2. オートメーション技術により生産性が向上した。— Productivity improved thanks to automation technology.
3. オートメーションによって多くの仕事がなくなった。— Many jobs disappeared because of automation.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** オートメーション is often verbalized as オートメーション化する ("to automate") rather than used as a plain suru-verb itself — learners should note this 化する pattern, paralleling 自動化する, which is the more commonly used term in business Japanese.

## オーバーする (オーバーする)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to exceed, go over

**Similar words:**
- 超える（こえる）— the native verb "to exceed/surpass," more formal and widely applicable (超過する also overlaps); オーバーする is a casual loanword verb often used for budgets, weight, time limits in everyday speech.
- 超過する（ちょうかする）— "to exceed" formally, often used in official/technical contexts (超過料金, excess fee), more formal-sounding than オーバーする.

**Opposite words:**
- 下回る（したまわる）— to fall below, be under

**Example sentences:**
1. 予算をオーバーしてしまった。— I ended up going over budget.
2. 体重が5キロオーバーした。— My weight went over by 5 kilograms.
3. 制限時間をオーバーすると失格になる。— If you go over the time limit, you'll be disqualified.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** オーバーする is casual/conversational and commonly used for quantifiable limits (budget, weight, time), while 超える or 超過する fit more formal or written contexts — also note オーバー alone (without する) can function as a noun/adjective meaning "excessive" (オーバーな反応, an exaggerated reaction).

## オープン (オープン)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** open, openhearted; to open (a shop, event)

**Similar words:**
- [開く](vocab-32.md#開く-ひらく)（ひらく）— the native verb "to open," broadly applicable (open a door, open a store); オープンする specifically is often used for the grand opening of a business or facility.
- 率直（そっちょく）— "frank, candid," closer to the "openhearted/open about feelings" sense of オープン when describing a person's communication style.

**Opposite words:**
- クローズ（クローズ）— closed; 閉鎖（へいさ）— closure

**Example sentences:**
1. 新しいカフェが来月オープンする。— A new cafe will open next month.
2. 彼はとてもオープンな性格だ。— He has a very open/candid personality.
3. オープンな議論を歓迎します。— We welcome open discussion.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** オープン covers both the literal "opening" of a business/event (store openings, ceremony) and the figurative "open-minded/candid" personality trait — learners should note it functions as both a suru-verb (オープンする, to open) and a na-adjective (オープンな性格).

## 大まかな (おおまかな)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** rough, broad, approximate

**Similar words:**
- [大雑把](vocab-09.md#大雑把-おおざっぱ)（おおざっぱ）— near-synonym, but often carries more negative connotation of carelessness; 大まかな tends to be more neutral/positive, simply meaning "in broad strokes" without implying sloppiness.
- おおよそ（おおよそ）— "roughly, approximately," an adverb often used with numbers/estimates, whereas 大まかな is an adjective modifying nouns (大まかな計画, a rough plan).

**Opposite words:**
- 細かい（こまかい）— detailed, fine, meticulous

**Example sentences:**
1. 大まかな予定を立てた。— I made a rough schedule.
2. 大まかな数字でいいので教えてください。— Please tell me, even just a rough figure is fine.
3. 彼は大まかな性格で細かいことは気にしない。— He has an easygoing personality and doesn't worry about details.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 大まかな is generally more neutral/forgiving than 大雑把, which can sound critical — when praising someone's "big-picture" thinking positively, 大まかな fits better than 大雑把, which risks sounding like an insult about carelessness.

## 大水 (おおみず)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a flood

**Similar words:**
- [洪水](vocab-18.md#洪水-こうずい)（こうずい）— the more common, standard word for "flood," used in news and everyday speech; 大水 is a more old-fashioned or regional/literary term for the same phenomenon.
- 氾濫（はんらん）— "overflow (of a river)," a more technical/formal term often used in weather reports (河川の氾濫, river overflow/flooding).

**Opposite words:**
- 干ばつ（かんばつ）— drought

**Example sentences:**
1. 大雨で川が大水になった。— The heavy rain caused the river to flood.
2. 昔この辺りはよく大水に見舞われた。— This area used to be frequently hit by floods.
3. 大水で家が流された。— The house was swept away by the flood.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 大水 is somewhat old-fashioned/literary compared to the standard 洪水, which is what you'd hear in modern news broadcasts — learners should default to 洪水 for contemporary conversation and reserve 大水 for recognizing it in older texts or regional speech.

## 大家 (おおや)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a landlord/landlady

**Similar words:**
- 大家さん（おおやさん）— the polite, commonly spoken form of address/reference, used when actually talking to or about one's landlord; 大家 alone is the plain noun.
- [家主](vocab-34.md#家主-やぬし)（やぬし）— a more formal/legal term for "property owner/landlord," often used in contracts and official documents, whereas 大家(さん) is the everyday conversational term.

**Opposite words:**
- 店子（たなこ）— a tenant

**Example sentences:**
1. 大家さんに家賃を払った。— I paid the rent to my landlord.
2. 大家さんはとても親切な人だ。— My landlord is a very kind person.
3. 水漏れについて大家に相談した。— I consulted the landlord about the water leak.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Note the irregular reading おおや (not だいか, the reading you'd expect from 大家 in other contexts like 大家（たいか）meaning "a great master/authority in a field") — the same kanji compound has a completely different reading and meaning depending on context, which often trips up learners.

## 公 (おおやけ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** public, official

**Similar words:**
- 公的（こうてき）— "official/public" as an adjective, more formal/administrative (公的機関, public institution); 公 as a standalone noun means "the public (domain)" or "openly, publicly" (公にする, to make public).
- [世間](vocab-23.md#世間-せけん)（せけん）— "the world/society at large, public opinion," more about social perception than formal/official status.

**Opposite words:**
- 私（わたくし/し）— private, personal

**Example sentences:**
1. その事実はまだ公になっていない。— That fact has not yet been made public.
2. 公の場で発言するのは緊張する。— Speaking in public settings makes me nervous.
3. 彼はついに真実を公にした。— He finally made the truth public.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 公 read as おおやけ is a native-reading noun meaning "the public/open, official," distinct from the same kanji read こう in compounds like 公共 (public, kōkyō) — the set phrase 公にする (to make public/disclose) is especially common and worth memorizing directly.

## 大凡 (おおよそ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** almost, roughly, approximately

**Similar words:**
- だいたい（だいたい）— very close synonym, "roughly, mostly"; largely interchangeable, though おおよそ can feel slightly more formal/written.
- [およそ](vocab-11.md#およそ)（およそ）— same word, often written without the 大 kanji; 大凡 is simply one kanji representation of およそ.

**Opposite words:**
- 正確に（せいかくに）— exactly, precisely

**Example sentences:**
1. 参加者はおおよそ100人だった。— There were roughly 100 participants.
2. おおよその見当はついている。— I have a rough idea already.
3. 工事はおおよそ完了した。— The construction is almost complete.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** おおよそ can function as both an adverb ("roughly, approximately" before a number) and a noun-like element in set phrases (おおよその見当, a rough estimate) — it's nearly synonymous with だいたい but appears more often in slightly more formal or written registers.

## 犯す (おかす)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to sin, commit a crime

**Similar words:**
- 侵す（おかす）— homophone, "to invade/violate" (territory, rights, privacy); different kanji, so care is needed not to confuse 犯す (commit a crime/sin) with 侵す (invade/encroach).
- 冒す（おかす）— another homophone, "to risk/brave" (danger), e.g. 危険を冒す (to brave a risk) — a third distinct meaning sharing the same reading.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は重大な罪を犯した。— He committed a serious crime.
2. 過ちを犯すのは誰にでもあることだ。— Making mistakes is something anyone can do.
3. 彼女は法律を犯したことを後悔している。— She regrets having broken the law.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** おかす has three common kanji (犯す commit a crime/sin, 侵す invade/violate rights, 冒す risk/brave danger) that are homophones but carry distinct meanings — learners must choose the correct kanji based on context (犯罪/過ちを犯す vs. 領土を侵す vs. 危険を冒す).

## お構いなく (おかまいなく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** Please don't trouble yourself (set phrase/greeting)

**Similar words:**
- 気にしないでください（きにしないでください）— "please don't worry about it," a more direct, plain way to say the same thing; お構いなく is a fixed polite phrase specifically used to decline a host's offer of hospitality.
- どうぞお構いなく（どうぞおかまいなく）— the fuller, even more polite version often used verbatim as a complete phrase when visiting someone's home.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. どうぞお構いなく、すぐに失礼しますので。— Please don't trouble yourself, I'll be leaving shortly.
2. お構いなく、お茶はいただいております。— Don't go to any trouble, I'm already having tea.
3. 「何かお飲み物は？」「お構いなく」— "Would you like something to drink?" "Please don't trouble yourself."

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** お構いなく is a fixed, polite set phrase used almost exclusively as a response to decline a host's hospitality (tea, snacks, special effort) — it's not a general-purpose phrase meaning "don't worry," which would instead be 気にしないでください in everyday contexts.

## 拝む (おがむ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to worship, pray; (humble) to see

**Similar words:**
- 祈る（いのる）— "to pray," more general and doesn't necessarily involve a physical gesture; 拝む specifically implies the physical act of pressing hands together and bowing, often before a shrine, altar, or statue.
- お目にかかる（おめにかかる）— humble expression "to meet/see (a respected person)," overlapping with 拝む's humble "to see" usage (お顔を拝む, to humbly see someone's face).

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 仏壇に手を合わせて拝んだ。— I pressed my hands together and prayed at the Buddhist altar.
2. 神社で神様を拝んだ。— I worshipped the god at the shrine.
3. 先生のお顔を拝むことができて光栄です。— I'm honored to have had the chance to see the teacher's face (humble).

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Beyond literal religious worship, 拝む has a humble usage meaning "to (humbly) see/look at" something precious or a respected person (お顔を拝む) — this secondary, self-deprecating usage is often missed by learners who only study the religious meaning.

## お代わり (おかわり)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a second helping, seconds

**Similar words:**
- おかわりする（おかわりする）— the verb form, "to have a second helping," commonly used when asking for more food/drink.
- 追加注文（ついかちゅうもん）— "additional order," a more formal/restaurant term for ordering more food, distinct from the casual, often free "seconds" implied by お代わり (especially rice/soup at home or set meals).

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. ご飯のお代わりをお願いします。— Could I have a second helping of rice, please?
2. このラーメン屋はスープのお代わりが無料だ。— At this ramen shop, soup refills are free.
3. 彼は三回もお代わりした。— He had seconds three times.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** お代わり specifically implies getting more of the same food/drink you already had (often free for rice/soup at Japanese restaurants), distinct from 追加注文 (ordering something additional/different) — don't use it for ordering a completely new dish.

## おき (おき)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** every other ~, at intervals of ~ (suffix)

**Similar words:**
- ごとに（ごとに）— "every ~, each ~," a related suffix but implies each instance without a gap (毎日ごとに, every day), whereas おき implies a skip/interval (一日おきに, every other day, i.e., with a day in between).
- [隔週](vocab-12.md#隔週-かくしゅう)（かくしゅう）— "every other week" as a fixed compound noun, a narrower lexical equivalent to 一週間おき.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 一日おきに薬を飲んでください。— Please take the medicine every other day.
2. このバスは15分おきに来る。— This bus comes every 15 minutes.
3. 彼とは一週間おきに会っている。— I meet him every other week.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 〜おき attaches to a time/distance unit and means "with that interval in between" — 一日おき literally means "every other day" (a day skipped between), which learners sometimes misinterpret as simply "daily"; compare carefully with 毎日 (every day, no skip).

## 補う (おぎなう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to supplement, make up for, compensate

**Similar words:**
- 補足する（ほそくする）— "to supplement/add (information)," often used for adding extra explanation to speech or writing; 補う is broader, covering compensating for deficiencies in general (money, nutrients, skills).
- 埋め合わせる（うめあわせる）— "to make up for (a loss or deficit)," closer in nuance to compensating for something missing, often used interpersonally (埋め合わせをする, to make it up to someone).

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 栄養不足をサプリメントで補った。— I made up for the nutritional deficiency with supplements.
2. 彼は経験不足を努力で補っている。— He compensates for his lack of experience with effort.
3. 不足している人員を補うため新人を雇った。— We hired new staff to make up for the shortage of personnel.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 補う implies filling a gap or deficiency to reach a needed/sufficient level (nutrients, manpower, skills), and is commonly paired with 不足 (shortage) — it's more about restoring adequacy than simply "adding extra," a subtle difference from words like 追加する (to add).

## お気の毒に (おきのどくに)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** I'm sorry (to hear that); that's too bad/unfortunate

**Similar words:**
- お気の毒様（おきのどくさま）— a slightly more old-fashioned/formal variant of the same expression of sympathy.
- かわいそうに（かわいそうに）— "poor thing, how pitiful," more casual and can sometimes carry a slightly condescending or pitying tone; お気の毒に is more neutral/polite sympathy appropriate for formal situations.

**Opposite words:**
- おめでとうございます（おめでとうございます）— congratulations

**Example sentences:**
1. ご主人が入院されたとか。それはお気の毒に。— I heard your husband was hospitalized. I'm so sorry to hear that.
2. お気の毒に、せっかくの旅行が中止になってしまって。— That's unfortunate, your long-awaited trip got cancelled.
3. 「財布を落としたんです」「それはお気の毒に」— "I lost my wallet." "Oh, I'm sorry to hear that."

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** お気の毒に is a set phrase of polite sympathy for someone else's misfortune, not for your own situation — and it should be used carefully, as overuse or use with minor inconveniences can sound exaggerated or insincere.

## 奥 (おく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** the inner part, the back, depths

**Similar words:**
- 裏（うら）— "the back/reverse side," more about the opposite side of something (back of a building, reverse of paper); 奥 implies depth/distance inward, not just a flat "back" surface.
- 内部（ないぶ）— "the interior/inside," a more formal/technical term for the inside of a structure or system, without the connotation of deep remoteness that 奥 carries.

**Opposite words:**
- 手前（てまえ）— the near side, this side

**Example sentences:**
1. 店の奥に座席がある。— There are seats at the back of the store.
2. 彼は山の奥に住んでいる。— He lives deep in the mountains.
3. 心の奥底にある思いを打ち明けた。— I confided the feelings deep in my heart.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 奥 frequently extends metaphorically to "deep inside (one's heart/mind)" (心の奥, 奥底) — not just physical depth — a figurative use that's very common in emotional/literary Japanese and worth learning alongside the literal spatial sense.

## 屋外 (おくがい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** outdoors

**Similar words:**
- 外（そと）— the plain, everyday word for "outside"; 屋外 is more formal/technical, often used in official contexts (屋外活動, outdoor activities; 屋外広告, outdoor advertising).
- アウトドア（アウトドア）— the loanword, typically associated with recreational outdoor activities (camping, hiking) rather than the neutral locational meaning of 屋外.

**Opposite words:**
- 屋内（おくない）— indoors

**Example sentences:**
1. 屋外でのイベントは天候に左右される。— Outdoor events are affected by the weather.
2. 屋外プールは夏の間だけ営業している。— The outdoor pool is only open during the summer.
3. 屋外での喫煙は禁止されています。— Smoking outdoors is prohibited.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 屋外 and 屋内 (indoors) form a clear formal pair often seen in signage and official notices — in casual conversation, people more often just say 外 (outside) and 中/室内 (inside), so learners should match register appropriately.

## 奥さん/様 (おくさん/さま)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** (your/someone's) wife; Mrs., madam

**Similar words:**
- 妻（つま）— the neutral, standard word for "(my own) wife," used when referring to one's own spouse; 奥さん is used to refer to someone else's wife respectfully (not your own, which would sound oddly distant/formal).
- 家内（かない）— a humble, old-fashioned way to refer to one's own wife, contrasting with the respectful 奥さん used for others' wives.

**Opposite words:**
- 旦那さん（だんなさん）— (your/someone's) husband

**Example sentences:**
1. 田中さんの奥さんはとても親切だ。— Mr. Tanaka's wife is very kind.
2. 奥様によろしくお伝えください。— Please give my regards to your wife.
3. あの奥さん、いつも元気だね。— That lady (housewife) is always so energetic, isn't she?

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 奥さん/奥様 is used to refer respectfully to someone else's wife (or sometimes as a general polite address to a married woman), never to one's own wife in formal reference — learners should use 妻 or 家内 for their own spouse to avoid sounding odd.

## 遅らす (おくらす)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to delay, postpone

**Similar words:**
- 遅らせる（おくらせる）— nearly identical in meaning and more commonly used in modern Japanese; 遅らす is a slightly older/alternate causative-like form of 遅れる, largely interchangeable with 遅らせる.
- 延期する（えんきする）— "to postpone" an event to a later date (more formal, implies an official rescheduling), whereas 遅らす is more general, about making something happen later than planned (not necessarily rescheduling an event).

**Opposite words:**
- 早める（はやめる）— to move up, make earlier

**Example sentences:**
1. 出発の時間を少し遅らそう。— Let's delay the departure time a little.
2. 会議の開始を遅らすことになった。— The start of the meeting ended up being delayed.
