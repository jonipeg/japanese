# ～たら最後／～たが最後 {#たら最後・たが最後}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～たら最後／～たが最後

**Furigana form:** ～たらさいご／～たがさいご

**Meaning:** "Once ~ happens, it's over / something terrible inevitably follows" — describes an action that, once it takes place, is guaranteed to lead to a bad, often irreversible, outcome.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞た形 ＋ ら最後／が最後
- 飲み始めたら最後、酔いつぶれるまで飲んでしまう — "once he starts drinking, that's it — he drinks until he passes out"

**Typical context:** Spoken and written, somewhat colloquial but emphatic register — used in everyday complaints or warnings about a person's predictable bad habit, or in dramatic narration about a point of no return.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**～が早いか**](grammar-が早いか.md) — "no sooner had ~ than," focuses purely on immediacy of sequence without the "guaranteed bad outcome" nuance of たら最後.
- [**～ばそれまでだ**](grammar-ばそれまでだ.md) — "if ~, then that's the end of it," similarly expresses a point of no return, but focuses on something becoming useless/meaningless rather than leading to an ongoing bad chain of events.
- **～と最後** is not standard; the fixed forms are specifically ～たら最後 and ～たが最後.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a phrase like ～ても大丈夫だ ("even if ~, it's fine") would describe the reverse — an action with no serious consequence.

**Example sentences:**
1. 兄は大酒飲みだから、飲み始めたら最後、酔いつぶれるまで飲んでしまう。
   (My brother is a heavy drinker, so once he starts drinking, that's it — he keeps going until he's completely drunk.)
2. うちの娘はパソコンの前に座ったが最後、声をかけても返事もしない。
   (Once my daughter sits down in front of the computer, that's it — she won't even answer when spoken to.)
3. 彼にお金を持たせたら最後、何に使われるかわからない。
   (Once you let him hold the money, there's no telling what he'll spend it on.)
4. あの政治家は一度マイクを握ったら最後、話が止まらない。
   (Once that politician grabs the microphone, that's it — he won't stop talking.)
5. この沼に足を踏み入れたが最後、二度と抜け出せないと言われている。
   (It's said that once you set foot in this swamp, that's the end — you can never get out again.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The clause following たら最後／たが最後 must describe something the speaker regards as clearly bad or undesirable — it cannot be used for a neutral or positive consequence. The pattern can describe either a habitually recurring, already-known outcome (as in examples 1-2) or a hypothetical one the speaker is warning about (as in example 3), but either way it conveys that there is no stopping the chain of events once the triggering action occurs.
