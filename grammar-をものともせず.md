# ～をものともせず(に)／しないで {#をものともせず}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～を物ともせず（に）／しないで

**Furigana form:** ～をものともせず（に）／しないで

**Meaning:** "Undaunted by X / not letting X be a hindrance" — describes bravely pushing forward despite a significant obstacle, hardship, or danger, often with an admiring tone.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + を物ともせず（に）
- Example base: 高熱 → 高熱をものともせずに

**Typical context:** Formal, written register; common in news reporting, biographical writing, and formal praise describing someone's courage or determination in the face of adversity.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**をおして**](grammar-をおして.md) — "doing X despite circumstances that should prevent it"; very close in meaning, both describing overcoming a hindrance, though をおして often focuses on physical hardship (illness, pain) while をものともせず can extend to broader obstacles (criticism, danger, difficulty).
- **にもめげず** — "without being discouraged by X"; similarly admiring, emphasizing resilience of spirit in the face of setbacks, overlapping closely with をものともせず.
- [**をよそに**](grammar-をよそに.md) — "ignoring X"; shares the surface structure of disregarding something, but をよそに usually carries a critical nuance (selfishly ignoring a legitimate concern), whereas をものともせず is admiring (bravely overcoming a real obstacle).

**Opposite grammar structures:** ～に屈して／～に負けて ("yielding to X / being defeated by X") expresses the reverse — succumbing to the obstacle rather than overcoming it.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女は周囲の批判をものともせず、自分の夢を追い続けた。 (Undaunted by criticism from those around her, she kept pursuing her dream.)
2. 選手たちは悪天候をものともせず、試合を続けた。 (The players continued the match, undaunted by the bad weather.)
3. 彼は障害をものともせずに、見事マラソンを完走した。 (Undaunted by his disability, he brilliantly completed the marathon.)
4. その会社は不況をものともせず、業績を伸ばし続けている。 (Undaunted by the recession, that company continues to grow its performance.)
5. 彼女は周囲の反対をものともせず、留学を決意した。 (Undaunted by opposition from those around her, she resolved to study abroad.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** をものともせず always carries an admiring tone toward the subject's courage or resolve in the face of a real, significant obstacle — it should not be used for trivial inconveniences, nor should it carry a critical nuance (for disregarding something one shouldn't, をよそに is more appropriate). 物ともせず literally means "not even treating it as a 'thing' (worth worrying about)," conveying the subject's fearless dismissal of the obstacle's significance.
