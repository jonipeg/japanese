# それも {#それも}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** それも (no kanji; conventionally written in kana)

**Furigana form:** それも

**Meaning:** "And what's more..." — adds emphatic, often exasperated or surprised reinforcement to the immediately preceding statement.

**Conjugation:** Sentence-initial conjunction, no conjugation needed — attaches at the sentence boundary, connecting the previous sentence/clause to the next. Frequently used to tack on a short, punchy clarifying detail right after the main statement, almost like an exclamation.

**Typical context:** Casual-to-neutral spoken and written register, carrying a distinctly emotional flavor of surprise, exasperation, or emphasis — the speaker is reacting to the preceding fact as if it weren't already enough. Common in conversation when venting or recounting something that got worse or more absurd than expected.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**それに**](grammar-それに.md) — adds a supplementary point reinforcing an argument in a neutral, matter-of-fact way; lacks それも's exasperated/surprised emotional charge.
- [**そのうえ**](grammar-そのうえ.md) — adds a notable extra point in a calmer, more composed tone; それも is more emotionally charged and reactive.
- [**しかも**](grammar-しかも.md) — adds a surprising/notable additional fact; shares それも's surprise nuance, but しかも more often introduces a genuinely new additional fact, while それも tends to emphasize/intensify the immediately preceding point itself (e.g. repeating or specifying it with added disbelief).
- [**さらに**](grammar-さらに.md) — "furthermore," escalating emphasis in a neutral, formal way; それも is more colloquial and emotionally reactive.
- [**あと**](grammar-あと.md) — casual spoken "also," plain afterthought; lacks それも's exasperation/surprise nuance entirely.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite — それも's narrow function (exasperated reinforcement of a preceding point) doesn't have a natural "opposite" conjunction; the nearest functional contrast would be a plain, deflating conjunction like でも used to downplay rather than amplify.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼、また遅刻したんだって。それも、三十分も。
   (I heard he was late again. And get this, by thirty minutes.)
2. この荷物、一人で運んだの? それも、階段で?
   (You carried this by yourself? And on top of that, up the stairs?)
3. 財布をなくしたんだ。それも、旅行の初日に。
   (I lost my wallet. And of all times, on the first day of the trip.)
4. 彼は会社を辞めたらしい。それも、何の相談もなしに。
   (I heard he quit the company. And what's more, without consulting anyone.)
5. この値段で売れるわけがない。それも、こんな状態で。
   (There's no way this will sell at this price. Especially not in this condition.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often confuse それも's exasperated, reactive emphasis with それに's neutral additive function — それに calmly adds a supporting reason, while それも expresses the speaker's surprise or frustration that the situation is even worse/more extreme than already stated. Using それも in a flat, unemotional context can sound oddly dramatic, while using それに where strong emotional reaction is intended can undersell the speaker's feelings.
