# ～ながらに {#ながらに}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ながらに／～ながらの／～ながらにして

**Furigana form:** ～ながらに／～ながらの／～ながらにして

**Meaning:** "While/as still being in the state of X" — describes doing something in, or possessing something from, an unchanged original state (e.g., "since birth," "with tears still in one's eyes"). In some uses it can also shift to a concessive sense equivalent to けれども ("although").

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + ながらに／ながらの + 名詞 (common fixed expressions: 生まれながらに, 涙ながらに, 昔ながらの)
- 動詞ます形 + ながらに (less common, mostly fixed set phrases)
- ながらにして adds emphasis, often with 居ながらにして ("without even leaving one's seat/home")

**Typical context:** Formal, written/literary register; appears in narrative prose, news writing, and formal speech. Not used in casual conversation. The everyday spoken equivalent, per the source's own glosses, is generally just そのまま or (in its concessive use) けれども/しかし.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**つつ**](grammar-つつ.md) — "while doing X"; a simultaneous-action connector for two ongoing actions by the same subject, whereas ながらに describes an unchanged inherent state, not two parallel actions.
- [**まま**](grammar-まま(に).md) — "leaving things as they are"; overlaps with ながらに's "unchanged state" sense but まま is far more general-purpose and usable in casual speech, while ながらに is restricted to a small set of literary fixed expressions.
- **つつも** — explicitly concessive "although doing X"; closer to the secondary けれども-like sense of ながらに, but つつも is more productive and commonly used for genuine concession, while ながらに's concessive use is a secondary, less frequent meaning.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; conceptually 変わって/一変して ("having changed completely") contrasts with ながらに's "unchanged" core meaning.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼の音感は生まれながらに備わった才能だ。 (His sense of pitch is a talent he was born with.)
2. 彼女は涙ながらにこれまでの苦労を語った。 (She recounted her hardships so far with tears streaming down her face.)
3. この町には昔ながらの商店街が残っている。 (This town still has a shopping street that remains just as it was long ago.)
4. 今はインターネットのおかげで、家に居ながらにして世界中のニュースが見られる。 (Thanks to the internet, we can see news from around the world without even leaving home.)
5. 彼は体が不自由ながらに、人一倍の努力を続けてきた。 (Although his body was physically limited, he kept making greater effort than anyone else.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often memorize ながらに only as "while in the state of X since Y," and are then surprised when it appears with a clearly concessive sense close to けれども (as in example 5, "although his body was limited..."). Do not confuse ながらに with the simultaneous-action ながら (e.g., 食べながら話す "talk while eating") — ながらに attaches mainly to nouns describing an inherent/original condition, not to a second simultaneous verb.
