# ～までもない {#までもない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～までもない

**Furigana form:** ～までもない

**Meaning:** Says that something is so minor or obvious that it isn't worth the effort of doing — "there's no need to go so far as to X" / "it's not even necessary to X."

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形 + までもない
- 傘をさすまでもない — "not even worth bothering to open an umbrella"

**Typical context:** Written and spoken Japanese, neutral-to-polite register — common in everyday reassurances, business writing, and explanatory speech when downplaying the need for an action.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**～までもなく**](grammar-までもなく.md) — the adverbial te-form-like variant, meaning "without even needing to X" (e.g., 言うまでもなく, "needless to say"), used mid-sentence rather than as a sentence-final predicate.
- [**～ことはない**](grammar-ことはない.md) — similarly means "there's no need to X," but is more general-purpose and slightly less formal than ～までもない, which specifically stresses that the matter is too trivial to warrant the action.
- **～なくてもいい** — a plainer way to say "you don't have to X," without the nuance that the action would be excessive or redundant given how minor the situation is.

**Opposite grammar structures:** ～ないわけにはいかない ("cannot avoid doing X") expresses the opposite idea — that an action is unavoidable or necessary, in contrast to ～までもない's claim that the action isn't even worth doing.

**Example sentences:**
1. このぐらいの雨なら、傘をさすまでもない。
   (With rain this light, there's no need to even bother with an umbrella.)
2. 確認するまでもないことですが、あしたの集合場所は駅前の広場です。
   (This hardly needs confirming, but tomorrow's meeting place is the plaza in front of the station.)
3. 予約したホテルは駅を降りたら探すまでもなく目の前にあった。
   (The hotel we'd booked was right in front of us the moment we got off the train — no need to even look for it.)
4. 言うまでもなく、学生にとっては勉強が一番大切だ。
   (Needless to say, studying is the most important thing for a student.)
5. 彼のミスは小さなものだったので、わざわざ上司に報告するまでもないと思った。
   (His mistake was so minor that I didn't think it was even worth reporting to the boss.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern attaches to verbs describing intentional, volitional actions (言う, 確認する, 探す, さす, etc.) — not to states or involuntary happenings. It specifically conveys that the action is unnecessary because the matter is too trivial or obvious, which is a different nuance from simply saying an action is optional; avoid using it for situations where the issue is genuinely uncertain or serious.
