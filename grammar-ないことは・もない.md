# ～ないことは/もない {#ないことは・もない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ないことは(も)ない

**Furigana form:** ～ないことは(も)ない

**Meaning:** "It's not that X doesn't happen" / "It's not like X isn't possible" — a double-negative construction that produces a soft, hedged affirmation ("I suppose it's possible," "there's some chance of X").

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ない形 + ことは(も)ない — 行かないことはない (it's not that I won't go / I could go)
- い形容詞ない形 + ことは(も)ない — 高くないことはない (it's not that it isn't expensive / it is a bit expensive)
- な形容詞/名詞 + でないことは(も)ない — 無理でないことはない (it's not impossible)

**Typical context:** Neutral, spoken and written — used when giving a reluctant, qualified, or cautious affirmation rather than a flat yes.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **ないでもない** — nearly identical meaning and register, interchangeable in most cases.
- [**わけではない**](grammar-わけではない.md) — "it's not that X (as a blanket statement)," broader partial-negation but without the double-negative structure.
- **なくはない** — a slightly more literary/compact variant with the same hedging nuance.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though a flat affirmative ～です/ます contrasts in directness.

**Example sentences:**
1. 忙しいが、手伝えないことはない。
   (I'm busy, but it's not that I can't help — I could.)
2. その話を聞いて、少し不安にならないことはなかった。
   (Hearing that, I wasn't entirely free of unease.)
3. このレストランは高いが、味を考えれば納得できないこともない。
   (This restaurant is pricey, but considering the taste, it's not unreasonable.)
4. 彼の言い分も、わからないことはない。
   (I can understand his reasoning, to some extent.)
5. 今から準備すれば、間に合わないこともないだろう。
   (If we start preparing now, it's not impossible that we'll make it in time.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This is a hedge, not a strong statement — overusing it where a direct answer is expected can sound evasive. Learners sometimes misread the double negative as a stronger negative rather than a soft positive; the nuance is "reluctantly/conditionally possible," not "definitely yes."
