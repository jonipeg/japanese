# それでも {#それでも}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** それでも

**Furigana form:** それでも

**Meaning:** "Even so, even then, still" — a concessive/contrastive conjunction stating that despite the preceding fact, the following statement remains true.

**Conjugation:** Sentence-initial conjunction, no conjugation needed — attaches at the sentence boundary, connecting the previous sentence/fact to the next statement that holds true despite it. It is almost always used sentence-initially rather than linking clauses mid-sentence; a mid-sentence equivalent would typically use のに or けれども instead.

**Typical context:** Neutral, usable in both spoken and written registers — common in everyday conversation (expressing quiet determination or resignation) as well as in essays, speeches, and narrative writing to mark a persistent or stubborn truth against expectation. It carries an emotional, often admirable or poignant tone ("despite everything, still...") and is neither particularly casual nor particularly formal, making it broadly usable across registers.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**それなら**](grammar-それなら.md) — its commonly confused counterpart: それでも is 逆接 (concessive — "despite the preceding, X is still true"), while それなら is 条件 (conditional — "given the preceding as a premise, here's what follows"); both react to a preceding statement, but in opposite logical directions.
- [**にもかかわらず**](grammar-にかかわらず・にもかかわらず.md) — "despite X"; a more formal/written way of expressing the same concessive relationship, often used with a stated fact rather than as a stand-alone reactive conjunction.
- [**でも**](grammar-でも.md) — casual "but"; a simpler, more general contrastive conjunction without それでも's specific "even given that circumstance, still..." nuance.
- [**たところで**](grammar-たところで.md) — "even if X were done, it wouldn't help"; a hypothetical futility construction, whereas それでも affirms that something remains true despite an already-stated fact, rather than downplaying a hypothetical action.

**Opposite grammar structures:** Its natural opposite-category counterpart is それなら, the conditional conjunction: それでも asserts something is true despite the preceding statement (concession), while それなら draws a conclusion that follows from accepting the preceding statement as a premise (condition).

**Example sentences:**
1. 何度失敗しても、それでも彼は諦めなかった。
   (No matter how many times he failed, he still didn't give up.)
2. 周りに反対された。それでも、彼女は留学を決意した。
   (She was opposed by those around her. Even so, she decided to study abroad.)
3. 医者に止められている。それでも、彼は毎朝走りに行く。
   (His doctor has told him to stop. Even then, he goes running every morning.)
4. もう会えないと分かっている。それでも、彼のことを忘れられない。
   (I know we can never meet again. Still, I can't forget him.)
5. 給料は上がらない。それでも、この仕事が好きだから続けている。
   (My salary doesn't go up. Even so, I keep doing this job because I love it.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The most common mistake is confusing それでも with それなら, since both are reactive, sentence-initial conjunctions responding to a preceding statement — but それでも signals concession ("despite that fact, X still holds"), while それなら signals a conditional conclusion ("given that fact, here's what I'd do/suggest"). A sentence like 「忙しい。それでも手伝うよ」("I'm busy. Even so, I'll help.") carries a very different meaning from 「忙しい。それなら手伝おうか?」("You're busy? If that's the case, should I help?") — learners should check whether the second sentence is holding firm against the first (それでも) or responding practically to it as new information (それなら).
