# ～というと／といえば／といったら {#というと・といえば・といったら}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～というと／といえば／といったら

**Furigana form:** ～というと／といえば／といったら (no special kanji readings needed)

**Meaning:** "Speaking of X," "when you mention X," "if we're talking about X" — used to pick up a topic just mentioned (or closely associated) and comment on it, often introducing an association, reminder, or tangent.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + というと／といえば／といったら
- Also usable after plain-form clauses: 動詞普通形 + というと
- 日本の料理というと、やっぱり寿司ですね。(Speaking of Japanese food, sushi is what comes to mind.)

**Typical context:** Conversational, neutral politeness; very common in casual-to-polite spoken Japanese when shifting topics or free-associating. といったら can also carry an exclamatory, emphatic nuance ("you wouldn't believe how X!").

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **といえば** — nearly interchangeable with というと, slightly more common in writing for "speaking of."
- **といったら** — same core meaning but can add emphatic/exclamatory force ("the cold that day — it was something else!").
- **に関して／について** — more neutral/formal "regarding X," lacking the associative "that reminds me" nuance.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 京都というと、やっぱり紅葉がきれいな場所ですね。
   (Speaking of Kyoto, it's really known for its beautiful autumn leaves.)
2. 旅行といえば、去年行ったハワイを思い出す。
   (Speaking of travel, it reminds me of Hawaii last year.)
3. 彼の料理のまずさといったら、もう食べられたものじゃなかった。
   (His cooking was so bad, it was just inedible.)
4. 田中さんといえば、今日は休みだそうですよ。
   (Speaking of Tanaka, I heard he's off today.)
5. 冬の寒さといったら、手足が凍りそうなほどだった。
   (The cold of winter was so intense, it felt like my hands and feet would freeze.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** というと can sometimes introduce a slight question/clarification ("when you say X, do you mean...?"), while といえば is purely associative. といったら's emphatic use (often with negative or extreme adjectives) trips up learners who treat all three as fully interchangeable — they overlap but aren't always 1:1 substitutable.
