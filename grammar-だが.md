# だが {#だが}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** だが (no kanji; always written in kana)

**Furigana form:** だが

**Meaning:** "But, however" — a terse, literary contrastive conjunction.

**Conjugation:** Sentence-initial conjunction, no conjugation needed — attaches at the sentence boundary, connecting the previous sentence to the next.

**Typical context:** Literary/written register, terse and clipped in tone. Common in novels, essays, newspaper columns, and narration; also associated with blunt, assertive male speech patterns in dialogue (e.g. in fiction or informal-authoritative spoken contexts). Not used in polite conversation — it can sound curt or overly direct if spoken casually in everyday conversation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**だけど**](grammar-だけど.md) — very casual spoken "but"; だが is its blunt written/literary cousin, not interchangeable in register.
- [**でも**](grammar-でも.md) — casual spoken "but"; much softer and more conversational than だが.
- [**けれども**](grammar-けれども.md) — polite-neutral, spoken/written, softer and more polite than だが.
- [**しかし**](grammar-しかし.md) — standard written/formal "however"; しかし is more neutral and widely acceptable in formal writing, while だが is terser and carries a more narrative/assertive feel.
- [**それなのに**](grammar-それなのに.md) — adds emotional surprise/frustration nuance; だが is more matter-of-fact and dry by comparison.
- [**一方**](grammar-一方.md) — contrasts two parallel facts structurally; だが directly negates/counters the prior statement instead.

**Opposite grammar structures:** そして/また (additive conjunctions) oppose だが structurally as continuation rather than contrast.

**Example sentences:**
1. 努力はした。だが、結果は出なかった。
   (I made the effort. But the results didn't show.)
2. 彼は天才だ。だが、努力を怠らない。
   (He is a genius. But he never neglects hard work.)
3. 雨は止んだ。だが、空はまだ暗かった。
   (The rain stopped. But the sky was still dark.)
4. 計画は完璧だった。だが、実行する者がいなかった。
   (The plan was perfect. But there was no one to carry it out.)
5. 彼女は若い。だが、その判断力は大人顔負けだ。
   (She is young. But her judgment rivals that of an adult.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes use だが in polite conversation, where it can sound abrupt or overly blunt — しかし or けれども would be safer choices in formal spoken contexts, and だけど/でも in casual ones. だが is best reserved for writing, narration, or quoting assertive/dry speech. Don't confuse its terse, dry tone with それなのに's emotionally charged "and yet" nuance — だが is neutral-blunt, not emotional.
