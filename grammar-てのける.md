# ～てのける {#てのける}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～てのける

**Furigana form:** ～てのける

**Meaning:** "To manage to do ~ (impressively/surprisingly, without apparent difficulty)" — describes accomplishing something difficult, bold, or unexpected with surprising ease, often impressing or surprising onlookers.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞て形 (verb te-form) + のける
- Example: 5歳の弟はわたしや兄が解けなかった数学の問題をたった1分でやってのけた。 (My 5-year-old brother solved, in just one minute, the math problem that neither my older brother nor I could solve.)

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-literary written and spoken Japanese; often used in narrative, biographical, or admiring descriptions of someone accomplishing something remarkable.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **やり遂げる** — "to accomplish/complete ~ (through perseverance)" — emphasizes completing something difficult through effort and determination, rather than emphasizing ease and surprise as てのける does.
- **あっさり～する** — "to do ~ easily/effortlessly" — conveys similar ease, but as a plain adverb + verb rather than a fixed grammatical compound, and lacks the connotation of surprising others.
- **平然と～する** — "to do ~ calmly/nonchalantly" — emphasizes composure while doing something, often overlapping with てのける when the difficulty is also implied.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though 四苦八苦する ("to struggle desperately") contrasts in spirit by implying great difficulty rather than effortless success.

**Example sentences:**
1. 5歳の弟はわたしや兄が解けなかった数学の問題をたった1分でやってのけた。
   (My 5-year-old brother solved, in just one minute, the math problem that neither my older brother nor I could solve.)
2. 彼女は誰もが無理だと思っていた企画を見事にやってのけた。
   (She brilliantly pulled off the project that everyone thought was impossible.)
3. 新人なのに、彼は難しい交渉をあっさりまとめてのけた。
   (Despite being a newcomer, he effortlessly wrapped up the difficult negotiation.)
4. そのピアニストは超難曲を一度の練習だけで弾いてのけた。
   (That pianist managed to play the extremely difficult piece after just one practice session.)
5. 彼は周囲の反対を押し切り、大胆な改革をやってのけた。
   (He pushed through everyone's opposition and pulled off a bold reform.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** てのける always implies the task was difficult or risky, yet accomplished with surprising ease or boldness — learners should avoid using it for ordinary, simple tasks, since the pattern inherently conveys admiration or surprise at overcoming real difficulty.
