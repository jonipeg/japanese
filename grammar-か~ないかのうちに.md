# ～か～ないかのうちに {#か~ないかのうちに}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～か～ないかのうちに

**Furigana form:** ～か～ないかのうちに

**Meaning:** "Almost before X happens / just as X is happening" — expresses extreme near-simultaneity between two events, stronger than ordinary "as soon as."

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形／た形 + か + 同じ動詞のない形 + かのうちに
- 見るか見ないかのうちに — "almost before one could see it"
- 鳴るか鳴らないかのうちに — "almost before it rang"

**Typical context:** Written, literary/formal narrative register — used to describe rapid sequences in stories, news reports, and descriptive writing. Overuse in casual speech sounds stilted.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **（か）と思うと／思ったら** — similar quick succession, but slightly less emphatic on exact simultaneity.
- **や否や** — "the moment that," also formal/literary, with a nuance of one action immediately triggering the next.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. ベルが鳴るか鳴らないかのうちに、生徒たちは教室を飛び出した。
   (Almost before the bell rang, the students dashed out of the classroom.)
2. 彼は席に着くか着かないかのうちに、質問を始めた。
   (He'd barely sat down before he started asking questions.)
3. 目を閉じるか閉じないかのうちに、眠ってしまった。
   (I fell asleep almost the instant I closed my eyes.)
4. 試合が終わるか終わらないかのうちに、観客は帰り始めた。
   (Almost before the match ended, the spectators started leaving.)
5. 雨が止むか止まないかのうちに、また降り出した。
   (Almost as soon as the rain stopped, it started again.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The same verb must be repeated in affirmative then negative form — learners sometimes mistakenly substitute a different verb for the second half. This is a formal/literary pattern; it sounds unnatural if used too casually in everyday speech.
