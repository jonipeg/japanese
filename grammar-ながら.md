# ～ながら {#ながら}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ながら

**Furigana form:** ～ながら (no special kanji readings needed)

**Meaning:** (1) "While doing X" — two actions performed simultaneously by the same subject. (2) "Although/even though X" (concessive, often ながらも) — expresses a contrast between a state and an unexpected outcome.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ます形(stem) + ながら: 音楽を聞きながら勉強する (study while listening to music)
- い形容詞／な形容詞／名詞 + ながら(も) (concessive): 小さいながらも立派な家 (a small but splendid house); 知っていながら黙っていた (he knew, yet stayed silent)

**Typical context:** Neutral register, both spoken and written; the simultaneous-action sense is extremely common in daily conversation, while the concessive ながら(も) sense is more common in written/formal contexts.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**つつ**](grammar-つつ.md) — near-synonym for simultaneous action, more literary/formal; also used concessively like ながら(も).
- **一方で** — "on the other hand/while," for contrasting two separate facts rather than one subject's simultaneous actions.
- **のに** — concessive "even though," but carries more surprise/complaint nuance than ながら(も).

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. テレビを見ながら、晩ご飯を食べた。
   (I ate dinner while watching TV.)
2. 彼は歩きながらスマホを見ている。
   (He's looking at his phone while walking.)
3. 狭いながらも、居心地のいい部屋だった。
   (Although it was small, it was a cozy room.)
4. 悪いと知りながら、つい嘘をついてしまった。
   (Knowing it was wrong, I still ended up lying.)
5. 彼女は忙しいながらも、毎日運動を続けている。
   (Even though she's busy, she keeps exercising every day.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often confuse the simultaneous-action sense (requires the same subject for both actions) with ながら's concessive use — context and the presence of も (often added) signal the concessive reading. Also note: in the simultaneous sense, the main action is the second verb, and ながら attaches to the stem (ます形), not the dictionary form.
