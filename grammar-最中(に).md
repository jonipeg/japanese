# ～最中(に) {#最中に}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～最中(に)

**Furigana form:** ～さいちゅう(に)

**Meaning:** "Right in the middle of (doing) X" — emphasizes that an action/event is at its peak or most intense ongoing point, often used to show that something else (often unexpected/interrupting) happens during that point.

**Conjugation:**
- Verb (ている form, dropping いる) + 最中に
- 名詞 + の最中に
- 会議の最中に (right in the middle of the meeting)
- 食事をしている最中に (right while in the middle of eating)

**Typical context:** Spoken and written; often paired with an interrupting event (電話が鳴った, 地震が起きた, etc.) to emphasize bad timing.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **ているところに** — very similar "right at the moment of doing X," interchangeable in many cases but ているところ is slightly more neutral/less intense.
- **間に** — "during the interval of X," broader time span, not necessarily the peak moment.
- [**うちに**](grammar-うちに.md) — "while X (before it changes)," different nuance focused on a window of opportunity.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 会議の最中に、突然電話が鳴った。
   (Right in the middle of the meeting, the phone suddenly rang.)
2. お風呂に入っている最中に、来客があった。
   (Right while I was in the bath, a visitor came.)
3. 試験の最中に、気分が悪くなってしまった。
   (Right in the middle of the exam, I started feeling unwell.)
4. 大事な話をしている最中に、子供が割り込んできた。
   (Right in the middle of an important conversation, the child interrupted.)
5. 料理をしている最中に、火災報知器が鳴り出した。
   (Right in the middle of cooking, the fire alarm went off.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 最中 stresses the peak/climax of an ongoing action, usually followed by a sudden interrupting event — using it for a calm, non-interrupted continuation feels unnatural. Also note the reading さいちゅう (not さいなか), a common misreading point.
