# ～とて {#とて}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～とて

**Furigana form:** ～とて

**Meaning:** "Even if ~ / even though ~" — a firm, literary concessive expression indicating that even given a certain condition or excuse, the expected outcome still won't follow. Carries a somewhat old-fashioned, dramatic nuance.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞た形/普通形 (verb past/plain form) + とて
- 名詞 (noun) + とて
- Example: 今さら謝ったとて、許してもらえないだろう。 (Even if you apologize now, you probably won't be forgiven.)

**Typical context:** Literary written Japanese; found in novels, essays, speeches, and old-fashioned or dramatic spoken Japanese. Rare in everyday conversation; signals a formal or classical tone.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**ても**](grammar-たとえ~ても.md) — "even if ~" — the standard, everyday equivalent of とて, usable in all registers; とて is simply its literary/archaic counterpart.
- [**たところで**](grammar-たところで.md) — "even if ~ (it won't amount to anything)" — very similar nuance of futility, slightly more common in modern spoken Japanese than とて.
- [**であれ**](grammar-であれ・であろうと.md) — "even if it be ~" — another literary concessive, often used for naming any case/example (誰であれ), rather than a specific hypothetical action.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though だからこそ ("precisely because of that") contrasts in spirit by affirming a strong causal connection rather than denying expected consequences.

**Example sentences:**
1. 今さら謝ったとて、許してもらえないだろう。
   (Even if you apologize now, you probably won't be forgiven.)
2. いくら後悔したとて、過ぎたことは取り戻せない。
   (No matter how much you regret it, what's past cannot be undone.)
3. 金持ちだとて、幸せとは限らない。
   (Even being rich doesn't necessarily mean being happy.)
4. どんなに努力したとて、生まれ持った才能には勝てないこともある。
   (No matter how hard you try, there are times you can't surpass natural talent.)
5. 子供とて、自分の意見を持つ権利はある。
   (Even a child has the right to hold their own opinions.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes mistake とて for a casual contraction of と言って; in fact it is a classical/literary concessive particle equivalent to ても, and using it in casual conversation can sound oddly formal or archaic — modern spoken Japanese favors ても or たところで instead.
