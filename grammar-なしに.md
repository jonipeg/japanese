# ～なしに／なしに(は)～ない {#なしに}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～無しに／無しに（は）～ない

**Furigana form:** ～なしに／なしに（は）～ない

**Meaning:** "Without doing/having X" — describes performing an action without a preceding step or accompanying element; the ～なしには～ない variant emphasizes that without X, the stated outcome is difficult or impossible.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + なしに（＋動詞）
- 名詞 + なしには + ～ない／～がたい／～難しい
- Example base: 許可 → 許可なしに／許可なしには出入りできない

**Typical context:** Formal, written register; common in official notices, rules, and formal explanatory writing. More formal than the everyday ないで.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **ないで** — plain "without doing X"; the everyday, casual-to-neutral equivalent, usable in any register, while なしに is distinctly more formal/written.
- **ずに** — a more literary negative connector ("without doing X"), similar register level to なしに, but ずに attaches to a verb stem while なしに attaches to a noun.
- **なくして（は）** — "without X" (often in the stronger ～なくしては～ない emphatic form); very close in meaning and register, largely interchangeable with なしに(は), though なくして leans slightly more toward abstract/emotional conditions (愛なくしては生きられない) while なしに often appears with concrete procedural nouns (許可, 連絡, 予告).

**Opposite grammar structures:** ～を伴って／～を得て ("accompanied by X / having obtained X") expresses the reverse — doing something with the necessary element present.

**Example sentences:**
1. 事前の連絡なしに訪問するのは失礼だ。 (Visiting without prior contact is rude.)
2. 許可なしにはこの施設に立ち入ることはできません。 (Without permission, you cannot enter this facility.)
3. 彼は何の説明もなしに、突然会社を辞めてしまった。 (He suddenly quit the company without any explanation at all.)
4. 努力なしに成功を手に入れることはできない。 (You cannot achieve success without effort.)
5. 予告なしに価格が変更される場合がございます。 (Prices may be changed without prior notice.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** なしに attaches directly to a noun (not a verb), so learners sometimes mistakenly try to attach it to a verb phrase as if it were ないで — remember to nominalize first if needed. The emphatic なしには～ない variant specifically stresses that the stated outcome genuinely depends on the missing element, giving it a stronger "impossible without X" nuance than the plain なしに, which simply describes an accompanying absence.
