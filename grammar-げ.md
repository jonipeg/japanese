# ～げ {#げ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～げ

**Furigana form:** ～げ

**Meaning:** "Looking/seeming X" — attached to adjective stems to describe an outward appearance or impression that someone or something seems to have a certain quality, typically based on visual observation.

**Conjugation:**
- い形容詞語幹（drop い）+ げ
- な形容詞語幹 + げ
- 嬉しげ (looking happy), 寂しげ (looking lonely), 不安げ (looking anxious), 意味ありげ (looking meaningful/significant)

**Typical context:** Written, literary register — common in novels and descriptive writing. Less common in everyday casual speech, where そう（様態）is the more natural colloquial equivalent.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **そうだ（様態）** — "looks like / seems," the common colloquial equivalent (嬉しそう vs. 嬉しげ), far more frequent in spoken Japanese.
- [**っぽい**](grammar-っぽい.md) — "-ish, has the quality of," broader and more casual, not limited to appearance-based impressions.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女は何か言いたげな顔をしていた。
   (She had a look on her face as if she wanted to say something.)
2. 子供は自慢げにおもちゃを見せた。
   (The child showed off his toy proudly.)
3. 彼は不安げに周りを見回した。
   (He looked around anxiously.)
4. その老人は寂しげに窓の外を眺めていた。
   (The old man gazed out the window with a lonely look.)
5. 彼女は意味ありげな笑みを浮かべた。
   (She wore a meaningful-looking smile.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** げ is more literary/written than そう and sounds unnatural in casual spoken conversation, where そう is strongly preferred. Don't confuse げ (an appearance-based impression attached to adjective stems) with 気味 (a slight tendency) — they can overlap in translation but differ in nuance and register.
