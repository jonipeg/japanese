## 独創 (どくそう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** originality, a unique/original idea

**Similar words:**
- [独自](vocab-29.md#独自-どくじ)（どくじ）— "one's own/original," broader and more commonly used adjectivally; 独創 emphasizes creative originality, especially in art/invention (独創的な作品, "an original creative work").
- 創造（そうぞう）— "creation," the broader act of creating something new, while 独創 specifically emphasizes the originality/uniqueness of that creation.

**Opposite words:**
- 模倣（もほう）— imitation, copying

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼の作品には独創性が感じられる。— His work conveys a sense of originality.
2. 独創的なアイデアを評価された。— His original idea was highly regarded.
3. 独創と模倣の違いについて議論した。— We discussed the difference between originality and imitation.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 独創 (often as 独創的/独創性) is frequently contrasted with 模倣 (imitation) in discussions of art, invention, and creativity — a pairing worth remembering for essay/discussion contexts.

## 特定 (とくてい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** specific, particular; to specify/identify

**Similar words:**
- 具体的（ぐたいてき）— "concrete/specific," emphasizes being detailed and tangible rather than abstract, a different nuance from 特定's "a particular one, as opposed to general."
- 明確（めいかく）— "clear/definite," emphasizes clarity of expression rather than singling out a particular item.

**Opposite words:**
- 一般的（いっぱんてき）— general

**Example sentences:**
1. 犯人が特定された。— The culprit was identified.
2. 特定の条件を満たす必要がある。— You need to meet specific conditions.
3. 原因を特定するのは難しい。— It's difficult to pinpoint the cause.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 特定 functions both as a na-adjective/noun ("specific/particular") and a suru-verb ("to identify/pinpoint," as in 犯人を特定する) — learners often only learn one of these two uses.

## 特典 (とくてん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a special privilege, perk, bonus

**Similar words:**
- 特権（とっけん）— "a privilege," often implies a more formal/structural advantage (e.g., legal or social privilege), whereas 特典 usually refers to a commercial perk or bonus (e.g., member benefits).
- [割引](vocab-36.md#割引-わりびき)（わりびき）— "a discount," a specific type of 特典 related to price reduction.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 会員になると特典が受けられる。— If you become a member, you get special privileges.
2. 予約特典として限定グッズがもらえる。— As a reservation bonus, you get limited-edition goods.
3. このカードには様々な特典がついている。— This card comes with various perks.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 特典 is heavily used in commercial/marketing contexts (membership perks, preorder bonuses) — distinct from the more formal/legal connotation of 特権.

## 独特 (どくとく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** characteristic, peculiar, distinctive

**Similar words:**
- [独自](vocab-29.md#独自-どくじ)（どくじ）— "one's own/original," emphasizes ownership/non-borrowed nature, whereas 独特 emphasizes a distinctive quality/flavor that sets something apart (often sensory, like taste or style).
- 特有（とくゆう）— "peculiar to/unique to," very close in meaning, often used for traits specific to a species, culture, or group.

**Opposite words:**
- 普通（ふつう）— ordinary, common

**Example sentences:**
1. このチーズには独特の匂いがある。— This cheese has a distinctive smell.
2. 彼女は独特の雰囲気を持っている。— She has a distinctive atmosphere/vibe.
3. 独特な表現方法で評判になった。— He became well-known for his distinctive style of expression.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 独特 often describes sensory or stylistic distinctiveness (smell, atmosphere, style) rather than intellectual originality — don't conflate with 独創, which specifically means creative originality.

## 特に (とくに)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** especially, particularly

**Similar words:**
- 特別に（とくべつに）— "specially," used for treating something as an exception or giving special treatment, slightly different nuance from the emphasis-adding 特に.
- とりわけ — "above all, among others," a more literary/emphatic synonym for 特に.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 夏は特に暑い日が続く。— In summer, especially hot days continue.
2. このレストランは特にデザートがおいしい。— At this restaurant, the desserts are especially delicious.
3. 特に問題はありません。— There's nothing in particular wrong.
 
**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 特に is an extremely common adverb used both to emphasize a point positively ("especially good") and in negation to mean "nothing in particular" (特に問題ない) — a dual pattern learners should memorize.

## 特派員 (とくはいん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a correspondent (news reporter stationed abroad)

**Similar words:**
- 記者（きしゃ）— "a journalist/reporter" in general, broader than 特派員, which specifically implies being dispatched to a foreign location.
- 駐在員（ちゅうざいいん）— "a resident representative/expatriate staff," used for business personnel stationed abroad, a parallel concept in a corporate (not journalistic) context.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼はパリ駐在の特派員だ。— He is a correspondent stationed in Paris.
2. 現地の特派員が最新情報を伝えた。— The on-the-ground correspondent reported the latest information.
3. 特派員として海外に派遣された。— He was dispatched abroad as a correspondent.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 特派員 specifically refers to journalists stationed overseas for their news organization — a general local reporter would just be called 記者, not 特派員.

## 取り替える (とりかえる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to exchange something for another, to replace

**Similar words:**
- 交換する（こうかんする）— "to exchange," more formal/neutral, often used in business or mutual-exchange contexts (交換留学, exchange student), whereas 取り替える is more everyday/concrete (replacing a part, swapping items).
- 替える（かえる）— a shorter, more casual synonym often used interchangeably with 取り替える in speech.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 電球を新しいものに取り替えた。— I replaced the lightbulb with a new one.
2. タイヤを取り替える必要がある。— The tires need to be replaced.
3. 席を取り替えてもらえますか。— Could you swap seats with me?

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 取り替える implies physically swapping one concrete item for another (parts, seats, clothes) — for abstract exchanges (ideas, currency), 交換する is more natural.

## 取り消す (とりけす)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to cancel, withdraw, revoke

**Similar words:**
- キャンセルする — the loanword, used casually especially for reservations/appointments, almost interchangeable with 取り消す in everyday contexts.
- 撤回する（てっかいする）— "to retract/withdraw," more formal, often used for statements or official decisions (発言を撤回する, "to retract a statement").

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 予約を取り消した。— I canceled the reservation.
2. 発言を取り消してほしい。— I want you to retract that statement.
3. 契約を取り消すことができますか。— Can the contract be canceled?

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 取り消す works for both everyday cancellations (reservations, orders) and more formal retractions (statements, contracts, licenses) — in very formal/written contexts, 撤回する may sound more appropriate for retracting words.

## 取り組む (とりくむ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to grapple with, tackle, work hard at (a task/problem)

**Similar words:**
- [取り掛かる](#取り掛かる-とりかかる)（とりかかる）— "to set about/begin working on" something, emphasizes the starting point rather than the ongoing effort implied by 取り組む.
- 努力する（どりょくする）— "to make an effort," broader and more about general diligence, whereas 取り組む implies engaging directly and specifically with a particular task/issue.

**Opposite words:**
- 放置する（ほうちする）— to neglect, leave unattended

**Example sentences:**
1. 環境問題に真剣に取り組んでいる。— We are seriously tackling environmental issues.
2. 彼は新しいプロジェクトに取り組んでいる。— He is working hard on a new project.
3. 課題に取り組む姿勢が大切だ。— The attitude of tackling challenges is important.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 取り組む strongly implies sustained, dedicated engagement with a problem or task (often serious issues like 環境問題) — don't use it for trivial, momentary actions.

## 取り締まる (とりしまる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to manage, control, regulate, crack down on

**Similar words:**
- 規制する（きせいする）— "to regulate," more formal/legal, emphasizing the establishment of rules, whereas 取り締まる emphasizes active enforcement/policing of those rules.
- 監視する（かんしする）— "to monitor/watch," emphasizes observation rather than active enforcement/crackdown.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 警察が違法駐車を取り締まっている。— The police are cracking down on illegal parking.
2. 密売を取り締まるための法律がある。— There's a law to crack down on illegal trading.
3. もっと厳しく取り締まるべきだ。— It should be regulated/enforced more strictly.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 取り締まる strongly implies active law enforcement or policing (police cracking down on violations), more concrete than the abstract rule-making sense of 規制する.

## 取り調べる (とりしらべる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to investigate, examine, interrogate

**Similar words:**
- 調査する（ちょうさする）— "to investigate/survey," a more neutral and general term for investigation (research, surveys), whereas 取り調べる specifically implies official/police interrogation of a suspect.
- 尋問する（じんもんする）— "to interrogate/question," closely related and often used interchangeably in legal/criminal contexts, slightly more formal.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 警察が容疑者を取り調べている。— The police are interrogating the suspect.
2. 事件の詳細を取り調べる必要がある。— It's necessary to investigate the details of the incident.
3. 長時間にわたって取り調べを受けた。— He was interrogated for a long time.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 取り調べる carries a strong legal/criminal investigation connotation (used for police interrogating suspects) — using it for a casual "let me look into this" would sound overly dramatic; 調べる alone is more neutral.

## 取り入れる (とりいれる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to take in, incorporate, introduce, adopt

**Similar words:**
- 導入する（どうにゅうする）— "to introduce (a system/technology)," more formal and institutional, whereas 取り入れる can apply to smaller-scale, everyday adoption of ideas/habits.
- 採用する（さいようする）— "to adopt/employ," often used for adopting proposals or hiring people, a related but distinct formal usage.

**Opposite words:**
- 排除する（はいじょする）— to exclude, eliminate

**Example sentences:**
1. 新しい技術を取り入れる。— We incorporate new technology.
2. 彼のアイデアを積極的に取り入れた。— We actively adopted his ideas.
3. 洗濯物を取り入れるのを忘れた。— I forgot to bring in the laundry.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 取り入れる has a surprisingly literal everyday meaning too — "to bring something in" (like laundry from outside, 洗濯物を取り入れる) — in addition to the figurative "adopt/incorporate" sense, which learners often miss.

## 取り扱う (とりあつかう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to treat, handle, deal with (goods, matters, people)

**Similar words:**
- [扱う](vocab-03.md#扱う-あつかう)（あつかう）— the shorter, plain form, nearly synonymous and very commonly used interchangeably with 取り扱う, which is slightly more formal/written.
- 処理する（しょりする）— "to process/deal with," more about administratively handling tasks or data, rather than physically handling goods or interpersonally handling people.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. この店では輸入食品を取り扱っている。— This shop deals in imported food products.
2. 壊れ物なので丁寧に取り扱ってください。— Please handle it carefully, as it's fragile.
3. 個人情報は慎重に取り扱う必要がある。— Personal information must be handled carefully.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 取り扱う is often seen on signage/labels (取扱注意, "handle with care") and in business contexts (取り扱う商品, "products handled/sold") — in casual speech, 扱う alone is more common.

## 取り上げる (とりあげる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to pick up, take away, confiscate; to take up (a topic)

**Similar words:**
- 没収する（ぼっしゅうする）— "to confiscate," a more formal/legal term specifically for seizing property, a narrower meaning than 取り上げる's broader "take away."
- [取り扱う](#取り扱う-とりあつかう)（とりあつかう）— "to handle/deal with," different in that it's about treatment/management rather than physically removing something or spotlighting a topic.

**Opposite words:**
- 与える（あたえる）— to give

**Example sentences:**
1. 先生が漫画を取り上げた。— The teacher confiscated the manga.
2. そのニュースは多くのメディアに取り上げられた。— That news was picked up/covered by many media outlets.
3. 受話器を取り上げて電話に出た。— I picked up the receiver and answered the phone.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 取り上げる has three distinct uses: physically picking something up, confiscating/taking away, and media "covering/featuring" a topic (そのニュースが取り上げられた) — the media sense often confuses learners who only know the "confiscate" meaning.

## 取り返す (とりかえす)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to take back, recover, regain

**Similar words:**
- 取り戻す（とりもどす）— nearly synonymous, "to get back/regain," very commonly interchangeable with 取り返す, perhaps slightly more common in everyday speech.
- 回復する（かいふくする）— "to recover," broader and more abstract, often used for health or economic recovery rather than physically taking back a specific item.

**Opposite words:**
- [失う](vocab-08.md#失う-うしなう)（うしなう）— to lose

**Example sentences:**
1. 盗まれたお金を取り返した。— I got back the stolen money.
2. 失った信頼を取り返すのは簡単ではない。— Regaining lost trust isn't easy.
3. 遅れを取り返すために頑張った。— I worked hard to make up for the delay.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 取り返す often appears in the set phrase 遅れを取り返す ("to make up for lost time/delay") in addition to the literal "take back" sense — nearly interchangeable with 取り戻す in most contexts.

## 取り掛かる (とりかかる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to set about, embark on, start working on

**Similar words:**
- [始める](vocab-31.md#始める-はじめる)（はじめる）— the plain, general verb "to begin/start," usable for anything, whereas 取り掛かる specifically implies starting a task or piece of work.
- 着手する（ちゃくしゅする）— "to commence/undertake," a more formal/written synonym, often used in business or official reports.

**Opposite words:**
- [終える](vocab-09.md#終える-おえる)（おえる）— to finish

**Example sentences:**
1. さっそく仕事に取り掛かった。— I got right down to work.
2. 新しいプロジェクトに取り掛かる準備ができた。— I'm ready to start on the new project.
3. 食事の支度に取り掛かろう。— Let's get started on preparing the meal.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 取り掛かる specifically marks the beginning of engaging with a task (often with さっそく, "right away") — it's not used for starting abstract states or events unrelated to tasks/work.

## 鳥 (とり)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** bird

**Similar words:**
- 鶏（にわとり）— specifically "chicken," a subset of 鳥 (birds in general).
- 鳥類（ちょうるい）— "birds" as a biological/taxonomic category, more formal/scientific than the everyday word 鳥.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 空を鳥が飛んでいる。— A bird is flying in the sky.
2. 鳥のさえずりが聞こえる。— I can hear birds chirping.
3. 彼は鳥を飼っている。— He keeps a pet bird.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** In casual speech, 鳥 often specifically implies chicken meat in culinary contexts (鳥肉, "chicken meat," sometimes just called 鳥) — context (restaurant menu vs. nature talk) determines the intended meaning.

## 取り柄 (とりえ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a redeeming feature, good point, strong point

**Similar words:**
- [長所](vocab-27.md#長所-ちょうしょ)（ちょうしょ）— "a strong point/merit," a more neutral and common term for someone's good qualities, often used in formal contexts like job interviews.
- 美点（びてん）— "a virtue/merit," more literary and formal, emphasizing moral or aesthetic goodness.

- [欠点](vocab-02.md#欠点-けってん)（けってん）— a fault, weak point

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 取り柄 is often used self-deprecatingly in the phrase 取り柄がない ("I have no redeeming features") — a humble/modest expression distinct from the more neutral, résumé-style 長所.

## 取り締まり (とりしまり)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** control, crackdown, regulation (esp. of crime/traffic)

**Similar words:**
- 規制（きせい）— regulation/restriction in general (laws, rules); 取り締まり is specifically the act of enforcing/cracking down.
- [監視](vocab-13.md#監視-かんし)（かんし）— surveillance/monitoring; watching for violations, not necessarily acting on them.
- [管理](vocab-14.md#管理-かんり)（かんり）— management/control in a broader administrative sense, lacks the law-enforcement nuance.

**Opposite words:**
- [放置](vocab-03.md#放置-ほうち)（ほうち）— neglect, leaving unregulated
- 野放し（のばなし）— letting run wild, unchecked

**Example sentences:**
1. 警察は飲酒運転の取り締まりを強化した。— The police strengthened the crackdown on drunk driving.
2. この道路はスピード違反の取り締まりが厳しい。— This road has strict enforcement against speeding.
3. 違法駐車の取り締まりが始まってから、路上駐車が減った。— Since the crackdown on illegal parking began, street parking has decreased.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Often used as 〜の取り締まり (crackdown on X); it implies active enforcement by authorities, not just having a rule on paper. Don't confuse with 取り扱い (handling/treatment of something).

## 取れる (とれる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to come off, to be removable, to be obtainable/available

**Similar words:**
- 外れる（はずれる）— to come off/detach, more mechanical (a button, a part); 取れる is broader and can mean "to be caught/obtained" too.
- 抜ける（ぬける）— to come out/fall out (hair, a nail); implies something pulling free from inside.
- 取る（とる）— the transitive counterpart "to take/remove"; 取れる is the intransitive/potential form.

**Opposite words:**
- 付く（つく）— to attach, stick on
- [取り付ける](#取り付ける-とりつける)（とりつける）— to fix, install

**Example sentences:**
1. シャツのボタンが取れてしまった。— The button on my shirt came off.
2. このシミはなかなか取れない。— This stain just won't come out.
3. 来月なら休みが取れると思う。— I think I can get time off next month.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 取れる is the potential/spontaneous form of 取る and covers many senses: stains/dirt coming off, fish being caught, reservations being obtained, pain going away (痛みが取れる). Learners often only learn the "comes off" sense and miss the "can obtain" sense.

## 泥 (どろ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** mud, dirt, mire

**Similar words:**
- [土](vocab-28.md#土-つち)（つち）— plain soil/earth, dry; 泥 specifically implies wet, sticky dirt.
- [汚れ](vocab-36.md#汚れ-よごれ)（よごれ）— general dirt/stains, not specifically mud.
- ぬかるみ — a muddy, slushy patch of ground; a specific muddy spot rather than the substance itself.

**Opposite words:**
- [清潔](vocab-22.md#清潔-せいけつ)（せいけつ）— cleanliness
- 砂（すな）— sand (dry, fine particles, contrast to wet mud)

**Example sentences:**
1. 雨で道が泥だらけになった。— The road became covered in mud from the rain.
2. 子供たちは泥んこになって遊んでいた。— The kids were playing and got covered in mud.
3. 靴に泥がついて洗うのが大変だった。— Mud got on my shoes and it was a pain to wash off.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Appears in idioms like 泥沼（どろぬま、a quagmire/hopeless situation) and 顔に泥を塗る (to disgrace someone, lit. "smear mud on someone's face"). Don't confuse 泥 with 土 — 泥 is always wet/sticky.

## とれーにんぐ
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** training (physical or skills practice)

**Similar words:**
- [練習](vocab-36.md#練習-れんしゅう)（れんしゅう）— practice/drill, more general and often academic or skill-based; トレーニング leans toward physical/athletic conditioning.
- [訓練](vocab-17.md#訓練する-くんれん)（くんれん）— training in a formal/professional or military sense.
- [稽古](vocab-17.md#稽古-けいこ)（けいこ）— traditional practice (martial arts, tea ceremony), more disciplined/cultural connotation.

**Opposite words:**
- [休養](vocab-15.md#休養-きゅうよう)（きゅうよう）— rest, recuperation

**Example sentences:**
1. 毎朝ジムでトレーニングをしている。— I train at the gym every morning.
2. 彼はマラソンのために厳しいトレーニングを続けている。— He keeps up tough training for the marathon.
3. 新入社員向けのトレーニングが来週始まる。— Training for new employees starts next week.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** トレーニング usually implies physical exercise/conditioning; for skill-based or academic practice, 練習 is more natural. Often used as トレーニングする or combined with words like 筋力トレーニング (strength training, often shortened to 筋トレ).

## 努力 (どりょく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** effort, endeavor

**Similar words:**
- 頑張り（がんばり）— effort/persistence in a more casual, encouraging sense; 努力 is more formal and often used in writing.
- 尽力（じんりょく）— exerting all one's effort/energy for someone else's benefit, more formal and other-directed.
- 奮闘（ふんとう）— struggling hard against difficulty, implies a tougher situation.

**Opposite words:**
- [怠慢](vocab-25.md#怠慢-たいまん)（たいまん）— negligence, laziness
- 手抜き（てぬき）— cutting corners

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼の努力が実を結び、試験に合格した。— His efforts bore fruit, and he passed the exam.
2. 成功するには継続的な努力が必要だ。— Continuous effort is necessary to succeed.
3. どんなに努力しても結果が出ないこともある。— Sometimes no matter how much effort you put in, results don't come.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Commonly used as 努力する (suru verb) or 努力家 (a hard-working person). It emphasizes sustained, often unglamorous work toward a goal, distinct from 頑張る which can describe a single burst of effort in the moment.

## 取り引き (とりひき)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** transaction, dealings, trade

- 急に（きゅうに）— also "suddenly," slightly more casual/conversational than 突然, which feels a bit more formal/written.
- いきなり — "suddenly, out of the blue," often with a nuance of being unexpected or even rude/abrupt in manner.
- 不意に（ふいに）— "unexpectedly," more literary, emphasizes catching someone off guard.

**Opposite words:**
- [徐々に](vocab-21.md#徐々に-じょじょに)（じょじょに）— gradually

**Example sentences:**

2. 彼は突然会社を辞めると言い出した。— He suddenly announced he was quitting the company.
3. 突然の知らせに驚いた。— I was shocked by the sudden news.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 突然 is the most neutral/all-purpose "suddenly" and works well in both speech and writing; いきなり can carry a slightly more negative/abrupt nuance (rudeness, lack of warning), while 突然 is more neutral-descriptive.
