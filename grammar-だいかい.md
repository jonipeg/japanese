# ～だい？／かい？ {#だいかい}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～だい？／かい？ *(no kanji; particle usage)*

**Furigana form:** ～だい？／かい？

**Meaning:** A casual, somewhat old-fashioned masculine sentence-final question particle — だい follows a noun/na-adjective predicate ("what time is it?"), while かい attaches more broadly for yes/no or softened questions.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞／な形容詞語幹 + だい (replacing plain だ in a question)
- 普通形 + かい (added after plain form, including verbs/i-adjectives)
- Example base: 何時 → 何時だい？ / 行く → 行くかい？

**Typical context:** Spoken, casual, distinctly masculine register, often associated with older men or a slightly old-fashioned/paternal speech style (seen in fiction/anime depicting older characters). Not gender-neutral and largely obsolete in everyday young speech.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **の？** — the standard casual question particle usable by any speaker; だい/かい are a more old-fashioned, masculine-coded alternative with the same basic question function.
- **かしら** — a feminine-coded sentence-final particle expressing wondering ("I wonder..."); stylistically parallel to だい/かい as a gendered speech marker, but かしら is feminine-coded and softer/more musing rather than a direct question.
- **の／のか** — neutral plain-form question markers; だい/かい add an extra layer of old-fashioned, familiar masculine color on top of the same basic question function.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a formal/neutral question with か or ですか would be the register-opposite of this casual masculine form.

**Example sentences:**
1. 今、何時だい？ (What time is it now?)
2. 君はどこへ行くんだい？ (Where are you off to?)
3. それは本当かい？ (Is that really true?)
4. もう宿題は終わったのかい？ (Have you finished your homework already?)
5. 一緒に来るかい？ (Will you come along with me?)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** だい/かい are strongly gendered and dated — using them in modern casual conversation (especially for learners, regardless of gender) can sound theatrical, like imitating an old man from a novel, rather than natural contemporary speech. They are mainly useful for recognizing this register in fiction, period dramas, or older literature, not for active daily use. Don't confuse だい with だ + い as separate pieces; it's a fused question particle, not だ plus another element.
