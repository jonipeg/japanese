# ～ではあるまいし {#ではあるまいし}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～ではあるまいし

**Furigana form:** ～ではあるまいし

**Meaning:** "It's not as if X" — points out that since the situation is clearly not X, the reaction or behavior under discussion doesn't make sense; often used to criticize or give advice. Spoken-language style.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + ではあるまいし
- 子供ではあるまいし — "it's not as if [you're] a child"
- Also appears with the plain form as ～わけではあるまいし／～のではあるまいし when attaching to things other than nouns.

**Typical context:** Spoken, informal to semi-formal register — used face-to-face when gently scolding, giving blunt advice, or expressing exasperated disbelief at someone's behavior; carries a slightly sharp, critical edge.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**～じゃあるまいし**](grammar-じゃあるまいし.md) — the more casual contracted spoken form of the same pattern, used identically in informal conversation.
- [**～まい**](grammar-まい.md) — the standalone negative volitional/conjecture form ("probably not," "won't"), from which ではあるまいし is built, but without the "since it's not..." critical nuance.
- [**～くせに**](grammar-くせに.md) — also expresses mild criticism based on a mismatch between circumstance and behavior, but points out a contradiction in the other direction (despite being X, they do Y).

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a sympathetic structure like ～のだから仕方がない ("since that's the case, it can't be helped") would instead excuse behavior rather than criticize it.

**Example sentences:**
1. 子供ではあるまいし、眠かったり空腹だったりするだけでそんなに不機嫌な顔をするものではない。
   (You're not a child — you shouldn't make such a sour face just because you're sleepy or hungry.)
2. 犬や猫じゃあるまいし、上の人の言葉に従うだけなんてごめんだ。
   (I'm not a dog or a cat — I refuse to just obey whatever my superiors say.)
3. 面接試験は初めてではあるまいし、今回はどうしてそんなに緊張するの？
   (This isn't your first interview — why are you so nervous this time?)
4. あなたが悪かったわけではあるまいし、そんなに自分を責めることはないよ。
   (It's not as if it was your fault — you don't need to blame yourself so much.)
5. プロの選手ではあるまいし、そこまで厳しい練習メニューを組む必要はない。
   (You're not a professional athlete — there's no need to set such a strict training schedule.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The clause that follows typically carries the speaker's judgment, complaint, or advice, pointing out that the current reaction or behavior doesn't fit since the "X" premise clearly doesn't apply. Note example ④'s pattern: with non-noun phrases it appears as ～わけではあるまいし or ～のではあるまいし rather than attaching directly.
