# それでは {#それでは}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** それでは

**Furigana form:** それでは

**Meaning:** "Well then, now then" — a polite/formal transitional conjunction used to move from the preceding topic/statement to a new action or segment, often used by hosts, announcers, teachers, or presenters to open or close a formal segment.

**Conjugation:** Sentence-initial conjunction, no conjugation needed — attaches at the sentence boundary, connecting the previous situation/statement to the next action or topic being introduced. Like では, it typically stands alone before an invitation/command (それでは、始めましょう "well then, let's begin") rather than linking clauses within a single sentence.

**Typical context:** Formal/polite, spoken-leaning register — the version most associated with public-facing, ceremonial, or professional transitions: TV/radio hosts introducing the next segment, teachers starting/ending class, MCs opening an event, or speakers transitioning between sections of a formal talk. It is more polished and performative than では and far more formal than its casual contraction じゃ, though it is still fundamentally a spoken/oral transition marker rather than a dense written-text connector.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**では**](grammar-では.md) — the neutral/written-leaning base form; usable in both writing and ordinary polite speech, less ceremonial/performative than それでは.
- [**じゃ**](grammar-じゃ.md) — casual spoken contraction (ultimately of では); used in relaxed daily conversation, the opposite end of the formality spectrum from それでは.
- [**そこで**](grammar-そこで.md) — narrates a decision/action taken in response to a situation in written narrative; unlike それでは, it implies responding to a problem rather than simply moving to the next planned step.
- [**それから**](grammar-それから.md) — purely sequential "after that, next"; lacks それでは's function of actively, often ceremonially, opening a new segment or action.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; it functions as a formal topic/action transition marker rather than a logical or causal connector, so there is no natural contrastive counterpart.

**Example sentences:**
1. それでは、本日の講演を始めさせていただきます。
   (Well then, I will now begin today's lecture.)
2. それでは、次のコーナーに参りましょう。
   (Now then, let's move on to the next segment.)
3. それでは、皆様、本日はご清聴ありがとうございました。
   (Well then, everyone, thank you very much for your attention today.)
4. それでは、ご起立ください。
   (Now then, please rise.)
5. それでは、ここで一旦休憩に入りたいと思います。
   (Well then, let's take a short break here.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes use それでは in casual daily conversation where it sounds overly formal or performative — as if announcing a TV segment to a friend — where では or more naturally じゃ would fit the moment. The reverse mistake, using じゃ in a formal presentation, speech, or classroom opening, undercuts the professional tone expected there. A useful way to keep the three straight: じゃ is for friends, では is the safe neutral default for most polite speech and writing, and それでは is specifically suited to ceremonial/public-facing transitions (hosting, teaching, presenting) where a slightly more polished, formal flourish is wanted.
