# ～と／があいまって {#とがあいまって}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～と／が相まって

**Furigana form:** ～と／があいまって

**Meaning:** "Combined with X (producing an enhanced effect)" — describes two or more factors interacting together to produce a combined, often amplified, result.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + と相まって
- 名詞 + が相まって
- Example base: 円安 → 原油高と円安が相まって

**Typical context:** Formal, written register; common in economic, analytical, and descriptive writing explaining how multiple factors jointly contribute to an outcome. Not casual speech.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**に加えて**](grammar-に加えて.md) — "in addition to X"; simply adds another factor without necessarily emphasizing an interactive, synergistic combination — が相まって specifically highlights that the factors interact to amplify an effect, not just coexist.
- [**とともに**](grammar-とともに.md) — "together with X"; a more neutral "alongside" connector, lacking相まって's implication of synergistic interaction producing a combined, often stronger result.
- **の相乗効果で** — "due to the synergistic effect of X"; very close in meaning, explicitly naming the synergy, while相まって achieves the same sense more implicitly through its grammatical structure.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; 単独で ("independently/on its own") would contrast with相まって's emphasis on combined, interactive factors.

**Example sentences:**
1. 原油高と円安が相まって、物価はさらに上昇した。 (Combined with the yen's depreciation, high crude oil prices pushed consumer prices up even further.)
2. 彼の才能と努力が相まって、大きな成功を収めた。 (His talent combined with his effort led to great success.)
3. 悪天候と交通渋滞が相まって、到着が大幅に遅れた。 (Combined with the traffic congestion, the bad weather caused a major delay in arrival.)
4. 美しい景色と心地よい気候が相まって、観光客が増加している。 (Combined with the pleasant climate, the beautiful scenery is drawing more and more tourists.)
5. 経験不足と準備不足が相まって、プロジェクトは失敗に終わった。 (Combined with insufficient preparation, the lack of experience led the project to end in failure.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** と／が相まって always requires at least two named factors that interact to jointly produce a result (often naming an amplified positive or negative outcome) — it shouldn't be used with just a single cause, where simple ので or により would suffice. Learners should recognize 相まって (from 相まる, "to combine/blend with each other") implies an interactive synergy, not merely an additive list of separate causes, distinguishing it from plain に加えて.
