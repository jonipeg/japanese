# ～ものか／～もんか {#ものか・もんか}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ものか／～もんか

**Furigana form:** ～ものか／～もんか (no special kanji reading)

**Meaning:** "Definitely not!" "As if I would!" — a strong rhetorical question expressing emphatic negation/denial, often with defiance, frustration, or stubborn refusal. もんか is the casual contracted form.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形 + ものか／もんか
- い形容詞 + ものか
- な形容詞 + な + ものか (or である + ものか)
- 誰があんな奴に頭を下げるものか。(As if I'd bow my head to a guy like that!)
- こんな簡単な問題、わからないものか。(As if I wouldn't understand such an easy problem!)

**Typical context:** Casual-to-neutral spoken Japanese, with strong emotional emphasis. Used when the speaker is adamantly refusing, denying, or rejecting something — often with frustration or pride.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**わけがない**](grammar-わけがない.md) — "there's no way that...," similarly strong negation but more about logical impossibility than personal refusal.
- **なんか／なんて** — casual exclamatory negation particles, e.g., 行くなんて絶対しない, overlapping emotional force.
- [**たまるか**](grammar-てたまるか・たまるか.md) — very similar strong rhetorical refusal, slightly more aggressive/masculine in tone.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a plain affirmative statement (〜と思う／〜に違いない) represents the non-rhetorical, calm contrast.

**Example sentences:**
1. 二度とあんな人に頼るものか。
   (As if I'd ever rely on someone like that again!)
2. こんな理不尽な扱いに、黙っているものか。
   (As if I'd stay silent over such unreasonable treatment!)
3. 彼に負けるもんか、絶対に勝ってみせる。
   (As if I'd lose to him — I'll definitely win, just watch!)
4. あんな店、二度と行くもんか。
   (As if I'd ever go to that shop again!)
5. こんなことで諦めるものか。
   (As if I'd give up over something like this!)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ものか is a rhetorical question — it grammatically looks like a question but functions as a strong statement of negation/defiance, not an actual question. Learners sometimes misread it as asking something literally. It's also distinct from もの/もん (casual reason-giving particle) and ものだ (general truth) — don't confuse the three もの-family patterns, as they serve very different functions despite similar appearance.
