# なぜなら {#なぜなら}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** 何故なら

**Furigana form:** なぜなら

**Meaning:** "Because, the reason is..." — a formal conjunction that introduces the explicit reason or justification for the statement just made, typically completed with 〜からだ or 〜のだ at the end of the following sentence.

**Conjugation:** Sentence-initial conjunction, no conjugation needed — attaches at the sentence boundary, connecting the previous sentence to the explanatory sentence that follows. Unlike pure cause-conjunctions, なぜなら requires its own sentence to end in からだ/のだ (or ためだ) to grammatically "close the loop," so it functions as a paired construction (なぜなら〜からだ) rather than a conjunction used alone mid-sentence.

**Typical context:** Formal, written register — common in essays, opinion pieces, academic writing, and formal speeches where the writer explicitly justifies a claim just made. It can appear in careful spoken explanations (e.g. a lecture or debate) but sounds too deliberate/formal for casual chat, where だって or just a plain からだ clause without なぜなら would be used instead.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**だから**](grammar-だから.md) — casual, spoken; gives a reason leading forward to a result, whereas なぜなら looks backward to justify a claim already stated.
- [**したがって**](grammar-したがって.md) — formal, written; states a conclusion that follows from a reason, the reverse direction of なぜなら's reason-first structure.
- [**というのは**](grammar-というのは.md) — written/spoken explanatory; very similar function (introducing the reason for a preceding statement) but slightly less formal and more conversational-explanatory than なぜなら, and does not require closing with からだ.
- [**だって**](grammar-だって.md) — very casual, spoken; a childish/petulant excuse-giving equivalent of "because," the casual opposite-register counterpart of なぜなら.
- [**それゆえ**](grammar-それゆえ.md) — very formal, literary; states a conclusion (forward-looking) rather than justifying a prior claim (backward-looking) like なぜなら.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; its structural counterpart is a conclusion-stating conjunction like したがって or それゆえ, which moves from reason to result, whereas なぜなら moves from result/claim back to reason.

**Example sentences:**
1. 私はこの政策に反対だ。なぜなら、長期的には財政を圧迫するからだ。
   (I am against this policy. Because it will strain public finances in the long run.)
2. 彼女を信頼している。なぜなら、これまで一度も約束を破ったことがないからだ。
   (I trust her. Because she has never once broken a promise.)
3. この本を強く勧めたい。なぜなら、現代社会の問題を鋭く分析しているからだ。
   (I strongly recommend this book. Because it sharply analyzes the problems of modern society.)
4. 子供の読書習慣は重要である。なぜなら、思考力や語彙力の基礎を築くからだ。
   (Children's reading habits are important. Because they build the foundation of thinking ability and vocabulary.)
5. 今回のプロジェクトは延期すべきだ。なぜなら、十分な準備期間が確保できていないからだ。
   (This project should be postponed. Because sufficient preparation time has not been secured.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** A frequent structural mistake is using なぜなら without ending the following sentence in からだ/のだ/ためだ — learners sometimes write なぜなら〜。as a plain declarative sentence, which feels grammatically incomplete to native ears, since なぜなら sets up an expectation that is only satisfied by the からだ-type ending. A register mistake is using なぜなら in casual conversation, where it can sound stilted or like a mini-essay; だって or a plain からだ-ending sentence without なぜなら is more natural in casual speech. Learners should also distinguish it from というのは, which can introduce a reason/elaboration without requiring the からだ closing pattern.
