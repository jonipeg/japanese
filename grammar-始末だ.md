# ～始末だ {#始末だ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～始末だ

**Furigana form:** ～しまつだ

**Meaning:** Describes how, after a bad situation continued for a while, things finally ended up in such-and-such an unfortunate conclusion.

**Conjugation:**
- 動辞書形 + 始末だ
- とうとう会社を辞めてしまう始末だ — "and in the end he ended up quitting the company altogether"

**Typical context:** Spoken and written, neutral-to-casual register — used to recount, with exasperation or disapproval, how a deteriorating situation reached a disappointing final outcome; often paired with words like ついに・とうとう・～まで.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～あげく（の果てに）** — "in the end, after all that" — very similar, both describe a bad final result after a chain of bad events; あげく more often explicitly lists the preceding negative events.
- **～というわけだ** — a neutral "that's the situation" structure, without 始末だ's negative, exasperated connotation.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a phrase describing a positive outcome reached through effort, like 努力の末に～を成し遂げた ("after much effort, finally achieved X"), expresses the positive counterpart.

**Example sentences:**
1. おいは遊んでばかりいてまじめに働きもせず、とうとう会社を辞めてしまう始末だ。
   (My nephew did nothing but play around and never worked seriously, and in the end he quit his job altogether.)
2. 田中さんはお酒を飲んで大きな声でしゃべり続けた後で、ついに泣き出す始末だった。
   (Tanaka kept talking loudly after drinking, and in the end he actually burst into tears.)
3. 兄は借金を返すためと言って家にあるものをお金に換え、父の時計まで売ってしまう始末だ。
   (My brother, saying it was to pay off his debts, turned things around the house into cash, and ended up even selling our father's watch.)
4. 彼は何度も遅刻を注意されていたが、ついには無断欠勤をする始末だ。
   (He'd been warned about being late many times, and in the end he started skipping work without notice.)
5. ダイエットのために始めたジョギングも三日坊主で、結局やめてしまう始末だった。
   (The jogging I took up to lose weight lasted all of three days, and in the end I just quit.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Requires that a bad state has already been continuing — the preceding context (stated or implied) should set up a chain of worsening events before 始末だ names the final result. It's often reinforced with words like ついに・とうとう・～まで, and carries a tone of exasperation, criticism, or resignation, whether directed at someone else or at oneself.
