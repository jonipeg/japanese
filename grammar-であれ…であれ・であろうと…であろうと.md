# ～であれ…であれ・～であろうと…であろうと {#であれ…であれ・であろうと…であろうと}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～であれ…であれ・～であろうと…であろうと

**Furigana form:** ～であれ…であれ・～であろうと…であろうと

**Meaning:** "Whether it's X or Y, the same thing holds true regardless" — lists representative examples within one category and asserts that something applies to all of them equally, irrespective of which one it is.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + であれ／であろうと
- 社長であれ新入社員であれ、ルールは守らなければならない — "whether it's the president or a new employee, the rules must be followed"

**Typical context:** Formal written and spoken register — essays, speeches, and argumentative writing emphasizing that a rule or truth applies universally regardless of circumstance.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～も…も** — plain "both X and Y," simpler and lacking the "regardless of which" universality nuance.
- **～によらず** — "regardless of X," a similar concessive universality, but attaches to a single noun rather than paired examples.
- **～にせよ…にせよ** — very close in meaning and register, largely interchangeable with であれ…であれ; にせよ is somewhat more common in formal speech.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a phrase restricting applicability to a single case, like ～の場合に限り ("only in the case of X"), contrasts with this pattern's universal, case-independent claim.

**Example sentences:**
1. 地震であれ火事であれ、緊急の場合に冷静になれる人は少ないだろう。
   (Whether it's an earthquake or a fire, few people can stay calm in an emergency.)
2. 文学であれ音楽であれ、芸術には才能が必要なのだ。努力だけではだめなのだ。
   (Whether it's literature or music, art requires talent — effort alone isn't enough.)
3. 禁煙であれ禁酒であれ、周りの人の協力が大切だと思う。
   (Whether it's quitting smoking or quitting drinking, the support of people around you is important.)
4. ビールであろうとワインであろうと、酒は酒だ。運転前に絶対飲んではいけない。
   (Whether it's beer or wine, alcohol is alcohol — you must never drink before driving.)
5. 子供であれ大人であれ、人の話はきちんと聞くべきだ。
   (Whether a child or an adult, one should listen properly when someone is speaking.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The two items listed must belong to the same conceptual category (e.g., types of disaster, types of art) — don't use it to join two unrelated nouns. The point is "no matter which member of this group," not an arbitrary pairing of unconnected things.
