# ～に限って {#に限って}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～に限って

**Furigana form:** ～にかぎって

**Meaning:** "Especially in the case of ~ / of all times/people" — expresses irony or frustration that something unfortunate happens precisely in the one situation where it was least expected or wanted.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + に限って — 例: こんな日に限って (of all days, on a day like this)

**Typical context:** Spoken, casual-to-neutral — commonly used to vent frustration or describe an ironic coincidence in daily conversation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**に限り**](grammar-限り・に限り・に限る.md) — formal restrictive "only in the case of," lacks the ironic/frustrated nuance entirely.
- **よりによって** — "of all things/times," very similar ironic nuance but slightly more emphatic/exclamatory.
- **だけは** — "only this," more neutral emphasis without the bad-luck irony.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 傘を持っていない日に限って、雨が降る。
   (Of all days, it rains on the day I don't have an umbrella.)
2. 急いでいる時に限って、電車が遅れる。
   (Of all times, the train is late exactly when I'm in a hurry.)
3. 大事な会議の日に限って、寝坊してしまった。
   (Of all days, I overslept on the day of the important meeting.)
4. 新しい靴を履いた日に限って、雨で道がぬかるんでいた。
   (Of all days, the road was muddy from rain on the day I wore my new shoes.)
5. うちの子に限って、そんな悪いことをするはずがない。
   (My child, of all people, would never do such a bad thing.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** に限って almost always implies bad luck or an ironic twist (sometimes also strong parental trust in example 5), unlike に限り which is a neutral, formal restriction — using them interchangeably sounds unnatural, especially since に限って rarely appears in formal writing.
