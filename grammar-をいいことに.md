# ～をいいことに {#をいいことに}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～をいいことに

**Furigana form:** ～をいいことに

**Meaning:** "Taking advantage of X (a convenient circumstance) to do something bad/selfish" — criticizes someone for exploiting a favorable situation, such as someone's absence or weakness, in order to misbehave.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞／動詞普通形 + の + をいいことに
- 先生がいないのをいいことに — "taking advantage of the teacher's absence"

**Typical context:** Spoken and written, critical/disapproving tone; used to describe someone opportunistically misbehaving because a lucky circumstance made it easy to get away with.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～に乗じて** — "taking advantage of/exploiting an opportunity," a more formal synonym that can occasionally be neutral rather than always negative.
- **～のをさいわい(に)** — "taking advantage of the fortunate circumstance that," an older, more literary expression with a similarly negative nuance.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct grammatical opposite; a phrase like ～にもかかわらず遠慮する ("to hold back despite X") would describe the restrained behavior that the subject of this pattern conspicuously fails to show.

**Example sentences:**
1. 先生がいないのをいいことにサボっている。
   (They're slacking off, taking advantage of the teacher's absence.)
2. 彼は相手がおとなしいのをいいことに一方的に文句を言い続けている。
   (He keeps unilaterally complaining, taking advantage of the fact that the other person is docile.)
3. 上司が出張中なのをいいことに、彼は仕事をさぼっていた。
   (Taking advantage of his boss being away on a business trip, he was slacking off on the job.)
4. 子供は親が忙しいのをいいことに、ゲームばかりしている。
   (The child keeps playing games nonstop, taking advantage of his parents being busy.)
5. 彼女は人がいいのをいいことに、いつも無理なお願いをする。
   (She always makes unreasonable requests, taking advantage of how kind-hearted people are.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Always carries a negative, critical nuance toward the subject's behavior — it cannot be used to describe a neutral or admirable use of a good opportunity. The circumstance being exploited is typically someone else's absence, weakness, or kindness, not something the speaker arranged for themselves.
