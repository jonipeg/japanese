# ～まじき {#まじき}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～まじき

**Furigana form:** ～まじき

**Meaning:** Describes behavior "that must not be done, unbecoming of X" — a formal, strongly disapproving judgment that an action is inexcusable given someone's position, role, or the context.

**Conjugation:**
- Verb-辞書形 + まじき + Noun (always modifies a following noun, e.g. まじき行為 "unforgivable conduct")
- Fixed collocation: あるまじき + Noun ("conduct that should not exist/be tolerated").

**Typical context:** Formal, literary, almost classical register — rarely if ever used in casual speech. Common in news reports, official condemnations, and formal writing expressing strong moral disapproval.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **べきではない** — "should not do X," a more neutral and general statement of inappropriateness, lacking the archaic, morally charged weight of まじき.
- **あるまじき** — the most common fixed form of this pattern itself (あるまじき行為 "deplorable conduct"), effectively a set phrase rather than a separate pattern.
- **てはならない** — "must not do X," a general prohibition that can apply to simple rules, whereas まじき specifically judges someone's character/conduct as morally unacceptable.

**Opposite grammar structures:** あるべき ("as it should be / proper, fitting conduct") functions as a natural positive counterpart.

**Example sentences:**
1. 自殺の方法など、授業で話すまじきことだ。
   (Talking about methods of suicide is something that should never be discussed in class.)
2. お年寄りをだますとは、許すまじきことだ。
   (Deceiving elderly people is utterly unforgivable conduct.)
3. 教師にあるまじき言動が問題視されている。
   (Conduct unbecoming of a teacher is being called into question.)
4. 患者の命を軽視するなど、医師としてあるまじき行為だ。
   (Disregarding a patient's life is conduct unbecoming of a doctor.)
5. 公務員にあるまじき汚職事件が発覚した。
   (A corruption scandal unbecoming of a public servant came to light.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** A margin note glosses this as "ought not to / deplorable," underscoring its formal, literary register and strong disapproval nuance. Students should recognize that まじき always modifies a following noun describing conduct/behavior (not a standalone predicate), and that it specifically evaluates behavior as morally inappropriate given someone's role or status — a weaker, more neutral "shouldn't" (べきではない) does not carry the same moral condemnation.
