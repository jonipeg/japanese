# ～つ…つ {#つ…つ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～つ…つ

**Furigana form:** ～つ…つ

**Meaning:** Describes two reciprocal or opposite actions happening repeatedly, back and forth (e.g. "going and coming," "overtaking and being overtaken"). It attaches only to a small, fixed set of verb pairs and functions almost entirely as set idiomatic expressions rather than a freely productive pattern.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ます形 + つ + 動詞ます形（対になる動詞）+ つ
- 行きつ戻りつ — "going back and forth"

**Typical context:** Mostly literary/written narration, though some of the fixed phrases (持ちつ持たれつ, 行きつ戻りつ) also appear in spoken descriptions of people's behavior or relationships.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～たり～たり** — the general, fully productive way to list repeated or alternating actions; usable with almost any verb pair, unlike the closed set allowed with ～つ…つ.
- [**～ながら**](grammar-ながら.md) — expresses two actions happening simultaneously, but does not carry the back-and-forth, reciprocal nuance of ～つ…つ.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; describing a single, one-directional action would simply use the plain verb form without the つ…つ repetition.

**Example sentences:**
1. あの人はさっきから店の前を行きつ戻りつしている。
   (That person has been going back and forth in front of the shop for a while now.)
2. マラソンの選手たちは抜きつ抜かれつ、激しい争いを繰り広げた。
   (The marathon runners fought fiercely, overtaking and being overtaken by turns.)
3. 二人は押しつ押されつしながら、改札口に向かった。
   (Pushing and being pushed in turn, the two of them made their way toward the ticket gate.)
4. 木の葉が浮きつ沈みつ、川を流れていく。
   (The leaf floats down the river, bobbing up and down.)
5. 長年の商売仲間として、二人は持ちつ持たれつの関係を続けてきた。
   (As longtime business partners, the two of them have kept up a relationship of mutual give-and-take.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This is not a productive grammar pattern you can apply to any verb pair — it survives mainly in a handful of fixed idiomatic expressions (行きつ戻りつ, 抜きつ抜かれつ, 持ちつ持たれつ, 浮きつ沈みつ). Learners should treat these as vocabulary-like chunks to memorize rather than try to generate new combinations freely.
