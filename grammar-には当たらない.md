# ～には当たらない {#には当たらない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～には当たらない

**Furigana form:** ～にはあたらない

**Meaning:** Says that something isn't serious or significant enough to warrant a strong emotional reaction or evaluation — "there's no need to X" / "it doesn't warrant X-ing," used to restrain an excessive response like surprise, pessimism, or blame.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形／名詞する + には当たらない
- 驚くには当たらない — "there's no need to be surprised"
- 悲観するには当たりません — "there's no need to be pessimistic"

**Typical context:** Formal written and spoken Japanese — editorials, formal advice, reassuring or moderating statements, often in professional or semi-formal spoken contexts.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**～までもない**](grammar-までもない.md) — also expresses "there's no need to X," but is broader and used for ordinary unnecessary actions, whereas ～には当たらない is specifically restricted to emotional/evaluative reactions (surprise, blame, praise, pessimism).
- **～には及ばない** — closely related, meaning "it doesn't reach the level of needing X," often used interchangeably with ～には当たらない in formal register.
- [**～ことはない**](grammar-ことはない.md) — a more general, less formal way to say "there's no need to X."

**Opposite grammar structures:** No single fixed opposite; a phrase emphasizing that a reaction is indeed warranted (e.g., ～するのも当然だ, "it's only natural to X") would convey the contrasting idea that the response is justified.

**Example sentences:**
1. 山田さんは通勤に1時間半かかるそうだが、驚くには当たらない。これは日本では珍しくない。
   (I hear Yamada-san's commute takes an hour and a half, but there's no need to be surprised — that's not unusual in Japan.)
2. 今度の大会での成績を悲観するには当たりません。これから先、チャンスはまだたくさんあります。
   (There's no need to be pessimistic about your results in this tournament. There will still be plenty of chances ahead.)
3. 彼の発言は責めるには当たらないと思う。彼の立場では、あのように言うのも当然だろう。
   (I don't think his remark deserves to be criticized — given his position, it's only natural he'd say something like that.)
4. あのホテルのサービスは称賛には当たらない。ホテルならあのくらいは当然だ。
   (That hotel's service doesn't really deserve praise — that level is just what you'd expect from a hotel.)
5. 新人がまだ慣れていないのは当然で、今の段階で失望するには当たらない。
   (It's only natural that a new employee isn't used to things yet — there's no need to be disappointed at this stage.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern attaches specifically to verbs expressing an emotional or evaluative reaction — 驚く, 悲観する, 責める, 非難する, 称賛する, and the like — it is not a general-purpose "no need to" expression like ～までもない. It functions to restrain someone from overreacting, implying the situation doesn't merit that degree of emotional response, so using it with a neutral action verb (e.g., 行くには当たらない in a non-evaluative sense) would sound unnatural.
