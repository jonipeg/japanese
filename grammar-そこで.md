# そこで {#そこで}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** そこで（漢字なし／平仮名のみ）

**Furigana form:** そこで

**Meaning:** "So, at that point, thereupon" — a conjunction narrating a deliberate decision or action taken by the subject in response to a situation just described.

**Conjugation:** Sentence-initial conjunction, no conjugation needed — attaches at the sentence boundary, connecting the previous sentence (describing a situation or problem) to the next one describing the action taken in response. It is used to open a new sentence narrating the responsive action and is not generally used to link clauses within a single sentence.

**Typical context:** Written narrative register, also usable in spoken storytelling/explanation — common in essays, news articles, business narratives, and recounted stories where the writer/speaker explains "given that situation, I/they then did X." It carries a slightly more formal, composed narrative tone than それで, and fits well in semi-formal spoken explanations (e.g. explaining to a boss how a problem was handled) as well as in written reports.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**すると**](grammar-すると.md) — narrates a natural/automatic consequence, often in written narrative style; unlike そこで, すると implies the next event happened on its own, without deliberate agency, whereas そこで implies the subject actively chose to act.
- [**それから**](grammar-それから.md) — purely sequential, "after that, next"; carries no implication of a decision made in response to a problem, unlike そこで.
- [**それで**](grammar-それで.md) — casual-neutral, spoken; a more casual/conversational version of a similar "so, in response, I..." narrative function.
- [**だから**](grammar-だから.md) — casual, spoken; states a direct justification for an opinion/claim rather than narrating a specific action taken in response to a situation.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; it has no contrastive counterpart, as it narrates a responsive decision/action rather than making a logical or evaluative claim.

**Example sentences:**
1. 会議で誰も意見を言わなかった。そこで、私が口火を切ることにした。
   (No one spoke up at the meeting. So, at that point, I decided to break the ice.)
2. 売上が伸び悩んでいた。そこで、新しい広告戦略を試すことにした。
   (Sales had stalled. So, we decided to try a new advertising strategy.)
3. 道に迷ってしまった。そこで、近くの交番で道を尋ねることにした。
   (I got lost. So, I decided to ask for directions at a nearby police box.)
4. 子供が野菜を食べたがらなかった。そこで、母は料理に工夫を凝らすことにした。
   (The child didn't want to eat vegetables. So, the mother decided to get creative with the cooking.)
5. 従来の方法では解決できなかった。そこで、専門家に相談することにした。
   (The conventional method couldn't solve it. So, we decided to consult an expert.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The key confusion is between そこで (a deliberate, agentive response: "so I/we decided to...") and すると (an automatic, often surprising natural consequence: "and then, [something] happened"). Using すると where a deliberate decision is meant can make the action sound like it just happened passively rather than being a conscious choice, and vice versa. Learners should also be careful not to confuse this narrative そこで with the unrelated locational そこで meaning "there, at that place" (e.g. そこで待っていて "wait there") — context (a situation + response, versus a physical location) disambiguates the two uses.
