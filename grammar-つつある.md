# ～つつある {#つつある}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～つつある

**Furigana form:** ～つつある (no kanji)

**Meaning:** "In the process of gradually happening/changing" — describes an ongoing change-of-state, formal register.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ます形 (verb, ます-stem) + つつある (mainly with change-of-state verbs like 増える、減る、進む、回復する)

**Typical context:** Formal written Japanese — news reports, academic writing, official statements.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **ている** — progressive form, more general and casual, can describe simple ongoing states without implying gradual change.
- [**つつ**](grammar-つつ.md) — simultaneous action marker, a distinct usage not about gradual change over time.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though 変わらない ("not changing") contrasts conceptually.

**Example sentences:**
1. 地球の気温は年々上昇しつつある。
   (The earth's temperature is gradually rising year by year.)
2. 彼の病状は少しずつ回復しつつある。
   (His condition is gradually recovering.)
3. この地域の人口は減少しつつある。
   (The population of this area is gradually decreasing.)
4. 伝統的な技術は徐々に失われつつある。
   (Traditional skills are gradually being lost.)
5. 経済は緩やかに成長しつつある。
   (The economy is gradually growing.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** つつある is used specifically with verbs expressing change or process (increase, decrease, recover, progress), not with static or momentary verbs. Learners often confuse it with ている, which can describe a simple ongoing state without the "gradual change" implication.
