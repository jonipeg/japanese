## 見せびらかす (みせびらかす)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** show off, flaunt

**Similar words:**
- 自慢する (じまんする) — to boast; can be purely verbal bragging with no physical object present ("I'm good at math"), whereas 見せびらかす specifically implies displaying something visible (an object, appearance) in front of others.
- 誇示する (こじする) — to display/flaunt; more formal/written, often used for power, strength, or wealth in writing rather than everyday ostentation.
- ひけらかす — to show off (knowledge/skill); a very close synonym, but typically applied to displaying talent or knowledge rather than physical objects.

**Opposite words:**
- [隠す](vocab-12.md#隠す-かくす) (かくす) — to hide (conceptual opposite, not a true antonym)
- 謙遜する (けんそんする) — to be modest/humble (behavioral opposite)

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は新しい車をみんなに見せびらかした。(He showed off his new car to everyone.)
2. そんなに高級腕時計を見せびらかさなくてもいいのに。(You don't need to flaunt such a luxury watch like that.)
3. 子供はもらったおもちゃを友達に見せびらかしたがる。(Children want to show off the toys they received to their friends.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often use 自慢する where 見せびらかす is more appropriate, or vice versa. 自慢する can be purely verbal boasting without a physical object present, while 見せびらかす specifically implies displaying/parading something visible. 見せびらかす always carries a negative, ostentatious nuance; it's rarely neutral.

## もめる
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** (have) discord, trouble; to be in dispute

**Similar words:**
- [争う](vocab-07.md#争う-あらそう) (あらそう) — to fight/dispute; more formal/serious, can involve physical or legal conflict, unlike the lingering interpersonal friction of もめる.
- 衝突する (しょうとつする) — to clash; literal collision or a sudden, intense figurative conflict, more abrupt than the prolonged friction of もめる.
- こじれる — to become complicated/strained; closely related — a matter that もめる's can further こじれる into a worse, harder-to-resolve state.

**Opposite words:**
- 和解する (わかいする) — to reconcile (no single-word true antonym, but captures the resolution)

**Example sentences:**
1. 遺産相続のことでもめている。(They're in discord over inheritance matters.)
2. 会議でもめて、結論が出なかった。(There was friction in the meeting, so no conclusion was reached.)
3. そのカップルは些細なことでよくもめる。(That couple often squabbles over trivial things.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** もめる is intransitive and describes a state of ongoing friction/trouble within a relationship or group (often about money or decisions) — it is not a single sharp argument like けんかする. Learners sometimes conflate the two, but もめる emphasizes unresolved, lingering complication rather than a one-off fight.

## 類似 (るいじ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** resemblance, similarity

**Similar words:**
- 似ている (にている) — to resemble; the everyday verb form, more casual than the formal noun 類似.
- 相似 (そうじ) — similarity; reserved for mathematical/geometric similarity (similar figures), a technical usage distinct from general resemblance.
- 共通点 (きょうつうてん) — common point; emphasizes specific shared features rather than overall resemblance.

**Opposite words:**
- [相違](vocab-23.md#相違-そうい) (そうい) — difference
- 差異 (さい) — difference/disparity

**Example sentences:**
1. この二つの商品には類似点が多い。(These two products have many similarities.)
2. 類似した事件が他県でも発生している。(Similar incidents are occurring in other prefectures too.)
3. 偶然とは思えないほどの類似性が見られる。(The similarity is so striking it seems more than coincidence.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 類似 is a formal/written noun, common in compounds (類似品, 類似点, 類似性). Learners often try to use it as a casual verb like 似ている, but 類似する sounds stiff in everyday conversation. Also keep it distinct from 相似, which is strictly mathematical/geometric similarity.

## 紛らわしい (まぎらわしい)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** confusing, easily mistaken

**Similar words:**
- 分かりにくい (わかりにくい) — hard to understand; about general difficulty of comprehension, not necessarily mistaken identity between two things.
- [ややこしい](vocab-34.md#ややこしい-ややこしい) — complicated/convoluted; about an inherently tangled matter, not specifically about resembling something else closely enough to be mixed up.
- [曖昧](#曖昧-あいまい) (あいまい) — ambiguous/vague; about something lacking clear definition, whereas 紛らわしい is about two similar things causing mix-ups.

**Opposite words:**
- 明白 (めいはく) — obvious/clear
- はっきりしている — clear-cut

**Example sentences:**
1. この二つの漢字は形が紛らわしい。(These two kanji have confusingly similar shapes.)
2. 紛らわしい表現は避けたほうがいい。(It's better to avoid confusing/ambiguous expressions.)
3. 名前が似ていて紛らわしい会社がある。(There's a company with a confusingly similar name.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 紛らわしい specifically describes two or more things so similar they get mistaken for each other (similar-looking products, names, signs). Learners often confuse it with ややこしい, which describes something inherently complicated, not necessarily a case of mistaken identity. 紛らわしい always implies a risk of confusion due to resemblance.

## 促す (うながす)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** urge, prompt, hasten

**Similar words:**
- 勧める (すすめる) — to recommend/suggest; softer, offering advice rather than pushing someone toward immediate action.
- 催促する (さいそくする) — to press/demand; more insistent, typically about reminding someone of an overdue obligation (e.g., 返済を催促する), with impatience built in that 促す lacks.
- せかす — to rush/hurry someone; casual, implies impatience and pressure specifically for speed.

**Opposite words:**
- 制止する (せいしする) — to restrain/stop
- 引き止める (ひきとめる) — to hold back

**Example sentences:**
1. 政府は国民に節電を促した。(The government urged citizens to save electricity.)
2. 彼の発言が議論の活発化を促した。(His remark prompted the discussion to become more active.)
3. 医師は早期の検査を促した。(The doctor urged an early checkup.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 促す is a fairly formal verb meaning to encourage or indirectly trigger an action, often with abstract subjects (政府, 法律, 発言) rather than only people. Learners sometimes mix it up with 催促する, which specifically means pressing someone about something overdue, carrying a more demanding, impatient tone that 促す does not have.

## 寮 (りょう)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** dormitory

**Similar words:**
- 下宿 (げしゅく) — boarding house; privately rented room, usually with meals, not tied to an institution the way 寮 is.
- アパート — apartment; a rented unit with no institutional affiliation or communal rules.
- 宿舎 (しゅくしゃ) — lodging/quarters; a broader, more bureaucratic official term, e.g. for employees, overlapping with but more formal than 寮.

**Opposite words:**
- No direct opposite (一人暮らし, "living alone," is the conceptual contrast)

**Example sentences:**
1. 大学の寮に住んでいる。(I live in the university dormitory.)
2. 彼は会社の寮に入ることにした。(He decided to move into the company dormitory.)
3. 寮には門限がある。(The dormitory has a curfew.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 寮 specifically refers to institution-affiliated communal housing (school or company dormitories) with shared facilities and rules like curfews. Learners sometimes use it interchangeably with アパート or 下宿, but those are privately rented housing without institutional ties.

## 廊下 (ろうか)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** corridor, hallway

**Similar words:**
- [通路](vocab-28.md#通路-つうろ) (つうろ) — passage/aisle; a more general term for any walkway, including on trains or planes, whereas 廊下 is specifically an indoor building hallway.
- 玄関 (げんかん) — entryway; the entrance area of a building, not a hallway.

**Opposite words:**
- No direct opposite (部屋, "room," is the conceptual contrast — a space passed through vs. occupied)

**Example sentences:**
1. 廊下を走ってはいけません。(You must not run in the hallway.)
2. 教室の前の廊下で待っていてください。(Please wait in the corridor in front of the classroom.)
3. 夜、廊下の電気が消えていて怖かった。(At night, the hallway light was off and it was scary.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 廊下 is specifically an indoor hallway inside a building (school, house, hospital). Learners sometimes overextend it to any passage, but 通路 is the broader word for aisles on trains/planes or general passageways, while 廊下 is reserved for architectural hallways.

## 火災 (かさい)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** a fire (disaster)

**Similar words:**
- [火事](vocab-13.md#火事-かじ) (かじ) — fire; the common, everyday spoken word for the same event, versus the formal/written 火災 used in news reports, official documents, and safety signage (火災報知器, 火災保険).
- 火元 (ひもと) — source/origin of a fire; refers to the point of origin, not the event itself.

**Opposite words:**
- No direct single-word opposite (鎮火, "fire extinguished," is the resolution, not a true antonym)

**Example sentences:**
1. 昨夜、近所で火災が発生した。(Last night, a fire broke out in the neighborhood.)
2. このビルには火災報知器が設置されている。(This building has fire alarms installed.)
3. 火災による死者はいなかった。(There were no deaths from the fire.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 火災 and 火事 refer to the same event, but 火災 is formal/written (news, legal, safety contexts) while 火事 is the everyday spoken word. Learners often use 火災 in casual speech where 火事 sounds more natural (e.g., shouting 「火事だ!」, never 「火災だ!」).

## 抵抗 (ていこう)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** resistance

**Similar words:**
- 反抗 (はんこう) — defiance/rebellion; about actively opposing authority (e.g., a teenager rebelling against parents), a deliberate pushback against rules that 抵抗 doesn't require.
- 反発 (はんぱつ) — backlash/repulsion; an emotional, often collective reaction against something, like public backlash against a policy.

**Opposite words:**
- 服従 (ふくじゅう) — obedience/submission
- 従順 (じゅうじゅん) — docility

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は警察に抵抗した。(He resisted the police.)
2. 彼女はその意見に抵抗を感じた。(She felt resistance/hesitation toward that opinion.)
3. 電気抵抗が大きいと電流が流れにくい。(If electrical resistance is high, current flows less easily.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 抵抗 spans physical resistance, emotional reluctance (抵抗がある/抵抗を感じる = "feel hesitant about something"), and a technical physics meaning (electrical resistance). Learners often confuse it with 反抗, which is specifically about rebelling against authority — 抵抗 is broader and not limited to authority relationships.

## 誇り (ほこり)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** pride

**Similar words:**
- 自尊心 (じそんしん) — self-esteem/self-respect; about one's internal sense of worth, more psychological than the outward-facing dignity of 誇り.
- プライド — pride (loanword); often tied to ego/self-image, sometimes with a slightly negative "wounded pride" nuance that 誇り lacks.
- 自負 (じふ) — self-confidence/pride in one's own ability; specifically about confidence in competence/achievement.

**Opposite words:**
- [恥](vocab-31.md#恥-はじ) (はじ) — shame
- 屈辱 (くつじょく) — humiliation

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は自分の仕事に誇りを持っている。(He takes pride in his work.)
2. この町の伝統は私たちの誇りです。(This town's tradition is our pride.)
3. 誇りを傷つけられて黙っていられなかった。(His pride was wounded and he couldn't stay silent.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Watch the homophone trap — 誇り (ほこり, "pride") vs. 埃 (ほこり, "dust") sound identical but are unrelated; context and kanji disambiguate them. Separately, 誇り is a positive, dignified self-worth tied to accomplishment or identity, distinct from プライド, which can carry a more critical "inflated ego" nuance (プライドが高い).

## 酪農 (らくのう)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** dairy farming

**Similar words:**
- 農業 (のうぎょう) — agriculture; the broad general term, of which 酪農 is a specific dairy-focused subset.
- [畜産](vocab-27.md#畜産-ちくさん) (ちくさん) — animal husbandry/livestock farming; broader category including meat and egg production, whereas 酪農 is specifically about milk/dairy.
- 牧畜 (ぼくちく) — livestock raising/ranching; emphasizes raising and grazing animals generally, not limited to dairy.

**Opposite words:**
- No direct opposite

**Example sentences:**
1. 北海道は酪農が盛んな地域だ。(Hokkaido is a region where dairy farming is thriving.)
2. 彼の実家は代々酪農を営んでいる。(His family has run a dairy farm for generations.)
3. 酪農家の高齢化が問題になっている。(The aging of dairy farmers has become a problem.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 酪農 specifically means dairy farming (milk, cheese, butter from cattle), narrower than 畜産 (livestock farming in general) or 農業 (agriculture overall). Learners sometimes use 農業 when they mean the specific dairy industry, losing precision.

## 励ます (はげます)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** encourage, cheer up

**Similar words:**
- 応援する (おうえんする) — to cheer on/support; often from the sidelines for a team or contestant, about rooting for success rather than verbally comforting someone who's down.
- 慰める (なぐさめる) — to console/comfort; focused on soothing sadness or grief, more passive/sympathetic than the motivating push of 励ます.
- [励む](vocab-01.md#励む-はげむ) (はげむ) — to strive/work hard; the intransitive, self-directed counterpart — easily confused with 励ます due to near-identical form.

**Opposite words:**
- 落胆させる (らくたんさせる) — to discourage/dishearten
- くじく — to dishearten, break someone's spirit

**Example sentences:**
1. 友達が試験に落ちて落ち込んでいたので励ました。(My friend was down after failing the exam, so I encouraged her.)
2. 先生の一言に励まされた。(I was encouraged by the teacher's one remark.)
3. 入院中の祖父を励ますために手紙を書いた。(I wrote a letter to encourage my grandfather, who was hospitalized.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners frequently confuse the transitive 励ます ("to encourage someone," 人を励ます) with the intransitive 励む ("to strive oneself," 勉強に励む) because of their near-identical form. Also distinguish from 慰める, which is for comforting sadness rather than motivating someone toward action.

## 猛烈 (もうれつ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** fierce, intense, violent (of degree)

**Similar words:**
- 激しい (はげしい) — intense/violent; a more general-purpose, neutral-register adjective for intensity (rain, emotion, competition) applicable in both formal and informal contexts.
- [強烈](vocab-15.md#強烈-きょうれつ) (きょうれつ) — strong/powerful; describes strong impact or impression (強烈な印象), a different nuance of intensity-of-effect rather than raw force.
- すさまじい — tremendous/terrific; used for awe-inspiring or frightening intensity, often of natural phenomena.

**Opposite words:**
- [穏やか](vocab-02.md#穏やか-おだやか) (おだやか) — mild/calm
- [緩やか](vocab-35.md#緩やか-ゆるやか) (ゆるやか) — gentle/gradual

**Example sentences:**
1. 今日は猛烈に暑い。(It's intensely hot today.)
2. 猛烈な台風が接近している。(A fierce typhoon is approaching.)
3. 彼は猛烈に働いて出世した。(He worked furiously and rose through the ranks.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 猛烈 is an emphatic intensifier for extremes (weather, effort, speed) and sounds more vivid/colloquial than the more neutral, broadly applicable 激しい. Learners sometimes overuse 猛烈 in contexts calling for calmer, more formal description, where 激しい is the safer general choice.

## 煩わしい (わずらわしい)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** troublesome, annoying, bothersome

**Similar words:**
- [面倒くさい](vocab-34.md#面倒くさい-めんどうくさい) (めんどうくさい) — bothersome/a hassle; more casual, everyday speech, versus the literary register of 煩わしい.
- うるさい — noisy/annoying; can describe literal noise or figurative "nagging/fussy," a different domain primarily about sound rather than tangled complexity.
- [厄介](vocab-34.md#厄介-やっかい) (やっかい) — troublesome/a nuisance; describes a difficult problem or burdensome person, leaning toward "a serious nuisance" rather than the repeated, low-grade nagging irritation of 煩わしい.

**Opposite words:**
- 簡単 (かんたん) — simple/easy
- [気楽](vocab-16.md#気楽-きらく) (きらく) — carefree

**Example sentences:**
1. 手続きが煩わしくて嫌になる。(The procedures are so troublesome it's off-putting.)
2. 人間関係が煩わしいと感じることがある。(Sometimes I feel human relationships are bothersome.)
3. 煩わしい書類仕事はもうたくさんだ。(I've had enough of this troublesome paperwork.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 煩わしい is more literary/formal than everyday 面倒くさい, and specifically connotes a tangled, nagging, low-grade irritation (repeated small tasks, complicated relationships) rather than acute difficulty. Learners often default to 面倒くさい in all registers and shouldn't swap in 厄介, which leans toward "a serious nuisance."

## 裸 (はだか)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** naked, bare

**Similar words:**
- [裸足](vocab-31.md#裸足-はだし) (はだし) — barefoot; a specific compound referring only to bare feet, not full-body nakedness.
- 丸裸 (まるはだか) — stark naked/completely bare; emphasizes totality, also figuratively "left with nothing" after losing everything.
- すっぽんぽん — colloquial/childish way to say "buck naked," informal register.

**Opposite words:**
- 着衣 (ちゃくい) — clothed (formal antonym, rarely used conversationally — 服を着ている is the natural phrase)

**Example sentences:**
1. 赤ちゃんを裸にしてお風呂に入れた。(I undressed the baby and put them in the bath.)
2. 裸で外を歩くことはできない。(You can't walk outside naked.)
3. 彼は事業に失敗して裸同然になった。(He failed in business and was left with virtually nothing.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 裸 extends figuratively beyond literal nudity to mean "stripped bare / with nothing added" — e.g., 裸の付き合い ("an unguarded, no-pretense relationship"), 裸一貫 ("starting with nothing but one's own effort"), 丸裸になる ("to lose everything financially"). Learners who only know the literal meaning often miss these common idiomatic extensions.

## 良識 (りょうしき)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** good sense, sound judgment

**Similar words:**
- 常識 (じょうしき) — common sense; baseline social norms expected of any reasonable member of society, more everyday than the elevated discernment of 良識.
- 分別 (ふんべつ) — discretion/good judgment; about distinguishing right from wrong or sensible from foolish in the moment, closely related but focused on practical discernment.

**Opposite words:**
- 非常識 (ひじょうしき) — lack of common sense/thoughtlessness

**Example sentences:**
1. 良識ある大人なら、そんなことはしない。(A sensible adult wouldn't do such a thing.)
2. 彼の発言は社会の良識を欠いている。(His remark lacks social good sense.)
3. 良識に従って行動してほしい。(I want people to act according to good sense.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 良識 is more elevated/formal than 常識 and often used in social or moral criticism (e.g., saying someone's behavior shows a lack of 良識), whereas 常識 is more everyday ("it's common sense to say thank you"). Learners sometimes use them interchangeably, but 良識 carries more weight about ethical maturity, not just factual social norms.

## 連帯 (れんたい)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** solidarity, joint responsibility

**Similar words:**
- 団結 (だんけつ) — unity/solidarity; emphasizes a group banding together for a common cause, more about collective spirit than the legal dimension of 連帯.
- [協力](vocab-15.md#協力-きょうりょく) (きょうりょく) — cooperation; working together on a task, more neutral/practical than the emotional or moral weight of 連帯 or 団結.

**Opposite words:**
- 分裂 (ぶんれつ) — division/split
- [対立](vocab-25.md#対立-たいりつ) (たいりつ) — confrontation/opposition

**Example sentences:**
1. 労働者たちは連帯して経営者に抗議した。(The workers united in solidarity to protest against management.)
2. 彼は友人の借金の連帯保証人になった。(He became a joint guarantor for his friend's debt.)
3. 国際社会の連帯が求められている。(International solidarity is being called for.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 連帯 has a distinct legal usage (連帯責任, 連帯保証人) meaning shared/joint liability — learners who only know the "solidarity" sense may not realize that in a contract, 連帯保証人 means someone equally liable for a debt, a much heavier commitment than an ordinary guarantor. Don't confuse with 団結, which lacks this legal liability dimension.

## 良心 (りょうしん)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** conscience

**Similar words:**
- 道徳心 (どうとくしん) — moral sense/ethical sense; about internalized ethical principles broadly, more abstract/academic than 良心.
- 罪悪感 (ざいあくかん) — guilt/sense of guilt; the emotional reaction when one's conscience is violated — what you feel when 良心 is pricked, not the faculty of conscience itself.
- Note the near-homophone trap: 両親 (りょうしん, "parents") sounds identical to 良心 (りょうしん, "conscience") — a classic listening-comprehension confusion.

**Opposite words:**
- No direct opposite (無慈悲, "mercilessness," or 悪意, "malice," are loosely contrastive but not true antonyms)

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は良心の呵責に苦しんでいる。(He is suffering from pangs of conscience.)
2. 良心に従って行動すべきだ。(One should act according to one's conscience.)
3. その嘘をついたことで良心が痛んだ。(My conscience ached from telling that lie.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The biggest trap is the homophone with 両親 ("parents") — in listening or quick speech, learners must rely on context/kanji to distinguish them. Separately, don't confuse 良心 (the inner moral faculty) with 罪悪感 (the guilty feeling resulting when that faculty is violated).

## 利潤 (りじゅん)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** profit

**Similar words:**
- [利益](vocab-33.md#利益-りえき) (りえき) — profit/benefit; the common, general-purpose word for profit or gain in everyday business and personal contexts.
- 儲け (もうけ) — earnings/gain; casual, colloquial word for making money, e.g. 儲けが出た.

**Opposite words:**
- [損失](vocab-24.md#損失-そんしつ) (そんしつ) — loss

**Example sentences:**
1. 企業は利潤を追求する。(Companies pursue profit.)
2. 利潤の最大化が経営の目的とされている。(Maximizing profit is considered the goal of management.)
3. 過度な利潤追求が格差を広げている。(Excessive profit-seeking is widening inequality.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 利潤 is more academic/economic in register than 利益, the everyday word for profit/benefit (including non-monetary benefit). Learners often default to 利益 everywhere, which is usually safe, but should recognize 利潤 as the more technical register used in economics texts, and avoid using the casual 儲け in formal writing.

## 夜更かし (よふかし)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** staying up late

**Similar words:**
- 徹夜 (てつや) — staying up all night; a complete, more extreme version involving no sleep at all, unlike 夜更かし which just means going to bed later than usual.
- 寝不足 (ねぶそく) — sleep deprivation; the resulting condition of insufficient sleep, not the act of staying up itself.

**Opposite words:**
- 早寝 (はやね) — going to bed early

**Example sentences:**
1. 夜更かしは体に悪い。(Staying up late is bad for your health.)
2. 子供が夜更かしするのを心配している。(I'm worried about my child staying up late.)
3. 昨日は映画を見て夜更かししてしまった。(I ended up staying up late watching a movie yesterday.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 夜更かし simply means going to bed later than usual, even if you still get some sleep, whereas 徹夜 specifically means staying awake the entire night without sleeping at all. Learners sometimes use 徹夜 when they just mean they went to bed a bit late, overstating the severity.

## 自ずから (おのずから)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** naturally, of itself, spontaneously

**Similar words:**
- 自然に (しぜんに) — naturally; the common, everyday equivalent, versus the literary/formal register of 自ずから.
- 自然と (しぜんと) — naturally/unconsciously; a slightly more casual variant for things that happen without conscious effort.
- おのずと — a near-identical, slightly more modern-sounding variant of 自ずから, largely interchangeable.

**Opposite words:**
- 人為的に (じんいてきに) — artificially/by human intervention

**Example sentences:**
1. 真剣に取り組めば、結果は自ずから付いてくる。(If you work at it earnestly, results will naturally follow.)
2. 時間が経てば、答えは自ずから明らかになるだろう。(As time passes, the answer will naturally become clear.)
3. 彼の人柄は、その行動から自ずから伝わってくる。(His character naturally comes through in his actions.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 自ずから is a literary, somewhat old-fashioned adverb that learners often confuse with everyday 自然に; 自ずから is inappropriate in casual conversation. Also note the kanji trap: 自ずから is distinct from 自ら (みずから, "oneself, in person") despite sharing the kanji 自 — they are read and mean differently.

## 頑固 (がんこ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** obstinate, stubborn

**Similar words:**
- [強情](vocab-01.md#強情-ごうじょう) (ごうじょう) — stubborn/headstrong; similar, but slightly more about refusing to yield in an argument, with willful defiance — unlike 頑固, it doesn't extend to stains or habits.
- 意地っ張り (いじっぱり) — stubborn/pig-headed; casual, often used affectionately or with mild exasperation about someone who won't back down out of pride.
- 頑な (かたくな) — obstinate/inflexible; emphasizes rigid refusal to change one's attitude or heart, often in emotional/relational contexts (頑なに心を閉ざす).

**Opposite words:**
- [素直](vocab-22.md#素直-すなお) (すなお) — obedient/compliant/open-minded
- [柔軟](vocab-20.md#柔軟-じゅうなん) (じゅうなん) — flexible

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は頑固で人の意見を聞かない。(He's stubborn and won't listen to others' opinions.)
2. このシミは頑固でなかなか落ちない。(This stain is stubborn and won't come out easily.)
3. 頑固親父という言葉がぴったりの人だ。(He's the perfect fit for the phrase "stubborn old man.")

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 頑固 uniquely extends to non-human things that are "stubbornly persistent" (頑固な汚れ "stubborn stain," 頑固な風邪 "a stubborn cold") — a usage learners often don't realize exists beyond describing personality. Distinguish from 強情, which is reserved for people's refusal to yield in a dispute and doesn't apply to stains or illnesses.

## 軽率 (けいそつ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** rashness, carelessness, thoughtlessness

**Similar words:**
- 軽はずみ (かるはずみ) — rash/thoughtless; a near-synonym, slightly more colloquial, for an impulsive action or remark done without thinking.
- 不用意 (ふようい) — careless/unguarded; emphasizes lack of preparation or caution, e.g. an unguarded remark made without considering consequences.
- うかつ — careless/inattentive; implies absent-mindedness or obliviousness rather than a flawed decision process specifically.

**Opposite words:**
- [慎重](vocab-21.md#慎重-しんちょう) (しんちょう) — careful/prudent/circumspect

**Example sentences:**
1. 軽率な発言で信頼を失った。(He lost trust due to a rash remark.)
2. そんな軽率な判断をするべきではない。(You shouldn't make such a rash decision.)
3. 軽率な行動が大きな問題を招いた。(Careless/rash action led to a big problem.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 軽率 specifically criticizes a lack of careful thought before speaking or acting, often in formal contexts (軽率な発言をお詫びします, "I apologize for my thoughtless remark"). Its direct antonym 慎重 is a frequently tested fixed pair (軽率⇔慎重) — don't confuse 軽率 with うかつ, which leans toward simple absent-mindedness rather than a flawed decision.

## 錯覚 (さっかく)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** illusion, misperception, optical illusion

**Similar words:**
- 幻覚 (げんかく) — hallucination; a much stronger term for perceiving something that isn't there at all, often associated with mental illness or drugs, unlike the normal misperception of 錯覚.
- 勘違い (かんちがい) — misunderstanding; a broader, everyday word for any mistaken belief, not limited to perceptual/sensory errors the way 錯覚 specifically is.
- 思い違い (おもいちがい) — a similar general misunderstanding, close to 勘違い.

**Opposite words:**
- No direct opposite

**Example sentences:**
1. 大きさが違って見えるのは目の錯覚だ。(The fact that the sizes look different is an optical illusion.)
2. 彼が自分を好きだと錯覚していた。(I was under the illusion that he liked me.)
3. これは有名な錯覚の例として知られている。(This is known as a famous example of an optical illusion.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 錯覚 specifically refers to a perceptual/sensory misjudgment (錯覚に陥る, "to fall into a misperception") — narrower than 勘違い, which covers any general misunderstanding, not necessarily perceptual (e.g., misremembering a date is 勘違い, not 錯覚). Also don't confuse with 幻覚, which implies perceiving something with no external stimulus at all.

## 所得 (しょとく)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** income

**Similar words:**
- [収入](vocab-20.md#収入-しゅうにゅう) (しゅうにゅう) — income/earnings; the broader, more common everyday term for all money coming in, including gross income before deductions.
- [給料](vocab-15.md#給料-きゅうりょう) (きゅうりょう) — salary/wages; specifically what you're paid by an employer, a subset/source of income rather than the general concept.

**Opposite words:**
- [支出](vocab-20.md#支出-ししゅつ) (ししゅつ) — expenditure/spending

**Example sentences:**
1. 所得税の申告をしなければならない。(I have to file an income tax return.)
2. 世帯の所得が減少している。(Household income is decreasing.)
3. 所得格差が社会問題になっている。(Income disparity has become a social problem.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 所得 is the term used in tax and economic/statistical contexts (income as defined by tax law), while 収入 is the everyday word for all money received before deductions. Learners sometimes use them interchangeably, but in official paperwork the distinction matters — per Japanese tax law, 所得 = 収入 minus necessary expenses.

## 対抗 (たいこう)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** rivalry, opposition, competing against

**Similar words:**
- [競争](vocab-15.md#競争-きょうそう) (きょうそう) — competition; a more neutral, general term for competing (sports, business, exams) without necessarily implying a reactive stance against a specific rival the way 対抗 does.
- [対立](vocab-25.md#対立-たいりつ) (たいりつ) — confrontation/opposition; about conflicting positions or interests, often ideological, broader than competitive rivalry.
- [ライバル](vocab-33.md#ライバル-らいばる) — rival (loanword); describes the person/entity itself, rather than the act of competing against them.

**Opposite words:**
- [協力](vocab-15.md#協力-きょうりょく) (きょうりょく) — cooperation
- 協調 (きょうちょう) — cooperation/coordination

**Example sentences:**
1. 二社は市場シェアを巡って対抗している。(The two companies are competing against each other over market share.)
2. 彼は対抗意識をむき出しにしていた。(He was openly showing his rivalry/competitive spirit.)
3. 値下げという対抗策を打ち出した。(They rolled out a countermeasure of price cuts in response to a rival.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 対抗 implies a reactive stance — positioning or acting against a specific rival or threat (対抗策, "countermeasure"; 対抗意識, "rivalry directed at someone") — distinct from 競争, which is neutral competition without necessarily implying a defensive reaction. Learners often use the two interchangeably, but 対抗 carries a stronger sense of "standing up against" a particular opponent.

## 中立 (ちゅうりつ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** neutrality

**Similar words:**
- [公平](vocab-19.md#公平-こうへい) (こうへい) — fair/impartial; about treating all sides equally/justly in judgment, a moral/procedural fairness concept that isn't quite the same as simply not taking a side.
- 客観的 (きゃっかんてき) — objective; about viewing something without personal bias, a cognitive stance rather than the political/relational stance of not aligning with any side.
- 不偏不党 (ふへんふとう) — impartial and non-partisan; a formal four-character idiom, often used for media or institutions claiming neutrality.

**Opposite words:**
- 偏向 (へんこう) — bias/partiality
- 加担 (かたん) — taking sides/complicity

**Example sentences:**
1. スイスは永世中立国として知られている。(Switzerland is known as a permanently neutral country.)
2. 報道機関は中立の立場を保つべきだ。(News organizations should maintain a neutral stance.)
3. 彼はどちらの意見にも中立を保った。(He remained neutral toward either opinion.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 中立 specifically means not aligning with or supporting either side of a conflict — it's about non-alignment, not necessarily about being "fair" to both sides (which is 公平). A judge should be both 中立 and 公平, but the concepts aren't identical — learners sometimes conflate "not taking sides" with "treating both sides well," which can diverge (silent/disengaged counts as 中立 but may not feel 公平 if one side needed more support).

## 苗 (なえ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** seedling

**Similar words:**
- [種](vocab-26.md#種-たね) (たね) — seed; the earlier stage before germination, from which a 苗 grows.
- 苗木 (なえぎ) — sapling; specifically a young tree, a subset of 苗 which can refer to any young plant (vegetables, rice), whereas 苗木 is tree-specific.
- [芽](vocab-02.md#芽-め) (め) — sprout/bud; the very first growth emerging from a seed, an earlier stage than a full seedling ready for transplanting.

**Opposite words:**
- No direct opposite (成木, "mature tree," is a loose growth-stage contrast)

**Example sentences:**
1. 田んぼに苗を植える。(We plant rice seedlings in the paddy field.)
2. トマトの苗を買ってきて庭に植えた。(I bought tomato seedlings and planted them in the garden.)
3. 苗がすくすくと育っている。(The seedlings are growing steadily.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 苗 refers to a young plant past the seed/sprout stage, ready (or nearly ready) for transplanting — distinct from 種 (the seed before planting) and 芽 (the first emerging sprout). Learners sometimes use these interchangeably, but they describe sequential growth stages (種→芽→苗), not synonyms.

## ひたすら
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** solely, single-mindedly, intently (also written 只管)

**Similar words:**
- 一生懸命 (いっしょうけんめい) — with all one's might/earnestly; emphasizes effort and exertion, whereas ひたすら emphasizes single-minded exclusivity of focus without necessarily implying strain.
- [只管](vocab-01.md#只管-ひたすら) (ひたすら) — the kanji spelling of the same word, not a separate word — historically associated with Zen Buddhist practice (只管打坐, "shikantaza," single-minded sitting meditation).
- 専ら (もっぱら) — exclusively/solely; close in meaning, often used for "mainly/exclusively" doing one thing, slightly more about exclusivity of topic than intensity of devotion.

**Opposite words:**
- No direct opposite

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼はひたすら謝り続けた。(He kept apologizing single-mindedly/profusely.)
2. 合格を目指してひたすら勉強した。(I studied intently, aiming to pass.)
3. ひたすら前を向いて歩き続けた。(I kept walking, looking only straight ahead, with single-minded focus.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ひたすら describes doing one thing to the exclusion of all else ("nothing but," single-mindedly) — describing the manner/exclusivity of focus, not the effort level, so don't conflate it with 一生懸命 (trying hard, which can apply even while juggling multiple concerns). Note: 只管 is simply the kanji spelling of the same word — not a separate vocabulary item, though learners may mistakenly treat it as one.

## もしくは
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** or, alternatively

**Similar words:**
- または — or; the most common, neutral equivalent used in everyday and written Japanese alike.
- あるいは — or, alternatively; also formal/written, often used for presenting an alternative possibility in a more reflective/literary tone.
- Both もしくは and あるいは are more formal than または; もしくは is especially common in legal/official text, where Japanese style nests もしくは for the narrower disjunction inside a larger または disjunction.

**Opposite words:**
- No direct opposite (かつ, "and," is the logical contrast, not a true antonym)

**Example sentences:**
1. 身分証明書は、パスポートもしくは運転免許証を提示してください。(Please present either a passport or a driver's license as ID.)
2. 申込書は郵送もしくはオンラインで提出できます。(The application can be submitted by mail or online.)
3. 会員もしくはその家族に限り、利用できます。(Only members or their families may use this.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** もしくは、または、あるいは are near-synonyms for "or," but differ in register — または is the all-purpose everyday choice; もしくは sounds formal/bureaucratic, common in legal/official text; あるいは leans literary/reflective. Learners often use または everywhere, which is safe casually, but it won't replicate legal-document register, nor will swapping もしくは into conversation where または is far more natural.

## 愛 (あい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** love, affection

**Similar words:**
- [愛情](#愛情-あいじょう)（あいじょう）— more explicitly emphasizes warm, caring feeling toward a specific person; 愛 is broader and more abstract/conceptual (love as a concept, romantic love, universal love).
- 恋（こい）— romantic infatuation/longing, usually one-directional or early-stage; 愛 implies a deeper, often mutual and lasting bond.
- 好き（すき）— casual "to like/love," used adjectivally in everyday speech; 愛 is more formal/literary and often used as a noun in writing or set phrases.

**Opposite words:**
- [憎しみ](vocab-30.md#憎しみ-にくしみ)（にくしみ）— hatred
- 無関心（むかんしん）— indifference

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女は子供たちに深い愛を注いでいる。— She pours deep love into her children.
2. 愛があれば困難も乗り越えられる。— With love, even hardships can be overcome.
3. その映画は愛と犠牲をテーマにしている。— The movie is themed around love and sacrifice.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 愛 alone sounds somewhat formal or literary in spoken Japanese; in casual conversation, 好き or 大好き is far more natural for "I love you/this." 愛してる is reserved for serious romantic declarations, not everyday affection.

**Similar words:**

- 変わらず（かわらず）— plain "unchangingly," less idiomatic/fixed than 相変わらず.

## 合図 (あいず)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a signal, a sign, a cue

**Similar words:**
- サイン — a loanword for "sign/signal," often used for more casual or sports/business contexts; 合図 feels more classic/Japanese and can involve physical gestures.
- [信号](vocab-21.md#信号-しんごう)（しんごう）— specifically refers to traffic lights or formal signaling systems, not interpersonal cues.
- 目配せ（めくばせ）— a signal specifically made with the eyes; 合図 is the general term covering any gesture, sound, or action.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼が手を上げたのを合図に、全員が走り出した。— At his raised-hand signal, everyone started running.
2. 開始の合図があるまで待ってください。— Please wait until the signal to start.
3. 彼女は目で合図を送った。— She sent a signal with her eyes.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 合図 is almost always used with する (合図する) or as 〜を合図に ("taking X as the cue/signal"); learners sometimes confuse it with 信号, which is reserved for mechanical/traffic signals, not human gestures.

## 間 (あいだ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** between (two things), interval, while

**Similar words:**
- 内（うち）— "within (a period)," e.g., 〜のうちに, focuses on completing something before a deadline rather than describing a duration.
- 中（なか）— "during/in the middle of," more about being inside a period or space, less about the gap between two points.
- 最中（さいちゅう）— emphasizes being right in the middle of an ongoing action, stronger than plain 間.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 東京と大阪の間には多くの都市がある。— There are many cities between Tokyo and Osaka.
2. 彼が寝ている間に出かけた。— I went out while he was sleeping.
3. 二人の間には深い信頼関係がある。— There is deep trust between the two of them.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 間 can mean a physical/temporal gap ("between A and B") or "while" (〜の間に), and learners often confuse 〜の間 (ongoing throughout) with 〜の間に (a single action occurring within that span) — the latter requires a punctual event.

## 相次ぐ (あいつぐ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to happen in succession, occur one after another

**Similar words:**
- [続く](vocab-28.md#続く-つづく)（つづく）— general "to continue," doesn't necessarily imply repeated distinct incidents; 相次ぐ specifically implies multiple separate events occurring close together.
- 連続する（れんぞくする）— "to occur consecutively," more neutral/statistical; 相次ぐ often carries a nuance of trouble or unwelcome events.
- 頻発する（ひんぱつする）— "to occur frequently," emphasizes frequency/rate rather than a direct chain of events.

**Opposite words:**
- 途絶える（とだえる）— to cease, stop occurring

**Example sentences:**
1. 今年は自然災害が相次いでいる。— Natural disasters have been occurring one after another this year.
2. 企業の倒産が相次いだ。— Corporate bankruptcies happened in succession.
3. 相次ぐ事故を受けて、安全対策が強化された。— Following the series of accidents, safety measures were strengthened.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 相次ぐ is almost always used for negative or newsworthy events (accidents, disasters, resignations) rather than neutral/positive repeated occurrences, and it's commonly used as 相次ぐ+noun (相次ぐ事故) or 相次いで+verb.

## 相手 (あいて)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** companion, partner, opponent, the other party

**Similar words:**

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 相手 shifts meaning heavily with context — sports/competition (opponent), conversation (the other person), or 〜を相手に (catering to/dealing with) — learners should not assume it always means "enemy."

#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** an idea

**Similar words:**
- [考え](vocab-13.md#考え-かんがえ)（かんがえ）— a more general "thought/opinion," while アイデア specifically suggests a creative or practical idea/solution.
- 発想（はっそう）— the broader mental approach or way of conceiving ideas, more about mindset than a single idea.

## 生憎 (あいにく)
#vocabulary

#vocabulary

**Meaning:** unclear, vague, ambiguous, equivocal

**Similar words:**
- 不明確（ふめいかく）— more formal/technical "unclear," often used in documents or reports; 曖昧 is more common in everyday and emotional contexts (vague feelings, unclear answers).

- [はっきり](vocab-31.md#はっきり-はっきり)（はっきり）— clearly, distinctly

3. 二人の関係は曖昧なままだった。— The relationship between the two remained ambiguous.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 曖昧 often carries a mildly negative nuance of evasiveness or irresponsibility (not committing to a clear answer), especially in 曖昧な態度/返事 — it's not neutral like "undefined" in English.

**Meaning:** a footprint, track; an achievement/legacy

**Similar words:**
- 痕跡（こんせき）— "trace/mark" left behind generally, broader than the specifically foot-related 足跡.

## あしからず (あしからず)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** please don't take offense; I'm sorry, but... (polite phrase softening a refusal)

**Similar words:**
- 悪しからず — alternate kanji form of the same phrase, same meaning and usage.
- 申し訳ございませんが — a more standard, broadly applicable polite apology phrase, while あしからず is a somewhat old-fashioned fixed expression specifically for softening a refusal/negative notice.
- ご了承ください — "please understand/accept," another common polite phrase used in similar contexts (notices, refusals).

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 都合によりキャンセルとなりました。あしからずご了承ください。— Due to circumstances, this has been cancelled. Please don't take offense and kindly understand.
2. 今回はお断りいたします、あしからず。— We must decline this time, please don't take it personally.
3. あしからずご容赦ください。— Please forgive us and not take offense.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** あしからず is a fairly formal/old-fashioned fixed phrase almost always used at the end of notices or polite refusals — it's rarely used in casual spoken conversation and tends to appear in written notices or formal announcements.

**Meaning:** underfoot, at one's feet; (figuratively) one's immediate situation/footing

**Similar words:**
- 足下（あしもと）— alternate kanji writing, identical meaning, stylistic choice only.

2. 暗くて足元が見えなかった。— It was dark and I couldn't see where I was stepping.

## 味わい (あじわい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** flavor, relish, an interesting/appealing quality

**Similar words:**
- 味（あじ）— plain "taste/flavor," more direct and literal; 味わい is more refined/nuanced, often describing a subtle or appreciated quality (of food, art, writing).
- 風味（ふうみ）— "flavor/aroma" especially of food, more technical/culinary than the broader, sometimes figurative 味わい.

- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. このお茶には独特の味わいがある。— This tea has a distinctive flavor.
2. 古い建物には味わいがある。— Old buildings have a certain charm/character.
3. 彼の文章には深い味わいがある。— His writing has a deep, appealing quality.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 味わい frequently extends beyond literal taste to describe aesthetic depth or charm (建物の味わい, 文章の味わい) — don't limit it to food contexts only.

## 味わう (あじわう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to taste, enjoy, appreciate, savor (food or experience)

**Similar words:**

- [楽しむ](vocab-26.md#楽しむ-たのしむ)（たのしむ）— "to enjoy," broader and less specifically about sensory/experiential appreciation than 味わう.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 味わう extends metaphorically to experiences and emotions (喜びを味わう, 苦しみを味わう), not just food — it implies deliberately, fully experiencing/appreciating something rather than just passing through it.

## 預かる (あずかる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to keep, look after, take charge of (something entrusted to you)

**Similar words:**
- [預ける](#預ける-あずける)（あずける）— the counterpart verb meaning "to entrust/deposit (to someone else)" — 預かる is from the receiving side, 預ける is from the giving side.
- 保管する（ほかんする）— "to store/keep in custody," more formal/institutional (e.g., warehouses, safes), less personal than 預かる.
- 世話をする（せわをする）— "to take care of," broader caregiving, while 預かる specifically implies temporary custody of something entrusted by another person.

**Opposite words:**
- [預ける](#預ける-あずける)（あずける）— to entrust, deposit

**Example sentences:**
1. 友達の荷物を預かっている。— I'm looking after my friend's luggage.
2. 子供を一時的に預かってもらった。— I had someone look after my child temporarily.
3. この書類は私が預かっておきます。— I'll take charge of keeping these documents.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 預かる (receive/hold in trust) and 預ける (entrust to someone) are a transitive pair often confused due to similar form — remember 預かる means "I am holding it for you," while 預ける means "I am giving it to you to hold."

## 預ける (あずける)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to entrust, deposit, leave in someone's care

**Similar words:**
- [預かる](#預かる-あずかる)（あずかる）— the reverse-direction counterpart meaning "to receive and keep (something entrusted)."
- 任せる（まかせる）— "to leave (a task/decision) up to someone," more about delegation of responsibility/judgment than physically entrusting an object.
- 託す（たくす）— "to entrust (hopes, a mission, an important matter)," more formal/emotionally weighted than the everyday 預ける.

**Opposite words:**
- [預かる](#預かる-あずかる)（あずかる）— to receive and look after

**Example sentences:**
1. 荷物をホテルのフロントに預けた。— I left my luggage with the hotel front desk.
2. 銀行にお金を預けている。— I keep my money deposited in the bank.
3. 子供を保育園に預けて働いている。— I work while leaving my child in the care of daycare.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 預ける takes を for the entrusted item and に for the recipient (荷物を人に預ける) — learners sometimes swap the particles or confuse direction with 預かる.

## 或る (ある)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a certain, one, some (used before a noun to introduce it vaguely)

**Similar words:**
- ある — the more common kana-only writing of the exact same word; 或る with kanji is rarer/more literary in modern text.
- とある — a near-synonym, slightly more casual/narrative, often used to open stories ("in a certain town...").
- 一つの（ひとつの）— "one (of)," more literal/numerical, lacking the vague "unspecified" nuance of ある.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 或る日、彼は突然失踪した。— One day, he suddenly disappeared.
2. 或る村に老人が住んでいた。— In a certain village lived an old man.
3. 或る人からこんな話を聞いた。— I heard this story from a certain person.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 或る is an adnominal (連体詞) and only modifies nouns directly (或る日、或る人) — it cannot be conjugated or used predicatively like the existential verb ある ("there is/to have"), which is written in kana and unrelated in this function.

## アルバイト (アルバイト)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a part-time job

**Similar words:**
- パート — also "part-time work," but typically associated with housewives/longer-term part-time staff, while アルバイト is more associated with students/younger workers and often shorter shifts.
- 副業（ふくぎょう）— a side job/second job held alongside a main job, a different concept from アルバイト which can be one's only job.
- 正社員（せいしゃいん）— full-time regular employee, the contrasting employment status to アルバイト.

- 正社員（せいしゃいん）— full-time employee

1. 大学生の時、カフェでアルバイトをしていた。— When I was a university student, I worked part-time at a café.

3. アルバイト代で旅行費用を貯めた。— I saved up travel funds with my part-time job wages.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** アルバイト (often shortened to バイト in casual speech) originally comes from German "Arbeit" (work) but in Japanese specifically means part-time/casual work, not "work" in general — don't confuse it with 仕事 (work/job broadly).

## あれ (あれ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** that (one) — distal demonstrative pronoun, referring to something far from both speaker and listener

**Similar words:**
- それ — "that (near the listener)," used for things closer to the listener than あれ, which is for things distant from both parties.
- これ — "this," for things near the speaker, forming the こ/そ/あ/ど demonstrative series with あれ.

**Opposite words:**
- これ（これ）— this

**Example sentences:**
1. あれは富士山ですか。— Is that Mt. Fuji (over there)?
2. あれ、財布がない。— Huh, my wallet's gone (expressing surprise).
3. あれから十年が経った。— Ten years have passed since that (event).

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** あれ also functions as an interjection expressing surprise or noticing something odd ("huh?," "oh?") — this usage is distinct from its demonstrative pronoun function and is very common in casual speech.

## あれこれ (あれこれ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** this and that; various things

**Similar words:**
- いろいろ — "various/many kinds," more general and widely applicable; あれこれ specifically implies going back and forth between multiple specific things (often with some fuss or deliberation).
- なんやかんや — colloquial "this and that/one thing or another," very casual, close in nuance to あれこれ but more conversational/Kansai-flavored.
- 様々（さまざま）— "various," more formal and written, lacking the back-and-forth nuance of あれこれ.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. あれこれ考えたが、結局結論は出なかった。— I thought about this and that, but in the end reached no conclusion.
2. 彼はあれこれ口出ししてくる。— He butts in with this comment and that.
3. あれこれ悩んだ末に決心した。— After agonizing over various things, I made up my mind.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** あれこれ often implies indecisive deliberation or fussing over multiple small things (あれこれ言う = "to nitpick/comment on everything") rather than simple neutral variety like いろいろ.

## 荒れる (あれる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to become rough/stormy; to fall into disorder/ruin; (skin) to become chapped

**Similar words:**
- 乱れる（みだれる）— "to become disordered/disheveled," overlaps for things like hair or a situation getting chaotic, but 荒れる has a stronger connotation of destructive roughness (weather, violence).
- [暴れる](vocab-03.md#暴れる-あばれる)（あばれる）— "to rampage/act violently," specifically about an agent's violent behavior, while 荒れる can describe weather or a general state without an active agent.
- 荒廃する（こうはいする）— "to fall into ruin/decay," more formal, often used for land, buildings, or society.

**Opposite words:**
- 治まる（おさまる）— to calm down, settle

**Example sentences:**
1. 海が荒れて船が出せなかった。— The sea became rough, so the boats couldn't set out.
2. 会議が荒れて収拾がつかなくなった。— The meeting became chaotic and got out of hand.
3. 冬になると肌が荒れやすい。— In winter, my skin tends to become chapped/rough.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 荒れる applies broadly to weather, meetings/situations turning chaotic, skin condition, and even a person's behavior becoming unruly (荒れた生活 = "a dissolute lifestyle") — the common thread is a shift from calm/orderly to rough/disordered.

## 泡 (あわ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a bubble, foam

**Similar words:**
- シャボン玉（しゃぼんだま）— specifically soap bubbles (the floating kind), a narrower concept than general 泡.
- 気泡（きほう）— "air bubble," more technical/scientific term, e.g., in liquids or materials.

- None notable
