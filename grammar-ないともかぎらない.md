# ～ないともかぎらない {#ないともかぎらない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ないとも限らない

**Furigana form:** ～ないともかぎらない

**Meaning:** "It's not necessarily true that X won't happen / X might possibly happen" — a cautious double-negative hedge acknowledging a low but nonzero possibility, often used to justify precaution.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ない形 + とも限らない
- Example base: ならない → ならないとも限らない

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal, usable in both speech and writing; common in cautionary advice or risk-conscious reasoning ("you never know, so better safe than sorry"). Polite and measured in tone.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **とはかぎらない** — "it's not always/necessarily true that X" (e.g., おもしろいとは限らない); the positive-stem counterpart, used to hedge against assuming something is always true, rather than hedging that something negative might still occur.
- **かもしれない** — "might happen"; a simpler, more general expression of possibility, without the double-negative cautionary framing of ないとも限らない, which specifically counters an assumption that something definitely won't happen.
- **ないでもない** — "it's not that X doesn't happen / there's a little bit of X"; a related double-negative softening construction, but used more for admitting a small degree of a feeling or possibility rather than warning about risk.

**Opposite grammar structures:** 絶対に～ない ("absolutely will not X") expresses the reverse — certainty that something won't happen, with no hedge.

**Example sentences:**
1. 将来重い病気にならないとも限らないから、保険に加入しようと考えている。 (You never know — I might end up with a serious illness someday, so I'm thinking of getting insurance.)
2. このデータが漏れないとも限らないので、厳重に管理すべきだ。 (This data might possibly leak, so it should be managed strictly.)
3. 地震がまた起こらないとも限らないから、備えは怠らないほうがいい。 (Another earthquake might well happen again, so you shouldn't neglect preparedness.)
4. 彼が約束を破らないとも限らない。慎重に進めよう。 (He might well break his promise. Let's proceed carefully.)
5. この計画が失敗しないとも限らないので、代替案も用意しておこう。 (This plan might possibly fail, so let's also prepare an alternative.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The double negative (ない...とも限らない) is logically "it's not limited to [not happening]," which equals "it could happen" — learners sometimes get tangled trying to parse the double negative literally. Treat the whole phrase as a fixed chunk meaning "you can't rule it out," almost always used to justify taking a precaution, rather than as a neutral statement of probability.
