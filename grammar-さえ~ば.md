# ～さえ～ば {#さえば}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～さえ～ば

**Furigana form:** ～さえ～ば (no special kanji reading)

**Meaning:** "If only X / as long as just X" — expresses that a single minimum condition is sufficient for a result to hold; emphasizes that nothing more than that condition is needed.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + さえ + Verb/adj (ば form)
- Verb (ます stem) + さえすれば
- お金さえあれば、幸せになれるわけではない (It's not that you'll be happy just because you have money)
- 練習さえすれば、上手になる (As long as you practice, you'll get better)

**Typical context:** Common in both spoken and written Japanese; used to stress a minimal sufficient condition, often in advice or conditional reasoning.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **たら／れば (alone)** — plain conditional, lacks the "only this much is needed" emphasis that さえ adds.
- **さえ (alone)** — the emphatic "even X" use without the conditional ば, different function.
- **さえあれば** — common fixed phrase variant meaning "if only there were X."

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though ～ても (even if X, [result doesn't follow]) can serve as a contrasting structure when the minimal condition turns out insufficient.

**Example sentences:**
1. 時間さえあれば、もっと勉強できるのに。
   (If only I had time, I could study more.)
2. この薬を飲みさえすれば、すぐに治りますよ。
   (As long as you just take this medicine, you'll get better right away.)
3. あなたさえいてくれれば、私は何も怖くない。
   (As long as you're with me, I'm not afraid of anything.)
4. 基本さえ理解していれば、応用問題も解ける。
   (As long as you understand the basics, you can solve applied problems too.)
5. 一言、謝りさえすれば、彼は許してくれただろう。
   (If he had just apologized, he would have been forgiven.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern requires BOTH さえ and the conditional ば/すれば to form the complete "if only X" meaning — using さえ alone without the conditional changes the meaning to simple emphatic "even." Also note that さえ attaches to the noun/stem before the conditional form, not after it.
