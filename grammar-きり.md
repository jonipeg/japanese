# ～きり {#きり}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～きり

**Furigana form:** ～きり

**Meaning:** Two related usages: (1) "only/just X" — a plain limiting function; (2) "ever since X, nothing has happened since / that was the last time" — marks a single past instance with no expected continuation since.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + きり（二人きり — "just the two of us"）
- 動詞た形 + きり + 否定表現（行ったきり帰ってこない — "went and hasn't come back since"）

**Typical context:** Spoken and written, neutral-casual register — common in storytelling and everyday conversation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**だけ**](grammar-だけ.md) — plain "only," a simple limiting word without きり's "and nothing since" temporal implication.
- **きる（切る）** — a separate grammar point meaning "to do completely" (see below); shares a reading and kanji origin but has a distinct meaning — don't confuse the two.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼とはあの日会ったきり、一度も連絡がない。
   (I haven't heard from him once since I saw him that day.)
2. 二人きりで話したいことがある。
   (There's something I want to talk about, just the two of us.)
3. 母は入院したきり、退院できていない。
   (My mother was hospitalized and hasn't been able to leave since.)
4. その本は一度読んだきり、本棚にしまったままだ。
   (I read that book once and it's been sitting on the shelf ever since.)
5. 彼女は国へ帰ったきり、戻ってこない。
   (She went back to her home country and hasn't come back since.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Don't confuse the noun-limiting usage (二人きり, "just the two") with the "nothing has happened since" usage (た形 + きり), which always implies an expected continuation that failed to occur. Also distinct from きる ("to do completely").
