# ～ながらに(して) {#ながらにして}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～ながらに(して)

**Furigana form:** ～ながらに(して)

**Meaning:** "While remaining in the state of ~" / "Just as ~ is, without change" — describes a state that stays exactly as it is while some action or situation takes place, often highlighting that something happens without needing to leave or change that state.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ます形／名詞 ＋ ながらに(して)
- 家にいながらにして世界中の人と交流できる — "can interact with people around the world without even leaving home"

**Typical context:** Written, somewhat formal/literary register — used in essays, news writing, and reflective narration. Several forms (生まれながらに, 涙ながらに, 昔ながらの) are fixed, idiomatic expressions rather than freely productive grammar.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**～まま**](grammar-まま(に).md) — "while remaining ~ / as is," a more general and productive way to express an unchanged state, usable with a much wider range of verbs and nouns than the restricted ながらに(して).
- [**～つつ**](grammar-つつ.md) — can express simultaneity ("while doing ~"), but lacks the "unchanged state" nuance central to ながらに(して).
- **昔ながらの** specifically pairs with **伝統的な** ("traditional") in meaning, describing something that has preserved an old style or atmosphere.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a phrase like ～が変わって／を離れて ("having changed from ~ / having left ~") would describe the reverse — a state no longer being preserved.

**Example sentences:**
1. この子は生まれながらに優れた音感を持っていた。
   (This child was born with an excellent sense of pitch.)
2. インターネットのおかげで、今は家にいながらにして世界中の人と交流できる。
   (Thanks to the internet, you can now interact with people all over the world without even leaving home.)
3. この辺りは昔ながらの田舎の雰囲気が残っている。
   (This area still retains the old-fashioned, rustic atmosphere it has always had.)
4. その女性は母親との死別を涙ながらに語った。
   (The woman spoke tearfully about the loss of her mother.)
5. 彼は病床にありながらにして、最後まで研究を続けた。
   (Even while confined to his sickbed, he continued his research to the very end.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ながらに(して) attaches only to a small, fairly fixed set of words (生まれ, 家, 涙, 昔, 女／男 in some expressions, etc.) — it is not a freely productive pattern like ながら or まま, so learners should treat most occurrences as set phrases to memorize rather than a template to build new sentences with freely. 涙ながらに specifically means "while crying" (= 泣きながら) and should be learned as its own idiom.
