# ～がてら {#がてら}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～がてら

**Furigana form:** ～がてら

**Meaning:** "While/on the occasion of doing X, also do Y" — describes combining two actions/purposes into a single trip or occasion, where Y is accomplished incidentally while primarily doing X.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ます形 (verb ます-stem) + がてら
- 名詞 (noun, especially action nouns like 散歩, 買い物) + がてら
- Example from source: 散歩がてら (while taking a walk)

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-slightly-formal, usable in both spoken and written Japanese. No politeness/rudeness connotation; commonly used in casual conversation to describe efficiently combining errands or activities. Slightly more common in speech than its near-synonym かたがた, which leans more formal/written.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**かたがた**](grammar-かたがた.md) — near-synonym, "combining two purposes" (often in formal greetings, e.g., お礼かたがた, "while expressing thanks"); かたがた is more formal/written and often used with set phrases of greeting or courtesy, while がてら is more casual and general-purpose. These were tested as mutual distractors.
- [**ながら**](grammar-ながら.md) — "while doing X" (simultaneous actions); ながら describes two actions happening at the same literal moment (e.g., 歩きながら話す, "talking while walking"), whereas がてら describes one trip serving two purposes, not necessarily simultaneous actions.
- [**ついでに**](grammar-ついでに.md) — "while at it, incidentally also doing Y"; very close in meaning to がてら, but ついでに is more casual/colloquial and can be used as a standalone adverbial phrase more flexibly, while がてら attaches directly to a specific verb/noun.
- [**かたわら**](grammar-かたわら.md) — "while also doing X (as a side activity, often long-term)," such as balancing a job and a hobby over an extended period; がてら is about a single combined trip/occasion, while かたわら describes an ongoing, parallel long-term activity.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 散歩がてら、買い物に行きませんか。
   (Why don't we go shopping while we're out for a walk?)
2. 駅まで送りがてら、ちょっと喫茶店に寄った。
   (While walking her to the station, I stopped by a cafe for a bit.)
3. 運動がてら、犬の散歩をしている。
   (I walk the dog as a way of getting some exercise.)
4. 友人の様子を見がてら、お土産を届けに行った。
   (I went to check on my friend while also delivering a souvenir.)
5. 観光がてら、現地の名物料理を食べ歩いた。
   (While sightseeing, I went around eating the local specialty dishes.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** No confirmed student wrong-answer is recorded in the source for this pattern, but it's explicitly noted as a near-synonym and mutual distractor with かたがた. The key tell: がてら is the more general, everyday choice for "doing Y incidentally while out doing X," while かたがた is reserved for more formal, often greeting-related contexts (e.g., visiting someone "かたがた" to offer thanks and pay respects). Students should default to がてら unless the context is clearly formal/ceremonial.
