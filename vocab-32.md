## 引き出す (ひきだす)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to draw out, pull out, bring out, withdraw (money)

**Similar words:**
- [取り出す](vocab-30.md#取り出す-とりだす)（とりだす）— "to take out," more general, often for retrieving an item from inside a container, while 引き出す often implies drawing out something less tangible (abilities, information) or cash from an account.
- 下ろす（おろす）— also used for "withdrawing money" (お金を下ろす), near-synonymous with 引き出す in banking contexts.

**Opposite words:**
- [預ける](vocab-06.md#預ける-あずける)（あずける）— to deposit

**Example sentences:**
1. ATMでお金を引き出した。— I withdrew money from the ATM.
2. 教師は生徒の潜在能力を引き出した。— The teacher drew out the students' latent potential.
3. 尋問で重要な情報を引き出すことに成功した。— They succeeded in drawing out important information during the interrogation.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 引き出す frequently appears in the figurative sense of "bringing out hidden potential/information" (能力・本音を引き出す), not just the literal act of withdrawing cash — both uses are common and worth distinguishing from context.

## 引き出し (ひきだし)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a drawer; (figurative) one's range of knowledge/skills to draw upon

**Similar words:**
- 戸棚（とだな）— "cupboard/cabinet," a larger storage furniture piece, distinct from the smaller sliding 引き出し.
- レパートリー — "repertoire," a loanword near-synonym for the figurative sense of 引き出し (持ち引き出しが多い, "having many things to draw upon").

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 机の引き出しに書類をしまった。— I put the documents away in the desk drawer.
2. 彼は引き出しの多い人で、話題が尽きない。— He's a person with a wide range of knowledge, so conversation with him never runs dry.
3. タンスの引き出しから洋服を取り出した。— I took clothes out of the dresser drawer.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Beyond the literal furniture meaning, 引き出し is commonly used figuratively to describe a person's "range" of knowledge, skills, or ideas they can draw upon (引き出しが多い/少ない) — an idiom learners often miss.

## 引き取る (ひきとる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to take over, take charge of, take back, take in (e.g. a person or animal)

**Similar words:**
- 引き受ける（ひきうける）— "to undertake/accept responsibility for" a task, overlapping with one sense of 引き取る, though 引き取る more often involves physically taking someone/something into one's care.
- 回収する（かいしゅう）— "to collect/retrieve," more neutral and often for objects (products, defective items), while 引き取る can apply to people too (孤児を引き取る).

**Opposite words:**
- 引き渡す（ひきわたす）— to hand over

**Example sentences:**
1. 親戚が孤児を引き取って育てた。— A relative took in the orphan and raised them.
2. 不良品を引き取ってもらった。— I had the defective product taken back.
3. 彼は会社の経営を引き取ることになった。— He ended up taking over management of the company.
4.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 引き取る covers taking responsibility for people (孤児を引き取る), returning defective goods, and taking over duties — it's a flexible verb whose exact nuance depends heavily on the object, so pay attention to context.

## 引き離す (ひきはなす)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to separate, keep apart, pull apart; to outdistance (a competitor)

**Similar words:**
- [分ける](vocab-36.md#分ける-わける)（わける）— general "to divide/separate," broader and more neutral than 引き離す, which often implies forcibly pulling two things/people apart.
- 突き放す（つきはなす）— "to push away/reject," more emotionally charged, implying deliberate rejection rather than just physical/competitive separation.

**Opposite words:**
- 近づける（ちかづける）— to bring closer together

**Example sentences:**
1. けんかする二人を無理やり引き離した。— I forcibly separated the two who were fighting.
2. 彼は2位の選手を大きく引き離してゴールした。— He finished the race, having pulled far ahead of the second-place runner.
3. 戦争が家族を引き離してしまった。— The war tore the family apart.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 引き離す has both a literal/emotional sense (separating people, as in family or a fight) and a competitive sense (outdistancing a rival in a race) — these are quite different contexts using the same verb.

## 引く (ひく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to pull, draw back, subtract, play (a stringed/keyboard instrument), catch (a cold), look up (a dictionary)

**Similar words:**
- [弾く](vocab-31.md#弾く-はじく)（ひく）— homophone with different kanji used specifically for playing stringed/keyboard instruments (ピアノを弾く); plain 引く covers a much wider range of meanings.
- 押す（おす）— "to push," the direct opposite action in the physical sense.

**Opposite words:**
- 押す（おす）— to push

**Example sentences:**
1. ドアを引いて開けてください。— Please pull the door open.
2. 合計から経費を引いた。— I subtracted the expenses from the total.
3. 彼女はピアノを美しく引いた。— (Note: for playing piano, 弾く is the correct kanji) She played the piano beautifully.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 引く is an extremely versatile verb covering subtraction (引き算), catching a cold (風邪を引く), looking something up (辞書を引く), and pulling — but playing an instrument specifically uses the different kanji 弾く (ひく), a common point of confusion in writing.

## 低い (ひくい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** low, short (in height)

**Similar words:**
- 短い（みじかい）— "short," used for length/duration, not height — don't confuse with 低い, which specifically means low height/altitude/level.
- 小さい（ちいさい）— "small," about overall size, while 低い is specifically about vertical height/level.

**Opposite words:**
- 高い（たかい）— high, tall, expensive

**Example sentences:**
1. この天井は低いので頭をぶつけないように。— This ceiling is low, so be careful not to hit your head.
2. 彼は背が低いほうだ。— He's on the shorter side (in height).
3. 気温が低い日は体調管理に気をつけよう。— Let's be mindful of our health on days when the temperature is low.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 低い is specifically used for height/level/altitude/pitch (低い山, 低い声) — don't substitute 短い (short in length/duration) where 低い (low in height) is needed; they are not interchangeable.

## 皮肉 (ひにく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** irony, sarcasm, satire

**Similar words:**
- 嫌味（いやみ）— "a snide/catty remark," more personally pointed and mean-spirited than the broader, sometimes situational irony of 皮肉.
- アイロニー — a loanword near-synonym for literary/situational "irony," often used in more academic or literary criticism contexts.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は皮肉を込めて「立派だね」と言った。— He said "How impressive" with a sarcastic tone.
2. 皮肉なことに、彼の失敗がチームを救った。— Ironically, his failure ended up saving the team.
3. 彼女の皮肉な笑みが気になった。— Her ironic/sarcastic smile bothered me.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 皮肉 can describe both a sarcastic remark aimed at a person (嫌味に近い) and a broader situational irony (皮肉なことに, "ironically") — don't assume it's always about mocking someone; it also covers life's ironic twists.

## 批判 (ひはん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** criticism, to criticize, comment critically

**Similar words:**
- 非難（ひなん）— "condemnation/blame," stronger and more emotionally charged than the more measured, often constructive 批判.
- [批評](#批評-ひひょう)（ひひょう）— "critique/review," often more analytical and evaluative (e.g. of art or literature), while 批判 leans toward pointing out faults or wrongdoing.

**Opposite words:**
- 称賛（しょうさん）— praise

**Example sentences:**
1. 政府の対応に対する批判が高まっている。— Criticism of the government's response is mounting.
2. 建設的な批判は成長につながる。— Constructive criticism leads to growth.
3. 彼はそのプランを厳しく批判した。— He criticized the plan harshly.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 批判 is somewhat more neutral than 非難 — it can mean fair, reasoned criticism (建設的な批判) rather than pure condemnation, though in harsher contexts it can overlap closely with 非難.

## 批評 (ひひょう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** criticism, critique, review, commentary

**Similar words:**
- レビュー — a loanword often used for casual reviews (of products, movies), while 批評 implies a more serious, often professional/artistic critique.
- 評論（ひょうろん）— "commentary/critical essay," very close to 批評, often used for published critical writing on literature, film, or society.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. この映画は批評家から高く評価された。— This movie was highly praised by critics.
2. 彼の小説に対する批評は賛否両論だった。— The critiques of his novel were mixed (both praise and criticism).
3. 文学批評を専門とする学者がいる。— There are scholars who specialize in literary criticism.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 批評 is generally more analytical/evaluative than 批判, and is strongly associated with art, literature, and media criticism (映画批評, 文芸批評) — it doesn't necessarily carry a negative connotation the way 批判 sometimes does.

## 皮膚 (ひふ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** skin (medical/biological term)

**Similar words:**
- [肌](vocab-31.md#肌-はだ)（はだ）— the everyday, more sensory/aesthetic word for skin (used in beauty contexts); 皮膚 is the clinical, medical term used by doctors and in biology.
- 表皮（ひょうひ）— "epidermis," a more technical subcategory referring specifically to the outer layer of skin.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 皮膚科で診てもらった。— I had it examined at the dermatology clinic.
2. 強い日差しは皮膚に悪影響を与える。— Strong sunlight has a bad effect on the skin.
3. 皮膚がかゆくて困っている。— My skin is itchy and it's bothering me.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Use 皮膚 in medical/clinical contexts (皮膚科, 皮膚病) and 肌 in everyday/beauty contexts (肌がきれい) — mixing them up isn't grammatically wrong but sounds unnatural in register (saying 皮膚がきれい sounds overly clinical for a compliment).

## 秘密 (ひみつ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a secret; privacy, secrecy

**Similar words:**
- プライバシー — "privacy," a loanword focused on personal information one wants kept private, while 秘密 is broader and can refer to any kind of secret (plans, facts, feelings).
- 内緒（ないしょ）— more casual synonym for "secret," often used between friends/family (ないしょ話, secret talk), while 秘密 is more neutral/formal.

**Opposite words:**
- 公開（こうかい）— disclosure, making public

**Example sentences:**
1. これは二人だけの秘密にしておこう。— Let's keep this a secret between just the two of us.
2. 彼は秘密主義で、自分のことをあまり話さない。— He's secretive and doesn't talk much about himself.
3. 会社の機密情報は秘密厳守だ。— The company's confidential information is kept strictly secret.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 秘密 is neutral in register and widely used in both casual and formal contexts, whereas 内緒 is distinctly casual/intimate — choose based on the formality of the situation (内緒話 among friends vs. 企業秘密 for corporate secrets).

## 皮肉る (ひにくる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to speak ironically, be sarcastic, make a sarcastic remark

**Similar words:**
- からかう — "to tease/make fun of," more playful and less pointed than the sharper 皮肉る.
- 嫌味を言う（いやみをいう）— "to make a snide remark," very close in meaning, often used interchangeably with 皮肉る in casual speech.

**Opposite words:**
- 称賛する（しょうさんする）— to praise

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は上司のやり方を皮肉った。— He made a sarcastic remark about his boss's approach.
2. 友人は私の遅刻を皮肉るように笑った。— My friend laughed sarcastically about my being late.
3. 彼女はいつも皮肉った言い方をする。— She always speaks in a sarcastic way.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 皮肉る is the verb form derived from the noun 皮肉 — it's less common in everyday spoken Japanese than just using 皮肉を言う (to say something ironic/sarcastic), so both forms are worth recognizing.

## 疲労 (ひろう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** fatigue, exhaustion, being tired

**Similar words:**
- [疲れ](vocab-28.md#疲れ-つかれ)（つかれ）— the plain, everyday noun for "tiredness," derived from 疲れる; 疲労 is the more formal/medical term, often used in health contexts (疲労回復, 慢性疲労).
- [過労](vocab-14.md#過労-かろう)（かろう）— "overwork," a specific cause of extreme fatigue from excessive working, narrower than general 疲労.

**Opposite words:**
- [元気](vocab-18.md#元気-げんき)（げんき）— energy, vitality

**Example sentences:**
1. 長時間労働で疲労がたまっている。— Fatigue has built up from long working hours.
2. 疲労回復には十分な睡眠が必要だ。— Adequate sleep is necessary for recovering from fatigue.
3. 彼は慢性的な疲労に悩まされている。— He is troubled by chronic fatigue.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 疲労 is the formal/medical register word (used in health and workplace contexts, 疲労回復・過労死) — in casual speech, 疲れ/疲れる is far more natural ("疲れた" rather than "疲労した" after a long day).

## 披露宴 (ひろうえん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a wedding reception, a banquet to announce a marriage

**Similar words:**
- 結婚式（けっこんしき）— "wedding ceremony," the formal ceremony itself (often religious/civil), distinct from 披露宴, which is the subsequent reception/banquet celebrating the marriage with guests.
- パーティー — a general loanword for "party," much broader and less specific than the formal, ritualized 披露宴.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 結婚式の後、披露宴が開かれた。— After the wedding ceremony, a reception was held.
2. 披露宴でスピーチを頼まれた。— I was asked to give a speech at the wedding reception.
3. 披露宴の料理はとても豪華だった。— The food at the wedding reception was very lavish.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 披露宴 is distinct from 結婚式 — the ceremony (式) and the reception (披露宴) are often separate events in a Japanese wedding, so don't use the two terms interchangeably.

## 開く (ひらく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to open, widen, spread; to hold (an event); to broaden (one's knowledge/mind)

**Similar words:**
- 開ける（あける）— very close synonym, often interchangeable for physically opening something (ドアを開ける/開く), though 開く is also intransitive and used more for abstract or formal senses (会を開く, "to hold a meeting").
- 開催する（かいさいする）— more formal synonym specifically for "to hold/host" an event, overlapping with one sense of 開く.

**Opposite words:**
- 閉じる（とじる）— to close
- 閉める（しめる）— to close (transitive)

**Example sentences:**
1. 窓を開くと涼しい風が入ってきた。— When I opened the window, a cool breeze came in.
2. 来月、展覧会が開かれる予定だ。— An exhibition is scheduled to be held next month.
3. 旅を通じて視野を開くことができた。— Through traveling, I was able to broaden my horizons.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 開く is both transitive and intransitive (ドアが開く/ドアを開く) unlike the strictly transitive 開ける — it's also used abstractly for holding events (会議を開く) and broadening perspective (視野を開く), extensions easy to overlook.

## 広場 (ひろば)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a public square, an open space (in a town/city)

**Similar words:**
- [公園](vocab-18.md#公園-こうえん)（こうえん）— "park," typically includes greenery/playground equipment, while 広場 is more of an open paved/gathering space, often for events or markets.
- 空き地（あきち）— "vacant lot," an undeveloped open space, without the public/communal purpose implied by 広場.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 駅前の広場でイベントが開かれた。— An event was held in the square in front of the station.
2. 子どもたちは広場で遊んでいる。— The children are playing in the square.
3. 広場には噴水がある。— There is a fountain in the square.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 広場 often implies a specifically public, urban gathering space (often with a fountain, monument, or market) — distinct from a green, leisure-oriented 公園, even though both are "open spaces."

## 広める (ひろめる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to spread, widen, popularize, broaden (knowledge, information, a custom)

**Similar words:**
- 広がる（ひろがる）— the intransitive counterpart ("to spread, become widespread"), while 広める is transitive ("to spread something").
- 普及させる（ふきゅうさせる）— "to popularize/disseminate," more formal, often used for technology or practices becoming widespread in society.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼はこの技術を世界に広めようとしている。— He is trying to spread this technology around the world.
2. 伝統文化を広める活動をしている。— I'm engaged in activities to spread traditional culture.
3. うわさはあっという間に広められた。— The rumor was spread in no time.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Remember the transitive/intransitive pair 広める(他)/広がる(自) — 広める takes an object (〜を広める, "to spread something"), while 広がる describes something spreading on its own (〜が広がる).

## 表 (ひょう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a table, a list, a chart

**Similar words:**
- [グラフ](vocab-16.md#グラフ-グラフ) — "graph," a visual chart with plotted data, distinct from 表, which is typically a grid of rows/columns (a data table).
- リスト — "list," simpler and less structured than a 表, which usually implies organized rows and columns.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 売上データを表にまとめた。— I compiled the sales data into a table.
2. この表を見れば、傾向が一目で分かる。— If you look at this chart, you can see the trend at a glance.
3. 時間割の表を作成した。— I created a timetable (schedule table).

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 表 (ひょう, table/chart) is a homograph with the far more common 表 (おもて, "the front/surface" or "outside," as in 表と裏) — same kanji, completely different reading and meaning depending on context, so don't assume the reading from the kanji alone.

## 評価 (ひょうか)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** evaluation, appraisal, assessment

**Similar words:**
- 判断（はんだん）— "judgment," the act of deciding something; 評価 specifically means assigning a value or rating to something.
- 査定（さてい）— "assessment" used in more formal/technical contexts like insurance or salary review; 評価 is broader and includes personal opinion.
- 採点（さいてん）— "grading/scoring," specifically for tests or assignments; narrower than 評価.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable (評価 can be positive or negative, e.g. 高い評価/低い評価, so it doesn't have a true antonym)

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼の仕事は上司から高く評価されている。— His work is highly rated by his boss.
2. この商品の評価を見てから買うかどうか決めよう。— Let's decide whether to buy it after checking the reviews.
3. 彼女の努力は正当に評価されるべきだ。— Her efforts should be fairly evaluated.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 評価する doesn't always mean "to praise" — it's neutral and means "to assess/rate," which can be high or low (評価が低い = rated poorly). Learners often assume 評価する always has a positive connotation like English "to value," but context determines whether it's good or bad.

## 評判 (ひょうばん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** reputation, popularity, rumor, word of mouth

**Similar words:**
- 名声（めいせい）— "fame/renown," more formal and typically positive; 評判 can be neutral or even negative.
- [噂](vocab-08.md#噂-うわさ)（うわさ）— "rumor/gossip," about a specific event or person's private matter; 評判 is about general public opinion of quality or character.
- [人気](vocab-30.md#人気-にんき)（にんき）— "popularity," focuses on being well-liked; 評判 is broader, about what people are saying in general.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable (depends on context: 良い評判 vs 悪い評判)

**Example sentences:**
1. このレストランは評判がいい。— This restaurant has a good reputation.
2. 彼の評判は会社中に広まっている。— His reputation has spread throughout the company.
3. そのニュースが評判になった。— That news became the talk of the town.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 評判 refers to what people say/think about someone or something (社会的な評価), distinct from 評価 which is a more deliberate assessment of quality or merit. Also note 評判になる means "to become a hot topic," not simply "to be rated."

## 表現 (ひょうげん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** expression, representation (of thoughts/feelings in words, art, etc.)

**Similar words:**
- 表明（ひょうめい）— "declaration," more formal, for explicitly stating an opinion/position; 表現 is broader, about any form of conveying meaning (artistic, verbal).
- 言い方（いいかた）— "way of speaking/phrasing," focuses on the specific wording used; 表現 is more general and can include non-verbal forms.
- [描写](vocab-04.md#描写-びょうしゃ)（びょうしゃ）— "depiction/description," specifically for describing a scene or situation in writing; 表現 covers all forms of expressing meaning.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼の絵は感情をよく表現している。— His paintings express emotion well.
2. その表現は少し失礼だと思う。— I think that way of putting it is a bit rude.
3. 日本語には微妙な表現がたくさんある。— Japanese has many subtle expressions.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 表現 refers to the act or result of expressing something (speech, writing, art), while 表情（ひょうじょう）refers specifically to facial expression — learners often confuse the two words because of the shared 表 character.

## 標準 (ひょうじゅん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** standard, benchmark, norm

**Similar words:**
- 基準（きじゅん）— "criterion/benchmark," used for a specific measure to judge by; 標準 is the general accepted norm or average level.
- 平均（へいきん）— "average," a calculated midpoint; 標準 is more about an established reference point, not necessarily a mathematical average.
- 規格（きかく）— "specification/standard (technical)," used for manufactured products; 標準 is broader and can apply to behavior, quality, or skill level too.

**Opposite words:**
- [特殊](vocab-04.md#特殊-とくしゅ)（とくしゅ）— special, exceptional
- 異例（いれい）— unusual, exceptional (case)

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼のテストの点は標準を上回っている。— His test score is above standard.
2. この製品は国際標準に従って作られている。— This product is made according to international standards.
3. 標準的な日本語を話すように心がけている。— I try to speak standard Japanese.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 標準 describes a neutral reference point (neither great nor bad), so 標準以下 (below standard) carries a negative nuance while 標準的 just means "typical/ordinary" — it's not inherently a compliment like "high standard" in English.

## 平等 (びょうどう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

2. 本舗直送の商品はここでしか買えない。— Products shipped directly from the main store can only be bought here.
3. 老舗本舗の味は今も変わらない。— The flavor of the long-established main shop hasn't changed to this day.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 本舗 is an old-fashioned, traditional term typically seen in shop names for long-established businesses (especially food/confectionery), evoking authenticity and heritage — modern companies almost always use 本店 instead, so 本舗 signals a traditional/historic brand identity.

## 止せる (よせる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to stop, give up, cancel (doing something)

**Similar words:**
- やめる — "to stop/quit," the extremely common everyday verb for stopping an action or habit; 止せる is a less common, slightly archaic-sounding alternative often seen in the imperative よせ (knock it off!).
- 中止する（ちゅうしする）— "to cancel/call off," formal, used for events/plans; 止せる is more personal, about stopping one's own or someone else's behavior.

**Opposite words:**
- [続ける](vocab-28.md#続ける-つづける)（つづける）— to continue

**Example sentences:**
1. 「もうけんかは止せ!」と父に怒られた。— "Stop fighting already!" my father scolded.
2. そんな危険なことは止せたほうがいい。— You'd better give up doing such dangerous things.
3. 彼を止めようとしたが、聞いてくれなかった。— I tried to stop him, but he wouldn't listen.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 止せ (the imperative form) is a blunt, somewhat rough way to tell someone to stop/knock it off, more commonly heard in speech than 止せる is used in its plain/dictionary form — in everyday Japanese, やめる is far more common and neutral.

## よそ
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** another place, somewhere else, elsewhere

**Similar words:**
- 他所（たしょ）— the kanji form of よそ, slightly more formal/written; both mean "another place."
- 別の場所（べつのばしょ）— "a different place," a more explicit, neutral phrase; よそ is more colloquial and can carry a nuance of "not here, not our concern."

**Opposite words:**
- ここ — here
- [うち](vocab-08.md#うち) — one's own home/group (contrasted with よそ as "outside/other")

**Example sentences:**
1. よそ見をしないで前を向きなさい。— Don't look elsewhere, look straight ahead.
2. それはよその国の話だ。— That's a story about another country.
3. よそはよそ、うちはうちだ。— Other households are other households, ours is ours.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** よそ often contrasts with うち (one's own home, family, or group) in the common expression よそはよそ、うちはうち (a parent's retort meaning "don't compare us to other families") — this cultural nuance of in-group/out-group contrast is key to using よそ naturally.

## 因って/依って (よって)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** accordingly, therefore, because of, due to

**Similar words:**
- したがって — "therefore/accordingly," a very close formal synonym, commonly used in written/academic Japanese interchangeably with よって.
- だから — "so/therefore," the casual everyday equivalent; よって is formal and appears in written reports, legal text, and formal speeches.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 大雨によって交通が麻痺した。— Traffic was paralyzed due to heavy rain.
2. 証拠不十分により、彼は無罪となった。— Due to insufficient evidence, he was found not guilty.
3. 本規定に違反した者は、よって処罰される。— Those who violate this rule shall accordingly be punished.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** よって is strictly formal/written, common in legal documents, academic papers, and official announcements — using it in casual conversation (instead of だから) would sound stiff and unnatural; also note its particle-like usage as "due to" (〜により/〜によって) versus its conjunction usage "therefore" at the start of a sentence.
