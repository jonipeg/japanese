# ～に決まっている {#に決まっている}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～に決まっている

**Furigana form:** ～にきまっている

**Meaning:** "It's surely/definitely X" / "X is bound to be the case" — expresses the speaker's strong, confident conviction based on common sense or obvious reasoning, often used to dismiss doubt.

**Conjugation:**
- 普通形 (plain form, verb/adjective) + に決まっている — 彼が勝つに決まっている (he's bound to win)
- 名詞/な形容詞 + に決まっている — 嘘に決まっている (it's obviously a lie)

**Typical context:** Casual-to-neutral, mainly spoken — expresses strong personal conviction, can sound blunt or assertive; less used in formal writing.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**に違いない**](grammar-に違いない.md) — "must be X," also expresses strong confidence but is more formal/logical and less emotionally blunt.
- **はずだ** — "should be X," based on reasoning/expectation, but weaker in certainty and tone than に決まっている.
- **んじゃない** — casual tag-question style conviction, softer and more hedged.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though かもしれない ("might be") contrasts in expressing uncertainty.

**Example sentences:**
1. そんな高い商品、売れるわけがない、失敗するに決まっている。
   (Such an expensive product — there's no way it'll sell, it's bound to fail.)
2. 彼が時間通りに来るわけがない、遅れるに決まっている。
   (There's no way he'll come on time — he's bound to be late.)
3. あんな簡単な噓、すぐにばれるに決まっている。
   (Such an obvious lie is bound to be found out right away.)
4. 練習もしないで試合に出たら、負けるに決まっている。
   (If you enter a match without practicing, you're bound to lose.)
5. この時期に海外旅行なんて、高いに決まっている。
   (Traveling abroad during this season is obviously going to be expensive.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** に決まっている carries a confident, sometimes dismissive or opinionated tone ("obviously!") — using it in polite/formal writing or toward a superior can sound presumptuous or rude; に違いない is safer in formal contexts.
