# ～におかれましては {#におかれましては}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～におかれましては

**Furigana form:** ～におかれましては *(no kanji; written in kana — the 置 kanji is conventionally avoided here)*

**Meaning:** An ultra-formal honorific topic marker used to respectfully refer to a superior, client, or esteemed party as the topic of a letter, speech, or greeting — roughly "as for [esteemed person/entity]..."

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞（人・組織など） (noun referring to a person or organization, usually one the speaker respects/is subordinate to) + におかれましては
- No further conjugation; it's a fixed honorific topic-marking phrase, typically opening a formal letter or speech.

**Typical context:** Extremely formal, almost exclusively written (business letters, formal greeting cards, ceremonial speeches). Carries strong honorific/polite connotation toward the referenced party — never used casually, and using it in casual speech would sound absurdly stiff or sarcastic.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**としたことが**](grammar-としたことが.md) — means "X, of all people" (surprise/self-reproach at an uncharacteristic mistake); entirely different function — not honorific address, but criticism/surprise.
- [**はさておき**](grammar-はさておき.md) — means "setting X aside"; a topic-deferral construction unrelated to honorific address.
- **ともあろう会社が／ともあろう者が** — means "a company/person of such standing as X (should not...)"; while also formal and about addressing esteemed subjects, ともあろう is used critically (to scold someone prestigious for a failure), whereas におかれましては is purely respectful with no critical undertone.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite exists; as a fixed honorific set-phrase, it has no "casual topic marker" counterpart beyond the plain topic particle は itself, which lacks any honorific weight. No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 貴社におかれましては、益々ご清栄のこととお慶び申し上げます。
   (We are delighted to hear that your company continues to prosper.)
2. 先生におかれましては、ますますご健勝のこととお慶び申し上げます。
   (We are pleased to hear that you, sensei, continue to be in excellent health.)
3. お客様におかれましては、日頃より格別のご愛顧を賜り、厚く御礼申し上げます。
   (We extend our deepest gratitude to our valued customers for their continued patronage.)
4. 会長におかれましては、この度の栄転、誠におめでとうございます。
   (Chairman, many heartfelt congratulations on your promotion this time.)
5. 皆様におかれましては、良いお年をお迎えのこととお慶び申し上げます。
   (We hope that all of you are welcoming a wonderful new year.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Confused on graded tests with としたことが and はさておき/ともあろう会社が — students may lump all "formal N+pattern" structures together, but におかれましては is purely a respectful topic marker with zero critical or surprising nuance, unlike としたことが (surprise at a mistake) or ともあろう (criticism of a prestigious subject's failure). Key test cue: におかれましては appears almost exclusively in opening greetings of formal letters/speeches, immediately followed by well-wishing language (ご健勝, ご清栄, etc.), never by a criticism or complaint.
