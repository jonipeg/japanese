# ～ものではない {#ものではない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ものではない

**Furigana form:** ～ものではない (no special kanji reading)

**Meaning:** "One should not do X," "it's not the kind of thing you should do" — a moral admonition or piece of social advice, stating that an action goes against common sense, decency, or proper conduct.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形 + ものではない
- 人の悪口を言うものではない。(One should not speak ill of others.)
- Colloquial contraction: ～もんじゃない (casual spoken form)

**Typical context:** Spoken and written, neutral-to-formal register. Commonly used by parents, teachers, or elders giving moral guidance; can sound like a lecture or scolding.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **べきではない** — "should not," a more direct/logical statement of what's morally wrong; ものではない has a softer, more proverb-like, generalized social-norm flavor.
- **てはいけない** — "must not," a stronger prohibition, often used for rules rather than general wisdom.
- **な (禁止形)** — blunt casual prohibition ("don't do X"), much more direct/commanding than ものではない.

**Opposite grammar structures:** [**ものだ**](grammar-ものだ.md) — the positive form describing what IS the natural/common way of things (see separate entry).

**Example sentences:**
1. 年上の人に向かって、そんな口の利き方をするものではない。
   (You shouldn't speak to your elders in that kind of tone.)
2. 他人の持ち物を勝手に使うものではない。
   (You shouldn't use other people's belongings without permission.)
3. 食べ物を粗末にするものではない。
   (You shouldn't waste food.)
4. 人を見た目だけで判断するものではない。
   (You shouldn't judge people by appearance alone.)
5. 約束を軽々しく破るものではない。
   (You shouldn't break promises lightly.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ものではない carries a gentler, proverb-like "this is just not done" nuance compared to the sharper logical/moral force of べきではない, and it's less harsh than the direct prohibition てはいけない. It's frequently used when giving general life advice rather than citing a specific rule, so it works well for timeless社会規範 (social norms) statements.
