# ～につけ {#につけ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～に付け

**Furigana form:** ～につけ

**Meaning:** "Whenever / every time X happens" — marks a recurring trigger (often sensory or situational) that reliably brings about a certain thought or feeling; frequently doubled as 何かにつけ or Xにつけ、Yにつけ ("whether X or Y...").

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形 + につけ (例: この曲を聞くにつけ)
- 名詞 + につけ (例: 何かにつけ、ことあるにつけ)
- Fixed paired form: Aにつけ、Bにつけ (例: 嬉しいにつけ、悲しいにつけ)

**Typical context:** Slightly literary/formal, more common in writing and reflective speech than casual conversation; often found in essays, memoirs, and emotional narration.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**たびに**](grammar-たびに.md) — "every time X," more neutral and commonly used in everyday speech, lacks the reflective/emotional nuance.
- [**につれて**](grammar-につれて.md) — "as X progresses," describes gradual parallel change rather than a repeated trigger-response.
- [**ことに**](grammar-ことに(は).md) — different usage (emphatic adjective + ことに), not a direct substitute but sometimes confused due to similar feel.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. この写真を見るにつけ、昔の思い出が蘇る。
   (Every time I see this photo, old memories come back to me.)
2. 子供の成長を見るにつけ、時間の流れの速さを感じる。
   (Whenever I see my child grow, I feel how fast time passes.)
3. 何かにつけ、彼は私を気にかけてくれる。
   (Whatever the occasion, he always looks out for me.)
4. 嬉しいにつけ悲しいにつけ、彼女はいつも冷静だった。
   (Whether happy or sad, she always remained calm.)
5. あの曲を聞くにつけ、学生時代を思い出す。
   (Every time I hear that song, I remember my student days.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** につけ is mainly used with verbs of perception (見る、聞く、考える) and implies an emotional or reflective reaction, not a simple mechanical repetition — don't confuse with たびに, which is more neutral/factual ("every time I go to Tokyo, I visit my aunt" is better with たびに than につけ).
