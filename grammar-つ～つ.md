# ～つ～つ {#つ～つ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～つ～つ *(classical verb-stem repetition, no additional kanji needed)*

**Furigana form:** ～つ～つ

**Meaning:** "Now X, now Y / alternately doing X and Y" — describes two opposite or alternating actions happening repeatedly, back and forth.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ます形 + つ + 対になる動詞ます形 + つ (verb stem + つ + opposite-meaning verb stem + つ)
- Example base: 行く／戻る → 行きつ戻りつ

**Typical context:** Formal, written/literary register; a classical grammatical remnant used almost exclusively in literary description of repeated back-and-forth physical movement or indecisive action. Not used in casual speech.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **たり～たり** — "doing X and Y (alternately/repeatedly)"; the modern, everyday equivalent for describing alternating or representative actions, used in any register, while つ～つ is restricted to literary/classical style and a small fixed set of paired verbs.
- [**ながら**](grammar-ながら.md) — "while doing X"; describes two simultaneous actions by one subject, but without つ～つ's specific "back and forth, alternating direction" nuance.
- **行きつ戻りつ** (as a fixed idiom) — essentially the single most common fossilized example of this pattern, often memorized as a set phrase rather than productively generated with other verb pairs.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a single, unwavering action (まっすぐ～する, "doing X in one direction without wavering") would contrast with つ～つ's back-and-forth indecisiveness.

**Example sentences:**
1. しばらく入り口の前で行きつ戻りつしていたが、意を決してドアを開けた。 (He paced back and forth in front of the entrance for a while, then resolved himself and opened the door.)
2. 川面を見ると、小舟が浮きつ沈みつ揺れていた。 (Looking at the river's surface, a small boat swayed, bobbing up and down.)
3. 彼女は迷いつ迷わずつ、ようやく決断を下した。 (Wavering back and forth, she finally made her decision.)
4. 子どもたちは追いつ追われつ、公園を駆け回っていた。 (The children ran around the park, chasing and being chased by turns.)
5. 月は雲に見えつ隠れつしながら、夜空に浮かんでいた。 (The moon hung in the night sky, now visible, now hidden behind the clouds.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** つ～つ is an archaic, literary construction restricted to a small handful of fixed verb pairs (行きつ戻りつ being by far the most common) — it is not a productive pattern learners should freely generate with arbitrary verbs in modern speech; for everyday "alternating actions," use たり～たり instead. Also, do not confuse this with the unrelated single-tilde-free pattern つつ (simultaneous action, "while doing X") — つ～つ is a doubled, alternating-action construction, while つつ attaches once to a single verb stem to show two things happening at the same time.
