# ～を控えて {#を控えて}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～を控えて

**Furigana form:** ～をひかえて

**Meaning:** "With X coming up soon" — describes a person or situation being in a particular state (often tense or busy) in anticipation of a significant event that is approaching.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + を控えて／を控えた＋名詞
- 卒業を控えて — "with graduation coming up"

**Typical context:** Neutral written/spoken register — used to describe situations near significant upcoming events such as exams, weddings, or graduations.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **を前に** — "ahead of X," very similar meaning and register, often interchangeable.
- [**に先立って**](grammar-に先立って.md) — "prior to X," more formal, often used for official events or announcements.

**Opposite grammar structures:** ～を終えて ("having finished X") describes a state after an event rather than in anticipation of one.

**Example sentences:**
1. 入試をひかえて、学生たちはぴりぴりしている。
   (With entrance exams coming up, the students are on edge.)
2. 卒業をひかえた先輩に記念品を送った。
   (We gave a commemorative gift to our senior who was about to graduate.)
3. 結婚式をひかえて、彼女は忙しい日々を送っている。
   (With her wedding coming up, she's having busy days.)
4. 本番をひかえて、緊張が高まっている。
   (With the real performance approaching, tension is rising.)
5. 開幕をひかえ、会場の準備が進められている。
   (With the opening approaching, preparations at the venue are underway.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** を控えて describes temporal proximity to an event and often implies some accompanying tension, anticipation, or busyness in the subject. It's commonly used both as a connective clause (describing a state) and as a modifier before a noun (卒業を控えた先輩). It is not used for events already past.
