# しかも {#しかも}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** しかも (no kanji; conventionally written in kana)

**Furigana form:** しかも

**Meaning:** "Moreover, what's more" — adds a surprising or notable additional fact, often making the situation even more so (more impressive, more severe, etc.).

**Conjugation:** Sentence-initial conjunction, no conjugation needed — attaches at the sentence boundary, connecting the previous sentence to the next.

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-written register, usable in both spoken and written Japanese. Carries a distinct nuance of surprise or added impact — the speaker is emphasizing that the additional fact makes the overall situation more extreme (better, worse, more impressive) than the first statement alone suggested. Common in storytelling, reviews, news reports, and everyday conversation when relating an unexpectedly compounding fact.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**それに**](grammar-それに.md) — adds a supplementary point reinforcing an argument; more neutral and lacks しかも's "surprising, compounding" flavor.
- [**また**](grammar-また.md) — neutral listing of parallel items; purely additive, without the escalating-surprise nuance of しかも.
- [**さらに**](grammar-さらに.md) — "furthermore, moreover," adds escalating emphasis; close synonym to しかも, but さらに is more about plain degree-escalation while しかも emphasizes the added fact as surprising or remarkable.
- [**そのうえ**](grammar-そのうえ.md) — very close synonym to しかも, similarly adding a notable extra fact, but そのうえ is slightly more neutral/written-leaning and less charged with surprise than しかも.
- [**それも**](grammar-それも.md) — adds emphatic/exasperated reinforcement to the immediately preceding statement; shares しかも's intensifying flavor but それも leans more toward exasperation/disbelief about a single preceding point, rather than introducing a separate additional fact.
- [**あと**](grammar-あと.md) — casual spoken "also," plain afterthought; lacks any of しかも's surprise/intensifying nuance.

**Opposite grammar structures:** しかし/だが (contrastive "however" conjunctions) are the functional opposite, since they counter the preceding statement rather than compound it with a reinforcing surprise.

**Example sentences:**
1. この店の料理は美味しい。しかも、値段も手頃だ。
   (This restaurant's food is delicious. What's more, the prices are reasonable too.)
2. 彼は試験に合格した。しかも、トップの成績で。
   (He passed the exam. Moreover, with the top score.)
3. 外は雨が降っていた。しかも、傘を持っていなかった。
   (It was raining outside. What's more, I didn't have an umbrella.)
4. 新しいモデルは軽量だ。しかも、バッテリーも長持ちする。
   (The new model is lightweight. Moreover, the battery lasts a long time too.)
5. 彼女は三か国語を話せる。しかも、まだ二十歳だ。
   (She can speak three languages. What's more, she's only twenty years old.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often use しかも interchangeably with それに or また, but しかも specifically implies the added fact is surprising or amplifies the overall impression (good news gets better, bad news gets worse) — using it for a mundane, unremarkable addition can sound oddly dramatic. Conversely, using それに/また where the added fact is genuinely surprising can undersell the intended impact. Also distinguish from それも, which leans toward exasperated emphasis rather than しかも's more flexible positive-or-negative surprise.
