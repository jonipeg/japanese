# ～まい {#まい}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～まい

**Furigana form:** ～まい (no kanji; auxiliary written in kana)

**Meaning:** "Will surely not," "probably won't" (negative conjecture) or "let's not," "I will not" (negative volitional) — a literary/formal auxiliary expressing strong negative intention or negative supposition, depending on person.

**Conjugation:**
- Group 1 (u-verbs) + まい using dictionary form: 行くまい (will probably not go / won't go)
- Group 2 (ru-verbs) + まい using dictionary form or stem: 食べまい／食べるまい
- する → するまい／すまい; くる → こまい／くるまい
- First person: expresses strong negative volition ("I won't do X"); third person/general: expresses negative conjecture ("probably won't").
- 二度とあの店には行くまいと思った。(I decided I would never go to that shop again.)

**Typical context:** Literary, written, and formal/dramatic spoken Japanese (speeches, novels, monologues). Rare in everyday casual conversation, where もう～ない or ～ないだろう are used instead.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **ないだろう** — the common everyday equivalent for negative conjecture ("probably won't").
- **つもりはない** — common equivalent for negative intention ("I don't intend to").
- **ないつもりだ** — similar expression of firm negative resolve, more conversational.

**Opposite grammar structures:** **だろう／つもりだ** — the positive conjecture/intention counterparts ("probably will" / "intend to").

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は二度とこの町には戻るまいと誓った。
   (He swore he would never return to this town again.)
2. あの人は約束を破るような人ではあるまい。
   (That person probably isn't the type to break a promise.)
3. もう二度と同じ失敗はすまいと心に決めた。
   (I resolved in my heart never to make the same mistake again.)
4. 彼女が簡単にあきらめるまい。
   (She probably won't give up easily.)
5. どんなに辛くても、人前では泣くまいと我慢した。
   (No matter how painful it was, I endured it, determined not to cry in front of others.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** まい is largely a literary/written form in modern Japanese and sounds stiff or dramatic if used in casual daily conversation — learners should recognize it for reading comprehension (common on JLPT) but default to ないだろう/つもりはない for natural speech. Also watch irregular conjugations with する and くる, which have two accepted forms each.
