# ～たまえ {#たまえ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～給え

**Furigana form:** ～たまえ

**Meaning:** A dated, authoritative command form ("do this") typically used by an older or higher-status man addressing a younger or subordinate person — a mild, somewhat paternal imperative.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ます形 + 給え
- Example base: 読む → 読みたまえ

**Typical context:** Spoken, distinctly masculine and hierarchical register, strongly associated with older professors, bosses, or military/academic settings in fiction. Largely obsolete/archaic in real contemporary speech, mostly encountered in novels, period dramas, or deliberately old-fashioned characterizations.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **命令形** (plain imperative, e.g. 読め) — a blunter, rougher command form usable by any speaker in appropriate contexts, while たまえ carries a more paternal/condescending-but-not-harsh nuance specific to an older authority figure addressing a younger person.
- **なさい** — a softer command form (e.g., 読みなさい), commonly used by parents/teachers to children; たまえ is gendered masculine and typically used adult-to-adult in formal/old-fashioned hierarchical contexts, unlike なさい's broader usage.
- **ください** — the standard polite request form; たまえ, by contrast, is a direct command with an air of authority and condescension, not a polite request.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a humble request form like ～ていただけますか ("could you please do X") expresses the reverse power dynamic — deference rather than command.

**Example sentences:**
1. 銀行で働いているなら、新聞ぐらい読みたまえ。 (If you work at a bank, at least read the newspaper, won't you.)
2. 君、少し落ち着きたまえ。 (Calm down a little, won't you.)
3. そこに座りたまえ、話がある。 (Sit down there — I have something to discuss.)
4. 若いうちにもっと勉強したまえ。 (Study more while you're young.)
5. 君の意見を聞かせてくれたまえ。 (Let me hear your opinion.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** たまえ is strongly gendered (masculine) and generational — modern learners should recognize it when encountered in fiction or formal/period speech but avoid using it in everyday conversation, where it would sound theatrical or anachronistic, much like だい/かい. It implies a clear status gap (senior speaking down to junior) and should never be used toward someone of equal or higher status.
