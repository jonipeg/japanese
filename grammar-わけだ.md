# ～わけだ {#わけだ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～訳だ

**Furigana form:** ～わけだ (訳 read as わけ)

**Meaning:** "That means..., so it's natural that..., no wonder..." — draws a logical conclusion from previously stated information; indicates that a situation is the natural/expected result of given facts.

**Conjugation:**
- 普通形 (plain form) + わけだ
- 動詞普通形 + わけだ: 彼が来ないわけだ (that's why/that means he's not coming)
- い形容詞普通形 + わけだ: 寒いわけだ (no wonder it's cold)
- な形容詞 + な + わけだ: 静かなわけだ (that's why it's quiet)
- 名詞 + という + わけだ / である + わけだ: 休みというわけだ (that means it's a day off)

**Typical context:** Spoken and written, neutral register. Common when realizing/confirming the reason behind something after learning new information ("Ah, so that's why...").

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **〜はずだ** — expresses expectation/confidence about something not yet confirmed, whereas わけだ confirms a logical conclusion from known facts.
- [**ということだ**](grammar-ということだ.md) — reports/summarizes information, less about personal logical deduction.
- **道理で〜わけだ** — fixed collocation emphasizing "no wonder," reinforcing the realization nuance.

**Opposite grammar structures:** [**わけではない**](grammar-わけではない.md) (partial negation) contrasts in function, though not a strict opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 3年間アメリカに住んでいたのか。道理で英語が上手なわけだ。
   (You lived in America for three years? No wonder your English is so good.)
2. 今日は祝日だ。道理で道が空いているわけだ。
   (Today's a holiday — no wonder the roads are empty.)
3. 彼は医者の家に生まれた。だから医学部を目指すわけだ。
   (He was born into a doctor's family, so it makes sense he's aiming for medical school.)
4. このレストラン、シェフがフランス人なんだ。それで本格的な味なわけだ。
   (The chef at this restaurant is French — that's why the taste is so authentic.)
5. 彼女は毎日10時間練習している。上達が早いわけだ。
   (She practices ten hours every day — no wonder she's improving so fast.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** わけだ draws a conclusion the speaker infers logically from existing facts — it is not used to state a brand-new fact out of nowhere. Learners sometimes confuse it with はずだ, which expresses an expectation about something uncertain rather than a conclusion from known evidence. Also distinct from わけではない (partial negation) and わけがない (impossibility) — all share 訳 but diverge sharply in meaning.
