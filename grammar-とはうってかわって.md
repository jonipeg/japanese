# ～とはうってかわって {#とはうってかわって}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～とは打って変わって

**Furigana form:** ～とはうってかわって

**Meaning:** "Completely different from X (as before)" — describes a sudden, striking contrast between a current state and a previous one.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + とは打って変わって
- Example base: きのうの大雨 → きのうの大雨とはうってかわって

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal, usable in both speech and writing; vividly emphasizes a dramatic before/after contrast, common in weather reports, narrative descriptions of mood/atmosphere changes, and news writing.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **とは違って** — plain "unlike X / different from X"; a simpler, more neutral contrast marker, lacking うってかわって's vivid "complete transformation/reversal" nuance.
- **一変して** — "having completely changed"; similar emphasis on dramatic change, but 一変して focuses on the change itself as a standalone event, whereas とはうってかわって specifically frames the new state in contrast against a named previous state.
- **がらりと変わって** — "having changed drastically/suddenly"; a colloquial near-synonym with similar vivid suddenness, often interchangeable in casual speech.

**Opposite grammar structures:** ～と同様に／～のまま ("the same as X / remaining as X") expresses the reverse — continuity rather than a dramatic contrast.

**Example sentences:**
1. きのうの大雨とはうってかわって、きょうは快晴になりましたね。 (In complete contrast to yesterday's heavy rain, today turned out to be gloriously clear.)
2. 試験前の緊張した様子とはうってかわって、彼は合格後とてもリラックスしていた。 (In complete contrast to his tense demeanor before the exam, he was very relaxed after passing.)
3. 昨夜の静けさとはうってかわって、朝の駅は大勢の人でにぎわっていた。 (In complete contrast to last night's quiet, the station was bustling with crowds in the morning.)
4. 普段の厳しい態度とはうってかわって、先生はその日とても優しかった。 (In complete contrast to his usually strict attitude, the teacher was very kind that day.)
5. オープン当初の混雑とはうってかわって、今では静かな店になった。 (In complete contrast to the crowds at the initial opening, it has now become a quiet shop.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** とはうってかわって always requires a stated prior state or situation to contrast against (named directly before とは) — it cannot be used as a standalone "things changed" statement without naming what it changed from. The idiom 打って変わる literally evokes something being "struck and transformed," conveying suddenness and completeness of the change, stronger than a plain とは違って.
