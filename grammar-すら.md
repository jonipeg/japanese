# ～すら {#すら}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～すら

**Furigana form:** ～すら

**Meaning:** Used to give an extreme example and imply that, since even this extreme case holds true, everything less extreme naturally holds true too — close to English "even." A stiff, literary-sounding expression similar to ～さえ but more formal.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞(+助詞) + すら
- 彼のうそには、怒りだけではなく悲しみすら覚えた — "I felt not just anger but even sadness at his lie"

**Typical context:** Formal written Japanese — essays, editorials, narrative prose, and formal speech. Rare in casual conversation, where ～さえ or ～も would be used instead.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**～さえ**](grammar-さえ.md) — nearly identical meaning ("even"), but more neutral in register and usable in casual speech; ～すら is its stiffer, more literary counterpart.
- **～でさえ** — the さえ equivalent of ですら, used the same way when attaching after a copula.
- [**～はおろか**](grammar-はおろか.md) — also sets up an extreme-case comparison, but specifically means "let alone X, not even Y," always paired with a negative.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct grammatical opposite; a plain statement without an extreme-case marker (e.g., ～も) would simply state the fact without emphasizing how surprising or extreme it is.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼のうそには、怒りだけではなく悲しみすら覚えた。
   (His lie made me feel not just anger, but even sadness.)
2. バスは雨の日などにはたびたび遅れる。30分も待たされることすらある。
   (The bus is often late on rainy days — there are even times you're made to wait a full 30 minutes.)
3. 自分が好きで選んだ仕事にすら自信が持てなくなってしまった。
   (I've lost confidence even in the job I chose because I loved it.)
4. これは専門家ですら直すのが難しい故障だ。素人のわたしには全く手がつけられない。
   (This is a malfunction that's hard to fix even for a professional. As an amateur, I can't do anything about it at all.)
5. 彼女は忙しすぎて、食事をする時間すらないそうだ。
   (She's apparently so busy that she doesn't even have time to eat.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** When attaching after a noun followed by the copula, the form becomes ～ですら, as in ④ (専門家ですら). This usage mainly corresponds to cases expressible with the subject marker が, carrying the nuance of "であっても" ("even if it were X"). Because ～すら is quite formal, overusing it in casual conversation will sound stiff or unnatural — reach for ～さえ there instead.
