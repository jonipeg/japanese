# ～にすぎない {#にすぎない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～に過ぎない

**Furigana form:** ～にすぎない

**Meaning:** "Merely/nothing more than X" — downplays something as being only to a limited extent, often used to minimize significance or correct an overestimation.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + にすぎない — 噂にすぎない (it's nothing more than a rumor)
- 普通形 + にすぎない — 単なる偶然にすぎない (it's merely a coincidence)

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal, common in both speech and writing — often used to argue against an exaggerated claim or to humbly minimize one's own contribution.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **だけだ** — plain "just/only X," more neutral, lacks the rhetorical "downplaying/correcting" nuance.
- [**にほかならない**](grammar-にほかならない.md) — "is nothing other than X," the opposite rhetorical direction — emphasizes that something IS definitively X, rather than minimizing it.
- **でしかない** — very close synonym, slightly more blunt/colloquial than にすぎない.

**Opposite grammar structures:** ～にほかならない ("is precisely/nothing other than X") is a near-opposite in rhetorical direction, asserting significance rather than minimizing it.

**Example sentences:**
1. これはほんの一例にすぎない。
   (This is merely one example among many.)
2. 彼の成功は運が良かったにすぎない。
   (His success was nothing more than good luck.)
3. 私はただの通訳にすぎません、決定権はありません。
   (I'm merely an interpreter; I have no decision-making authority.)
4. その計画はまだ案にすぎず、正式に決まったわけではない。
   (That plan is still just a proposal; it hasn't been officially decided.)
5. 彼の発言は単なる憶測にすぎない。
   (His remark is nothing more than mere speculation.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** にすぎない carries a deliberate rhetorical effect of minimizing/downplaying — it's not purely neutral like だけ, and using it about someone else's achievement can sound dismissive or insulting if not intended that way.
