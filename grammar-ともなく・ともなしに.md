# ～ともなく／ともなしに {#ともなく・ともなしに}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ともなく／～ともなしに

**Furigana form:** ～ともなく／～ともなしに (no kanji reading changes; とも is read as-is)

**Meaning:** "Without particularly meaning/intending to ~" — describes an action done unconsciously, vaguely, or without clear purpose or awareness. Often used with verbs of perception (見る, 聞く, 待つ) to mean one did something absent-mindedly.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形 (verb dictionary form) + ともなく／ともなしに
- Often the same verb is repeated before and after: 見るともなく見る (watching without really meaning to watch)
- Example: 見るともなく窓の外を見ていたら、彼が歩いているのが見えた。 (Looking out the window without particularly meaning to, I saw him walking by.)

**Typical context:** Written and literary spoken Japanese; found in novels, essays, and reflective narration. Neutral-to-formal register, rarely used in casual conversation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **なんとなく** — "somehow, for no particular reason" — similar vagueness but used as a standalone adverb, not attached to a repeated verb.
- **ふと** — "suddenly, on impulse" — emphasizes the sudden, unplanned nature of an action rather than its unconscious/aimless quality.
- **ぼんやりと** — "vaguely, absent-mindedly" — describes the manner of an action directly rather than grammatically pairing a verb with itself.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though 意図的に ("intentionally") or わざと ("on purpose") contrast in spirit.

**Example sentences:**
1. 見るともなく窓の外を見ていたら、彼が歩いているのが見えた。
   (Looking out the window without particularly meaning to, I saw him walking by.)
2. テレビを見るともなしに見ていたら、いつの間にか夜中になっていた。
   (I was watching TV without really paying attention, and before I knew it, it was midnight.)
3. 聞くともなく隣の会話を聞いていたら、自分の名前が出てきて驚いた。
   (I was listening to the conversation next to me without meaning to, and I was surprised to hear my own name.)
4. 待つともなく駅のホームで電車を待っていると、懐かしい歌が聞こえてきた。
   (As I waited for the train on the platform without really waiting intently, I heard a nostalgic song.)
5. 考えるともなく考えていたことが、ふと答えとしてひらめいた。
   (An idea I had been mulling over without really trying to suddenly came to me as the answer.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often try to use this with a different verb before and after, but the standard pattern repeats the same verb (見るともなく見る). Also, it should not be confused with なんとなく, which can stand alone and doesn't require a verb pairing.
