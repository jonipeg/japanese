## 加わる (くわわる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to increase, to join, to participate, to be added (to a group/total)

**Similar words:**
- 参加する（さんかする）— "to participate," the more common, general verb for joining an activity or event.
- 入る（はいる）— "to enter/join" (e.g. a club), simpler and more general than 加わる.
- [加える](vocab-16.md#加える-くわえる)（くわえる）— the transitive counterpart, "to add something."

**Example sentences:**
1. 新しいメンバーがチームに加わった。— A new member joined the team.
2. 圧力が加わるとガラスは割れる。— When pressure is added, the glass breaks.
3. 議論に加わりたいと思った。— I wanted to join the discussion.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 加わる is intransitive ("to join/be added"), while 加える is transitive ("to add something") — learners frequently confuse which one takes が vs. を, since both share the same root.

## 郡 (ぐん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a county, a district, a township (administrative unit, now mostly historical/rural)

**Similar words:**
- 市（し）— "city," a more common modern administrative unit; 郡 is now mostly used for rural, less urbanized areas in Japan.
- [区](vocab-15.md#区-く)（く）— "ward," an administrative subdivision used in large cities like Tokyo.

**Example sentences:**
1. この地域はかつて郡と呼ばれていた。— This area used to be called a county.
2. 郡部には自然が多く残っている。— Rural districts (county areas) still have a lot of nature.
3. その町は〇〇郡に属している。— That town belongs to 〇〇 County.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 郡 is a legacy administrative division mostly surviving in addresses for rural towns/villages in Japan — it has largely been superseded by 市 (city) and 区 (ward) in daily administrative use, so learners won't encounter it often outside addresses or historical contexts.

## 軍艦 (ぐんかん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a warship, a battleship

**Similar words:**
- 船（ふね）— the general word "ship/boat"; 軍艦 specifies a military vessel.
- 戦艦（せんかん）— specifically "battleship," a subtype of 軍艦 with heavy armament.
- 空母（くうぼ）— "aircraft carrier," another specific type of warship.

**Example sentences:**
1. 軍艦が港に停泊している。— A warship is anchored in the port.
2. 歴史の授業で軍艦の写真を見た。— I saw photos of warships in history class.
3. 軍艦巻きという寿司の形がある。— There's a type of sushi shape called "gunkan-maki" (battleship roll).

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 軍艦 appears in the sushi term 軍艦巻き ("battleship roll," sushi wrapped in nori to hold loose toppings) — a fun, non-military usage learners often encounter in food contexts before the literal military meaning.

## 軍事 (ぐんじ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** military affairs, military matters

**Similar words:**
- [軍隊](#軍隊-ぐんたい)（ぐんたい）— "the armed forces/army" itself, a concrete organization rather than the abstract field of "military affairs."
- 防衛（ぼうえい）— "defense," often used in the context of national defense policy, a related but distinct focus from general military affairs.

**Example sentences:**
1. その国は軍事力を強化している。— That country is strengthening its military power.
2. 軍事に関するニュースをよく見る。— I often watch news related to military affairs.
3. 軍事訓練が行われた。— Military training was conducted.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 軍事 is an abstract noun/modifier (軍事力, 軍事訓練, 軍事的) referring to the domain of military matters — don't confuse it with 軍隊, the concrete noun for "the armed forces" as an organization.

## 君主 (くんしゅ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a monarch, a sovereign, a ruler

**Similar words:**
- [王](vocab-09.md#王-おう)（おう）— "king," a more common, concrete term for a specific type of monarch.
- [天皇](vocab-29.md#天皇-てんのう)（てんのう）— "the Emperor (of Japan)," a specific title rather than the general category 君主.
- 統治者（とうちしゃ）— "a ruler/governor," broader and not necessarily hereditary/royal like 君主.

**Opposite words:**
- 臣民（しんみん）— subjects (of a monarch)

**Example sentences:**
1. 君主制の国はまだ存在する。— Monarchies still exist today.
2. その国の君主は非常に人気がある。— That country's monarch is extremely popular.
3. 君主が絶対的な権力を持っていた時代があった。— There was an era when monarchs held absolute power.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 君主 is a general/formal term for "monarch" used especially in political science or historical discussion (君主制 "monarchy system") — for Japan's specific emperor, use 天皇 rather than the generic 君主.

## 群衆/群れ (ぐんしゅう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a crowd (of people)

**Similar words:**
- [群れ](vocab-03.md#群れ-むれ)（むれ）— "a herd/flock/group," often used for animals or a loosely gathered group of people, while 群衆 specifically describes a large crowd of people.
- 観衆（かんしゅう）— "spectators/audience," a crowd specifically watching an event.
- 人混み（ひとごみ）— "a crowded place/throng of people," emphasizing the crowdedness/congestion rather than the group as a collective entity.

**Example sentences:**
1. 駅前に群衆が集まっていた。— A crowd had gathered in front of the station.
2. 群衆は事故現場を取り囲んだ。— The crowd surrounded the accident scene.
3. 鳥の群れが空を飛んでいった。— A flock of birds flew across the sky.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Note the two readings/kanji given are actually distinct words with different nuances — 群衆（ぐんしゅう）specifically means "a crowd of people," while 群れ（むれ）is broader, covering flocks/herds of animals as well as loose human groups; don't treat them as fully interchangeable despite the shared listing.

## 軍隊 (ぐんたい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** armed forces, army, troops

**Similar words:**
- 軍（ぐん）— "army/military," a shorter, slightly more general term often used in compounds.
- 自衛隊（じえいたい）— "the Self-Defense Forces," Japan's specific modern military organization (Japan avoids the term 軍隊 for its own forces due to its pacifist constitution).

**Example sentences:**
1. 軍隊が国境に配備された。— Troops were deployed to the border.
2. 彼は若い頃、軍隊に入っていた。— He was in the army when he was young.
3. 軍隊式の規律が求められた。— Military-style discipline was required.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Due to Japan's postwar constitution, Japan's own military is officially called 自衛隊 (Self-Defense Forces), not 軍隊 — using 軍隊 to refer to Japan's current forces is a subtle but notable inaccuracy that learners of Japanese politics/history should be aware of.

## 軍服 (ぐんぷく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a military uniform

**Similar words:**
- [制服](vocab-22.md#制服-せいふく)（せいふく）— "uniform" in general (school, work), a broader category that includes but isn't limited to military uniforms.
- 戦闘服（せんとうふく）— "combat uniform/fatigues," a more specific type worn in active combat.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は軍服姿で写真に写っていた。— He was photographed wearing a military uniform.
2. 博物館に古い軍服が展示されている。— Old military uniforms are on display at the museum.
3. 軍服のボタンが光っていた。— The buttons on the military uniform were shining.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 軍服 specifically denotes a military uniform — for school or workplace uniforms, the broader 制服 is the correct, more common word; don't overuse 軍服 outside military contexts.

## 訓練(する) (くんれん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** training, drilling, practice (often physical or disciplined)

**Similar words:**
- [練習](vocab-36.md#練習-れんしゅう)（れんしゅう）— "practice," a more general, everyday word (sports, instruments) without 訓練's connotation of rigorous, often institutional discipline.
- トレーニング（トレーニング）— the loanword "training," often used for physical fitness training.
- [研修](vocab-18.md#研修-けんしゅう)（けんしゅう）— "training/workshop," specifically for professional or job-related skill development.

**Example sentences:**
1. 消防士は厳しい訓練を受けている。— Firefighters undergo rigorous training.
2. 避難訓練が学校で行われた。— An evacuation drill was conducted at the school.
3. 犬に芸を覚えさせるため訓練した。— I trained the dog to learn tricks.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 訓練 implies rigorous, often repetitive and disciplined training (military, emergency drills, animal training) — for casual hobby practice (like practicing guitar), 練習 is the more natural, lighter word.

## ~毛 (~け)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** hair, fur, wool (suffix/noun)

**Similar words:**
- 髪（かみ）— specifically head hair (on a person), narrower than the general 毛, which covers body hair, animal fur, and wool.
- [羊毛](vocab-35.md#羊毛-ようもう)（ようもう）— "wool" specifically from sheep, a compound built on this word.

**Example sentences:**
1. このセーターは毛でできている。— This sweater is made of wool.
2. 猫の毛がソファについている。— Cat fur is stuck on the sofa.
3. 腕の毛が濃い。— The hair on my arm is thick.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 毛 is the general term covering head hair, body hair, animal fur, and wool/fiber — 髪 is reserved specifically for human head hair, so learners should not use 毛 alone when specifically talking about someone's hairstyle (use 髪 or 髪の毛 instead).

## ~形/型 (~けい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** type, tense (grammar), shape, model (suffix)

**Similar words:**
- 種類（しゅるい）— "kind/type," a more general standalone noun for classifying things, not a bound suffix.
- タイプ（タイプ）— the loanword "type," casual and very common in everyday speech (e.g. 彼女は私のタイプだ "she's my type").

**Example sentences:**
1. この動詞のて形を覚えてください。— Please memorize the te-form of this verb.
2. 新しい型のスマートフォンが発売された。— A new model of smartphone was released.
3. 血液型は何ですか。— What is your blood type?

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This suffix has two different kanji depending on meaning — 形 for grammatical/abstract "form" (て形, た形) and 型 for physical "type/model" (血液型, 新型) — learners should learn the kanji pairing with specific compounds rather than treating them as interchangeable.

## 計 (けい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a plan, a plot; the grand total; a gauge/meter

**Similar words:**
- [合計](vocab-18.md#合計する-ごうけい)（ごうけい）— "the total sum," the much more commonly used compound built from this word.
- [計画](#計画する-けいかく)（けいかく）— "a plan," the full, common word for the "plan" sense of 計.
- [メーター](vocab-34.md#メーター-めーたー)（メーター）— the loanword "meter/gauge," often used interchangeably with 〜計 (温度計 "thermometer," etc.) in casual speech.

**Example sentences:**
1. 出費の計を立てる。— I make a plan of expenses.
2. 参加者は計100人だった。— The total number of participants was 100 people.
3. 体温計で熱を測った。— I measured my temperature with a thermometer.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 計 rarely stands alone in modern Japanese; it mostly appears as a suffix meaning "total" (合計, 総計) or "meter/gauge" (温度計, 体重計) — learners should primarily learn it through these common compounds rather than as an independent word.

## 芸 (げい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** accomplishments, arts, a performance, a trick

**Similar words:**
- [芸術](#芸術-げいじゅつ)（げいじゅつ）— "art" in the fine-arts sense (painting, sculpture), more elevated/formal than the broader, sometimes playful 芸.
- [芸能](#芸能-げいのう)（げいのう）— "performing arts/entertainment," the industry sense (芸能人 "celebrity/entertainer").
- 技（わざ）— "a skill/technique," often for martial arts or crafts, overlapping with the "skill" aspect of 芸.

**Example sentences:**
1. 犬に芸を教えた。— I taught the dog a trick.
2. 彼は多芸多才な人だ。— He's a person of many talents and skills.
3. その役者は芸が細かい。— That actor's performance/craft is meticulous.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 芸 covers a wide range from "animal tricks" (犬の芸) to "traditional performing arts" (伝統芸能) to a general sense of "skilled craft" (芸が細かい, meaning "meticulous/skillful") — context determines which register is meant.

## 経営(する) (けいえい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** management, administration (of a business)

**Similar words:**
- [運営](vocab-08.md#運営-うんえい)（うんえい）— "operation/running" (of an organization, event), often used for non-profit, administrative, or event-based contexts rather than for-profit business management.
- マネジメント（マネジメント）— the loanword "management," often used in more casual/modern business contexts.
- 経理（けいり）— "accounting," a narrower function within business management, not management itself.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は小さな会社を経営している。— He manages/runs a small company.
2. 経営状態が悪化している。— The business's management situation (financial health) is worsening.
3. 経営学を大学で学んだ。— I studied business administration at university.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 経営 specifically implies managing a business/enterprise (often with financial responsibility), while 運営 is broader and can apply to managing an event, website, or non-profit organization without necessarily implying profit-seeking — don't use them interchangeably in business contexts.

## 経過(する) (けいか)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** passage of time, progress, course (of events/a situation)

**Similar words:**
- 進行（しんこう）— "progress/advancement," often describing a process moving forward, with more active connotation than the more neutral, time-focused 経過.
- [経緯](vocab-02.md#経緯-けいい)（けいい）— "the sequence of events/background," describing the detailed history leading to a situation rather than the ongoing elapse of time.

**Example sentences:**
1. 手術後の経過は順調だ。— The recovery progress (course) after the surgery is going smoothly.
2. 時間の経過とともに傷は癒える。— The wound heals as time passes.
3. 事件の経過を詳しく説明した。— I explained the course of the incident in detail.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 経過 is frequently used in medical contexts for "recovery progress" (経過観察 "observation of progress/follow-up") — this specialized medical usage is extremely common and worth memorizing separately from the general "passage of time" meaning.

## 軽快 (けいかい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** light, nimble, brisk, rhythmical (na-adjective)

**Similar words:**
- 軽やか（かろやか）— "light/buoyant," a close synonym often describing movement or mood, slightly more poetic.
- テンポがいい（テンポがいい）— "to have good tempo/rhythm," a descriptive phrase overlapping with the musical sense of 軽快.
- 身軽（みがる）— "agile/nimble" (of body movement specifically), narrower than the broader 軽快, which also covers music/mood.

**Opposite words:**
- 重い（おもい）— heavy, sluggish
- 鈍い（にぶい）— dull, slow

**Example sentences:**
1. 軽快な音楽が流れていた。— Light, upbeat music was playing.
2. 彼は軽快な足取りで歩いていた。— He walked with a light, brisk step.
3. 軽快な会話を楽しんだ。— We enjoyed light, breezy conversation.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 軽快 conveys a positive sense of lightness/briskness (movement, music, mood) — unlike 軽い alone, which can sometimes carry a negative nuance (e.g. "flippant/superficial"), 軽快 is almost always complimentary.

## 計画(する) (けいかく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a plan, to plan (to do something)

**Similar words:**
- プラン（プラン）— the loanword "plan," more casual, often for travel/events.
- 企画（きかく）— "a project/proposal plan," often for a specific business or creative initiative, more formal than a general 計画.
- [予定](vocab-33.md#予定-よてい)（よてい）— "a schedule/plan," refers to specific upcoming events/appointments rather than a broader strategic plan.

**Example sentences:**
1. 来年の旅行の計画を立てている。— I'm making plans for next year's trip.
2. その計画は失敗に終わった。— That plan ended in failure.
3. 計画通りに物事が進んだ。— Things proceeded according to plan.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 計画 is a broad, general word for "a plan" (strategic, long-term, or everyday) — distinguish it from 予定 (a scheduled event/appointment) and 企画 (a specific proposal, often for a project or event), which are narrower in scope.

## 景気 (けいき)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** business conditions, the economy (its state), economic climate

**Similar words:**
- 経済（けいざい）— "the economy" as a whole system/field, broader than 景気, which specifically refers to the current state/trend of the economy (good or bad).
- 好景気（こうけいき）/ 不景気（ふけいき）— "economic boom" / "recession," the common compounds built from this word.

**Example sentences:**
1. 最近景気がいいらしい。— I hear the economy has been doing well lately.
2. 景気の回復には時間がかかる。— It takes time for the economy to recover.
3. 景気が悪くて就職が難しい。— Because of the poor economy, finding a job is difficult.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 景気 specifically describes the current economic climate/trend (good or bad), not the economy as an abstract system — don't substitute it for 経済 when talking about economic theory, policy, or structure in general.

## 軽減(する) (けいげん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** reduction, alleviation, mitigation (of a burden, penalty, pain)

**Similar words:**
- 削減（さくげん）— "reduction/cutback," often used for quantities like costs or numbers rather than burdens/pain.
- 緩和（かんわ）— "easing/relaxation" (of regulations, tension, symptoms), close to 軽減 but emphasizes loosening restriction rather than reducing a quantity/weight.

**Opposite words:**
- [増大](vocab-24.md#増大-ぞうだい)（ぞうだい）— increase, growth
- 加重（かじゅう）— aggravation (of a burden/penalty)

**Example sentences:**
1. 税金の負担を軽減する政策が発表された。— A policy to reduce the tax burden was announced.
2. この薬は痛みを軽減する効果がある。— This medicine has the effect of alleviating pain.
3. ストレスを軽減するために運動を始めた。— I started exercising to reduce stress.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 軽減 specifically implies lessening something burdensome (pain, tax, stress, penalty) rather than simply decreasing a neutral quantity — for neutral numeric reduction (e.g. cutting a budget), 削減 is more natural.

## 経験(する) (けいけん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** experience, to experience

**Similar words:**
- [体験](vocab-25.md#体験-たいけん)（たいけん）— "firsthand experience," often emphasizing a personal, direct, often singular experience (e.g. 体験談 "personal account"), while 経験 can be broader/cumulative (work experience, life experience).
- [キャリア](vocab-15.md#キャリア)（キャリア）— "career," focuses on professional experience/history rather than experience in a general sense.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は豊富な経験を持っている。— He has abundant experience.
2. 初めての海外旅行を経験した。— I experienced my first trip abroad.
3. 失敗も貴重な経験になる。— Failure can also become valuable experience.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 経験 often refers to accumulated or general experience (仕事の経験, 人生経験), whereas 体験 emphasizes a specific, often vivid, personal firsthand experience — both can sometimes overlap, but swapping them in set collocations (経験者 vs 体験談) can sound unnatural.

## 稽古 (けいこ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** practice, training, a lesson (esp. traditional arts, martial arts)

**Similar words:**
- [練習](vocab-36.md#練習-れんしゅう)（れんしゅう）— the general, modern word for "practice" (sports, music), while 稽古 specifically carries a traditional, often disciplined connotation (tea ceremony, martial arts, traditional dance).
- [修行](vocab-20.md#修行-しゅぎょう)（しゅぎょう）— "ascetic training/discipline," implies a more spiritual or rigorous self-cultivation, often in martial arts or religious contexts.

**Example sentences:**
1. 毎朝剣道の稽古をしている。— I practice kendo every morning.
2. 茶道の稽古に通っている。— I attend tea ceremony lessons.
3. 稽古を怠ると上達しない。— If you neglect practice, you won't improve.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 稽古 has a traditional/classical connotation (martial arts, tea ceremony, traditional performing arts) — for modern sports or music lessons, 練習 or レッスン is more natural; using 稽古 for, say, piano practice would sound oddly old-fashioned.

## 傾向 (けいこう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a tendency, a trend

**Similar words:**
- トレンド（トレンド）— the loanword "trend," often used for fashion/pop-culture trends rather than general behavioral tendencies.
- 風潮（ふうちょう）— "a social trend/climate of opinion," broader, describing a prevailing attitude in society rather than an individual tendency.
- [性質](vocab-22.md#性質-せいしつ)（せいしつ）— "a nature/characteristic," describing an inherent trait rather than a directional trend.

**Example sentences:**
1. 最近、若者の間で読書離れの傾向がある。— Recently, there's a tendency among young people to read less.
2. 彼は怒りっぽい傾向がある。— He has a tendency to get angry easily.
3. 物価上昇の傾向が続いている。— The trend of rising prices continues.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 傾向 is used both for statistical/societal trends (物価上昇の傾向) and for personal behavioral tendencies (怒りっぽい傾向) — the construction 〜傾向がある ("there is a tendency to...") is a very common, useful pattern worth memorizing as a set phrase.

## 蛍光灯 (けいこうとう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a fluorescent light/lamp

**Similar words:**
- 電球（でんきゅう）— "a (incandescent) light bulb," a different lighting technology from fluorescent tubes.
- LED（エルイーディー）— modern LED lighting, which has largely replaced 蛍光灯 in newer buildings.

**Example sentences:**
1. 蛍光灯が切れたので交換した。— The fluorescent light burned out, so I replaced it.
2. この部屋は蛍光灯で明るく照らされている。— This room is brightly lit by fluorescent lights.
3. 古いオフィスはまだ蛍光灯を使っている。— The old office still uses fluorescent lights.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 蛍光灯 specifically refers to the tube-style fluorescent lighting common in older Japanese homes/offices — it's increasingly being replaced by LED lighting, so the word may carry a slightly dated/utilitarian connotation in modern contexts.

## 警告(する) (けいこく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a warning, a caution, to warn/advise

**Similar words:**
- [注意](vocab-27.md#注意する-ちゅういする)（ちゅうい）— "caution/attention," milder, often just a reminder to be careful rather than a formal, serious warning.
- [忠告](vocab-27.md#忠告-ちゅうこく)（ちゅうこく）— "advice/admonition," more personal and well-meaning, often from a close relation, rather than an official or authoritative warning.

**Example sentences:**
1. 警察は住民に警告を発した。— The police issued a warning to the residents.
2. 医者から健康上の警告を受けた。— I received a health warning from the doctor.
3. 警告を無視した結果、大きな問題になった。— Ignoring the warning resulted in a big problem.

**Meaning:** to kick; to reject, turn down

**Similar words:**
- [蹴とばす](#蹴とばす-けとばす)（けとばす）— a more forceful/emphatic variant of 蹴る, implying kicking something away hard.
- 拒否する（きょひする）— to refuse/reject formally, the figurative sense overlapping with 蹴る's "turn down an offer" meaning but more neutral in register.

**Opposite words:**
- [受け入れる](vocab-08.md#受け入れる-うけいれる)（うけいれる）— to accept

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼はボールを力いっぱい蹴った。— He kicked the ball with all his strength.
2. その依頼を彼は蹴った。— He turned down that request.
3. 馬に蹴られないよう注意してください。— Please be careful not to get kicked by the horse.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Besides the literal "to kick," 蹴る is commonly used figuratively to mean bluntly rejecting an offer, proposal, or invitation — a nuance learners who only know the sports/physical meaning often miss.
