# ～をなおざりにして {#をなおざりにして}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～を等閑にして

**Furigana form:** ～を等閑(なおざり)にして

**Meaning:** "Neglecting X" — expresses that someone is leaving X unattended or treating it carelessly, usually while focusing on something else instead.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 (noun) + をなおざりにして
- Often followed by a clause describing what the subject is doing instead (the thing they're absorbed in rather than X).

**Typical context:** Formal-to-neutral written register; used in both spoken scolding/criticism (a parent or boss pointing out neglect) and formal written commentary (social critique, news). No strong rudeness, but it does carry an implicit critical/disapproving tone toward the subject's behavior.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **をぜんていに (を前提に)** — means "on the premise/assumption of X"; a completely different meaning about basis/condition, not neglect.
- [**はいうにおよばず**](grammar-はいうにおよばず.md) — means "needless to say X"; unrelated function (emphasis via obviousness, not criticism of neglect).
- [**としたことが**](grammar-としたことが.md) — means "X, of all people" (surprise at an uncharacteristic single mistake); differs because をなおざりにして describes an ongoing pattern of neglect, not necessarily a single surprising lapse, and doesn't carry the "of all people" surprise nuance.

**Opposite grammar structures:** A natural conceptual opposite is ～に専念して ("devoting oneself to X") or ～を大切にして ("cherishing/valuing X") — these describe giving full attention/care to something, the reverse of neglecting it. No fixed single-pattern "direct opposite," but these phrases serve as the semantic inverse.

**Example sentences:**
1. 息子は勉強を等閑にして、パソコンゲームに夢中になっている。
   (My son is neglecting his studies, absorbed in computer games instead.)
2. 彼は健康管理を等閑にして、仕事ばかりに没頭していた。
   (He neglected his health management, immersing himself only in work.)
3. 会社は顧客対応を等閑にして、利益拡大ばかりを追求していた。
   (The company neglected customer service, pursuing only profit growth.)
4. その政治家は地域の声を等閑にして、自分の利益ばかりを優先した。
   (That politician neglected the voices of the community, prioritizing only his own interests.)
5. 若い頃は家族との時間を等閑にして、キャリアばかりを追いかけてしまった。
   (When I was young, I neglected time with family, chasing only my career.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Confused on graded tests with ならいざ知らず, はいうにおよばず, and としたことが. The core distinguishing feature students must grasp: をなおざりにして specifically describes neglect/carelessness toward an object of duty or responsibility, usually implying the subject is instead absorbed in something else — it is not about surprise (としたことが), obviousness (はいうにおよばず), or contrastive exception (ならいざ知らず). Look for a juxtaposed clause showing what the subject is doing INSTEAD of attending to X.
