# ～に足るN／に足りない {#にたるN}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～に足るN／に足りない

**Furigana form:** ～にたるN／にたりない

**Meaning:** "Worthy/deserving of X" (に足るN) or "not worth X / unworthy of X" (に足りない) — evaluates whether something or someone meets the standard required to merit a particular description or treatment.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形／名詞 + に足る + 名詞
- 動詞辞書形／名詞 + に足りない
- Example base: 信頼する → 信頼に足る人物

**Typical context:** Formal, written/literary register; common in formal evaluations, character assessments, and persuasive writing about whether someone/something merits trust, respect, or concern.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **にあたいする** — "deserves/is worth X" (e.g., 称賛に値する, "deserves praise"); very close in meaning, both formal evaluative constructions, largely interchangeable though にあたいする is slightly more common in modern formal writing.
- **に見合う** — "matches/is commensurate with X"; shares an evaluative comparison sense but focuses on proportional matching (e.g., 労力に見合う報酬, "reward commensurate with effort") rather than に足る's "meets the minimum worthy standard" framing.
- **に十分だ** — "is sufficient for X"; a more neutral, practical sufficiency statement, lacking に足る's more elevated, character-judging connotation.

**Opposite grammar structures:** に足りない directly serves as the built-in negative counterpart to に足る, so no separate opposite construction is needed.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は信頼に足る人物だと、みんなが認めている。 (Everyone acknowledges that he is a person worthy of trust.)
2. この程度のミスは、取り立てて言うに足りない。 (A mistake of this degree isn't worth making a fuss about.)
3. 彼女の功績は賞賛するに足るものだ。 (Her achievements are worthy of praise.)
4. その証拠は裁判で採用するに足る十分なものだった。 (That evidence was sufficient to be admitted worthy of use in the trial.)
5. 恐れるに足りない相手だと、彼は余裕を見せた。 (He showed composure, treating the opponent as not worth fearing.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** に足る directly modifies a following noun (信頼に足る人物, "a person worthy of trust") — it functions attributively, so learners should remember it needs a noun afterward, unlike predicate-final evaluative words. に足りない, by contrast, is commonly used predicatively on its own (恐れるに足りない, "not worth fearing") as a dismissive judgment — both forms derive from 足る/足りる ("to be sufficient"), framing worthiness as meeting a sufficiency threshold.
