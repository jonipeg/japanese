## 指摘 (してき)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Indication, pointing out (a mistake, problem, or fact)

**Similar words:**
- [指示](#指示-しじ) (しじ) — "instruction/directive," tells someone what to *do*, whereas 指摘 just points out a fact or flaw without necessarily ordering action.
- [批判](vocab-32.md#批判-ひはん) (ひはん) — "criticism," carries a stronger judgmental, often negative evaluative tone; 指摘 is more neutral/factual.
- 言及 (げんきゅう) — "mention, reference," lacks the corrective nuance of 指摘.

**Opposite words:** No direct single-word opposite; 無視する (to ignore) is sometimes used as a situational contrast.

**Example sentences:**
1. 先生に文法の間違いを指摘された。 — I had my grammar mistake pointed out by the teacher.
2. 彼は会議で問題点を指摘した。 — He pointed out the problem at the meeting.
3. ご指摘ありがとうございます、すぐ修正します。 — Thank you for pointing that out; I'll fix it right away.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often use 指摘 as if it means "to criticize harshly," but it is tonally neutral — it simply flags something for attention. Don't confuse with 指示, which issues a command.

## 姿勢 (しせい)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Posture, stance, attitude

**Similar words:**
- [態度](vocab-25.md#態度-たいど) (たいど) — "attitude/manner," more about behavior/demeanor toward people; 姿勢 can be literal (body posture) or figurative (stance on an issue).
- 構え (かまえ) — "stance/posture" especially in martial arts or psychological readiness; narrower in scope.
- 心構え (こころがまえ) — "mental preparedness," focuses only on the mental/attitudinal side.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite; context-dependent (e.g., 猫背 "slouching" as a literal antonym of good posture).

**Example sentences:**
1. 正しい姿勢で座ってください。 — Please sit with correct posture.
2. 彼は仕事に対して真剣な姿勢を見せた。 — He showed a serious attitude toward his work.
3. 政府は慎重な姿勢を崩さなかった。 — The government did not abandon its cautious stance.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes only learn the literal "posture" meaning and miss the figurative "stance/attitude toward an issue" usage, which is extremely common in news and business Japanese.

## 使命 (しめい)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Mission, calling

**Similar words:**
- 任務 (にんむ) — "duty/task assigned," more concrete and official (e.g., military/work task); 使命 is grander, often tied to one's purpose in life.
- 役目 (やくめ) — "role, function," a more everyday word for one's assigned part in something.
- 天職 (てんしょく) — "vocation/calling," overlaps with 使命 but emphasizes a job perfectly suited to the person.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は教育者としての使命を感じている。 — He feels a sense of mission as an educator.
2. 私たちの使命は地域社会に貢献することだ。 — Our mission is to contribute to the local community.
3. 使命を果たすまで諦めない。 — I won't give up until I fulfill my mission.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Note: this word appeared twice in the source list, reinforcing it's high-frequency. Don't confuse with 指名 (しめい, "nomination") — same reading, different kanji and meaning entirely; context and kanji disambiguate.

## 悟る (さとる)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To realize, perceive, attain enlightenment

**Similar words:**
- 判る/分かる (わかる) — plain "to understand/know," much lower register; 悟る implies a sudden, deep, often intuitive realization.
- [承る](vocab-08.md#承る-うけたまわる) (うけたまわる) — humble "to hear/receive (an order)," totally different meaning despite superficial kanji-cluster association; a false friend for learners grouping them.
- 解る (わかる) — another reading of "to understand," emphasizing comprehension rather than insight.

**Opposite words:** 誤解する (ごかいする, "to misunderstand") functions as a loose situational opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼はついに自分の過ちを悟った。 — He finally realized his own mistake.
2. 僧は長い修行の末に悟りを開いた。 — The monk attained enlightenment after long training.
3. 彼女の態度から、もう愛されていないと悟った。 — From her attitude, I realized I was no longer loved.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This is the key confusion cluster from the source note: 悟る (deep realization/enlightenment) vs 判る/分かる (plain understanding) vs 承る (humble "to receive/hear," used in customer service Japanese). Learners often misread 悟る as a formal synonym for わかる — it's not; it implies insight gained through reflection or experience, not simple comprehension.

## 垣根 (かきね)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Fence, hedge

**Similar words:**
- 塀 (へい) — a solid wall/fence (concrete, wood), more substantial than the often plant-based 垣根.
- 柵 (さく) — a fence/railing, often simpler, utilitarian (e.g., around a field).
- [境界](vocab-15.md#境界-きょうかい) (きょうかい) — "boundary," the abstract concept rather than the physical structure.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 庭の垣根にきれいな花が咲いている。 — Pretty flowers are blooming on the garden hedge.
2. 隣の家との垣根を修理した。 — We repaired the hedge between our house and the neighbor's.
3. 心の垣根を取り払って話し合おう。 — Let's tear down the walls between our hearts and talk things through.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 垣根 is also used figuratively for psychological/social "barriers" (心の垣根), similar to English "walls between people" — learners who only know the literal meaning may miss this common idiomatic extension.

## 指図 (さしず)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Instructions, directions (often with a nuance of being bossy or unsolicited)

**Similar words:**
- [指示](#指示-しじ) (しじ) — neutral "instruction," used in professional/formal contexts without the bossy connotation.
- 命令 (めいれい) — "order/command," stronger and more authoritative, often military or hierarchical.
- [指導](vocab-21.md#指導-しどう) (しどう) — "guidance," implies teaching/mentoring rather than simply telling someone what to do.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 人に指図されるのは好きではない。 — I don't like being told what to do by others.
2. 彼女はいつも上から指図する。 — She always gives orders from a position of superiority.
3. 余計な指図はしないでください。 — Please don't give unnecessary orders.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Unlike 指示, which is neutral, 指図 often carries a negative, "who are you to tell me" nuance — especially in the common phrase 指図される (being bossed around). Learners sometimes use it interchangeably with 指示 in polite/professional writing, where it can sound rude.

## 懲りる (こりる)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To learn one's lesson, be discouraged from repeating a mistake

**Similar words:**
- 反省する (はんせいする) — "to reflect/reconsider one's actions," more deliberate and conscious; 懲りる often implies an almost instinctive aversion from pain/failure.
- 懲らしめる (こらしめる) — "to punish/chastise (someone else)," the transitive/causative counterpart targeting another person.
- 身に染みる (みにしみる) — "to be deeply felt/learned the hard way," overlaps in nuance but is more poetic.

**Opposite words:** 懲りない (こりない, "to never learn," i.e., repeating the same mistake) is the natural negated opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 一度失敗して懲りたので、もう無謀な投資はしない。 — Having failed once and learned my lesson, I won't make reckless investments again.
2. 彼は何度叱られても懲りない。 — No matter how many times he's scolded, he never learns.
3. あんな目に遭ったら誰でも懲りるだろう。 — Anyone who went through that would learn their lesson.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Often used in the negative form 懲りない人 (someone who never learns), which is a very common idiomatic criticism. Learners sometimes mistakenly conjugate it like a regular transitive verb targeting others; it's intransitive, describing the speaker's own change in behavior/attitude.

## 偽造 (ぎぞう)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Forgery, counterfeiting

**Similar words:**
- 模造 (もぞう) — "imitation," neutral, legal (e.g., imitation jewelry); 偽造 implies illegal intent to deceive.
- 捏造 (ねつぞう) — "fabrication (of facts/data)," applies to information/evidence rather than physical objects.
- 贋作 (がんさく) — "forged artwork," narrower, specific to art/antiques.

**Opposite words:** 本物 (ほんもの, "the genuine article") as a conceptual opposite noun, though not a verb antonym.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼はパスポートを偽造した罪で逮捕された。 — He was arrested for forging a passport.
2. 偽造通貨が市場に出回っている。 — Counterfeit currency is circulating in the market.
3. 署名を偽造するのは重大な犯罪だ。 — Forging a signature is a serious crime.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes confuse 偽造 (forgery, always illegal/deceptive) with 模造 (imitation, can be legal, e.g., 模造品 "replica product"). The key distinguishing factor is intent to deceive for illicit gain.

## 譲歩 (じょうほ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Concession, compromise

**Similar words:**
- [妥協](vocab-25.md#妥協-だきょう) (だきょう) — "compromise," more mutual — both sides give something; 譲歩 can be one-sided (one party yields).
- 歩み寄り (あゆみより) — "meeting halfway," emphasizes the mutual approach process rather than the act of yielding.
- 和解 (わかい) — "reconciliation/settlement," focuses on resolving a dispute rather than the act of conceding ground.

**Opposite words:** 強硬 (きょうこう, "hard-line, unyielding") functions as a situational opposite stance.

**Example sentences:**
1. 両国は譲歩し合って合意に達した。 — The two countries reached an agreement by making mutual concessions.
2. これ以上の譲歩はできない。 — We cannot make any further concessions.
3. 彼は相手の立場を考えて譲歩した。 — He made a concession considering the other party's position.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Flagged in source as having a possible reading mismatch — confirm it is read じょうほ (not じょうぶ or similar). Learners often conflate 譲歩 with 妥協; 譲歩 emphasizes "yielding ground" (can be unilateral), while 妥協 emphasizes "both sides settling for less than ideal" (mutual).

## 趣旨 (しゅし)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Purport, gist, purpose

**Similar words:**
- [目的](vocab-19.md#目的-もくてき) (もくてき) — "purpose/goal," more concrete and action-oriented; 趣旨 is about the underlying intent/meaning of a statement or plan.
- [要旨](vocab-35.md#要旨-ようし) (ようし) — "summary/main point," focuses on condensing content rather than intent.
- 趣意 (しゅい) — near-synonym, slightly more formal/old-fashioned, used in official documents.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. この会議の趣旨をご説明します。 — Let me explain the purpose of this meeting.
2. 彼の発言の趣旨がよく分からなかった。 — I didn't quite understand the gist of his remarks.
3. 趣旨に賛同して寄付した。 — I donated in support of the cause's purpose.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often translate 趣旨 simply as "purpose," missing that it specifically refers to the *underlying intent or meaning behind words/a plan*, not a concrete goal like 目的. Common in formal writing: 〜という趣旨で ("with the intent/gist that...").

## 淑やか (しとやか)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Graceful, demure

**Similar words:**
- 上品 (じょうひん) — "elegant/refined," broader, can describe objects or manners, not just a person's demeanor.
- [優雅](#優雅-ゆうが) (ゆうが) — "elegance/grace," emphasizes beauty of movement/style rather than modest demeanor.
- おしとやか — same word with honorific お-, used in polite conversation about a woman's manner.

**Opposite words:** がさつ (gasatsu, "coarse, rough-mannered") is a natural antonym.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女は淑やかな振る舞いで知られている。 — She is known for her graceful, demure manner.
2. 淑やかな女性が茶道を教えている。 — A demure woman teaches the tea ceremony.
3. もっと淑やかに話しなさいと母に言われた。 — My mother told me to speak more demurely.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Almost exclusively describes women's demeanor in traditional/classical contexts; learners should avoid applying it to men or to objects. It implies quiet, modest elegance — not flashy or assertive beauty (contrast with 優雅, which can describe grander, more visible elegance).

## 宿命 (しゅくめい)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Fate, destiny

**Similar words:**
- [運命](vocab-09.md#運命-うんめい) (うんめい) — "fate/destiny," more neutral and broader; 宿命 often implies something fixed from birth/unavoidable and slightly heavier/darker.
- 天命 (てんめい) — "heaven's mandate," carries a more positive, purposeful connotation (a destined mission).
- 因縁 (いんねん) — "karmic connection/fate," emphasizes causal/karmic links between people or events.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite (偶然 "chance/coincidence" is a loose conceptual contrast).

**Example sentences:**
1. 二人は出会う宿命にあったのかもしれない。 — The two of them may have been destined to meet.
2. 戦争は彼の宿命を大きく変えた。 — The war greatly changed his fate.
3. これは逃れられない宿命だと感じた。 — I felt this was an inescapable fate.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often use 宿命 and 運命 interchangeably, but 宿命 specifically suggests something predetermined and unchangeable (often negative/heavy), while 運命 is more neutral and can describe lucky turns of fate too.

## 資格 (しかく)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Qualification, credential

**Similar words:**
- [免許](vocab-19.md#免許-めんきょ) (めんきょ) — "license," specifically a government-issued permit (e.g., driver's license); 資格 is broader, including certifications and eligibility.
- 権利 (けんり) — "right," a legal entitlement, distinct from having the qualifications/skills for something.
- 条件 (じょうけん) — "condition/requirement," the criteria one must meet, not the credential itself.

**Opposite words:** 失格 (しっかく, "disqualification") is the direct antonym/loss of qualification.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は教師の資格を持っている。 — He holds a teaching qualification.
2. この試験に合格すれば資格が取得できる。 — If you pass this exam, you can obtain the certification.
3. 参加する資格がないと言われた。 — I was told I didn't have the qualification to participate.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes confuse 資格 (a qualification/certificate one earns) with 免許 (a license specifically issued by an authority, like driving). Also watch for 資格がない meaning "not qualified/entitled" in a broader sense, not just about formal credentials.

## 施設 (しせつ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Facility, institution

**Similar words:**
- [設備](vocab-23.md#設備-せつび) (せつび) — "equipment/facilities (within a building)," refers to installed equipment, not the building/institution itself.
- 機関 (きかん) — "institution/organization," emphasizes the organizational body rather than the physical place.
- [建物](vocab-26.md#建物-たてもの) (たてもの) — "building," purely the physical structure without institutional function.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. この町には高齢者向けの施設が多い。 — This town has many facilities for the elderly.
2. 新しいスポーツ施設がオープンした。 — A new sports facility opened.
3. 福祉施設でボランティアをしている。 — I volunteer at a welfare facility.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 施設 refers to the institution/facility as a whole (e.g., a care home, a gym complex), while 設備 refers to the equipment/infrastructure inside it. Learners often mix these up when translating "facility."

## 躾 (しつけ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Discipline, upbringing, training (especially of children's manners)

**Similar words:**
- 教育 (きょういく) — "education," broader, includes academic learning; 躾 is specifically about manners/behavior training.
- しつけ糸 — unrelated homophone meaning "basting thread" in sewing; a false friend for beginners.
- [訓練](vocab-17.md#訓練する-くんれん) (くんれん) — "training," more about skills/drills (e.g., animal or physical training) than moral/social upbringing.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite; 甘やかし (あまやかし, "spoiling/indulging a child") is a contrasting parenting approach.

**Example sentences:**
1. 親の躾がしっかりしている子供は礼儀正しい。 — Children raised with strict discipline are well-mannered.
2. この犬は躾がよくできている。 — This dog is well-trained/disciplined.
3. 躾の厳しい家庭で育った。 — I was raised in a strictly disciplined household.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This is a kokuji (Japan-made kanji: body 身 + beautiful 美), often tested for its unusual kanji. Note it also applies to pet training, not just child-rearing. Don't confuse with the homophone しつけ meaning "basting" in sewing — context makes this clear but it can surprise learners in dictionaries.

## 生涯 (しょうがい)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** One's whole life, lifetime

**Similar words:**
- 一生 (いっしょう) — "a lifetime," more casual/common synonym, largely interchangeable but 一生 is used more in daily speech.
- 人生 (じんせい) — "life (as a journey/experience)," emphasizes the quality and meaning of life rather than its duration.
- 終生 (しゅうせい) — "for the rest of one's life," formal, emphasizes permanence until death.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite (瞬間 "a moment" is a loose contrast in duration).

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は生涯を通じて研究に捧げた。 — He devoted his entire life to research.
2. 彼女は生涯独身を貫いた。 — She remained single her whole life.
3. この経験は生涯忘れないだろう。 — I will never forget this experience for the rest of my life.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Source note flags this as homophone-adjacent to 障害 (しょうがい, "disability/obstacle") — same reading, completely different kanji and meaning. Learners must rely on kanji/context, as mixing these up in writing is a common and embarrassing error (e.g., 生涯学習 "lifelong learning" vs 障害者 "person with a disability").

## 指示 (しじ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Instruction, directive

**Similar words:**
- [指図](#指図-さしず) (さしず) — "instructions," often with a bossy/unsolicited nuance; 指示 is neutral and professional.
- 命令 (めいれい) — "order/command," stronger, more hierarchical/authoritative.
- [指導](vocab-21.md#指導-しどう) (しどう) — "guidance," implies teaching or mentoring rather than simple directives.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 上司の指示に従って作業を進めた。 — I proceeded with the work following my boss's instructions.
2. 医師の指示通りに薬を飲んでください。 — Please take the medicine as instructed by the doctor.
3. 詳しい指示を待っています。 — I'm waiting for detailed instructions.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Compare directly with 指図: 指示 is neutral/professional (e.g., workplace instructions), while 指図 often implies unwanted bossiness. Using 指図 where 指示 is meant can sound unintentionally rude.

## 失脚 (しっきゃく)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Downfall, loss of position (especially political)

**Similar words:**
- 辞任 (じにん) — "resignation," a voluntary act; 失脚 often implies being forced out due to scandal or power struggle.
- 左遷 (させん) — "demotion/relegation (to a lesser post)," one keeps a position but at lower rank, unlike total downfall.
- 失墜 (しっつい) — "fall (of reputation/authority)," close synonym, often used for loss of prestige rather than literal position.

**Opposite words:** 台頭 (たいとう, "rise to power/prominence") is a natural opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 汚職事件で大臣が失脚した。 — The minister fell from power due to a corruption scandal.
2. クーデターにより独裁者は失脚した。 — The dictator was overthrown in a coup.
3. 彼の失脚は誰も予想していなかった。 — No one had predicted his downfall.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Source flags a possible key mismatch with じたい (自体/辞退) in test materials — confirm the reading is しっきゃく, not confused with 辞退 (じたい, "declining/withdrawal"). 失脚 specifically implies losing power/position, often through scandal or political defeat, not simply quitting.

## 執着 (しゅうちゃく)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Attachment, obsession

**Similar words:**
- 愛着 (あいちゃく) — "attachment/fondness," positive and warm; 執着 often carries a negative, obsessive, hard-to-let-go connotation.
- こだわり — "particularity/fussiness about details," can be positive (craftsmanship) whereas 執着 leans negative/unhealthy.
- 固執 (こしゅう) — "stubborn insistence (on an opinion)," closer synonym, emphasizing refusal to change one's stance.

**Opposite words:** 諦め (あきらめ, "giving up/resignation") is a natural opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は過去の失敗にいつまでも執着している。 — He remains obsessively attached to past failures.
2. お金に執着するのはよくない。 — Being obsessed with money isn't good.
3. 彼女はその考えに執着しすぎている。 — She's too attached to that idea.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Note dual reading しゅうちゃく／しゅうじゃく — both exist, with しゅうちゃく being the modern standard reading and しゅうじゃく more traditional/Buddhist. Learners should distinguish from 愛着, which is warm and positive, whereas 執着 usually signals an unhealthy inability to let go.

## 主催 (しゅさい)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Organizing, hosting (an event)

**Similar words:**
- 主宰 (しゅさい, same reading different kanji) — "presiding over/leading (an organization)," emphasizes leadership role rather than event organization.
- [開催](vocab-12.md#開催-かいさい) (かいさい) — "holding/opening (an event)," focuses on the event taking place, not who organizes it.
- 後援 (こうえん) — "sponsorship/backing," supporting an event without being the main organizer.

**Opposite words:** 参加 (さんか, "participation") is a situational opposite role.

**Example sentences:**
1. この展覧会は市が主催している。 — This exhibition is hosted by the city.
2. 彼らはチャリティーイベントを主催した。 — They organized a charity event.
3. 主催者に感謝の意を伝えた。 — I expressed my gratitude to the organizer.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Homophone trap: 主催 (organizing an event) vs 主宰 (presiding over a group/school of thought, e.g., 劇団を主宰する). Learners must check kanji carefully, as both read しゅさい.

## 承諾 (しょうだく)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Consent, agreement

**Similar words:**
- [同意](vocab-29.md#同意-どうい) (どうい) — "agreement," more general, can be about opinions; 承諾 specifically means formally agreeing to a request/proposal.
- [了承](vocab-33.md#了承-りょうしょう) (りょうしょう) — "understanding and approval," slightly softer, often used when acknowledging and accepting a situation.
- [許可](vocab-16.md#許可-きょか) (きょか) — "permission," granted by someone in authority, whereas 承諾 is agreeing to a request between relatively equal parties.

**Opposite words:** 拒否 (きょひ, "refusal") is the direct antonym.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は契約条件に承諾した。 — He consented to the contract terms.
2. ご承諾いただきありがとうございます。 — Thank you for your consent.
3. 承諾を得てから作業を始めた。 — I started the work after obtaining consent.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often use 承諾 and 了承 interchangeably, but 承諾 is more formal/binding (e.g., contracts), while 了承 is softer, often used to mean "understood and accepted" in everyday business correspondence.

## 奨励 (しょうれい)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Encouragement, promotion (of a policy or activity)

**Similar words:**
- [推奨](vocab-21.md#推奨-すいしょう) (すいしょう) — "recommendation," suggests something is good/advisable, often for products or methods, more neutral than 奨励.
- [激励](vocab-17.md#激励する-げきれい) (げきれい) — "encouragement (emotional support)," cheering someone on personally, not promoting a policy.
- [促進](vocab-24.md#促進-そくしん) (そくしん) — "promotion/acceleration (of a process)," focuses on speeding something up rather than encouraging adoption.

**Opposite words:** 抑制 (よくせい, "suppression/restraint") is a natural opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 政府は再生可能エネルギーの利用を奨励している。 — The government encourages the use of renewable energy.
2. 学校は読書を奨励している。 — The school encourages reading.
3. 貯蓄を奨励する政策が導入された。 — A policy encouraging savings was introduced.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 奨励 is often used in institutional/governmental contexts promoting a behavior for the public good, distinct from 推奨 (a more neutral recommendation, often in product reviews) and 激励 (personal, emotional cheering-on).

## しくじる
#vocabulary

## 欠陥 (けっかん)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Defect, flaw

**Similar words:**
- [欠点](#欠点-けってん) (けってん) — "shortcoming/fault," broader, can describe personality flaws too; 欠陥 is more serious, often implies a structural/functional defect (e.g., product defects, safety issues).
- [欠損](#欠損-けっそん) (けっそん) — "loss/deficit," refers to something missing or a financial/physical loss, not a design flaw.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This is part of the 欠-cluster the source flags: 欠陥 (defect/flaw, serious/structural) vs 欠点 (shortcoming, can be personal/minor) vs 欠損 (loss/deficit, missing part or money) vs 欠勤 (absence from work). #vocabulary

**Meaning:** Shortcoming, fault

**Similar words:**
- [欠陥](#欠陥-けっかん) (けっかん) — "defect," more serious/structural, often of products or systems rather than personal traits.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 欠点 is commonly used for personality traits (unlike 欠陥, reserved for products/systems). The standard pairing 長所と短所/欠点 ("strengths and weaknesses") is a staple of job interviews and self-introductions in Japan.

## 欠損 (けっそん)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Loss, deficit (financial or physical)

**Similar words:**

- [赤字](vocab-06.md#赤字-あかじ) (あかじ) — "deficit/being in the red," specifically financial, informal/common term.

## 厳しい (きびしい)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Strict, severe

**Similar words:**
- 著しい (いちじるしい) — "remarkable/striking (degree of change)," describes the magnitude of something (e.g., 著しい進歩 "remarkable progress"), unrelated to strictness.
- 甚だしい (はなはだしい) — "extreme, to a great degree" (often negative), e.g., 甚だしい誤解 "a gross misunderstanding" — about degree, not discipline.
- 激しい (はげしい) — "intense, violent/fierce," describes intensity of action/emotion (e.g., 激しい雨 "heavy rain"), different domain from strictness.

**Opposite words:** 甘い (あまい, "lenient/soft," literally "sweet") is the common antonym in this context.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女は子供に厳しい先生だ。 — She is a strict teacher to the children.
2. 今年の冬はとても厳しい寒さだった。 — This winter was extremely severe/harsh cold.
3. 厳しい競争を勝ち抜いた。 — I made it through fierce competition.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This is the source-flagged confusion cluster: all four words (厳しい/著しい/甚だしい/激しい) can describe "a lot/a high degree" of something, but each attaches to a different domain — 厳しい (strictness/harshness), 著しい (remarkable degree, usually positive/neutral change), 甚だしい (extreme degree, usually negative, more literary), 激しい (intensity/violence of physical phenomena or emotion). Learners often misuse one for another in essays.

#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Reached, arrived at (a state or conclusion)

## 経緯 (けいい)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Circumstances, background, details of how something came about

**Similar words:**
- [経営](vocab-17.md#経営する-けいえい) (けいえい) — "management (of a business)," completely different meaning despite the shared 経 kanji — a common false-friend trap.
- [経過](vocab-17.md#経過する-けいか) (けいか) — "progress/course (of events over time)," overlaps but focuses on the passage of time/development, while 経緯 focuses on the causal chain of events leading to a result.
- いきさつ — native Japanese word, near-exact synonym, slightly more colloquial than 経緯.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 事件に至った経緯を説明してください。 — Please explain the circumstances that led to the incident.
2. 詳しい経緯はまだ分かっていない。 — The detailed background is still unknown.
3. これまでの経緯を踏まえて判断する。 — I will make a decision based on the circumstances so far.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Reconfirmed as high-frequency in scan4 source material. The main trap is confusing 経緯 (background/how things came to be) with the completely unrelated 経営 (management) — both share けい but mean entirely different things; also distinguish from 経過 (progress over time) which doesn't necessarily explain *why/how* something happened, just that time passed.

**Example sentences:**

3. ニンニクを先に炒めてから他の具材を加える。 — Stir-fry the garlic first before adding the other ingredients.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often confuse the different Japanese cooking verbs (焼く/揚げる/炒める/煮る) since English "fry" covers multiple distinct Japanese cooking methods — 炒める specifically implies quick cooking with a small amount of oil while tossing/stirring (stir-fry), distinct from deep-frying (揚げる) or pan-searing (焼く).

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite (connective word).

**Example sentences:**
1. 私は彼を信頼している。なぜなら、彼は一度も嘘をついたことがないからだ。 — I trust him. The reason is, he has never once lied.
2. 試験に落ちた。なぜなら、全く勉強しなかったからだ。 — I failed the exam. Because I didn't study at all.
3. なぜならその理論には証拠が欠けているからだ。 — This is because that theory lacks evidence.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This is the exact source-flagged student error: a student picked おまけに (moreover, on top of that — an additive connector) instead of なぜなら (because, an explanatory/causal connector) — these serve entirely different logical functions (addition vs. causation), a critical distinction in formal/essay Japanese. なぜなら is almost always paired with a sentence ending in 〜からだ/〜からです.

## 要するに (ようするに)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** In short, to sum up

**Similar words:**
- つまり — "in other words, that is to say," very close synonym, slightly more common/neutral in everyday speech.
- [結局](vocab-17.md#結局-けっきょく) (けっきょく) — "in the end, ultimately," emphasizes a final outcome after a process, rather than summarizing a point just made.
- 言い換えれば (いいかえれば) — "in other words," more literally means "if you rephrase it," used for clarifying/restating rather than condensing to an essential point.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 要するに、彼は謝りたくないのだ。 — In short, he doesn't want to apologize.
2. 長々と説明したが、要するに計画は中止ということだ。 — I explained at length, but in short, the plan is cancelled.
3. 要するに、努力が足りなかったということだ。 — To sum up, there simply wasn't enough effort.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 要するに is used to distill a long explanation down to its essential point — learners should note it typically follows a longer preceding explanation and introduces a concise summary, often with a slightly blunt or conclusive tone.

## ただし
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** However, provided that

**Similar words:**
- しかし — "however, but," a more general contrastive connector; ただし specifically introduces a condition/exception/caveat to what was just stated, rather than a full contrast.
- もっとも — "although, to be sure," introduces a qualifying remark, close in function but slightly more literary.
- ただ — "however, only that," casual, close synonym, commonly used in spoken Japanese for a similar caveat function.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 参加は自由です。ただし、事前登録が必要です。 — Participation is free. However, advance registration is required.
2. 明日は休みだ。ただし、緊急の場合は連絡すること。 — Tomorrow is a day off. However, contact me in case of an emergency.
3. 値段は安い。ただし、品質はそれなりだ。 — The price is cheap. However, the quality is just so-so.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ただし specifically adds a condition, exception, or caveat to a preceding statement (not a full contrast like しかし) — very common in official notices, rules, and contracts (e.g., 〜ただし、...の場合を除く "however, except in the case of...").

## なお
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Additionally, furthermore (often introducing supplementary information)

**Similar words:**
- さらに — "furthermore, moreover," adds emphasis/escalation to a point; なお is more neutral, often used to add a supplementary note or remark almost as an aside.
- ちなみに — "by the way, incidentally," introduces a related but tangential piece of information, similar register to なお.
- また — "also, additionally," a more basic, general additive connector.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 会議は3時に始まります。なお、資料は事前に配布します。 — The meeting starts at 3 PM. Additionally, materials will be distributed beforehand.
2. なお、詳細は後日お知らせします。 — Additionally, details will be announced at a later date.
3. 彼はなお元気に働いている。 — He is still working energetically. (Note: なお also means "still/yet" in this adverbial usage.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** なお has two distinct uses learners often conflate: (1) a formal connector meaning "additionally/furthermore" introducing supplementary notes (common in official announcements), and (2) an adverb meaning "still, yet" (なお〜, similar to 依然として). Context determines which sense applies.

## いわば
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** So to speak, as it were

**Similar words:**
- 言ってみれば — "if you were to say it," a more literal paraphrase with the same function, slightly more casual/explicit.
- まるで — "just like, as if," used for similes/comparisons but doesn't carry the same "figuratively speaking" framing device function.
- たとえて言えば — "to use a metaphor," more explicitly flags that a comparison/metaphor follows, similar but more verbose than いわば.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼はいわば会社の顔だ。 — He is, so to speak, the face of the company.
2. この街はいわば第二の故郷だ。 — This town is, in a sense, my second hometown.
3. それはいわば氷山の一角に過ぎない。 — That is, so to speak, merely the tip of the iceberg.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** いわば signals that a figurative/metaphorical expression follows, softening a bold comparison — learners should use it specifically before metaphorical statements, not for literal factual additions (where また or さらに would be appropriate instead).

## ゆえに
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Therefore, hence

**Similar words:**
- だから — "so, therefore," the everyday equivalent; ゆえに is formal/literary, common in philosophical, academic, or legal writing (famously in Descartes' translated "我思う、ゆえに我あり" — "I think, therefore I am").
- したがって — "therefore, accordingly," formal, close synonym, very common in academic/business writing as well.
- [それゆえ](vocab-24.md#それゆえ) — a slightly fuller variant of ゆえに with the same meaning and register.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite (connective word), though それでも ("even so, nevertheless") functions as a contrastive counter-connector.

**Example sentences:**
1. 我思う、ゆえに我あり。 — I think, therefore I am.
2. 証拠は不十分である。ゆえに、彼を有罪とすることはできない。 — The evidence is insufficient. Therefore, he cannot be found guilty.
3. 彼は経験豊富だ。ゆえに、この仕事に適任である。 — He is highly experienced. Therefore, he is suited for this job.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This is the exact source-flagged student error: a student picked それでも (even so, nevertheless — a concessive/contrastive connector) instead of ゆえに (therefore — a causal/conclusive connector) — these point in opposite logical directions (despite X vs. because of X), making this a critical logical-connector distinction for formal writing and exam reading comprehension.

## かつ
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Moreover, and also (simultaneously)

**Similar words:**
- そして — "and then, and," a more general sequential/additive connector; かつ specifically emphasizes two qualities or conditions holding true simultaneously, common in formal/legal language.
- しかも — "moreover, what's more," adds an often surprising or intensifying additional point, slightly more emphatic than かつ.
- 同時に (どうじに) — "at the same time," explicitly temporal, whereas かつ can connect qualities without strict literal simultaneity.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は優秀であり、かつ謙虚だ。 — He is talented, and moreover, humble.
2. この製品は安価であり、かつ高品質である。 — This product is inexpensive and, at the same time, high quality.
3. 条件を満たし、かつ期限内に提出すること。 — Meet the requirements and submit within the deadline.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** かつ is a formal connector joining two simultaneously-true qualities or conditions, very common in legal/official documents and formal writing — learners should reserve it for formal register rather than casual conversation, where そして or し would be more natural.

## および
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** And, as well as

**Similar words:**
- と — the basic everyday "and" particle, used for simple listing in casual speech.
- ならびに — "and, together with," a very formal synonym, often used alongside および in official lists (おしゃれ区別: および for the main connector, ならびに for a secondary-level grouping).
- や — "and (among others)," casual, implies an incomplete/illustrative list, unlike the exhaustive listing implied by および.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 氏名および住所を記入してください。 — Please fill in your name and address.
2. 本規則は社員および契約社員に適用される。 — This rule applies to employees as well as contract workers.
3. 会議には部長および課長が出席した。 — The department head and section chief attended the meeting.
4. (Note: three examples given per field is standard count, this entry included four for thoroughness.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** および is a formal written connector for listing items (especially in official documents, forms, and legal text) — learners should avoid it in casual conversation, where と or や are standard. It is often paired with ならびに in documents with nested/hierarchical lists.

## 甲板 (かんぱん)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Deck (of a ship)

**Similar words:**
- デッキ — loanword synonym, commonly used interchangeably, especially for smaller boats or casual contexts; 甲板 sounds more technical/nautical.
- 船体 (せんたい) — "ship's hull/body," the overall structure of the ship, not specifically the deck surface.
- 船室 (せんしつ) — "cabin (on a ship)," an interior room, the opposite of the open-air deck.

**Opposite words:** 船底 (せんてい, "the bottom of the ship/hull") is a loose structural opposite (top vs. bottom of the vessel).

**Example sentences:**
1. 乗客たちは甲板に出て景色を眺めた。 — The passengers went out on deck to admire the scenery.
2. 嵐の中、船員が甲板で作業していた。 — The crew worked on deck in the middle of the storm.
3. 甲板から海に飛び込む人もいた。 — Some people even jumped from the deck into the sea.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Appears frequently in reading-comprehension passages about ships, voyages, or disasters at sea — learners should simply recognize the kanji reading かんぱん and that it specifically refers to a ship's open deck, not an interior room.

## 骸骨 (がいこつ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Skeleton

**Similar words:**
- 骨格 (こっかく) — "skeletal framework/bone structure," refers to the structural framework of bones within a living being, more anatomical/technical than the often eerie/visual connotation of 骸骨.
- ガイコツ (same word, katakana) — informal/visual rendering often used in casual or Halloween-related contexts.
- 死体 (したい) — "corpse," refers to a dead body with flesh still present, an earlier stage than a bare 骸骨.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 洞窟の中で古い骸骨が発見された。 — An old skeleton was discovered inside the cave.
2. ハロウィンの飾りに骸骨の人形を使った。 — I used a skeleton figure as a Halloween decoration.
3. 彼は痩せて骸骨のようになっていた。 — He had become so thin he looked like a skeleton.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 骸骨 often carries an eerie, visual connotation (horror, Halloween, archaeological discovery), distinct from the more clinical/anatomical 骨格 (skeletal structure, used in medical/biological contexts) — learners should note this register difference.

## 屍体 (したい)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Corpse

**Similar words:**
- 死体 (したい, same reading, more common kanji) — the standard modern way to write this word; 屍体 uses an older/more literary kanji for "corpse" and appears mainly in literary or classical contexts.
- 遺体 (いたい) — "the remains (of a deceased person)," a more respectful, formal term used when referring to someone's body with dignity (e.g., in funeral contexts).
- 死骸 (しがい) — "carcass/corpse," often used for animals or in a more clinical/forensic sense.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 森の中で動物の屍体が見つかった。 — An animal's corpse was found in the forest.
2. 事件現場には屍体が横たわっていた。 — A corpse lay at the scene of the incident.
3. 小説の中で屍体の描写が続いた。 — The description of the corpse continued in the novel.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 屍体 and 死体 are essentially the same word (したい); 死体 is the standard modern spelling used in news/forensic contexts, while 屍 is a more literary/archaic kanji seen in novels or poetic writing. For respectful reference to a deceased person's body, 遺体 is the appropriate and more commonly used term in formal/public announcements.

## 決めこむ (きめこむ)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To assume/decide arbitrarily (often incorrectly, without basis)

**Similar words:**
- [決める](vocab-14.md#決める-きめる) (きめる) — plain "to decide," neutral, doesn't carry the connotation of an unfounded or presumptuous assumption.
- [思い込む](vocab-11.md#思い込む-おもいこむ) (おもいこむ) — "to be convinced (often mistakenly), to assume," very close synonym, emphasizing a firm (sometimes false) belief.
- 勝手に決める (かってにきめる) — "to decide arbitrarily/selfishly," a descriptive paraphrase capturing the same presumptuous nuance as 決めこむ.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は自分が正しいと決めこんでいる。 — He has arbitrarily convinced himself that he is right.
2. 誰も来ないだろうと決めこんで準備をしなかった。 — Assuming no one would come, I didn't prepare.
3. 勝手に失敗すると決めこまないでほしい。 — I wish you wouldn't just arbitrarily assume it will fail.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 決めこむ implies deciding/assuming something without proper basis, often turning out to be wrong or presumptuous — distinct from neutral 決める (simply "to decide"). Learners should note the added nuance of unfounded conviction or presumption that こむ contributes here.

## いとま
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Spare time, leisure (also used in the sense of "excuse me, I must take my leave")

**Similar words:**
- 暇 (ひま) — "free time," the everyday, common word; いとま is more old-fashioned/literary, often used in set phrases.
- 余裕 (よゆう) — "margin, leeway (of time or resources)," emphasizes having extra capacity rather than simply unoccupied time.
- お暇する (おいとまする) — related polite set phrase meaning "to take one's leave," derived from the same root word.

**Opposite words:** 多忙 (たぼう, "being very busy") is a natural opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 忙しくて本を読むいとまもない。 — I'm so busy I don't even have spare time to read.
2. そろそろお暇いたします。 — I should be taking my leave now.
3. 彼は寸暇を惜しんで勉強した。 — He studied, begrudging even a moment's spare time.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** いとま has two related but distinct common usages: (1) literary "spare time" (often in the negative, 〜するいとまもない "no time even to..."), and (2) the polite phrase お暇する "to take one's leave" when departing — learners should recognize both, as they appear in different registers (literary writing vs. polite conversational farewell).

## 加担する (かたんする)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To lend support, be complicit (often in something wrongful)

**Similar words:**
- 協力する (きょうりょくする) — "to cooperate," neutral/positive connotation of helping, unlike 加担する which often implies complicity in something illicit or morally questionable.
- 荷担する (かたんする, alternate kanji) — the same word with different kanji, used interchangeably in some older texts.
- 共犯 (きょうはん) — "accomplice (in a crime)," a noun referring to the person, rather than the act of lending support.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は犯罪に加担した疑いがある。 — He is suspected of being complicit in the crime.
2. 不正行為に加担するのは許されない。 — Being complicit in fraudulent conduct is not permissible.
3. 知らず知らずのうちに悪事に加担していた。 — I had unknowingly lent support to a wrongdoing.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 加担する almost always carries a negative connotation (lending support to something wrong, illegal, or harmful) — learners should avoid using it for neutral/positive cooperation, where 協力する is the appropriate word instead.

## 生態系 (せいたいけい)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** Ecosystem

**Similar words:**
- [環境](vocab-13.md#環境-かんきょう) (かんきょう) — "environment," broader, refers to surroundings generally, not specifically the interconnected web of living organisms and their habitat.
- 自然 (しぜん) — "nature," the broadest general term for the natural world, without the systemic/scientific framing of 生態系.
- 食物連鎖 (しょくもつれんさ) — "food chain," a specific component/mechanism within an ecosystem, not the ecosystem as a whole.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. この湖は独自の生態系を持っている。 — This lake has its own unique ecosystem.
2. 外来種の侵入が生態系を破壊する恐れがある。 — The invasion of non-native species risks destroying the ecosystem.
3. 生態系のバランスを保つことが重要だ。 — It's important to maintain the balance of the ecosystem.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 生態系 is a precise scientific/ecological term (the English loan-translation of "ecosystem") — learners should use it in scientific/environmental contexts rather than as a loose synonym for 自然 (nature) or 環境 (environment), which are broader and less systemic in meaning.

## 思いを馳せる (おもいをはせる)
#vocabulary

**Meaning:** To imagine, ponder (especially about far-off places, times, or people)

**Similar words:**
- 想像する (そうぞうする) — "to imagine," more general and neutral; 思いを馳せる carries a wistful, often nostalgic or longing quality, usually directed at something distant in time or space.
- 偲ぶ (しのぶ) — "to fondly remember/reminisce about (someone, often deceased)," narrower, specifically about missing/honoring someone's memory.
- 懐かしむ (なつかしむ) — "to feel nostalgic about," close in emotional tone but specifically about one's own past memories, not necessarily distant places or other people.

**Opposite words:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 故郷にいる家族に思いを馳せた。 — I thought wistfully of my family back home.
2. 遠い昔の出来事に思いを馳せる。 — I ponder events from the distant past.
3. 彼は宇宙の彼方に思いを馳せた。 — He let his thoughts wander to the far reaches of space.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This is a poetic, literary expression — learners should reserve it for reflective/emotional writing (essays, literature) rather than casual conversation, where 想像する or 考える would be more natural. It specifically implies letting one's mind wander wistfully toward something distant (in space, time, or emotional closeness), not simply forming a mental image.
