# ～わけではない {#わけではない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～訳ではない

**Furigana form:** ～わけではない (訳 read as わけ)

**Meaning:** "It's not that..., it doesn't mean that..." — a partial negation, denying a full/absolute interpretation of a previous statement without completely rejecting it. Often used to soften or qualify a generalization.

**Conjugation:**
- 普通形 (plain form) + わけではない
- 動詞普通形 + わけではない: 嫌いなわけではない (it's not that I dislike it)
- い形容詞普通形 + わけではない: 高いわけではない (it's not that it's expensive)
- な形容詞 + な + わけではない: 便利なわけではない (it's not that it's convenient)
- 名詞 + という + わけではない: 全員が反対というわけではない (it's not that everyone's opposed)

**Typical context:** Spoken and written, neutral register. Frequently paired with 必ずしも ("not necessarily") for emphasis: 必ずしも〜わけではない.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**わけがない**](grammar-わけがない.md) — strong, total denial of possibility; much stronger than the soft partial negation of わけではない.
- [**とは限らない**](grammar-とは限らない.md) — "isn't necessarily the case," very similar nuance, often interchangeable with わけではない.
- **〜ないわけでもない** — double negative variant, meaning "it's not that it's NOT X either" (a mild affirmation).

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct single opposite; わけだ (affirming a conclusion) functions as a structural contrast.

**Example sentences:**
1. 野菜が嫌いなわけではないが、あまり食べたくない時もある。
   (It's not that I dislike vegetables, but sometimes I just don't feel like eating them.)
2. 彼の意見に反対というわけではないが、少し心配な点がある。
   (It's not that I'm against his opinion, but there's a point that worries me a bit.)
3. お金があれば幸せというわけではない。
   (It's not that having money means you're happy.)
4. 全部の宿題が終わったわけではなく、まだ少し残っている。
   (It's not that all the homework is finished — there's still a bit left.)
5. 彼女が怒っているわけではなく、ただ疲れているだけだ。
   (It's not that she's angry — she's just tired.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Key nuance: わけではない is a *partial* negation that qualifies rather than denies entirely — don't confuse it with わけがない, which flatly denies all possibility. A common learner error is using わけではない when total denial (わけがない or 絶対にない) is intended, which creates an unintentionally hedgy, uncertain-sounding statement.
