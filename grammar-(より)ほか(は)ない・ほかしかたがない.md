# ～(より)ほか(は)ない／～ほかしかたがない {#(より)ほか(は)ない・ほかしかたがない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～(より)他(は)ない／～他仕方がない

**Furigana form:** ～(より)ほか(は)ない／～ほかしかたがない (他 read ほか)

**Meaning:** "Have no other choice but to X," "there's nothing to do except X" — expresses that, after considering the situation, only one option remains; a resigned acknowledgment of limited choice.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形 + (より)ほか(は)ない
- 動詞辞書形 + ほか仕方がない／しかたがない
- もう謝るよりほかはない。(There's nothing to do but apologize now.)
- 彼に頼むほかしかたがない。(There's no choice but to ask him.)

**Typical context:** Spoken and written, neutral-to-formal. Conveys resignation or reluctant acceptance of a limited option; common when describing difficult circumstances.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**しかない**](grammar-しかない.md) — the most common/casual equivalent ("there's nothing to do but..."), interchangeable in most contexts.
- [**ざるを得ない**](grammar-ざるを得ない.md) — "cannot help but do," more formal, emphasizes being forced by circumstances rather than simply having no alternative.
- **よりほかに方法がない** — a more explicit/elaborated variant emphasizing "no other method."

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; いくらでも方法がある ("there are plenty of options") represents the conceptual opposite of having abundant choice.

**Example sentences:**
1. 終電を逃したので、タクシーで帰るほかない。
   (I missed the last train, so there's no choice but to go home by taxi.)
2. こうなったら、最後まで戦うよりほかはない。
   (Now that it's come to this, there's nothing to do but fight to the end.)
3. 薬が効かないなら、手術するほかしかたがない。
   (If the medicine doesn't work, there's no choice but to have surgery.)
4. 彼が謝らない以上、こちらから折れるほかない。
   (As long as he won't apologize, there's no choice but for us to back down.)
5. 資金が足りないので、計画を縮小するよりほかはない。
   (Since there isn't enough funding, there's no choice but to scale down the plan.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ほかない/しかない both mean "no other choice," but しかない pairs with the plain negative-feeling しか (always needs context of limitation), while ほかない can stand alone more naturally in formal writing. Learners sometimes drop は or より incorrectly — all of ほかない／ほかはない／よりほかない are acceptable variants, but mixing particles incorrectly (e.g., をほかない) is a common error.
