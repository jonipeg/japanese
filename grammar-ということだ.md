# ～ということだ {#ということだ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ということだ

**Furigana form:** ～ということだ (no special kanji readings needed)

**Meaning:** (1) "I heard that..." — reporting information from a secondhand source (hearsay, news, something someone said). (2) "It means that..." — restating or drawing a conclusion/paraphrase from something previously said.

**Conjugation:**
- Plain form of verb/adjective + ということだ
- 名詞 + だ + ということだ (often 名詞＋ということだ)
- 天気予報によると、明日は雨が降るということだ。(According to the weather forecast, it will rain tomorrow.)
- つまり、彼は来月引っ越すということだ。(In other words, it means he's moving next month.)

**Typical context:** Formal/neutral, common in news reports, announcements, and written reports relaying information from another source (according to ~, によると～ということだ). Also used in spoken conversation to summarize/paraphrase what someone said.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **そうだ (伝聞)** — also hearsay, but more casual/direct report of what was heard; ということだ sounds slightly more formal and often used with によると.
- **らしい** — hearsay with a nuance of the speaker's own inference added, less purely "quoting."
- **とのことだ** — nearly identical to ということだ for hearsay, slightly more formal/written.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though direct firsthand statements without any reporting marker contrast with it.

**Example sentences:**
1. ニュースによると、台風は明日上陸するということだ。
   (According to the news, I heard that the typhoon will make landfall tomorrow.)
2. 彼女の話では、来年結婚するということだ。
   (According to her, I heard she's getting married next year.)
3. つまり、このプロジェクトは中止するということですね。
   (In other words, it means this project is being cancelled, right?)
4. 社長のメールによると、来月から給料が上がるということだ。
   (According to the president's email, I heard that salaries will go up starting next month.)
5. 彼が何も言わないということは、賛成だということだ。
   (The fact that he's saying nothing means he agrees.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often confuse the hearsay sense with the paraphrase/conclusion sense — context (presence of によると vs. つまり/～ということは) distinguishes them. Also don't confuse with ということにする (to decide to treat something as being the case), which is a different, related pattern.
