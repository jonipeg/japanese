# ～としたら／とすれば {#としたら・とすれば}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～としたら／とすれば

**Furigana form:** ～としたら／とすれば (no special kanji readings needed)

**Meaning:** "If (we) assume X," "supposing that X" — introduces a hypothetical premise and considers its logical consequence; often used for hypothetical reasoning rather than real/likely conditions.

**Conjugation:**
- 普通形 + としたら／とすれば
- 名詞 + だ + としたら／とすれば
- 宝くじが当たったとしたら、何をしますか。(If you were to win the lottery, what would you do?)

**Typical context:** Neutral, spoken and written; used for hypothetical "what if" reasoning, logical deduction from an assumption, or polite suggestions based on a hypothetical scenario.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **なら** — more general conditional, can be used for both hypothetical and realistic conditions; としたら／とすれば lean more toward pure hypotheticals.
- **もし～ば／たら** — basic conditional forms; としたら／とすれば add a more deliberate "assuming/supposing" framing, often paired with もし for emphasis.
- **と考えると** — "if we think about it this way," similar logical-reasoning framing but less strictly conditional.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. もし生まれ変わるとしたら、何になりたいですか。
   (If you were to be reborn, what would you want to become?)
2. 彼の話が本当だとすれば、大変なことになる。
   (If what he's saying is true, that would be a serious problem.)
3. このままのペースで進むとしたら、来月には完成するだろう。
   (If we continue at this pace, it will probably be finished by next month.)
4. 一つだけ願いが叶うとすれば、健康を選びます。
   (If only one wish could come true, I would choose health.)
5. 彼が犯人だとしたら、動機は何だろう。
   (If he is the culprit, what could the motive be?)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** としたら／とすれば present a deliberate hypothetical assumption for the sake of reasoning, often introduced by もし for emphasis — they're less natural for simple everyday conditionals (use たら／なら instead for routine "if" statements). としたら and とすれば are largely interchangeable, though とすれば can feel slightly more formal/logical in tone.
