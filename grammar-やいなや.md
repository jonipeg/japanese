# ～や(否や) {#やいなや}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～や（否や）

**Furigana form:** ～や（いなや）

**Meaning:** "The moment/right after X happens, Y" — describes an action happening immediately after, almost simultaneously with, another action.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形 + や（否や）
- Example base: 着く → 着くや否や

**Typical context:** Formal, written/literary register; common in narrative writing to convey a dramatic, immediate sequence of events. Not used in casual conversation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**が早いか**](grammar-が早いか.md) — "no sooner had X than Y"; nearly synonymous and equally literary, both emphasizing near-simultaneous sequential action, often interchangeable.
- [**たとたんに**](grammar-たとたんに.md) — "the instant X happened, Y"; similar immediacy but slightly more neutral/common register, usable more broadly than や否や's literary flavor.
- [**そばから**](grammar-そばから.md) — "no sooner X than Y (repeatedly)"; shares the immediacy nuance but そばから specifically implies a repeated, almost futile cycle (e.g., cleaning up as fast as a mess is made), whereas や否や describes a single immediate sequence.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; しばらくしてから ("after some time had passed") would contrast with や否や's immediate, near-simultaneous timing.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は家に着くや否や、倒れるように眠ってしまった。 (The moment he got home, he collapsed into sleep.)
2. ベルが鳴るや否や、生徒たちは教室を飛び出した。 (The instant the bell rang, the students burst out of the classroom.)
3. その知らせを聞くや否や、彼女は泣き出した。 (The moment she heard the news, she burst into tears.)
4. 開場するや否や、ファンたちが一斉になだれ込んだ。 (The moment the doors opened, the fans came flooding in all at once.)
5. 彼は新しい情報を得るや否や、すぐに上司に報告した。 (The moment he obtained the new information, he immediately reported it to his boss.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** や(否や) always attaches to a dictionary-form verb and emphasizes that the second action follows the first essentially instantaneously — it is a literary pattern reserved for narrative drama, not everyday conversation (where とたんに or すぐに would be used instead). The 否や portion is often dropped in casual literary use (just や), but both forms carry the same meaning; don't confuse this や with the sentence-final question particle や in dialectal speech.
