# ～ものがある {#ものがある}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ものがある

**Furigana form:** ～ものがある (no special kanji reading)

**Meaning:** "There is a certain quality of X," "there's something that..." — expresses the speaker's subjective emotional impression or deep feeling that a certain quality is present, without stating it as a flat objective fact. Often used for nuanced, hard-to-fully-articulate impressions.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形 + ものがある
- い形容詞 + ものがある
- な形容詞 + な + ものがある
- 彼の演技には心に訴えるものがある。(There's something in his acting that appeals to the heart.)
- この話には考えさせられるものがある。(There's something in this story that makes you think.)

**Typical context:** Written and formal spoken Japanese — common in reviews, essays, commentary, and reflective speech. Carries a literary/contemplative tone.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **感じがする** — "it feels like," more casual way to express a subjective impression.
- **ところがある** — "there's a side/aspect of," similar structure for describing a partial characteristic, often of a person.
- **と言わざるを得ない** — "one cannot help but say," stronger, more assertive expression of an impression.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a flat factual statement without ものがある (e.g., simply stating it as objective fact) would be the structural contrast.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女の歌声には聞く人の心を打つものがある。
   (There is something in her singing voice that moves the hearts of those who hear it.)
2. この映画のラストシーンには、涙を誘うものがある。
   (There's something in this movie's final scene that brings tears.)
3. 若くして成功した彼の人生には、学ぶべきものがある。
   (There is something to learn from his life, having succeeded at such a young age.)
4. あの選手のプレーには、見る者を圧倒するものがある。
   (There's something in that player's performance that overwhelms the viewer.)
5. 彼の言葉には、どこか説得力に欠けるものがある。
   (There's something in his words that somehow lacks persuasiveness.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ものがある is used to express a subjective, often hard-to-pin-down impression — it should not be used for simple objective facts (e.g., don't use it to state "this book has 300 pages"). It's reserved for emotional/qualitative judgments, commonly appearing in reviews and critiques. Learners sometimes overuse it for plain factual statements where it doesn't fit naturally.
