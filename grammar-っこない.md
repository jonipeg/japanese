# ～っこない {#っこない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～っこない

**Furigana form:** ～っこない (no kanji)

**Meaning:** Casual, strong negation of possibility — "there's no way that X," "X will never happen."

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ます形 (verb, ます-stem) + っこない

**Typical context:** Very casual spoken Japanese.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**わけがない**](grammar-わけがない.md) — "there's no way," more neutral and usable in written Japanese too.
- **はずがない** — "shouldn't be the case," based on logical expectation rather than casual emphasis.
- **絶対に～ない** — "absolutely not," a more direct casual negation.

**Opposite grammar structures:** 絶対に～はずだ／に違いない ("certainly will be the case").

**Example sentences:**
1. こんな難しい問題、私に解けっこない。
   (There's no way I can solve such a difficult problem.)
2. 今から急いでも、間に合いっこない。
   (Even if we hurry now, there's no way we'll make it in time.)
3. そんな高い車、買えっこないよ。
   (There's no way I can afford such an expensive car.)
4. 彼が約束を守るなんて、信じられっこない。
   (There's no way I can believe he'll keep his promise.)
5. 一日でこんなにたくさんの仕事、終わりっこない。
   (There's no way I can finish this much work in one day.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** っこない is casual and pairs naturally with volitional/potential-type verbs; it doesn't combine well with all verb types, such as purely stative verbs. Learners sometimes overuse it in formal writing, where わけがない is the more appropriate register.
