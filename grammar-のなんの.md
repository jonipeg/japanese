# ～のなんの {#のなんの}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～の何の *(largely kana in practice)*

**Furigana form:** ～のなんの

**Meaning:** "X and all sorts of excuses/things like that" — lists a dismissive or exasperated series of complaints/excuses, implying there were many such remarks, often with a critical undertone.

**Conjugation:**
- 普通形 + のなんの + と + 言う／文句を言う etc.
- Example base: バスが遅れた → バスが遅れたのなんのと言い訳をした

**Typical context:** Spoken, casual register; conveys mild exasperation or mockery toward someone's string of excuses or complaints. Common in casual storytelling about someone else's behavior.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **とか～とか** — plain "things like X and Y"; a neutral listing construction, lacking のなんの's exasperated, dismissive flavor toward excuses/complaints specifically.
- **だの～だの** — "X and Y and that sort of thing"; very similar exasperated listing tone, often interchangeable with のなんの, both implying a tiresome litany.
- **何だかんだ（と）** — "this and that / one thing or another"; a related idiom about a vague accumulation of reasons/events, usually less specifically about excuses than のなんの.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a plain, single neutral reason (理由は一つだけだ, "there was only one reason") would contrast with のなんの's implication of a drawn-out string of excuses.

**Example sentences:**
1. 遅刻したアルバイトの学生はバスが遅れたのなんのと店長に言い訳をした。 (The part-time student who was late made excuses to the manager about the bus being delayed and all sorts of other things.)
2. 彼は疲れただの眠いだの、いや、忙しいのなんのと文句ばかり言う。 (He's always complaining about being tired, sleepy, busy, and whatnot.)
3. 値段が高いのなんのと文句をつける客もいる。 (There are also customers who complain about the price being too high and so on.)
4. 面倒だのなんだのと言わずに、さっさとやってしまいなさい。 (Stop saying it's a hassle and whatnot, and just get it done quickly.)
5. 子どもは眠いのなんのとぐずり続けた。 (The child kept whining about being sleepy and all sorts of other things.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** のなんの almost always carries a dismissive or mildly critical tone toward the content being quoted — it implies the speaker doesn't fully buy the excuses/complaints being listed, similar in spirit to English "...or whatever" said with an eye-roll. Learners should avoid using it to neutrally and sincerely list someone's genuine reasons; for that, plain とか or simple conjunctions are more appropriate.
