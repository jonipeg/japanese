# ～こそすれ {#こそすれ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～こそすれ

**Furigana form:** ～こそすれ *(no kanji; entirely kana)*

**Meaning:** "If anything, X (but never Y)" — a concessive-emphatic construction that emphasizes the stated (often positive/neutral) outcome while categorically denying an opposite, usually negative, outcome.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ます形 (verb stem/masu-stem form) + こそすれ
- 名詞 (noun) + こそすれ
- Almost always followed by a clause with a negative predicate that denies the opposite case (e.g., ～こそすれ、～ことはない／～ない).

**Typical context:** Formal, written/literary N1 grammar; often used in persuasive or argumentative writing to politely but firmly correct a listener's assumption (e.g., "If anything I feel gratitude, never annoyance"). Carries a slightly formal, somewhat rhetorical tone — not rude, but elevated above casual speech.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**はさておき**](grammar-はさておき.md) — means "setting X aside"; a topic-deferral construction, unrelated to the concessive-emphatic "if anything" meaning of こそすれ.
- [**そばから**](grammar-そばから.md) — means "no sooner X than Y," a temporal-immediacy construction; shares no semantic overlap with こそすれ beyond surface pattern similarity in being N1-level quiz distractors.
- [**こととて**](grammar-こととて.md) — means "since it's a matter of X" (reason/excuse); structurally and semantically distinct.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct single opposite, but conceptually ～どころか ("far from X, actually Y") inverts the direction — どころか denies the stated mild case in favor of a stronger one, while こそすれ affirms the mild case while denying the opposite. No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. あなたの行為には感謝こそすれ、誰も迷惑だなんて思っていませんよ。
   (If anything, we're grateful for what you did — nobody thinks it's a bother.)
2. 彼の努力は称賛こそすれ、非難される理由などどこにもない。
   (His effort deserves praise if anything; there's no reason whatsoever for it to be criticized.)
3. その提案は歓迎こそすれ、反対する者などいないだろう。
   (If anything, that proposal will be welcomed — surely no one will oppose it.)
4. この程度の遅れは許容こそすれ、問題視するほどのことではない。
   (A delay of this degree is, if anything, tolerable — not something to treat as a problem.)
5. 彼女の意見には同意こそすれ、疑いを持つ余地はない。
   (If anything, I agree with her opinion — there's no room for doubt.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Confused on graded tests with はさておき, そばから, and こととて — students may conflate the "こそ" emphatic particle shape with other similarly archaic-sounding literary patterns. The margin note in the source material notes こそすれ can be paraphrased plainly as けれども in casual understanding, but more precisely it means "X is true if anything, while the opposite (Y) is firmly denied" — the key test-taking cue is a following negative clause that explicitly rules out an opposite reaction.
