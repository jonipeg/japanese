# ～こととて {#こととて}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～こととて

**Furigana form:** ～こととて *(事とて — 事 is conventionally written in kana here)*

**Meaning:** "Since it is a matter of X" — offers a mitigating explanation or excuse for a situation, implying the listener should be understanding/forgiving because of the circumstances described.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞・い形容詞・な形容詞・名詞 の名詞修飾型 (noun-modifying/attributive form) + こととて
- E.g., 子供のしたこととて ("since it is something a child did"), 慣れぬこととて ("since it is something unfamiliar")

**Typical context:** Formal, literary, somewhat old-fashioned written register; frequently used in apologies or requests for understanding (e.g., formal letters asking for leniency). Carries a polite, mitigating, almost humble tone — not rude at all, but distinctly bookish and rarely heard in casual conversation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**かたわら**](grammar-かたわら.md) — means "while also doing X" (a side activity alongside a main one, e.g., 仕事のかたわら "alongside work"); completely different function, describing parallel activities rather than offering an excuse.
- **しまつで** — means "to the point that X (a bad result ensued)," often concluding a narrative of escalating problems (〜しまつだ); differs because しまつで describes an undesirable culminating outcome, not a mitigating reason/excuse for leniency.
- [**ごとく**](grammar-ごとく・ごとき.md) — means "like/as if" (simile marker); unrelated semantically.
- [**こそすれ**](grammar-こそすれ.md) — means "if anything, X (never Y)" (concessive emphasis); unrelated function.
- [**そばから**](grammar-そばから.md) — means "no sooner X than Y" (temporal immediacy); unrelated function.
- [**のなんの**](grammar-のなんの.md) — an intensifying exclamation ("incredibly X"); unrelated function.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though a phrase demanding accountability despite circumstances, such as ～にもかかわらず ("despite X"), would conceptually reject the leniency こととて requests. No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 子供のしたこととて、お許しください。
   (Please forgive it, since it was something a child did.)
2. 不慣れなこととて、ご迷惑をおかけしました。
   (Since I'm unfamiliar with this, I apologize for the trouble I caused.)
3. 急なこととて、十分な準備ができませんでした。
   (Since it was so sudden, I wasn't able to prepare adequately.)
4. 初めてのこととて、至らぬ点も多々あるかと存じます。
   (Since this is my first time, I imagine there are many shortcomings.)
5. 人手が足りぬこととて、対応が遅れてしまい申し訳ございません。
   (Since we're short-staffed, I apologize that our response was delayed.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Confused on graded tests with かたわら, しまつで, ごとく, こそすれ, そばから, and のなんの. The crucial distinguishing nuance: こととて is exclusively an excuse/mitigating-circumstance construction that asks for the listener's understanding or forgiveness — it should always be paired semantically with an apology, request for leniency, or acknowledgment of a shortcoming. Students should check whether the sentence's second clause is an apology or request for understanding, which rules out unrelated distractors like ごとく (simile) or しまつで (bad outcome, not leniency request).
