# ～ことに(は) {#ことには}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ことに（は）

**Furigana form:** ～ことに（は） (no special kanji reading)

**Meaning:** "To one's (surprise/regret/relief, etc.)" — placed at the beginning of a sentence before the main clause to express the speaker's emotional reaction to what follows.

**Conjugation:**
- い-adj / な-adj(な) / Verb (た form) + ことに（は）
- 驚いたことに (To my surprise)
- 残念なことに (Regrettably)
- 幸いなことに (Fortunately)

**Typical context:** Both written and spoken; common in narration, news reports, and storytelling to frame the emotional tone of the following statement.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**ことか**](grammar-ことか.md) — exclamatory degree expression ("how much...!"), different structurally and semantically.
- **には** (alone) — lacks the fixed emotional-adjective + ことに pattern.
- **なんと～ことだろう** — more literary exclamation of surprise/degree.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 驚いたことに、彼は試験に満点を取った。
   (To my surprise, he got a perfect score on the exam.)
2. 残念なことに、旅行は中止になってしまった。
   (Regrettably, the trip was cancelled.)
3. 幸いなことに、怪我人は一人もいなかった。
   (Fortunately, there were no injuries.)
4. 困ったことに、財布を家に忘れてきてしまった。
   (To my dismay, I forgot my wallet at home.)
5. うれしいことに、長年の夢がついに叶った。
   (Happily, my long-held dream finally came true.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The emotional adjective/verb must match the speaker's own reaction, and this phrase always comes at the start of the sentence, commenting on the clause that follows (not before it) — unlike ことか which attaches after the clause it emphasizes.
