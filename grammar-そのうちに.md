# そのうちに {#そのうちに}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** その内に

**Furigana form:** そのうちに

**Meaning:** "One of these days / eventually / before long" — a vague, non-committal way of referring to an unspecified future time, often used to make a casual promise or prediction without pinning down exactly when it will happen.

**Conjugation:**
- そのうちに + [future clause] (often paired with volitional forms, intentions, or predictions)
- Can also appear without に: そのうち連絡するよ
- Example: そのうちに遊びに行きますね (I'll come visit one of these days)

**Typical context:** Spoken, casual register. Carries a relaxed, non-committal, somewhat vague tone — often used to soften a promise or intention without obligating oneself to a specific date, and sometimes used self-deprecatingly or knowingly, acknowledging that the promised action may be postponed indefinitely (similar to the English "one of these days..." said with a wink).

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **いつか** — "someday," similarly vague about timing, but can refer to a more hopeful or aspirational distant future (e.g., a dream to be fulfilled someday), whereas そのうちに often implies a nearer, more casual "sometime soon" with less weight.
- **近いうちに** — "in the near future, soon," a more explicit commitment to a relatively short timeframe, more formal/polite and often used in business or semi-formal contexts (e.g., 近いうちにご連絡いたします), compared to そのうちに's looser, more casual vagueness.
- **やがて** — "before long, in time," more literary/narrative in tone, often used in writing to describe an inevitable future development, lacking そのうちに's conversational, non-committal flavor.
- **気が向いたら** — "whenever I feel like it," emphasizes the conditional, whim-based nature of doing something, often combined with or implied alongside そのうちに's vague timing.

**Opposite grammar structures:** 今すぐに ("right away, immediately") or ただちに ("promptly, at once") — both specify immediate action, the opposite of そのうちに's deliberately vague, deferred timing.

**Example sentences:**
1. また今度、そのうちにご飯でも食べに行きましょう。
   (Let's go grab a meal together sometime, one of these days.)
2. 片付けなきゃと思いつつ、そのうちにと先延ばしにしている。
   (I keep thinking I should clean up, but I just keep putting it off for "one of these days.")
3. そのうちに景気も良くなるでしょう。
   (The economy will probably improve before long.)
4. 今は忙しいから、そのうちに落ち着いたら連絡するよ。
   (I'm busy right now, so I'll get in touch once things settle down, one of these days.)
5. そのうちに雨もやむだろうから、少し待ってみよう。
   (The rain will probably stop before long, so let's wait a bit.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes use そのうちに when they actually intend to commit to a specific near-term plan, which can come across as evasive or insincere in Japanese, since native speakers understand そのうちに as deliberately non-committal. If a concrete plan or deadline is intended, 近いうちに or a specific date should be used instead. Also note that そのうちに implies the speaker (or situation) is not in control of exactly when the event will occur — it's used for things expected to happen in due course, not things the speaker can simply schedule at will.
