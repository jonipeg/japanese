# ～より {#より}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～より

**Furigana form:** ～より

**Meaning:** A formal/stiff written equivalent of から, marking the starting point of something — a sender, source, or starting time — commonly seen in letters, citations, and official announcements.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + より
- 3時より配布 — "distributed starting at 3 o'clock"; 山田ゆり子より — "from Yuriko Yamada" (letter signature)

**Typical context:** Formal written Japanese — letter sign-offs, citations/quotations from publications, and official notices or event announcements (e.g. start times).

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～から** — the everyday, neutral equivalent used in ordinary speech and writing for both source and starting time.
- **～以降** — "from ~ onward," a related but more explicitly temporal formal expression, often used for ongoing periods rather than a single starting point.

**Opposite grammar structures:** [**～まで**](grammar-まで(のことをして).md) ("until/up to ~") marks the endpoint of a range rather than the starting point, making it the natural counterpart when giving a from–to range (例: 3時より5時まで).

**Example sentences:**
1. 【手紙】8月18日　山田ゆり子より。
   (Letter: August 18th, from Yuriko Yamada.)
2. 雑誌『旅』9月号30ページより抜粋。
   (Excerpted from page 30 of the September issue of the magazine "Tabi.")
3. 入場整理券は3時より配布します。
   (Entry tickets will be distributed starting at 3 o'clock.)
4. このサービスは来月より有料となります。
   (This service will become a paid service starting next month.)
5. 開演は午後6時より。
   (The performance begins at 6:00 pm.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Don't confuse this formal "from" usage with the comparison function of より (e.g. 彼より背が高い, "taller than him") — it's the same word used for two distinct grammatical functions. The "from" sense covered here sounds formal and is standard in letters, citations, and notices, but reads as overly stiff if used in casual spoken conversation, where ～から is the natural choice.
