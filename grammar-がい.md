# ～がい(かい) {#がい}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～甲斐(がい)

**Furigana form:** ～がい

**Meaning:** "Worth doing X" — expresses that an action is rewarding or meaningful to do, as in やりがい (worth doing), 生きがい (reason/worth for living), or 〜甲斐がある/ない (there is/isn't value in doing X).

**Conjugation:**
- Verb-ます形 (stem) + がい + がある／がない (生きがいがある, 努力したかいがあって)
- [Noun-の] + かい (combined in compounds like 働きがい "worth working for")

**Typical context:** Used in both spoken and written Japanese; register-neutral. Carries a positive, rewarding connotation when paired with ある, and a disappointed/futile connotation when paired with ない.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **すべ** — "a means/way to do something" (e.g., なすすべがない "there's no way to do anything"); a confirmed real-test confusion pair with がい/かい, but すべ is about the existence of a method, not about whether something is "worth doing."
- **詮（せん）** — "use/point" (言っても詮ない "no use in saying it"), a related futility nuance but a different word and more archaic/literary.
- **やりがい** — the most common compound built from this pattern itself, specifically meaning "worth doing (a task)."

**Opposite grammar structures:** 無駄（むだ）"futile, wasted effort" functions as a natural semantic opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 努力した甲斐があって、見事合格した。
   (Thanks to the effort I put in, I passed brilliantly — the effort was worth it.)
2. この仕事はやりがいがあって、毎日楽しい。
   (This job is rewarding to do, and I enjoy it every day.)
3. 彼女には生きがいと呼べるものがなく、日々をぼんやりと過ごしていた。
   (She had nothing she could call a reason for living, and spent her days in a daze.)
4. 長時間並んだかいがあって、人気のラーメン店に入ることができた。
   (Waiting in line for so long paid off — we got into the popular ramen shop.)
5. 一生懸命勉強したかいもなく、試験に落ちてしまった。
   (Despite all my hard studying, it was for nothing — I failed the exam.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** A confirmed wrong-answer note states a student picked すべ instead of がい(かい) on a real graded test — the distinguishing test is whether the sentence is about whether an effort was "worth it" / "rewarding" (がい) versus whether there exists "a method/way" to do something (すべ, almost always in negative constructions like なすすべがない). These are unrelated concepts despite surface similarity in how they appear in negative sentences (「〜がない」 in both cases).
