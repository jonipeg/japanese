## 自治 (じち)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** self-government, autonomy

**Similar words:**
- 独立（どくりつ）— "independence," broader and more absolute (often national sovereignty); 自治 specifically refers to self-governance within a larger system (local, regional autonomy).
- [自立](#自立-じりつ)（じりつ）— "self-reliance/standing on one's own," more personal/individual, distinct from 自治's political/administrative scope.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. この地域は自治権を持っている。— This region has autonomy/self-governing rights.
2. 大学の自治は重要な原則だ。— University autonomy is an important principle.
3. 地方自治体が独自の政策を進めている。— Local autonomous bodies are advancing their own policies.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 自治 commonly appears in the compound 地方自治体 ("local government/municipality") — learners should recognize this as standard administrative vocabulary in Japanese civics, not just an abstract philosophical concept.

## 実 (じつ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** truth, reality, substance (also: fruit, in different contexts)

**Similar words:**
- [真実](#真実-しんじつ)（しんじつ）— "truth," more emphatic and complete, often contrasted with lies; 実 alone is more subtle, often used in set phrases like 実は ("actually/the truth is").
- 本当（ほんとう）— "true/real," the common everyday word; 実 is more literary/formal, frequently appearing as a prefix in compounds (実力, 実際, 実行) rather than standing alone in casual speech.

**Opposite words:**
- 虚（きょ）— falsehood, emptiness

**Example sentences:**
1. 実を言うと、私はその話を信じていない。— To tell the truth, I don't believe that story.
2. 彼の実の兄は医者だ。— His actual (biological) older brother is a doctor.
3. 実のところ、計画は失敗に終わった。— In reality, the plan ended in failure.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 実 most frequently appears in the fixed expression 実は ("actually, the truth is...") used to introduce a surprising or confidential fact — standalone usage of 実 as a bare noun ("truth/reality") is less common in casual speech than its role as a word-building prefix (実際, 実用, 実力, etc.).

## 実感 (じっかん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a realistic feeling, to really feel/sense something as real

**Similar words:**
- 感じる（かんじる）— the plain verb "to feel," general and broad; 実感する specifically means to feel something concretely as real/true (not just emotionally, but as a recognized reality).
- 体感（たいかん）— "physical/bodily sensation," more about directly experiencing something through the senses/body, a narrower physical subset of 実感.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 合格の実感がまだわかない。— I still don't really feel like I've actually passed.
2. 彼の死を実感できずにいる。— I still can't really process/feel the reality of his death.
3. 自分の成長を実感する瞬間があった。— There was a moment when I truly felt my own growth.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 実感 often appears with わく/わかない ("to well up" / "not to well up") describing whether a feeling of reality has set in (実感がわかない "it doesn't feel real yet") — this collocation is key and different from simply saying 感じない.

## 実技 (じつぎ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** practical skill, practical techniques, hands-on practice

**Similar words:**
- [実践](#実践-じっせん)（じっせん）— "putting into practice," broader and more conceptual (applying theory to action); 実技 specifically refers to a hands-on skill or test component (like a practical exam).
- [技能](vocab-14.md#技能-ぎのう)（ぎのう）— "skill/technique," the ability itself, while 実技 often refers to the practical exam/session demonstrating that skill.

**Opposite words:**
- 学科（がっか）— academic/written subject (as in exams), the theoretical counterpart

**Example sentences:**
1. 運転免許の実技試験に合格した。— I passed the practical driving license exam.
2. 実技指導を受けてからコースに出た。— I went onto the course after receiving practical instruction.
3. この授業は実技が中心だ。— This class is centered on practical skills.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 実技 is most commonly paired with 試験/テスト to mean "practical exam" (as opposed to 学科試験 "written/theory exam") — a key vocabulary pair for anyone discussing licensing exams (driving, professional certifications) in Japan.

## 実況 (じっきょう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** actual situation/conditions; live broadcast/commentary

**Similar words:**
- [中継](vocab-27.md#中継-ちゅうけい)（ちゅうけい）— "live relay/broadcast," focuses on the transmission aspect of live coverage; 実況 specifically refers to the live commentary/narration describing events as they happen.
- [現状](vocab-18.md#現状-げんじょう)（げんじょう）— "current state of affairs," more neutral/general description of a present situation, without the live-broadcast connotation.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 野球の実況中継を聞いていた。— I was listening to the live baseball commentary broadcast.
2. 彼はゲーム実況者として有名だ。— He is famous as a game commentary streamer.
3. 現場の実況を伝えるレポーターがいた。— There was a reporter conveying the live situation on the scene.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** In modern usage, 実況 (particularly ゲーム実況 "game commentary/let's-play streaming") has become a major internet/streaming culture term — learners should know this contemporary usage alongside its traditional "actual situation" meaning.

## 実行 (じっこう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** practice, execution, carrying out (a plan)

**Similar words:**
- [実施](#実施-じっし)（じっし）— "enforcement/implementation," often used for official policies, programs, or exams being put into effect; 実行 is broader, used for carrying out any plan or action, including personal ones.
- 遂行（すいこう）— "accomplishment/carrying out (a duty/mission)," more formal and emphasizes completing something through to the end, often in professional/military contexts.
- やる — the plain, casual verb "to do," everyday equivalent lacking 実行's formal, deliberate-action nuance.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 計画を実行に移す時が来た。— The time has come to put the plan into action.
2. 彼はその提案を実行した。— He carried out that proposal.
3. 実行力のある人材が求められている。— Personnel with the ability to execute (get things done) are in demand.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 実行 emphasizes the act of carrying out a decided plan/intention (計画を実行する), distinct from 実施 which usually applies to official/institutional measures (法律を実施する) — personal resolutions and individual plans pair naturally with 実行, not 実施.

## 実施 (じっし)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** enforcement, implementation, putting into practice (official measures)

**Similar words:**
- [実行](#実行-じっこう)（じっこう）— broader "carrying out" of any plan, while 実施 specifically implies official/institutional implementation (laws, policies, exams, surveys).
- 施行（しこう）— "enforcement (of a law)," a legal-specific term, narrower than 実施's broader institutional application scope.

**Opposite words:**
- 廃止（はいし）— abolition

**Example sentences:**
1. 新しい制度が来月から実施される。— The new system will be implemented starting next month.
2. アンケート調査を実施した。— We carried out a questionnaire survey.
3. このイベントは毎年実施されている。— This event is held every year.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 実施 is the standard word for official/organizational actions being "put into effect" (laws, exams, surveys, programs) — it's rarely used for small personal actions, where 実行 or simply やる would be more natural.

## 実質 (じっしつ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** substance, essence, real/effective nature (as opposed to nominal)

**Similar words:**
- 本質（ほんしつ）— "essence/true nature," more philosophical, about the fundamental core nature of something; 実質 is more practical, often contrasted with 名目 (nominal/on paper).
- 内容（ないよう）— "content," refers to what's actually inside/included, a more neutral term than 実質's "real vs. apparent" nuance.

**Opposite words:**
- 名目（めいもく）— nominal, in name only

**Example sentences:**
1. 実質的な権力を握っているのは彼だ。— He is the one who actually holds real power.
2. 実質賃金が上がらない状況が続いている。— The situation where real wages don't rise continues.
3. 名目上は部長だが、実質はただの管理職だ。— Nominally he's a department head, but in substance he's just a regular manager.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 実質 is frequently paired with its antonym 名目 (nominal) in economic contexts (実質賃金 vs 名目賃金 "real wages vs nominal wages") — a key economics vocabulary pair tested at N1/N2 level.

## 実践 (じっせん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** (actual) practice, putting into practice, praxis

**Similar words:**
- [実行](#実行-じっこう)（じっこう）— "carrying out" a specific plan/action; 実践 is broader and more philosophical, often meaning "practicing" a principle, theory, or teaching in one's life/work over time.
- 理論（りろん）— "theory," the direct conceptual counterpart often contrasted with 実践 (理論と実践 "theory and practice").

**Opposite words:**
- 理論（りろん）— theory

**Example sentences:**
1. 学んだ知識を実践に移すことが大切だ。— It's important to put learned knowledge into practice.
2. 彼は理論より実践を重視する。— He values practice more than theory.
3. 環境保護を実践している家庭が増えている。— More households are putting environmental protection into practice.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 実践 is commonly paired with 理論 as a classic dichotomy (理論と実践 "theory and practice") in academic and self-help writing — it carries a more ongoing/lifestyle nuance (practicing a philosophy or habit) compared to the more task-specific 実行.

## 実情/実状 (じつじょう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** actual condition, real state of affairs

**Similar words:**
- [現状](vocab-18.md#現状-げんじょう)（げんじょう）— "present/current situation," more neutral and simply describes the state of things now; 実情 often implies the "true" state behind appearances, sometimes hidden or not obvious from outside.
- [実態](#実態-じったい)（じったい）— "actual state/reality," very close synonym, both imply uncovering the truth behind a situation (often used in investigative/survey contexts).

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 現場の実情を調査した。— We investigated the actual conditions on the ground.
2. 実情を知らない人が批判している。— People who don't know the real situation are criticizing it.
3. 経済の実情は統計データだけではわからない。— The real state of the economy can't be understood from statistics alone.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 実情 (and its less common variant 実状) implies the true, often less visible reality behind a surface appearance — frequently used when contrasting official statistics or public perception with what's "really" happening, close in nuance to 実態.

## 実績 (じっせき)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** actual results, track record, achievements

**Similar words:**
- 成果（せいか）— "results/outcome," can refer to a single accomplishment; 実績 emphasizes an accumulated track record over time (often used in résumés, business history).
- 業績（ぎょうせき）— "business performance/achievements," specifically used for company/organizational financial or work performance, a narrower business-context version of 実績.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. この会社には豊富な実績がある。— This company has an abundant track record.
2. 営業成績の実績を評価された。— His sales performance track record was evaluated.
3. 実績のあるエンジニアを採用したい。— We want to hire an engineer with a proven track record.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 実績 implies a demonstrated, verifiable history of accomplishment (often used when evaluating job candidates, companies, or products — 実績のある〜 "a 〜 with a proven track record") — distinct from 成果 which can refer to the result of a single, recent effort.

## 実費 (じっぴ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** actual expenses, cost price (without markup)

**Similar words:**
- 費用（ひよう）— "expenses/cost" in general; 実費 specifically emphasizes the actual, exact amount spent (often reimbursed as-is, without profit added).
- 原価（げんか）— "cost price," used specifically in manufacturing/retail contexts for the base production cost before markup, closely related to 実費's "no markup" nuance.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 交通費は実費で支給されます。— Transportation costs will be reimbursed at actual cost.
2. 実費のみで材料を提供した。— I provided the materials at cost price only.
3. 実費精算のため領収書が必要だ。— A receipt is necessary for actual-cost reimbursement.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 実費 commonly appears in reimbursement contexts (実費精算 "expense reimbursement at actual cost," 実費支給 "paid at actual cost") — important vocabulary for business/work-related Japanese, specifically meaning no profit margin is added to the reimbursed amount.

## 実物 (じつぶつ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** the real thing, actual object (as opposed to a picture/model)

**Similar words:**
- 本物（ほんもの）— "the genuine article/real deal," emphasizes authenticity (not fake/counterfeit); 実物 emphasizes the physical actual item itself (as opposed to a photo, sample, or representation).
- サンプル — "sample," a representative example, the opposite reference point to 実物 when comparing to a display/representation.

**Opposite words:**
- [模型](vocab-19.md#模型-もけい)（もけい）— model, replica

**Example sentences:**
1. 写真よりも実物の方がずっときれいだ。— The real thing is far more beautiful than the photo.
2. 実物を見てから購入を決めたい。— I want to decide on the purchase after seeing the actual item.
3. 彼女は実物よりテレビの方が痩せて見える。— She looks thinner on TV than in person (compared to the real thing).

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 実物 is specifically used to contrast something physically present/real against a representation (photo, video, model, description) — commonly heard in phrases like 実物を見る ("to see it in person") or 実物大 ("actual size/life-size").

## 失望 (しつぼう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** disappointment, despair

**Similar words:**
- がっかり — the common everyday/casual word for "disappointed," more conversational; 失望する is more formal/written, often with stronger emotional weight.
- 落胆（らくたん）— "dejection/discouragement," similarly formal and often interchangeable with 失望, emphasizing the feeling of having one's spirit dampened.
- [絶望](vocab-04.md#絶望-ぜつぼう)（ぜつぼう）— "despair/hopelessness," a much stronger word implying total loss of hope, whereas 失望 is disappointment that doesn't necessarily eliminate all hope.

**Opposite words:**
- 満足（まんぞく）— satisfaction

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼の態度に失望した。— I was disappointed by his attitude.
2. 期待が大きかっただけに失望も大きかった。— Because expectations were high, the disappointment was also great.
3. 結果を聞いて失望の色を隠せなかった。— Upon hearing the result, he couldn't hide his disappointment.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 失望 is stronger and more formal than casual がっかり — don't confuse it with 絶望 (despair/hopelessness), which implies a much deeper, often irreversible loss of hope rather than simple disappointment.

## 質問 (しつもん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** question, to ask a question

**Similar words:**
- [疑問](vocab-14.md#疑問-ぎもん)（ぎもん）— "doubt/question (in one's mind)," refers to an internal uncertainty one has, not necessarily voiced aloud; 質問 specifically means the act of asking someone a question.
- 尋ねる（たずねる）— the plain verb "to ask/inquire," more casual/direct than 質問する which sounds slightly more formal (often used in classroom/interview settings).

**Opposite words:**
- 回答（かいとう）— answer, response

**Example sentences:**
1. 先生に質問した。— I asked the teacher a question.
2. 何か質問はありますか。— Do you have any questions?
3. 彼女は的確な質問をする。— She asks precise questions.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 質問する takes を (質問をする/質問する) and is the standard verb for "asking a question" in formal settings (classes, interviews, press conferences) — distinct from 疑問に思う which describes merely wondering/doubting something internally without necessarily asking aloud.

## 実用 (じつよう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** practical use, utility

**Similar words:**
- 実用的（じつようてき）— the common adjectival form, "practical," describing something useful in real application; often used interchangeably with the base noun 実用.
- 便利（べんり）— "convenient," broader and more about ease of use, while 実用 specifically emphasizes genuine practical/functional utility (as opposed to decorative or theoretical).
- [応用](vocab-09.md#応用-おうよう)（おうよう）— "application," refers to applying a concept/technology to a specific use, related but distinct from 実用's focus on everyday practicality.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. このアイデアは実用化が難しい。— This idea is difficult to put into practical use.
2. 実用性の高い製品が求められている。— Highly practical products are in demand.
3. 実用英語を学ぶために教室に通っている。— I'm attending classes to learn practical English.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 実用 often appears in compounds like 実用化 ("putting into practical/commercial use") and 実用性 ("practicality") and 実用英語 ("practical/applied English," as opposed to academic/test-focused English) — common in tech and business vocabulary.

## 実例 (じつれい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** example, instance, concrete illustration

**Similar words:**
- 例（れい）— the plain, general word for "example"; 実例 specifically emphasizes a real, concrete case (as opposed to a hypothetical one).
- 事例（じれい）— "case example," commonly used in business/academic contexts for documented instances or case studies, closely overlapping with 実例.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 実例を挙げて説明してください。— Please explain by giving concrete examples.
2. この理論を裏付ける実例は少ない。— There are few concrete examples that support this theory.
3. 実例に基づいた研究が行われた。— Research based on actual cases was conducted.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 実例 emphasizes that the example is drawn from real life/actual occurrence (not invented or hypothetical) — in academic writing, pairing a claim with 実例 strengthens credibility more than a generic 例, which could be illustrative/made-up.

## 失礼 (しつれい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** impoliteness, rudeness; "excuse me" (as a set phrase)

**Similar words:**
- 無礼（ぶれい）— "rudeness/discourtesy," a stronger and more severe word than 失礼, which is often used lightly as a polite social cushion.
- すみません — "excuse me/sorry," the more common everyday apology phrase, while 失礼します specifically marks entering/leaving a room, interrupting, or ending a phone call.

**Opposite words:**
- [礼儀正しい](vocab-33.md#礼儀正しい-れいぎただしい)（れいぎただしい）— polite, courteous

**Example sentences:**
1. 失礼ですが、お名前を伺ってもよろしいですか。— Excuse me, but may I ask your name?
2. 彼の態度は非常に失礼だった。— His attitude was extremely rude.
3. それでは失礼します。— Well then, excuse me (I'll be taking my leave).

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 失礼します/失礼しました function as fixed polite phrases for entering/leaving rooms, ending phone calls, or interrupting someone — this grammaticalized social-ritual usage is distinct from its literal adjectival meaning of "(being) rude," and learners must master both registers.

## 失恋 (しつれん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** heartbreak, unrequited/broken love

**Similar words:**
- 片思い（かたおもい）— "unrequited love/one-sided crush," describes the state of loving someone who doesn't reciprocate (before any breakup); 失恋 specifically refers to the painful experience of a romance ending or being rejected.
- [別れる](vocab-36.md#別れる-わかれる)（わかれる）— the plain verb "to break up/part ways," more neutral — doesn't necessarily carry the emotional devastation that 失恋 implies.

**Opposite words:**
- 成就（じょうじゅ）— fulfillment (of love), as in 恋が成就する "love is fulfilled/realized"

**Example sentences:**
1. 失恋して何週間も落ち込んでいた。— After a heartbreak, I was depressed for weeks.
2. 失恋の痛みを乗り越えた。— I overcame the pain of heartbreak.
3. 彼女は失恋をきっかけに留学を決意した。— Triggered by her heartbreak, she decided to study abroad.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 失恋 specifically implies the emotional loss/end of a romantic relationship (through rejection or breakup), distinct from 片思い which describes unreciprocated feelings before any relationship or rejection has occurred.

## 指摘する (してきする)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to point out (a problem, mistake, or fact)

**Similar words:**
- 言う（いう）— the plain, neutral verb "to say," much more general; 指摘する specifically means identifying and calling attention to a problem or overlooked point.
- 批判する（ひはんする）— "to criticize," implies a more negative, evaluative judgment, whereas 指摘する is more neutral — simply drawing attention to something (which could be positive, negative, or neutral).

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 上司にミスを指摘された。— My mistake was pointed out by my boss.
2. 専門家は問題点を指摘した。— The expert pointed out the problem areas.
3. 彼の指摘は的確だった。— His observation (point raised) was accurate.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 指摘する is more neutral/objective than 批判する — it simply means drawing attention to a fact or issue (which could even be constructive/helpful), whereas 批判する implies a critical, evaluative judgment; learners should avoid treating them as interchangeable.

## 私鉄 (してつ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** private railway (non-government-operated train line)

**Similar words:**
- JR（ジェイアール）— "Japan Railways," the privatized former national railway company, technically also private but usually distinguished from 私鉄 in common speech, which refers to the other independent regional/local railway companies.
- 国鉄（こくてつ）— "national railway," the historical pre-1987 government-run railway system, the contrasted predecessor concept to 私鉄.

**Opposite words:**
- 国鉄（こくてつ）— national railway (historical)

**Example sentences:**
1. この地域には私鉄が3路線通っている。— Three private railway lines run through this area.
2. 私鉄の運賃はJRより高いこともある。— Private railway fares can sometimes be higher than JR's.
3. 私鉄沿線に引っ越すことにした。— I decided to move along a private railway line.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** In everyday Japanese usage, 私鉄 specifically contrasts with JR (even though JR is technically also privatized) — commuters and real estate listings (私鉄沿線 "along private rail lines") use this distinction regularly, which can confuse learners who assume "private" should include JR too.

## 失業 (しつぎょう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** unemployment

**Similar words:**
- 無職（むしょく）— "jobless/no occupation," describes a person's current status of not having a job; 失業 specifically implies having lost a job (often involuntarily), with stronger economic/social policy connotations.
- 解雇（かいこ）— "dismissal/being fired," the specific act that often leads to 失業, a narrower cause-focused term.

**Opposite words:**
- 就職（しゅうしょく）— getting a job, employment

**Example sentences:**
1. 失業率が上昇している。— The unemployment rate is rising.
2. 彼はリストラで失業した。— He became unemployed due to restructuring/layoffs.
3. 失業保険を受給している。— He is receiving unemployment insurance.
4. 

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 失業 specifically implies the state of having lost employment (often analyzed statistically as 失業率 "unemployment rate"), distinct from 無職 which is a more neutral/broader descriptor of simply not currently having a job (which could include students, homemakers, retirees).

3. このプロジェクトを推進するチームが結成された。— A team was formed to advance this project.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Used both literally (physical propulsion, 推進力) and figuratively (advancing a policy or project) — don't confuse with 推奨 (すいしょう, "recommend"), a similar-sounding word with a very different meaning.

## 推奨 (すいしょう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** recommendation, to recommend

**Similar words:**
- お勧め（おすすめ）— the more casual, everyday word for "recommendation" (used in shops, menus, apps); 推奨 is more formal, often used in technical/official contexts (推奨環境, 推奨設定).
- 推薦（すいせん）— "recommendation" of a person for a position/role, more personal than 推奨 which is typically for products or settings.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. このソフトは最新バージョンへの更新を推奨しています。— This software recommends updating to the latest version.
2. 医師は十分な睡眠を推奨している。— The doctor recommends getting enough sleep.
3. 推奨されている設定に変更した。— I changed it to the recommended settings.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Formal/technical register, common in manuals and instructions (推奨環境, 動作推奨ブラウザ) — distinct from 推薦 (すいせん), which specifically recommends a person (e.g., for a job or school admission).

## 水平 (すいへい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** level, horizontal

**Similar words:**
- 平ら（たいら）— "flat," describes a flat surface generally; 水平 specifically emphasizes being perfectly level/horizontal relative to the ground, often in technical or measurement contexts.
- フラット — loanword "flat," casual equivalent often used for design/surfaces.

**Opposite words:**
- 垂直（すいちょく）— vertical

**Example sentences:**
1. 棚を水平に取り付けるのは意外と難しい。— Installing a shelf horizontally is surprisingly difficult.
2. 水平線に夕日が沈んでいく。— The sunset sinks below the horizon.
3. このテーブルは水平が取れていない。— This table isn't level.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Appears in the common compound 水平線 (すいへいせん, "horizon," specifically over water) as distinguished from 地平線 (ちへいせん, "horizon" over land) — learners sometimes use them interchangeably, but Japanese distinguishes sea horizon from land horizon.

## 睡眠 (すいみん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** sleep, slumber

**Similar words:**
- 眠り（ねむり）— the native Japanese word for "sleep," more poetic/casual; 睡眠 is the formal, often medical/scientific term (睡眠不足, 睡眠時間).
- 休息（きゅうそく）— "rest," broader than sleep specifically, can include any kind of relaxation or rest period.

**Opposite words:**
- 覚醒（かくせい）— wakefulness, awakening

**Example sentences:**
1. 十分な睡眠を取ることが健康には欠かせない。— Getting enough sleep is essential for health.
2. 睡眠不足が続くと集中力が落ちる。— Continued lack of sleep reduces concentration.
3. 彼は毎晩8時間の睡眠を心がけている。— He makes an effort to get eight hours of sleep every night.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 睡眠 is the formal/clinical term used in compounds like 睡眠不足 (sleep deprivation) and 睡眠薬 (sleeping pill) — in casual conversation, 寝る (ねる, "to sleep") is the natural verb choice rather than 睡眠する, which sounds stilted.
