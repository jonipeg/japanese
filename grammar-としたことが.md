# ～としたことが {#としたことが}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～としたことが

**Furigana form:** ～としたことが *(no difficult kanji; 事 is typically written in kana as こと here)*

**Meaning:** "X, of all people/things" — expresses surprise and mild self-reproach/criticism at an uncharacteristic mistake made by someone (often oneself) who is normally competent or careful.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 (noun, typically a person or role) + としたことが
- The noun is usually the subject who made the mistake: 「プロの君としたことが」「私としたことが」

**Typical context:** Formal-to-neutral spoken and written register; often used self-deprecatingly ("I, of all people...") or to gently needle someone competent for a slip-up. Carries a wry, somewhat ironic tone rather than harsh criticism — common in both business apologies and casual-but-polished conversation among adults.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**におかれましては**](grammar-におかれましては.md) — an ultra-formal honorific topic marker used for addressing a superior in letters/speeches; entirely different register and function (not about mistakes).
- **ときたひには／ときた日には** — means "when it comes to X" often with a negative/critical follow-up about a recurring bad trait; distinct from としたことが's "uncharacteristic single mistake" nuance — ときた日には criticizes a persistent characteristic, while としたことが highlights a one-off lapse by someone usually reliable.
- [**こととて**](grammar-こととて.md) — means "since it's a matter of X" (an excuse for a situation, often someone else's); lacks the "of all people" surprise/self-reproach nuance.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though a phrase praising someone's reliability (〜さすが, "as expected of X") sits conceptually opposite — さすが affirms that something is expected/unsurprising given X's reputation, while としたことが expresses that something is surprising precisely because it contradicts X's reputation.

**Example sentences:**
1. こんな初歩的なミスをするなんて、プロの君としたことが一体どうしたんだい？
   (Making such a basic mistake — what on earth happened to you, of all people, a professional?)
2. 鍵をかけ忘れるなんて、慎重な私としたことが情けない。
   (Forgetting to lock the door — how pathetic of me, of all people, someone usually so careful.)
3. 約束の時間を間違えるとは、几帳面な田中さんとしたことが珍しい。
   (Getting the appointment time wrong — how unusual for Tanaka-san, of all people, someone so meticulous.)
4. ベテラン選手としたことが、あんな単純なミスをするとは信じられない。
   (I can't believe a veteran player, of all people, would make such a simple mistake.)
5. 校長先生としたことが、生徒の名前を間違えてしまうとは。
   (To think the principal, of all people, would get a student's name wrong.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** On a graded test (Q4), a student mistakenly chose ときた日 as the distractor instead of としたことが (or vice versa) — the source data explicitly notes this as confirmed wrong-answer data. The key distinguishing nuance: としたことが is about a single surprising lapse by someone normally competent (self-reproach/surprise), while ときた日には criticizes an ongoing, repeated negative trait or tendency. Also confused with におかれましては and こととて, which are both formal set phrases but serve entirely different functions (honorific address vs. excuse-giving).
