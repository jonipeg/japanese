# ～おぼえはない {#おぼえはない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～覚えはない

**Furigana form:** ～おぼえはない

**Meaning:** "I don't recall doing X / I have no reason to be X" — denies having done something or having a reputation, often defensively, asserting no memory or basis for an accusation.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形／た形 + 覚えはない
- 名詞＋と／な形容詞＋な + 言われる覚えはない (common accusation-denying pattern)
- Example base: 言う → 言った覚えはない

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal, usable in both speech and writing; often has a defensive, slightly indignant tone when denying an accusation or unwarranted criticism.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **たことがない** — plain "have never done X"; a neutral experiential negative, whereas 覚えはない specifically emphasizes the speaker's memory/consciousness of the act, often used defensively.
- **心当たりがない** — "I have no idea / nothing comes to mind"; close in the sense of denying awareness, but 心当たりがない is broader (denying any clue about a situation), while 覚えはない specifically denies having personally done or said something.
- **はずがない** — "there's no way X"; expresses logical impossibility generally, rather than the specific "I don't recall this" denial of 覚えはない.

**Opposite grammar structures:** 覚えがある ("I do recall / I have a memory of X") expresses the reverse — affirming a memory exists.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼が怒るようなことを言った覚えはありません。 (I don't recall saying anything that would make him angry.)
2. そんな約束をした覚えはない。 (I don't recall making any such promise.)
3. 人に非難される覚えはないと彼は反論した。 (He countered that he had no reason to be criticized by others.)
4. その書類にサインした覚えはありません。 (I don't recall signing that document.)
5. あなたにそこまで言われる覚えはない。 (I have no reason to be told that by you, of all people.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 覚えはない is frequently used defensively in response to an accusation ("I don't recall doing that" / "I have no reason to be blamed"), carrying an implicit pushback against someone else's claim — it's rarely a neutral statement about simple forgetfulness. Learners should distinguish it from 覚えていない ("I don't remember," neutral memory lapse) — 覚えはない more strongly implies "and therefore your accusation is baseless," not just "my memory is fuzzy."
