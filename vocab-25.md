## 損得 (そんとく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** loss and gain, profit and loss, advantages and disadvantages

**Similar words:**
- [利害](vocab-33.md#利害-りがい)（りがい）— "interests/stakes" (gain and harm), a more formal/abstract synonym often used in discussions of conflicting interests.
- 得失（とくしつ）— "gains and losses," a more literary/formal equivalent of 損得.
- メリット・デメリット — the loanword equivalent "merits and demerits," commonly used in casual business discussions.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 損得を考えずに人助けをした。— I helped the person without thinking about gain or loss.
2. 彼は何事も損得勘定で判断する。— He judges everything based on cost-benefit calculation.
3. この取引の損得をよく検討しよう。— Let's carefully examine the pros and cons of this deal.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 損得勘定（そんとくかんじょう）"calculating gain and loss" is a common fixed phrase, often used critically to describe someone who acts purely out of self-interest.

## 隊 (たい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a party, a corps, a unit/team

**Similar words:**
- チーム — the loanword "team," more casual and used broadly in sports/business; 隊 is more formal, often military/organized expedition contexts.
- 班（はん）— "squad/group," a smaller subdivision, often within a larger 隊 or organization.
- 軍（ぐん）— "army/military force," a much larger-scale term than 隊, which can refer to small units.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 登山隊は山頂を目指して出発した。— The climbing party set off aiming for the summit.
2. 救助隊が現場に到着した。— The rescue team arrived at the scene.
3. 彼は自衛隊に所属している。— He belongs to the Self-Defense Forces.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 隊 almost always appears as a suffix attached to a noun (登山隊, 救助隊, 自衛隊) rather than standing alone — learn it as a compound-forming word for organized groups/units.

## 体育 (たいいく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** physical education, P.E.

**Similar words:**
- スポーツ — "sports" in general, broader than the school-subject-specific 体育.
- 運動（うんどう）— "exercise/physical activity" in general, not limited to the school subject.
- [体操](#体操-たいそう)（たいそう）— "gymnastics/calisthenics," a specific type of physical activity often done within 体育 class.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 今日の体育の授業はマラソンだった。— Today's P.E. class was a marathon.
2. 体育館でバスケットボールをした。— We played basketball in the gym.
3. 彼は体育の成績がいつもいい。— He always gets good grades in physical education.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 体育 specifically refers to the school subject/curriculum, while 運動 and スポーツ describe physical activity more generally outside an academic context — 体育館 (gymnasium) is a common related compound.

## 対応 (たいおう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** correspondence; to respond to, deal with, handle

**Similar words:**
- [対処](#対処-たいしょ)（たいしょ）— "to cope with/deal with" a problem; 対応 is broader, covering both "handling a situation" and "correspondence/matching."
- [対策](#対策-たいさく)（たいさく）— "a countermeasure," a concrete plan/policy response, narrower than the general act of 対応.
- [応対](vocab-09.md#応対-おうたい)（おうたい）— "to attend to/receive" (customers, visitors), a narrower subset focused on interpersonal reception.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. クレームへの対応が丁寧だった。— The response to the complaint was courteous.
2. この部品は旧モデルに対応していない。— This part is not compatible with the old model.
3. 緊急事態に迅速に対応する必要がある。— We need to respond swiftly to the emergency.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 対応する has two senses — "to respond/deal with (a situation/customer)" and "to correspond/be compatible with (a system/model)" — learners often only learn one and miss the other, especially the technical compatibility sense.

## 体温 (たいおん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** one's body temperature

**Similar words:**
- 気温（きおん）— "air temperature," an environmental measure, distinct from one's own body temperature.
- 熱（ねつ）— "a fever," specifically elevated body temperature due to illness, not the neutral measurement 体温 represents.
- 平熱（へいねつ）— "normal body temperature," the baseline reference compared against 体温 readings.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 毎朝体温を測るようにしている。— I make it a habit to measure my body temperature every morning.
2. 彼女の体温は平熱より少し高かった。— Her body temperature was a bit higher than normal.
3. 体温計が壊れてしまった。— The thermometer broke.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 体温 is the neutral measurement term; 熱がある ("to have a fever") specifically means an elevated/abnormal reading — don't conflate 体温 itself with "fever."

## 大家 (たいか)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** an authority, a master, a distinguished expert

**Similar words:**
- [権威](vocab-18.md#権威-けんい)（けんい）— "an authority (figure)," close synonym, emphasizing recognized expertise/influence.
- 巨匠（きょしょう）— "a great master" (especially in art), narrower and more elevated in tone than 大家.
- 専門家（せんもんか）— "a specialist/expert," more neutral and general, without the connotation of eminence that 大家 carries.

**Opposite words:**
- 初心者（しょしんしゃ）— a beginner

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は日本画の大家として知られている。— He is known as a great master of Japanese painting.
2. その分野の大家に意見を求めた。— We sought the opinion of an authority in that field.
3. 大家の作品が美術館に展示されている。— The works of the master are on display at the museum.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 大家 (たいか, "master/authority") is a homograph with 大家 (おおや, "landlord") — same kanji, completely different reading and meaning; context and furigana are essential to distinguish them.

## 大会 (たいかい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a mass meeting, a general assembly, a tournament/competition

**Similar words:**
- [大会](#大会-たいかい)（たいかい）often used for sports/competitions — compare with 試合（しあい）"a match/game," which refers to a single contest within a larger 大会.
- [集会](vocab-20.md#集会-しゅうかい)（しゅうかい）— "a gathering/rally," broader and more general-purpose than the often competitive/large-scale 大会.
- [総会](vocab-23.md#総会-そうかい)（そうかい）— "a general meeting" (of shareholders/members), more formal and administrative than 大会.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 全国大会に出場することが決まった。— It was decided that we would compete in the national tournament.
2. 来月、党の大会が開かれる予定だ。— The party's convention is scheduled to be held next month.
3. 彼はマラソン大会で優勝した。— He won first place in the marathon competition.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 大会 covers both "tournament/competition" (sports) and "large-scale convention/assembly" (political, academic) — the common thread is a large-scale gathering, not necessarily competitive.

## 大概 (たいがい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** generally, mostly, for the most part

**Similar words:**
- だいたい — very close casual synonym, essentially interchangeable in everyday speech.
- ほとんど — "almost/nearly all," emphasizes proportion/extent more precisely than the vaguer 大概.
- 大抵（たいてい）— near-identical synonym, used interchangeably with 大概 in most contexts.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 休日は大概家でのんびり過ごす。— On holidays, I generally spend time relaxing at home.
2. 彼の話は大概本当だ。— Most of what he says is generally true.
3. 大概のことには慣れてしまった。— I've gotten used to most things by now.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 大概 is near-synonymous with 大抵 and だいたい; it can also mean "within reason/moderation" in expressions like いい加減にしなさい大概にしろ ("that's enough, cut it out") — a somewhat idiomatic usage distinct from the "generally" sense.

## 体格 (たいかく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** build, physique

**Similar words:**
- 体型（たいけい）— "body shape/figure," focuses more on shape/silhouette (slim, stout) than overall physical strength/frame implied by 体格.
- 体つき（からだつき）— "physical build," a more casual, descriptive equivalent.
- 筋肉質（きんにくしつ）— "muscular," describes a specific quality of build rather than build in general.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼はラグビー選手らしいがっしりした体格をしている。— He has a sturdy build typical of a rugby player.
2. 体格に恵まれているので力仕事が向いている。— Being blessed with a good physique, he's suited to physical labor.
3. 子供の頃から体格が大きかった。— He's had a large build since childhood.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 体格 emphasizes overall physical frame/robustness (often praised, e.g. がっしりした体格), while 体型 is more neutral and focused on shape/proportions (useful for discussing weight/diet) — don't use them interchangeably.

## 退学 (たいがく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to leave/quit school, be expelled

**Similar words:**
- [卒業](vocab-24.md#卒業する-そつぎょう)（そつぎょう）— "to graduate," the normal/successful completion of school, opposite in connotation to 退学.
- [休学](vocab-15.md#休学-きゅうがく)（きゅうがく）— "to take a leave of absence from school" (temporary), distinct from the permanent departure implied by 退学.
- 中退（ちゅうたい）— "to drop out partway through," a very close synonym often used interchangeably with 退学.

**Opposite words:**
- [入学](vocab-30.md#入学-にゅうがく)（にゅうがく）— to enter school

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は経済的な理由で大学を退学した。— He left university for financial reasons.
2. 問題行動を理由に退学処分になった。— He was expelled due to problematic behavior.
3. 退学届を提出しなければならない。— I have to submit a withdrawal notice from school.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 退学 can mean either voluntarily leaving school or being expelled (退学処分 specifically implies forced expulsion) — context determines which, unlike 中退, which is neutral about voluntary dropping out.

## 大学院 (だいがくいん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a graduate school

**Similar words:**
- 大学（だいがく）— undergraduate university; 大学院 is specifically the postgraduate institution attached to or associated with a university.
- 大学院生（だいがくいんせい）— "graduate student," the person enrolled, a related compound.
- 修士課程（しゅうしかてい）— "master's program," a specific level of study within 大学院.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は大学院で経済学を研究している。— He is researching economics at graduate school.
2. 大学院に進学することを決めた。— I decided to go on to graduate school.
3. 大学院修了後、研究者として働き始めた。— After completing graduate school, he started working as a researcher.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 大学院 specifically means postgraduate study (master's/doctoral); don't confuse with 大学, which refers to undergraduate university education in Japan's education system.

## 大気 (たいき)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** the atmosphere, the air

**Similar words:**
- 空気（くうき）— "air" in the everyday, breathable sense; 大気 is more scientific/formal, referring to the atmosphere as a whole layer surrounding Earth.
- 天候（てんこう）— "weather conditions," related but distinct — describes atmospheric state/phenomena rather than the atmosphere itself.
- オゾン層（オゾンそう）— "ozone layer," a specific part of 大気 often discussed in environmental contexts.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 大気汚染が深刻な問題になっている。— Air pollution has become a serious problem.
2. ロケットは大気圏を抜けて宇宙に向かった。— The rocket passed through the atmosphere and headed into space.
3. 大気中の二酸化炭素濃度が上昇している。— The concentration of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere is rising.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 大気 is the scientific/formal term for "the atmosphere" (used in 大気汚染, 大気圏), while 空気 is the everyday word for breathable "air" in a room or environment — don't use 大気 for casual contexts like "the air in this room."

## 大金 (たいきん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a large sum of money

**Similar words:**
- 高額（こうがく）— "a high/large amount (of money)," often used adjectivally (高額な); 大金 is more often a standalone noun.
- 巨額（きょがく）— "a huge sum," an intensified version implying an even larger, often eye-watering amount than 大金.
- 財産（ざいさん）— "fortune/assets," refers to accumulated wealth/property rather than a single sum of money.

**Opposite words:**
- 少額（しょうがく）— a small amount (of money)

**Example sentences:**
1. 宝くじで大金を手に入れた。— He won a large sum of money in the lottery.
2. 大金をはたいて新車を買った。— I spent a large sum of money to buy a new car.
3. その事業には大金が必要だ。— That business requires a large amount of capital.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 大金をはたく ("to spend a large sum, splurge") is a common fixed collocation; don't confuse 大金 (たいきん, a sum of money) with the homophone-adjacent 代金 (だいきん, the price/cost of goods) — different kanji, different meaning.

## 代金 (だいきん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** the price, the cost (of goods/services), payment due

**Similar words:**
- 値段（ねだん）— "price," the everyday casual term; 代金 is more formal, specifically referring to the amount owed for goods/services rendered.
- [料金](vocab-33.md#料金-りょうきん)（りょうきん）— "fee/charge" (for services, utilities); 代金 is used more for goods purchased.
- 費用（ひよう）— "cost/expense" in a broader sense, including costs beyond simple purchase price.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 商品の代金を支払った。— I paid for the goods.
2. 代金引換で荷物を受け取った。— I received the package by cash on delivery.
3. 代金はカードでお支払いいただけます。— Payment can be made by card.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 代金引換（だいきんひきかえ, COD）is a common fixed term in online shopping; don't confuse 代金 (だいきん, price owed for goods) with 大金 (たいきん, a large sum of money) — similar sound, different kanji and meaning.

## 大工 (だいく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a carpenter

**Similar words:**
- 建築家（けんちくか）— "an architect," who designs buildings; 大工 is the craftsperson who physically builds/constructs them.
- 職人（しょくにん）— "a craftsman/artisan" in general, a broader category that includes 大工 as a specific trade.
- 工務店（こうむてん）— "a construction company/contractor firm," the business entity that employs 大工.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 大工が家の修理をしてくれた。— A carpenter came and repaired the house for us.
2. 彼の父は腕のいい大工だった。— His father was a skilled carpenter.
3. 大工道具を揃えて日曜大工を楽しんでいる。— I've gathered carpentry tools and enjoy DIY woodworking on Sundays.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 日曜大工 (literally "Sunday carpenter") is a common fixed phrase meaning "DIY/amateur home improvement," distinct from professional 大工 — worth learning as a set expression.

## 待遇 (たいぐう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** treatment, pay, reception, terms of employment

**Similar words:**
- [給料](vocab-15.md#給料-きゅうりょう)（きゅうりょう）— "salary/pay," a narrower subset of the broader 待遇, which also includes benefits and working conditions.
- 処遇（しょぐう）— "treatment/handling" (of an employee), close synonym often used in formal HR contexts.
- もてなし — "hospitality/reception" (of a guest), overlaps with the "reception" sense of 待遇 but used more for social hosting than employment terms.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. この会社は待遇がいいと評判だ。— This company has a reputation for good treatment/benefits.
2. 彼は破格の待遇で迎えられた。— He was welcomed with exceptionally generous terms.
3. 待遇改善を求めてストライキが行われた。— A strike was held demanding improved working conditions.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 待遇 in job contexts covers the whole package — salary, benefits, status — not just the salary figure; 待遇がいい/悪い is the standard way to evaluate a job offer comprehensively.

## 退屈 (たいくつ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** tedious, boring, dull

**Similar words:**
- つまらない — "boring/uninteresting," a more casual, general-purpose word; 退屈 specifically emphasizes the feeling of having nothing to do/time dragging.
- [飽きる](vocab-06.md#飽きる-あきる)（あきる）— "to get tired of/bored with (something)," describes a process of losing interest rather than the static state of 退屈.
- 暇（ひま）— "free time/idleness," overlaps since boredom often stems from having too much free time, but 暇 is neutral, not necessarily negative.

**Opposite words:**
- 面白い（おもしろい）— interesting, fun

**Example sentences:**
1. 退屈な会議が長く続いた。— The tedious meeting dragged on for a long time.
2. 一人で家にいると退屈してしまう。— Staying home alone makes me bored.
3. 退屈しのぎに本を読んだ。— I read a book to kill the boredom.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 退屈しのぎ ("to pass the time/kill boredom") is a useful fixed phrase; 退屈 emphasizes the restless feeling of boredom from inactivity, distinct from つまらない, which judges something as simply "not interesting" regardless of idleness.

## 体系 (たいけい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a system, systematic structure

**Similar words:**
- システム — the loanword equivalent, often used for technical/mechanical systems; 体系 leans toward abstract, organized bodies of knowledge or theory.
- [制度](vocab-22.md#制度-せいど)（せいど）— "a system/institution" (often legal/social), more about established rules than the organized structure of ideas implied by 体系.
- [組織](vocab-24.md#組織-そしき)（そしき）— "organization," refers to a group of people/entities, whereas 体系 refers to an organized structure of concepts or components.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. この理論は体系的に整理されている。— This theory is systematically organized.
2. 言語体系を学ぶことは興味深い。— Studying language systems is fascinating.
3. 教育体系の見直しが求められている。— A review of the education system is being called for.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 体系的 (けいてき, "systematic") is a frequent adjectival form worth learning alongside the noun; 体系 is typically used for abstract structures of knowledge/theory/organization, not physical mechanical systems (which would use システム or 装置).

## 対決 (たいけつ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** confrontation, to confront, face off

**Similar words:**
- [対立](#対立-たいりつ)（たいりつ）— "opposition/conflict (of views)," more abstract and ongoing, whereas 対決 implies a direct, often decisive confrontation/showdown.
- 衝突（しょうとつ）— "collision/clash," can be literal or figurative, often more sudden/violent than the more formal 対決.
- 決戦（けっせん）— "a decisive battle," narrower, specifically the climactic final confrontation.

**Opposite words:**
- 和解（わかい）— reconciliation

**Example sentences:**
1. 両チームの決勝対決が注目を集めている。— The two teams' final showdown is drawing attention.
2. 彼は上司と対決する覚悟を決めた。— He resolved to confront his boss directly.
3. 選挙は与野党の対決構図となった。— The election took the shape of a confrontation between the ruling and opposition parties.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 対決 implies a direct, often dramatic face-off (sports finals, political rivalries, personal confrontations) — distinguish from 対立, which describes an ongoing state of conflicting positions without necessarily a single decisive clash.

## 体験 (たいけん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** an experience, to experience, undergo

**Similar words:**
- [経験](vocab-17.md#経験する-けいけん)（けいけん）— "experience" in a broader, often cumulative sense (career/life experience); 体験 emphasizes a direct, often vivid personal/sensory experience.
- 実体験（じったいけん）— "firsthand experience," an intensified form emphasizing authenticity, built on 体験.
- [味わう](vocab-06.md#味わう-あじわう)（あじわう）— "to savor/experience (an emotion)," more poetic, often used for experiencing feelings rather than events.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 貴重な体験をさせてもらった。— I was given a valuable experience.
2. 彼は戦争を体験した世代だ。— He belongs to the generation that experienced the war firsthand.
3. 子供たちに自然体験をさせたい。— I want to give the children a hands-on experience of nature.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 体験 emphasizes a specific, often memorable firsthand encounter (often used in 体験談 "personal accounts," 体験入学 "trial school attendance"), while 経験 is broader and can refer to accumulated skills/knowledge gained over time.

## 太鼓 (たいこ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a drum (traditional Japanese)

**Similar words:**
- ドラム — the Western drum kit, a loanword contrasted with the traditional 太鼓.
- 和太鼓（わだいこ）— explicitly "Japanese taiko drum," often used to disambiguate from Western drums.
- 鼓（つづみ）— a smaller, hand-held traditional Japanese drum, distinct in shape/use from 太鼓.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 祭りで太鼓の演奏が披露された。— A taiko drum performance was showcased at the festival.
2. 彼は子供の頃から太鼓を習っている。— He has been learning taiko drumming since childhood.
3. 太鼓の音が遠くまで響いた。— The sound of the drum resonated far into the distance.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 太鼓 appears in the idiom 太鼓持ち（たいこもち）meaning "a flatterer/yes-man" (originally a term for a performer who entertained patrons) — a figurative usage worth knowing beyond the literal instrument.

## 滞在 (たいざい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a stay, a sojourn (in a place, usually away from home)

**Similar words:**
- [宿泊](vocab-20.md#宿泊-しゅくはく)（しゅくはく）— specifically "lodging/staying overnight," emphasizes the accommodation itself rather than the duration of the visit.
- 滞留（たいりゅう）— more formal, often used for things/people being "held up" or stagnating somewhere, not just a pleasant stay.
- 在留（ざいりゅう）— legal/administrative term for foreign residents staying in a country (e.g. 在留資格).

**Opposite words:**
- 出発（しゅっぱつ）— departure
- 帰国（きこく）— returning to one's country

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は一週間ほど京都に滞在する予定だ。— He plans to stay in Kyoto for about a week.
2. 長期滞在のお客様には割引があります。— There is a discount for guests staying long-term.
3. 滞在中にいくつかの寺を訪れたい。— I want to visit several temples during my stay.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 滞在 implies a temporary stay away from one's base (travel, business trip), unlike 住む which means to actually live/reside somewhere permanently. It's a noun that takes する (滞在する).

## 対策 (たいさく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** measures, countermeasures, a policy response (to a problem)

**Similar words:**

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** A fairly harsh, critical evaluative term, mostly used for creative works (films, novels, games) — not typically applied to a person's character or everyday objects.
