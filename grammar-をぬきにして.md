# ～を抜きにして {#をぬきにして}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～を抜きにして

**Furigana form:** ～をぬきにして

**Meaning:** "Without X, leaving X aside" — indicates that something is excluded or set aside, often implying that proceeding without it would be unusual, impossible, or incomplete.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + を抜きにして: 冗談を抜きにして (joking aside); 彼の協力を抜きにしては (without his cooperation...)
- Often paired with は for emphasis: 〜を抜きにしては + negative predicate: この成功は彼の努力を抜きにしては語れない (this success can't be discussed without his effort)
- Related fixed form: 〜抜きで (casual, simpler "without X," e.g., 砂糖抜きで — without sugar)

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal, both spoken and written. Common when stressing that a topic, person, or factor is essential and can't reasonably be ignored, or when setting aside a side-topic (like jokes) to get to serious matters.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **〜抜きで** — more casual, simple "without X" (used often for food/drink: ガス抜きで, 氷抜きで), lacks the rhetorical "can't proceed without" emphasis.
- [**〜なしに**](grammar-なしに.md) — "without X," more literary, similar meaning but slightly different collocational range.
- **〜を除いて** — "excluding X," more neutral/factual exclusion without the rhetorical weight.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though ～を含めて ("including X") contrasts by incorporating rather than excluding.

**Example sentences:**
1. 冗談を抜きにして、真剣に話し合いましょう。
   (Joking aside, let's talk about this seriously.)
2. 彼の尽力を抜きにして、このプロジェクトの成功はあり得なかった。
   (Without his tireless effort, this project's success would have been impossible.)
3. 政治の話を抜きにして、純粋にスポーツを楽しみたい。
   (Setting politics aside, I just want to enjoy the sport purely.)
4. チームワークを抜きにしては、この結果は得られなかっただろう。
   (Without teamwork, we likely wouldn't have achieved this result.)
5. お世辞を抜きにして言うと、君の作品は本当に素晴らしい。
   (Flattery aside, your work is truly wonderful.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** を抜きにして carries rhetorical weight implying the excluded element is actually significant (often used to say something can't be properly discussed/achieved without it, especially in the 〜を抜きにしては〜ない construction). Don't confuse with the more mundane 〜抜きで, which is used for simple everyday exclusions (like removing an ingredient) without this emphatic nuance.
