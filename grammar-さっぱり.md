# さっぱり {#さっぱり}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** さっぱり

**Furigana form:** さっぱり

**Meaning:** "Not at all / no good whatsoever" — used with a negative predicate to emphatically state complete failure, lack of understanding, or absence of a quality. (さっぱり also has a separate, unrelated sense meaning "refreshed, clean, light" as in food or feeling after a shower, e.g. さっぱりした味 "a light/clean taste" — this card focuses on the negative-emphasis usage most relevant to JLPT grammar.)

**Conjugation:**
- さっぱり + 〜ない (negative predicate required for the "not at all" sense)
- Example: 彼の話はさっぱり分からない (I don't understand his story at all)
- Example: 最近、仕事がさっぱりうまくいかない (Lately, work isn't going well at all)

**Typical context:** Spoken, casual-to-neutral register. Carries a tone of frustration, bewilderment, or resignation when describing a total lack of success or comprehension — often used to express that the speaker is completely stumped or that something has utterly failed to meet expectations.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **全然〜ない** — "not at all," a very close synonym in the negative-emphasis sense, though 全然 is more common in casual modern speech generally, while さっぱり carries a slightly more old-fashioned or exasperated flavor, especially common with わからない (さっぱりわからない is a very fixed, idiomatic combination).
- **まるで〜ない** — "not in the least / as if not at all," similarly emphasizes a complete negative, often used with comparisons (まるで子供のようだ in the affirmative sense, or まるでだめだ in the negative), slightly more literary/figurative in flavor than さっぱり.
- **これといって〜ない** — "nothing in particular," also pairs with negatives but describes an absence of anything noteworthy rather than a total failure or total lack of understanding.
- **ちっとも〜ない** — "not even a little," another close synonym for emphatic negation, common in casual/feminine speech patterns, interchangeable with さっぱり in many contexts.

**Opposite grammar structures:** よく分かる or すっかり〜た ("completely understood / completely did") — both affirm total success or comprehension, the opposite of さっぱり's total negative.

**Example sentences:**
1. この問題の解き方がさっぱり分からない。
   (I have absolutely no idea how to solve this problem.)
2. 新しい上司とはさっぱりうまくいかない。
   (Things aren't going well at all with my new boss.)
3. 最近、営業成績がさっぱりで困っている。
   (Lately my sales results have been terrible, and I'm in trouble.)
4. 彼の冗談はさっぱり面白くなかった。
   (His joke wasn't funny in the slightest.)
5. 入院してから、食欲がさっぱりなくなってしまった。
   (Ever since being hospitalized, I've completely lost my appetite.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes confuse this さっぱり with its homonymous "refreshed/clean" sense (e.g. シャワーを浴びてさっぱりした, "I felt refreshed after a shower") — the two uses are unrelated in meaning and are distinguished entirely by context: the "refreshed" sense is typically used in affirmative sentences about physical/mental sensation, while the "not at all" sense always requires a negative predicate. Another common error is dropping the negative ending, which breaks the "not at all" meaning entirely — さっぱり alone, without a following ない/だめ/分からない etc., does not convey "not at all."
