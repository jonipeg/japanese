# ～たりとも {#たりとも}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～たりとも

**Furigana form:** ～たりとも

**Meaning:** "Not even X (however small/brief)" — emphasizes that not even the smallest unit or briefest instance of something is permitted or occurs, typically paired with a negative predicate.

**Conjugation:**
- 数量を表す名詞（一瞬、一刻、一人など）+ たりとも + ～ない
- Example base: 一瞬 → 一瞬たりとも気を抜けない

**Typical context:** Formal, written/literary register; used for emphatic, dramatic statements about strict vigilance, discipline, or absolute prohibition. Common in formal speeches, military/sports contexts, and solemn writing.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **も～ない** — plain emphatic negation (e.g., 一人も来ない, "not even one person came"); the everyday equivalent, while たりとも is markedly more formal and dramatic.
- **すら／さえ** — "even X"; both intensify a minimal case, but すら／さえ are more general-purpose emphatic particles usable in many contexts, while たりとも is restricted to small time/quantity units paired with a negative.
- **一切～ない** — "not at all / none whatsoever"; a strong blanket negation, similar emphatic force but without たりとも's specific "not even the smallest unit" image.

**Opposite grammar structures:** いつでも／いくらでも ("anytime / any amount") expresses the reverse — unrestricted allowance rather than a strict zero-tolerance prohibition.

**Example sentences:**
1. 試合中は一瞬たりとも気を抜けない。 (During the match, you can't let your guard down even for a single instant.)
2. この秘密は一刻たりとも漏らしてはならない。 (This secret must not be leaked even for a moment.)
3. 彼は約束を一度たりとも破ったことがない。 (He has never broken a promise, not even once.)
4. 国境は一歩たりとも越えさせない。 (We will not let them cross the border, not even by a single step.)
5. 責任者として、一瞬たりとも油断は許されない。 (As the person in charge, not even a moment's carelessness is permitted.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** たりとも always requires a small quantity/time-unit noun (一瞬, 一刻, 一人, 一歩, 一度) before it and a negative predicate after it — it cannot attach to ordinary nouns without that "minimal unit" framing. This is a dramatically emphatic, almost solemn construction; overusing it for mundane situations sounds overwrought, so reserve it for contexts of strict discipline, vigilance, or absolute prohibition.
