# ～べきだ {#べきだ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～べきだ

**Furigana form:** ～べきだ (no special kanji reading; べき has no common kanji form in modern use)

**Meaning:** "Should," "ought to" — expresses a moral, normative, or logical obligation: what is the correct/proper thing to do based on common sense, rules, or ethics (not a personal preference).

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形 + べきだ (する → すべきだ／するべきだ, both accepted; すべきだ is the traditional form)
- Negative: 動詞辞書形 + べきではない ("should not")
- Past regret: 動詞辞書形 + べきだった ("should have done")
- 学生は授業に出るべきだ。(Students should attend class.)

**Typical context:** Spoken and written, neutral-to-formal. Common in opinions, debates, advice columns, and moral arguments. Can sound preachy/strong in casual conversation if overused.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **たほうがいい** — softer "should/had better," more like friendly advice than moral obligation.
- **なければならない** — "must," expresses necessity/requirement rather than moral rightness.
- [**ものだ**](grammar-ものだ.md) — can express general social norms ("that's how things should be done"), softer and more about convention.

**Opposite grammar structures:** **べきではない** — "should not," the direct negative form.

**Example sentences:**
1. 約束は必ず守るべきだ。
   (You should always keep your promises.)
2. 若いうちにいろいろな経験をするべきだ。
   (You should have various experiences while you're young.)
3. 彼はもっと早く謝るべきだった。
   (He should have apologized sooner.)
4. 政府はもっと環境問題に力を入れるべきだ。
   (The government should put more effort into environmental issues.)
5. 他人の悪口を言うべきではない。
   (You should not speak ill of others.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** べきだ expresses what is objectively/morally "right," so using it for simple personal suggestions (e.g., "you should try this restaurant") sounds overly forceful — たほうがいい is more appropriate there. Also, する verb traditionally takes すべきだ (not するべきだ) in formal/classical usage, though both are accepted in modern Japanese — N2 tests may expect recognition of すべきだ as the "correct" traditional form.
