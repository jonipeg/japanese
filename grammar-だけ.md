# ～だけ {#だけ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～だけ

**Furigana form:** ～だけ (no kanji)

**Meaning:** "Only, just, as much as" — a basic limiter marking exclusivity or extent.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 (noun) + だけ
- 動詞 (verb, plain form) + だけ
- い形容詞 (plain form) + だけ
- な形容詞 + な + だけ (or plain form + だけ)

**Typical context:** Neutral, used across all registers of spoken and written Japanese.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **しか～ない** — "only" with an obligatory negative predicate; carries a stronger nuance of scarcity or insufficiency than だけ.
- **のみ** — formal, written equivalent of だけ, common in official documents and notices.
- **ばかり** — "nothing but," emphasizing repetition or exclusive focus, often with mild criticism.

**Opposite grammar structures:** 以外 ("except for, other than"); 全部／すべて ("everything, all").

**Example sentences:**
1. このケーキは一つだけ残っている。
   (Only one piece of this cake remains.)
2. 好きなだけ食べてください。
   (Please eat as much as you like.)
3. 彼は英語だけでなく、フランス語も話せる。
   (He can speak not only English but also French.)
4. 時間がある時だけ、ジムに行く。
   (I go to the gym only when I have time.)
5. できるだけ早く来てください。
   (Please come as soon as possible.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** だけ is neutral and simply states a limit or extent, while しか～ない requires a negative verb and carries a stronger nuance of insufficiency or lack. Learners often forget that しか must always be paired with a negative predicate, whereas だけ is used with affirmative predicates.
