# ～ともあろう {#ともあろう}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～ともあろう

**Furigana form:** ～ともあろう

**Meaning:** Expresses surprise, criticism, or disbelief that a person of such fine ability, reputation, or responsible position would do something so unbefitting of that status — or, conversely, expresses a wish that such a person behave in a manner that matches their standing.

**Conjugation:**
- 名 + ともあろう + 名
- 社長ともあろう人が — "a person who is supposedly the company president"

**Typical context:** Formal written and spoken register — common in news commentary, formal criticism, and speeches expressing shock or disapproval at the conduct of someone in a respected position.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～にあるまじき** — "unbefitting of one's position," also criticizes behavior that doesn't match someone's status, but more literary/formal and used as a pre-noun modifier (あるまじき行為).
- **～らしくない** — a much more casual way to say "that's not like X," without the same weight of social status.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a phrase like ～にふさわしく振る舞う ("to behave in a manner befitting X") describes the matching, expected behavior instead.

**Example sentences:**
1. 国会議員ともあろう人が、差別的な発言をするなんて信じられない。
   (I can't believe that someone who is supposed to be a member of the Diet would make a discriminatory remark.)
2. 大学の学長ともあろうお方が、そのような無責任なことを言ってどうするんですか。
   (What is a person who holds the position of university president doing, saying something so irresponsible?)
3. 山田さんともあろう人が、こんな単純なミスをするなんて。どうしたのでしょう。
   (For someone like Mr. Yamada to make such a simple mistake — what happened?)
4. 学会の会長ともあろう人は、最新の研究テーマについてしっかり把握しておくべきだ。
   (A person who holds the position of society president ought to have a firm grasp of the latest research topics.)
5. 警察官ともあろう者が、交通違反を見逃すとは呆れてものも言えない。
   (For a police officer to overlook a traffic violation is simply appalling.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Attaches only to nouns referring to people the speaker holds in high regard or who occupy a position of responsibility — it does not work for ordinary people or for oneself. Most often expresses surprise or dissatisfaction at behavior that fails to live up to that status, but as in example 4 it can also state what such a person *should* do.
