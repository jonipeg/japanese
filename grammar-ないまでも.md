# ～ないまでも {#ないまでも}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ないまでも

**Furigana form:** ～ないまでも

**Meaning:** "Although not going so far as to ~, at least ~" — acknowledges that a higher-level action/standard isn't being met, but asserts that a lesser, more minimal version of it should still be done.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ない形 (verb negative form) + までも
- Example: 結婚式には出席できないまでも、お祝いぐらいは贈るものだよ。 (Even if you can't attend the wedding, you should at least send a gift.)

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal spoken and written Japanese; used when setting a minimum acceptable standard after conceding that an ideal/higher standard isn't achievable.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **ずとも** — "even without doing ~" — a literary equivalent with a similar concessive nuance, more common in written/classical style.
- **とは言わないが** — "I won't say ~, but ~" — a more explicit, conversational way to concede a lesser standard, functionally similar but less fixed/grammaticalized.
- **なくても** — "even without doing ~" — overlaps somewhat but lacks the specific "at least do the minimum" follow-up structure that ないまでも implies.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, though 当然のように ("as a matter of course, without question") contrasts in spirit by implying no concession is needed.

**Example sentences:**
1. 結婚式には出席できないまでも、お祝いぐらいは贈るものだよ。
   (Even if you can't attend the wedding, you should at least send a gift.)
2. 完璧とは言えないまでも、彼のプレゼンはかなり良かった。
   (Although I can't call it perfect, his presentation was quite good.)
3. 毎日とは言わないまでも、週に一度は運動したほうがいい。
   (Even if not every day, you should exercise at least once a week.)
4. 優勝はできないまでも、決勝までは進みたい。
   (Even if we can't win the championship, I want to at least make it to the finals.)
5. 全額返済できないまでも、できる範囲で少しずつ返していくつもりだ。
   (Even if I can't repay the full amount, I intend to pay back little by little within what I can manage.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes forget the clause after ないまでも must present a lesser, achievable alternative — simply ending the sentence after the concession (without stating the minimum standard) results in an incomplete thought, since the whole point of the pattern is contrasting "not this much, but at least that much."
