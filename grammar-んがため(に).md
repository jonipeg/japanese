# ～んがため（に） {#んがため（に）}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～んがため（に）

**Furigana form:** ～んがため（に）

**Meaning:** "For the purpose of X" — expresses carrying out an action with a strong, deliberate goal in mind. Written-language style, more intense and archaic than ために.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ない形 + んがため（に）（例外：する → せん）
- 実現させんがため — "in order to make [it] happen"
- 生きんがために — "in order to survive"

**Typical context:** Formal written Japanese only — literary works, essays, and dramatic narration about weighty, serious goals (survival, ambition, dreams); never used in daily conversation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**～べく**](grammar-べく.md) — a similarly literary purpose expression, somewhat less intense and slightly more common in formal writing.
- **～ために** — the everyday, neutral equivalent usable in both speech and writing, lacking the dramatic weight of んがため.
- **～んばかりに** — shares the archaic ん- form but expresses "as if about to," a manner/degree meaning rather than purpose.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a causative/result structure like ～た結果 ("as a result of X") would instead describe an outcome, not a driving purpose.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女は歌手になりたいという夢を実現させんがため、上京した。
   (She moved to Tokyo in order to make her dream of becoming a singer come true.)
2. ライオンがしまうまを食べるのは残酷に見えるが、ライオンは生きんがために、そうするのである。
   (It may look cruel that lions eat zebras, but lions do so purely in order to survive.)
3. 自分の利益を得んがための発言では、人の心を動かせない。
   (Words spoken only for the sake of one's own gain can't move people's hearts.)
4. 彼は家族を守らんがため、危険を顧みずに現場に飛び込んだ。
   (He rushed into the scene without regard for danger, purely in order to protect his family.)
5. 真実を明らかにせんがため、記者は長年取材を続けた。
   (In order to bring the truth to light, the reporter continued his investigation for years.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This attaches only to intentional verbs expressing a weighty, serious goal — it sounds unnatural with trivial, everyday purposes. The clause that follows must describe a deliberate action by the same subject, never something imposed on someone else, and the phrase is essentially confined to formal/literary writing, so it should not be used in casual speech.
