# ～てはばからない {#てはばからない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～て憚らない

**Furigana form:** ～てはばからない

**Meaning:** "Doesn't hesitate to (boldly say/do) X" — describes someone stating or doing something bold, often arrogant or provocative, without any reservation.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞て形 + はばからない (Vて + はばからない)
- Most commonly used with verbs of speaking: 言ってはばからない, 公言してはばからない

**Typical context:** Formal, written/literary register — common in newspaper articles, biographies, and formal commentary describing a person's bold or brazen statement. Carries a slightly critical or at least note-worthy nuance about the speaker's confidence/audacity. Not used in casual conversation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**てやまない**](grammar-てやまない.md) — "never ceases to X" (e.g., 願ってやまない "never stops hoping"); expresses a continuous sincere feeling rather than boldness in speech/action.
- [**てはかなわない**](grammar-てはかなわない.md) — unrelated in meaning ("can't bear X"); shares only the ては... surface shape.
- **公言する** — a plain verb meaning "to declare publicly"; てはばからない adds the extra nuance of doing so unhesitatingly/brazenly, which 公言する alone does not convey.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct single opposite, but 言い渋る ("hesitate to say") or ためらいがちに言う ("say hesitantly") convey the reverse attitude.

**Example sentences:**
1. 吉田課長は「次に部長になるのは自分だろう」と言ってはばからない。 (Section Chief Yoshida boldly declares, without hesitation, "I'll surely be the next department head.")
2. その評論家は「この作品は駄作だ」と言ってはばからない。 (That critic doesn't hesitate to say, "This work is garbage.")
3. 彼は自分が天才だと公言してはばからない。 (He openly and unabashedly declares himself a genius.")
4. 社長は会議で他社の技術を「時代遅れだ」と言ってはばからなかった。 (The president didn't hesitate to call the other company's technology "outdated" in the meeting.)
5. 彼女は上司の前でも自分の意見を主張してはばからない。 (She doesn't hesitate to assert her own opinion even in front of her boss.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The base verb 憚る (はばかる) means "to hold back / restrain oneself out of consideration for others," so てはばからない literally means "does X without even that restraint" — implying the subject's statement or act might normally warrant some hesitation or tact, but they show none. Learners sometimes treat it as a neutral "boldly states," missing the built-in critical or impressed nuance about audacity. It is almost always used about a third person's statement, rarely about the speaker's own.
