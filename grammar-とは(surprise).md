# ～とは {#とは(surprise)}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～とは

**Furigana form:** ～とは

**Meaning:** "To think that X! / How surprising/shocking that X" — expresses strong surprise, disbelief, or emotional reaction to a fact just stated or learned, often left trailing without a main clause.

**Conjugation:**
- 普通形 + とは（＋驚いた／思わなかった、often omitted）
- Example base: 合格する → まさか合格するとは

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal, usable in both speech and writing; conveys genuine astonishment, admiration, or disbelief. The sentence is frequently left incomplete (ending right at とは), with the shock implied rather than stated.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**なんて**](grammar-など・なんて・なんか.md) — "to think that X! / what a X"; a more casual, everyday equivalent expressing similar surprise or exclamation, usable in informal speech, while とは is slightly more composed/written.
- **とは思わなかった** — the fuller, explicit version stating "I didn't think that X," often what とは implies when left trailing without a stated reaction.
- [**ことに**](grammar-ことに(は).md) — "to my surprise, X" (e.g., 驚いたことに); a different grammatical structure (sentence-initial) achieving a similar surprised framing, but とは attaches at the end of the surprising statement itself rather than opening the sentence.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; 予想通り ("as expected") would contrast with とは's framing of an unexpected, surprising fact.

**Example sentences:**
1. まさか彼が優勝するとは、誰も予想していなかった。 (No one had predicted that he, of all people, would win the championship.)
2. こんなに早く結果が出るとは思わなかった。 (I didn't think the results would come out this quickly.)
3. あの二人が結婚するとは驚いた。 (I was surprised that those two were getting married.)
4. まさかこんな場所で彼女に会うとはね。 (To think I'd run into her in a place like this!)
5. 一流企業が倒産するとは、時代も変わったものだ。 (To think that a top-tier company would go bankrupt — times really have changed.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** とは is frequently used with a trailing, unfinished sentence structure (the surprise is implied by the construction itself, often with まさか at the start reinforcing disbelief) — learners shouldn't feel they must always complete it with an explicit "I'm surprised" clause, since leaving it hanging is natural and idiomatic. This とは (expressing surprise at a fact) should not be confused with the unrelated topic-defining とは used in definitions (e.g., 愛とは何か, "what is love?") — context (a surprising fact vs. a question of definition) distinguishes the two.
