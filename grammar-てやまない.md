# ～てやまない {#てやまない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～てやまない

**Furigana form:** ～てやまない *(kana only; attaches to verb て-form)*

**Meaning:** "Never stop doing X" / "continue to feel X without end" — expresses that a feeling (hope, wish, respect, love) persists endlessly and unabated. Used almost exclusively with emotion/wish verbs like 願う, 祈る, 愛する, 尊敬する.

**Conjugation:**
- V-て形 + やまない
- Restricted to a small set of emotion/volition verbs (願う, 祈る, 愛する, 期待する, 尊敬する)

**Typical context:** Very formal, written/literary N1 register. Common in speeches, formal prefaces, eulogies, and congratulatory writing. Essentially never used in casual conversation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**を禁じえない**](grammar-を禁じえない.md) — "cannot help but feel X": describes a sudden, involuntary emotional reaction welling up at one moment, whereas てやまない describes a steady, continuous, ongoing wish or feeling.
- [**のいたり**](grammar-のいたり.md) — "the utmost extreme of X": a self-referential, often humble exclamatory peak feeling (光栄の至り), distinct from てやまない's sustained verb-based wish.
- [**のきわみ**](grammar-のきわみ.md) — "the peak of X": an objective, noun-based description of an emotional extreme reached, rather than a continuously expressed wish.
- [**かぎりだ**](grammar-かぎりだ.md) — "extremely X": a momentary adjective-based personal feeling exclamation, not a continuous verbal wish like てやまない.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 早く先輩の病気が良くなることを願ってやみません。
   (I never stop hoping that my senior's illness will get better soon.)
2. 彼の幸せを心から祈ってやまない。
   (I pray from my heart, without end, for his happiness.)
3. 恩師には感謝してやまない。
   (I will never stop being grateful to my mentor.)
4. 両親は子どもの成功を願ってやまなかった。
   (My parents never stopped wishing for their child's success.)
5. 彼女は祖国の平和を愛してやまない。
   (She loves her homeland's peace without end.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** てやまない belongs to the N1 "extreme degree / can't help but" confusable cluster drilled heavily in MC tests. The most common confusion is with を禁じえない: students mistake てやまない's continuous verbal wish for を禁じえない's sudden, involuntary single-moment emotional outburst. A quick test: てやまない attaches to a verb (願って, 祈って) describing an ongoing wish, while を禁じえない attaches to a noun of emotion (同情を禁じえない) describing a reaction you can't suppress right now.
