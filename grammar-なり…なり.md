# ～なり…なり {#なり…なり}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～なり…なり

**Furigana form:** ～なり…なり

**Meaning:** Suggests doing one thing or another (or anything else of the kind), presenting two or more examples as acceptable, interchangeable options without pinning down which one — "whether it's X or Y, just do something."

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞(+助詞)・動詞辞書形 + なり
- わからないことは先生になり友達になり聞いてみたらどう？ — "why not ask the teacher, or a friend, or whoever, about what you don't understand?"

**Typical context:** Spoken and written advice-giving register — used when suggesting options to someone, in casual-to-neutral conversation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～とか～とか** — a more casual way of listing examples with an "or whatever" nuance, but without the suggestive "do whichever" force of なり…なり.
- **～か～か** — simply presents alternatives or poses a question, lacking the active suggestion/recommendation tone of なり…なり.
- **～（よ）うと～（よ）うと** — "whether X or Y," expresses indifference to which happens, rather than offering a suggestion of what to do.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; specifying a single mandatory option, like ～しかない ("there's no choice but X"), contrasts with the open-ended suggestion of なり…なり.

**Example sentences:**
1. 昼休みは40分しかないんだから、おにぎりなりサンドイッチなり何か買って早く食べたほうがいい。
   (We only have 40 minutes for lunch, so you'd better buy something — a rice ball, a sandwich, whatever — and eat quickly.)
2. お手伝いできることはいたしますよ。わたしになり兄になり言ってください。
   (I'll help in any way I can. Just tell me, or tell my older brother, whichever is easier.)
3. 言葉の意味がわからなかったらわからないままにしないで、辞書で調べるなりインターネットで探してみるなりしてみたらどうですか。
   (If you don't understand the meaning of a word, don't just leave it — why not look it up in a dictionary, or search for it online, or something like that?)
4. この魚、僕が釣ったんだ。焼くなり煮るなりして食べてみて。
   (I caught this fish myself. Try grilling it, or boiling it, or whatever you like, and eat it.)
5. 困ったことがあったら、電話するなりメールするなりして連絡してください。
   (If you run into trouble, get in touch somehow — call, email, whatever works.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern deliberately avoids pinning down one specific option — it signals "anything in this category is fine." It cannot be followed by a past-tense or completed-action sentence, since it's inherently a suggestion aimed at the future; what follows is typically the speaker's own wish or intention, or a request/suggestion directed at someone else.
