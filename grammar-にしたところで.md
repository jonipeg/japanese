# ～にしたところで {#にしたところで}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～にしたところで

**Furigana form:** ～にしたところで

**Meaning:** "Even in the case of X / even granting X, [it would still be powerless/meaningless/no different]" — concedes that even the person or thing in question, who might seem like an exception, is ultimately no different or no more capable than anyone else.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + にしたところで
- わたしにしたところで — "even in my case/even if it were me"

**Typical context:** Written and spoken, somewhat formal register; used in argumentative or reflective statements to preemptively concede a possible exception and then deny that it changes anything.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～としたところで** — near-synonym, "even supposing X," slightly more hypothetical in nuance.
- [**～たところで**](grammar-たところで.md) — attaches to verbs rather than nouns, expressing "even if one were to do X, [it wouldn't matter]" — same underlying nuance of futility, different attachment point.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a phrase like ～ならでは ("precisely because of X, uniquely possible") would instead emphasize X's special effectiveness — the reverse of this pattern's "no different/powerless" nuance.

**Example sentences:**
1. 厳しい批判をしてしまったが、わたしにしたところでいい案を持っているわけではない。
   (I was harsh in my criticism, but it's not as if I myself have a good solution either.)
2. 日本はテロ対策が甘いと思う。災害対策にしたところで同じことだ。
   (I think Japan's counter-terrorism measures are lax. The same goes even for disaster preparedness.)
3. 社長にしたところで、この不況をどうすることもできないだろう。
   (Even the company president probably couldn't do anything about this recession.)
4. 専門家にしたところで、将来を正確に予測することはできない。
   (Even experts cannot accurately predict the future.)
5. 新しいリーダーにしたところで、根本的な問題は解決できないだろう。
   (Even with a new leader, the fundamental problems probably won't be solved.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern concedes that even a seemingly capable or exceptional person or thing is subject to the same limitation as everything else — don't confuse it with ～にしては ("considering X," evaluating something against an expectation), which serves a completely different function.
