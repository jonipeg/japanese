# ～ためしがない {#ためしがない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～試しがない

**Furigana form:** ～ためしがない

**Meaning:** "X has never once happened" — emphasizes that despite repeated opportunity or expectation, a particular outcome has never occurred even a single time.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞た形 + ためしがない (past-tense verb + ためしがない)
- Example base: 来る → 来たためしがない

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal, usable in both speech and writing; carries a mildly exasperated or complaining nuance about a consistent pattern of failure/non-occurrence. Common in everyday complaints as well as written commentary.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **たことがない** — plain "have never done/experienced X"; a neutral experiential negative, whereas ためしがない carries an implicit nuance of repeated expectation being disappointed ("and it STILL never happens").
- [**ばかりだ**](grammar-ばかりだ.md) (negative pattern context) — not directly similar in form, but both can describe a frustrating recurring pattern; ばかりだ describes "all that happens is X" rather than "X never happens."
- **一度も～ない** — "not even once"; a plain adverbial emphasis achieving a similar meaning without the idiomatic 試し ("trial/example") noun.

**Opposite grammar structures:** いつも～ている／必ず～する ("always does X / X always happens") expresses the reverse — consistent occurrence rather than consistent non-occurrence.

**Example sentences:**
1. 伊藤さんは約束の時間通りに来たためしがない。 (Mr. Ito has never once come at the promised time.)
2. この店の料理が美味しかったためしがない。 (The food at this restaurant has never once been delicious.)
3. 彼が締め切りを守ったためしがない。 (He has never once met a deadline.)
4. 天気予報が当たったためしがない。 (The weather forecast has never once been right.)
5. あのチームが接戦で勝ったためしがない。 (That team has never once won a close game.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ためしがない requires a past-tense verb stem (not dictionary form) and almost always implies repeated chances that were each disappointed — it's not used for a single isolated event. Learners sometimes substitute it directly for たことがない without noticing the extra nuance of "despite repeated occasions, this pattern of failure continues," which gives ためしがない a more exasperated, complaining flavor than the neutral たことがない.
