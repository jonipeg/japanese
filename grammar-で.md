# で {#で}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** で (no kanji; always written in kana)

**Furigana form:** で

**Meaning:** "So, and then, and" — a casual contraction of それで used to prompt or continue a conversation, often asking "so then what?"

**Conjugation:** Sentence-initial conjunction, no conjugation needed — attaches at the sentence/utterance boundary, connecting the previous remark to the next. Unlike most conjunctions in this set, で is used almost exclusively mid-conversation (not between written sentences/paragraphs) to prompt the listener to continue their story or to transition to the next point in one's own speech.

**Typical context:** Very casual spoken register, used constantly in everyday conversation to keep a story moving or to ask for more details (e.g. "で、どうなったの?" = "So, what happened [then]?"). Essentially never appears in formal writing — its full, written-appropriate counterpart is それで.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**それで**](grammar-それで.md) — the fuller, slightly more neutral spoken/written form of で, meaning "so, and so"; で is its clipped, very casual contraction used mainly as a conversational prompt.
- [**だから**](grammar-だから.md) — means "so, therefore," but emphasizes logical/causal conclusion rather than simply prompting continuation of a narrative.
- [**そして**](grammar-そして.md) — neutral sequential "and then" connector for listing events; lacks で's conversational "prompting" function and is more narrative/written in feel.
- **じゃあ/それじゃ** — casual "well then," used to transition to a new topic or action, rather than to request continuation of the current one.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite — で is a narrow conversational-prompting function rather than part of the contrast/addition clusters, so it doesn't have a natural "opposite" conjunction.

**Example sentences:**
1. 「昨日彼女に会ったよ。」「で、どうだった?」
   ("I met her yesterday." "So, how was it?")
2. で、結局あの話はどうなったの?
   (So, what ended up happening with that matter?)
3. 「面接受けてきたんだ。」「で? 受かりそう?」
   ("I went to the interview." "So? Think you'll get it?")
4. で、君はこれからどうするつもりなの?
   (So, what are you planning to do from here?)
5. 「財布忘れてきちゃって。」「で、お金どうしたの?」
   ("I forgot my wallet." "So, what did you do about money?")

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes write で as a sentence-connector in formal writing, where それで (or more formal alternatives like そのため/したがって) should be used instead — で alone reads as an abrupt fragment outside of casual dialogue. Also, で is almost always a prompt for more information or the next part of a story, distinct from そして's neutral listing function or だから's causal-conclusion function; using で where だから's logical "therefore" is meant can make the sentence sound like a non-sequitur in writing, though in speech context usually disambiguates it.
