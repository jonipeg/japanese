# ～やしない {#やしない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～やしない

**Furigana form:** ～やしない

**Meaning:** Emphatic negative — "there's no way X" / "X doesn't happen at all" (しやしない, わかりやしない, etc.); strengthens a negation with a dismissive, emphatic tone.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ます形 (verb ます-stem) + やしない — e.g., し + やしない → しやしない; わかり + やしない → わかりやしない
- Inferred from common usage since no example sentence is given in the source; this is the colloquial contraction of ～はしない (emphatic negative using the topic particle は + しない), with や replacing は in casual speech.

**Typical context:** Casual, spoken Japanese — distinctly colloquial and somewhat blunt/dismissive in tone. Common in informal conversation, especially when expressing frustration, disbelief, or flat denial. Can sound curt or slightly rude depending on tone and relationship between speakers; not used in formal writing.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **はしない** — the non-contracted, slightly more neutral source form of やしない; はしない is usable in more neutral/formal contexts, while やしない is specifically casual/colloquial.
- **もしない** — "doesn't even do X"; adds an "not even" nuance (one among several things not done), distinct from やしない's blunt, standalone emphatic denial.
- [**わけがない**](grammar-わけがない.md) — "there's no way X (is the case)"; expresses logical impossibility/disbelief about a situation, whereas やしない is a direct emphatic negation of an action or state, often in response to someone's claim.
- [**ものか**](grammar-ものか・もんか.md) — "there's absolutely no way X!"; a strongly emphatic rhetorical negation, similar in intensity to やしない but typically used in exclamatory, standalone sentences rather than attached mid-sentence to a verb stem.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a conceptual contrast would be 必ず～する ("will definitely do X"), the emphatic affirmative counterpart to やしない's emphatic negative.

**Example sentences (no example sentence given in source for this entry; all below are original):**
1. そんなことを言っても、誰も信じやしないよ。
   (Even if you say something like that, nobody's going to believe it at all.)
2. いくら頼んでも、彼は手伝いやしない。
   (No matter how much I ask, he won't help at all.)
3. こんな説明じゃ、わかりやしないよ。
   (With an explanation like this, there's no way anyone will understand.)
4. 薬を飲んだって、すぐには治りやしない。
   (Even if you take the medicine, it won't heal right away at all.)
5. あの人に何を言っても、聞きやしないから無駄だよ。
   (No matter what you say to that person, they won't listen at all, so it's pointless.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** No confirmed student wrong-answer or example sentence is recorded in the source for this pattern (scan4 only notes it as MC-confirmed). A key nuance for learners: やしない is a contraction of ～はしない and is strictly casual/spoken — using it in formal writing or polite conversation would sound jarringly out of register. Students should also note the attachment point is the ます-stem, not the dictionary or て-form.
