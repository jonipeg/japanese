# ～きらいがある {#きらいがある}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～きらいがある

**Furigana form:** ～きらいがある

**Meaning:** "Has a tendency to ~ (which is not desirable)" — points out an undesirable tendency, trait, or habit that someone (usually a third person) has, often softened with a hedging adverb.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形／ない形・名詞＋の ＋ きらいがある
- 物事を悲観的に考えるきらいがある — "has a tendency to think about things pessimistically"

**Typical context:** Written and spoken register, somewhat formal/critical tone — used in evaluative writing (reports, reviews, character assessments) and in conversation when gently criticizing someone's personality or behavior.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～傾向がある** — "has a tendency to ~," a more neutral and broadly usable synonym that doesn't necessarily carry criticism; きらいがある specifically implies the tendency is undesirable.
- **～がちだ** — "tends to ~ (often unintentionally)," closer to describing a recurring unfortunate pattern of events (e.g. 遅刻しがちだ) rather than a personality flaw.
- **～きらいなしとしない** — a more formal, written variant expressing a similar hedge ("one cannot say there is no tendency to ~"), found in stiffer written registers.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct grammatical opposite; describing a positive trait would instead use ～という美点がある ("has the virtue of ~") or simply ～のが得意だ ("is good at ~").

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼はどうも物事を悲観的に考えるきらいがある。
   (He does tend to think about things rather pessimistically.)
2. うちの部長は自分と違う考え方を認めようとしないきらいがある。
   (Our manager has a tendency not to accept ways of thinking different from his own.)
3. 松本さんは一度言い出したら人の意見に耳を傾けない。少し独断のきらいがある。
   (Once Matsumoto-san has said something, he won't listen to others' opinions — he's a bit of a tendency to be high-handed.)
4. 彼女は完璧を求めすぎて、細かいことにこだわりすぎるきらいがある。
   (She tends to demand too much perfection and get overly caught up in small details.)
5. この制度は手続きが煩雑になりすぎるきらいがある。
   (This system has a tendency to become excessively complicated in its procedures.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern is almost always used to criticize someone else's personality or a system's flaw — it sounds unnaturally self-deprecating or odd when applied to the speaker themselves. It is typically softened with hedging adverbs like どうも, 少し, or ともすれば, and should not be used to make a blunt, harsh accusation; the overall tone stays mild and indirect even though the content is critical.
