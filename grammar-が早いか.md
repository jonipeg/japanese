# ～が早いか {#が早いか}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～が早いか

**Furigana form:** ～がはやいか

**Meaning:** "No sooner than X, Y happens" — describes two actions occurring in near-instantaneous succession, with Y following X almost simultaneously. Literary, in the same family as ～そばから and ～なり.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形 (verb dictionary form) + が早いか
- 動詞た形 (verb た-form) + が早いか
- Example from source: 鳴るが早いか (the moment it rings)

**Typical context:** Formal, literary written style — common in narrative prose describing quick, dramatic sequences of action. Rarely used in casual conversation; it has a slightly dramatic, novelistic feel. Neutral register-wise (neither polite nor rude), but distinctly "bookish."

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**そばから**](grammar-そばから.md) — "no sooner does X happen than Y happens," but with a nuance of repeated, cyclical futility (X keeps getting undone); が早いか is about a single swift succession, not repetition/futility.
- [**なり**](grammar-なり.md) — nearly synonymous "the instant X, Y"; both describe immediate succession, but なり more strongly implies the second action is surprising or unexpected, while が早いか emphasizes sheer speed/near-simultaneity.
- **や否や (やいなや)** — also "as soon as X"; very close in meaning, largely interchangeable, though が早いか leans slightly more literary/narrative.
- **とたんに** — "the instant X happened, Y" — more neutral/common register, used more freely in everyday writing, whereas が早いか is reserved for literary narrative.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a contrasting idea would be ～てからしばらくして ("some time after doing X"), which emphasizes a delay rather than immediacy.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は授業終了のベルが鳴るが早いか、教室を飛び出してトイレへ向かった。
   (The moment the end-of-class bell rang, he dashed out of the classroom to the bathroom.)
2. 子供はおもちゃを見るが早いか、泣きやんでしまった。
   (The instant the child saw the toy, he stopped crying.)
3. 彼女は電話が鳴るが早いか、受話器を取った。
   (As soon as the phone rang, she picked up the receiver.)
4. ピストルの音が鳴るが早いか、選手たちは一斉に走り出した。
   (The moment the starting gun fired, the runners all took off at once.)
5. 犯人は警官の姿を見るが早いか、逃げ出した。
   (The instant the criminal saw the police officer, he ran off.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern was tested (文法テスト4回目 Q15, scan4) alongside confusable distractors like たところで, にもまして, いかんでは, 急ぐべく, and 急いだところが. The key tell for が早いか is that both clauses describe concrete, completed, sequential events happening almost simultaneously — not a hypothetical/futile result (that would be たところで) and not a conditional contingency (いかんでは). Students should confirm the sentence describes two real actions in rapid succession before choosing this pattern.
