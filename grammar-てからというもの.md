# ～てからというもの／というもの {#てからというもの}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～てからというもの／というもの

**Furigana form:** ～てからというもの／というもの

**Meaning:** "Ever since X (things have been dramatically different) / for X (duration), continuously (in a negative state)" — emphasizes a lasting, noticeable change that began at a specific point, or a prolonged period of continuous negative experience.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞て形 + からというもの
- 期間を表す名詞 + というもの (e.g., ここ一週間というもの)
- Example base: 結婚する → 結婚してからというもの

**Typical context:** Formal-to-neutral, usable in both speech and writing; conveys a reflective, often slightly dramatic tone emphasizing how markedly things changed after a turning point, or how persistently a difficult state has continued.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **てから** — plain "since doing X"; the everyday, neutral equivalent for marking a starting point in time, lacking てからというもの's emphatic "and everything changed dramatically since then" flavor.
- [**て以来**](grammar-て以来.md) — "ever since X"; very close in meaning and also fairly formal, largely interchangeable with てからというもの, though て以来 is slightly more neutral/factual while てからというもの carries more emotional emphasis.
- **というもの** (duration variant, e.g., ここ数日というもの) — emphasizes an entire stretch of time has passed with a consistent (often negative) state throughout, a related but distinct usage from the てからというもの "turning point" sense.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; 以前と変わらず ("unchanged from before") would contrast with てからというもの's emphasis on a dramatic, lasting change.

**Example sentences:**
1. 結婚してからというもの、彼はすっかり落ち着いた。 (Ever since getting married, he has settled down completely.)
2. 子供が生まれてからというもの、生活が一変した。 (Ever since our child was born, our life has completely changed.)
3. ここ一週間というもの、ろくに眠れていない。 (For this past week, I haven't been able to sleep properly at all.)
4. 彼女に出会ってからというもの、人生観が変わった。 (Ever since meeting her, my outlook on life has changed.)
5. 退職してからというもの、毎日が自由で充実している。 (Ever since retiring, every day has been free and fulfilling.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** てからというもの always implies a noticeable, lasting change that followed the triggering event — it should be paired with a clause describing that ongoing transformation, not a single isolated incident. The duration-based というもの variant (ここ数日というもの) instead emphasizes an entire stretch of time experienced continuously, often in a negative or trying state — learners should distinguish these two related but distinct usages of というもの.
