# ～次第だ／次第で(は) {#次第だ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～次第だ／～次第で(は)

**Furigana form:** ～しだいだ／～しだいで(は)

**Meaning:** "That's how it turned out / it depends on X" — ～次第だ explains the circumstances or reasoning behind a situation (often at the end of an explanation); ～次第で(は) means "depending on X," indicating that the outcome varies based on a certain factor.

**Conjugation:**
- [explanation of events in plain form] + というわけで、～次第です
- 名詞 + 次第で(は)
- 結果は本人の努力次第だ (The result depends on the person's own effort)
- 天候次第では、イベントは中止になります (Depending on the weather, the event may be cancelled)

**Typical context:** Formal spoken and written Japanese; 次第だ is often used at the end of explanations (business reports, apologies) to summarize "this is the situation/reason"; 次第で(は) is widely used in everyday conditional statements about outcomes depending on a variable.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**によって**](grammar-によって・により.md) — "depending on/by means of X," overlaps with 次第で in conditional-outcome contexts but is more neutral/general.
- [**わけだ**](grammar-わけだ.md) — "that's why/it means that," used for logical conclusions rather than dependency on a variable.
- **次第 (immediate "as soon as")** — a different grammatical function using the same word, meaning "as soon as X" when attached to a verb stem.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 今回の出張は、急な取引先の都合で延期になった次第です。
   (This business trip was postponed due to the client's sudden circumstances — that's the situation.)
2. 合格できるかどうかは、これからの努力次第だ。
   (Whether you can pass depends on your effort from here on.)
3. 旅行が楽しいかどうかは天気次第だ。
   (Whether the trip is fun or not depends on the weather.)
4. 状況次第では、計画を変更する可能性もあります。
   (Depending on the situation, there's a possibility the plan will change.)
5. 彼が来るかどうかは、本人の気分次第らしい。
   (Whether he comes or not apparently depends on his mood.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 次第だ (explaining how things came to be) and 次第で(は) (depending on X) share the same root word but serve different functions — don't confuse them with the earlier "as soon as X" 次第 pattern, which attaches to verb stems rather than nouns and has a completely different meaning (immediacy, not dependency/explanation).
