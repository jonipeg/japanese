# ～っぱなし {#っぱなし}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～っ放し

**Furigana form:** ～っぱなし

**Meaning:** "Leaving X in an ongoing/neglected state" — an action is performed and then left without the expected follow-up or correction, often implying carelessness (つけっぱなし, "left turned on").

**Conjugation:**
- V-ます形(stem) + っぱなし

**Typical context:** Casual/conversational register — notably more colloquial than most of this cluster, very common in everyday speech about forgetfulness or neglect (電気をつけっぱなしにする, "leave the lights on").

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**ずくめ**](grammar-ずくめ.md) — describes something being entirely composed of one quality or category, a different concept (composition) from っぱなし's idea of an action left in a neglected, ongoing state.
- [**まみれ**](grammar-まみれ.md) — describes being covered in a physical substance, a static description rather than っぱなし's sense of an unfinished/neglected action continuing.
- **続ける** — a plain verb meaning "continue doing X" without any negative "left neglected" nuance, in contrast to っぱなし's implication that something SHOULD have been stopped/finished but wasn't.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 弟はいつもテレビをつけっぱなしにして出かける。
   (My little brother always leaves the TV on when he goes out.)
2. ドアを開けっぱなしにしないでください。
   (Please don't leave the door open.)
3. 靴を脱ぎっぱなしにして、玄関が散らかっている。
   (The entryway is messy because shoes are left scattered where they were taken off.)
4. 彼は宿題をやりっぱなしで提出すら忘れていた。
   (He did his homework and then just left it — he even forgot to turn it in.)
5. 水道を出しっぱなしにしていたので、水道代が高くなった。
   (Because I left the faucet running, the water bill went up.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** っぱなし stands out in the N1 extreme-degree cluster as its most casual, everyday member, and students sometimes mistakenly assume all patterns in this cluster share the same formal/literary register — っぱなし is the clear counterexample. The key nuance: っぱなし always implies a NEGLECTFUL or careless failure to follow up on an action (something was started/done and then just abandoned in that state), distinguishing it from plain continuation verbs like 続ける, which carry no such implication of carelessness.
