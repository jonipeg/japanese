# ～とあって {#とあって}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～とあって

**Furigana form:** ～とあって

**Meaning:** "Since it's the special circumstance of X" — explains that, because the situation described is unusual or special, a correspondingly special/notable result naturally followed.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞・普通形 + とあって
- 久しぶりの快晴の連休とあって — "since it was a rare clear-weather holiday"
- 初めて映画の主役を務めるとあって — "since it's [her] first time playing the lead role in a movie"

**Typical context:** Written register — common in news reports, magazine articles, and narrative description of noteworthy public events; rarely used in casual conversation about oneself.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**～こととて**](grammar-こととて.md) — also gives a reason for a special situation, but more old-fashioned/apologetic in tone, often used to excuse a shortcoming.
- **～ため（に）** — a neutral, all-purpose reason marker without the nuance of a special, newsworthy circumstance.
- **～というので** — "since it's said that X," a more conversational way to report a reason based on circumstance.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a plain causal connective like ～ので would instead present an ordinary reason without implying anything special about the situation.

**Example sentences:**
1. 久しぶりの快晴の連休とあって、行楽地はどこも人でいっぱいだった。
   (Since it was a rare sunny holiday after a long spell of bad weather, every tourist spot was packed with people.)
2. その女優は初めて映画の主役を務めるとあって、とても緊張している様子だ。
   (Since it's her first time playing a lead role in a film, that actress seems extremely nervous.)
3. 新聞で店主の絵のことが報道されたとあって、この店に来る客はみんな店に飾られた絵を眺めていく。
   (Since the shop owner's paintings were reported on in the newspaper, every customer who comes to this shop stops to look at the paintings on display.)
4. 10年ぶりの同窓会とあって、会場には懐かしい顔が次々と集まった。
   (Since it was the first class reunion in ten years, familiar faces kept gathering one after another at the venue.)
5. 有名歌手のサイン会とあって、店の前には朝から長い行列ができていた。
   (Since it was a signing event for a famous singer, a long line had formed in front of the store since morning.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern is used to describe third-party or public situations observed by the speaker — it is not natural for describing the speaker's own personal circumstances. The clause that follows must be a result that plausibly and naturally follows from the special situation, not an unrelated or contradictory outcome.
