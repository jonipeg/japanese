# ～にかまけて {#にかまけて}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～に（かまけて）*(usually written in kana; 構けて is non-standard)*

**Furigana form:** ～にかまけて

**Meaning:** "Being preoccupied with X (and neglecting other things)" — describes focusing so much on one task or matter that other responsibilities, often people, are neglected.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + にかまけて
- Example base: 仕事 → 仕事にかまけて

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal, usable in both speech and writing; carries a mildly critical or regretful tone about misplaced priorities, often used in self-reflection or criticism of someone's neglect.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**にかこつけて**](grammar-にかこつけて.md) — "using X as an excuse"; related in surface form, but にかこつけて means using something as a pretext/excuse for a different action, while にかまけて means being genuinely absorbed in X to the neglect of other duties — not necessarily as an excuse.
- **に没頭して** — "being absorbed/immersed in X"; similar "preoccupied" meaning but more neutral/positive, lacking にかまけて's implied criticism about resulting neglect of other duties.
- **をおろそかにして** — "neglecting X"; describes the result (the neglected thing) directly, whereas にかまけて describes the cause (the thing absorbing attention) that leads to that neglect.

**Opposite grammar structures:** バランスよく～する ("doing X in a balanced way") expresses the reverse — attending properly to multiple responsibilities without neglect.

**Example sentences:**
1. 夫は仕事にかまけて、子どもと遊んでやろうとしない。 (My husband is so preoccupied with work that he won't play with the children.)
2. 彼は趣味にかまけて、勉強を疎かにしている。 (He's so absorbed in his hobby that he's neglecting his studies.)
3. 育児にかまけて、自分の健康管理がおろそかになっていた。 (Being so preoccupied with childcare, I had been neglecting my own health.)
4. 彼女は恋愛にかまけて、友人との約束をすっぽかすようになった。 (She became so absorbed in romance that she started standing up her friends.)
5. 目先の仕事にかまけていると、長期的な計画を見失いがちだ。 (If you get too preoccupied with immediate tasks, you tend to lose sight of long-term plans.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** にかまけて always implies a negative consequence of the preoccupation — something else is being neglected as a result — so it should not be used for simple, consequence-free focus (for that, 没頭する or 夢中になる would be more neutral). Learners sometimes confuse it with にかこつけて due to similar sound and shape; remember かまける is about genuine absorption/neglect, while かこつける is about using something as a deliberate pretext.
