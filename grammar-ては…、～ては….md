# ～ては…、～ては… {#ては…、～ては…}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～ては…、～ては…

**Furigana form:** ～ては…、～ては…

**Meaning:** Describes a single action (or short sequence of actions) being repeated over and over, often conveying a sense of persistence, habit, or restless repetition — "keeps doing A, then A again, then A again."

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞て形 + は + （結果・続く行動）、動詞て形 + は + ...（繰り返し）
- 書いては消し、書いては消して — "writes, then erases, writes, then erases, over and over"

**Typical context:** Natural in both spoken and written Japanese when narrating someone's repetitive behavior — common in storytelling, descriptions of habits, and everyday anecdotes.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～たり～たり** — a more general way to list repeated or alternating actions; doesn't require the て+は structure and doesn't necessarily imply the same action repeating identically.
- [**～つ…つ**](grammar-つ…つ.md) — also repeats paired actions, but restricted to a small set of fixed, often opposite-meaning verb pairs and more literary in tone.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; describing a single, one-time action would just use the plain -te form or a simple sentence without the repeated ～ては construction.

**Example sentences:**
1. 書いては消し、書いては消して、手紙を書き上げた。
   (He finished the letter by writing and erasing it, over and over again.)
2. 子供は積み木の家を作っては壊し、作っては壊している。
   (The child keeps building a block house and knocking it down, again and again.)
3. 彼はさっきから時計を見てはそわそわしている。
   (He's been glancing at the clock over and over, fidgeting restlessly.)
4. 彼女はその手紙を読んでは涙を流していた。
   (She kept reading that letter and bursting into tears, again and again.)
5. 母は窓の外を見ては、時計を気にしていた。
   (Mother kept glancing out the window and checking the clock, over and over.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The pattern doesn't always require the exact same verb phrase to literally repeat twice in the sentence — sentence 3 (時計を見てはそわそわしている) shows that a single ～ては clause followed by a stative/habitual description can still convey ongoing repetition. The core meaning is "repeated/habitual action," not a strict two-clause template.
