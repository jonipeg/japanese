# ～にして {#にして}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～にして

**Furigana form:** ～にして

**Meaning:** Indicates that something matches (or fails to match) a high level, special stage, or exceptional condition described by the preceding noun — often translated as "even at/for X" or "only at the stage of X."

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + にして
- 8年目にしてようやく授かった — "it was only in the 8th year that we were finally blessed [with a child]"

**Typical context:** Formal written and spoken Japanese — essays, speeches, and somewhat dramatic or reflective statements. Not used in casual daily conversation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**～ともなると**](grammar-ともなると・ともなれば.md) — similarly marks reaching a notable stage or level, implying "once you get to the level of X," but focuses more on changed circumstances than on a single turning-point moment.
- [**～ならでは**](grammar-ならでは.md) — expresses "something only possible because of X," overlapping with the "special qualification" sense of ～にして used with はじめて (このような天才にしてはじめて書ける, "only a genius like this could write it").
- [**～にあって**](grammar-にあって.md) — expresses "in the special circumstance of X," another formal pattern tied to a particular state or position, though without the "high level" nuance.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a plain statement using even a low-level or ordinary noun (e.g., 初心者でも, "even a beginner can...") would instead downplay rather than emphasize the exceptional nature of the subject.

**Example sentences:**
1. 結婚してすぐに子供が欲しかったが、8年目にしてようやく授かった。
   (We wanted a child right after marrying, but it was only in our 8th year that we were finally blessed with one.)
2. プロの職人にして失敗をするのだ。君がうまくいかなくてもしょうがないだろう。
   (Even a professional craftsman makes mistakes — it's no wonder things aren't going well for you.)
3. この曲はベートーベンのような天才にしてはじめて書ける作品だ。
   (This piece is a work that only a genius like Beethoven could have written.)
4. やれやれ、この父にしてこの息子あり。二人ともとてもよく食べる。
   (Well, well — like father, like son. They both eat an enormous amount.)
5. この分野の権威にして、いまだに新しい発見に驚かされるという。
   (Even as an authority in this field, he says he's still amazed by new discoveries.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern attaches to words that express reaching a high stage or being something special, and the clause that follows must describe either fitting or not fitting that high standard. It's easy to confuse with the simple copula-like use of にして in time expressions, but here the focus is specifically on an exceptional or elevated condition — not a neutral point in time.
