# ～これといって～ない {#これといって～ない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～これと言って～ない

**Furigana form:** ～これといって～ない

**Meaning:** "Nothing in particular / nothing special (to mention)" — denies there being any notable or specific thing worth pointing out.

**Conjugation:**
- これといって + 名詞／形容詞 + ない
- Example base: 不満 → これといって不満はない

**Typical context:** Neutral, usable in both speech and writing; a common way to politely or casually say there's nothing noteworthy to report, often in response to a question.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **特に～ない** — plain "nothing in particular"; the everyday, simpler equivalent, while これといって carries a slightly more deliberate, considered "nothing stands out as worth singling out" nuance.
- **とりたてて～ない** — "nothing worth singling out"; very close in meaning and register, both implying a deliberate scan for anything notable that comes up empty.
- **何も～ない** — plain "nothing at all"; a blanket negative without これといって's implication of having actively considered whether anything stands out.

**Opposite grammar structures:** 特筆すべき／これといった～がある ("there is something noteworthy") expresses the reverse — the existence of a notable point.

**Example sentences:**
1. 今の仕事にこれといって不満はありません。 (I have no particular complaints about my current job.)
2. 最近これといって変わったことはない。 (Nothing in particular has changed recently.)
3. 彼の経歴にはこれといって目立つ点がない。 (There's nothing particularly notable in his career history.)
4. その映画にはこれといった見どころがなかった。 (That movie didn't have any particularly notable highlights.)
5. 休日はこれといって予定もなく、のんびり過ごした。 (I had no particular plans on my day off, so I spent it relaxing.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** これといって always pairs with a negative predicate (ない) — it functions as a set phrase meaning "nothing stands out," not a standalone positive statement. Note the variant これといった directly modifies a following noun (これといった特徴がない, "no particularly distinguishing feature"), while これといって modifies a following adjective/verb phrase — learners should track which form fits the following grammatical slot.
