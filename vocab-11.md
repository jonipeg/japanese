## 覚え (おぼえ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** memory, learning ability

**Similar words:**
- [記憶](vocab-02.md#記憶-きおく)（きおく）— memory; a more general/formal noun.
- 物覚え（ものおぼえ）— memory/retentiveness for learning; emphasizes learning speed.

**Opposite words:**
- 忘却（ぼうきゃく）— forgetting, oblivion

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は覚えが早い。— He's a quick learner (lit. "his memory/learning is fast").
2. 覚えのある光景だった。— It was a scene I had some memory of.
3. 上司の覚えがめでたい。— He's in his boss's good graces (idiomatic).

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The idiom 覚えがめでたい means "to be in someone's good graces/favor," unrelated to literal memory — a common trap in reading comprehension.

## 溺れる (おぼれる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to drown; (figurative) to be absorbed in, indulge in

**Similar words:**
- [沈む](vocab-20.md#沈む-しずむ)（しずむ）— to sink; more neutral, can apply to objects, not just drowning.
- のめり込む（のめりこむ）— to get absorbed/obsessed; figurative "drowning" in something.

**Opposite words:**
- 浮く（うく）— to float

**Example sentences:**
1. 子供が川で溺れかけた。— The child nearly drowned in the river.
2. 彼はギャンブルに溺れてしまった。— He became absorbed (drowned) in gambling.
3. 溺れる者は藁をも掴む。— A drowning man will grasp at straws (proverb).

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Frequently used figuratively for being "drowned in" an addiction or indulgence (酒に溺れる, "to drown in drink"), not only literal drowning in water.

## お参り (おまいり)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a visit (to a shrine/grave), worship

**Similar words:**
- 参拝（さんぱい）— shrine/temple worship visit; more formal term.
- お墓参り（おはかまいり）— specifically visiting a grave.

**Opposite words:**
None notable.

**Example sentences:**
1. 正月に神社へお参りに行く。— We go to visit the shrine for New Year's.
2. 毎年お盆にお墓参りをする。— Every year during Obon, we visit the family grave.
3. お参りを済ませてからおみくじを引いた。— After finishing our worship visit, we drew a fortune slip.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Covers both shrine/temple worship visits and grave visits — a broader everyday term than the more formal/specific 参拝.

## お前 (おまえ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** you (informal/rough)

**Similar words:**
- あなた — you; more neutral/polite.
- きみ（君）— you; informal but softer, often used by men to juniors or romantic partners.

**Opposite words:**
None notable.

**Example sentences:**
1. お前、何してるんだよ。— Hey, what are you doing?
2. お前には関係ないだろ。— This has nothing to do with you.
3. お前がそう言うなら信じるよ。— If you say so, I'll believe you.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Can sound rough, condescending, or even aggressive depending on tone and relationship — generally avoid using it with strangers or superiors; mainly used among close (usually male) friends or in anger/confrontation.

## おまけ
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a bonus, extra; on top of that

**Similar words:**
- 景品（けいひん）— a prize/giveaway, often from a lottery or promotion; more commercial.
- ボーナス — bonus (loanword); usually for salary contexts.

**Opposite words:**
- 本体（ほんたい）— the main item (as opposed to the extra)

**Example sentences:**
1. このお菓子にはおまけのシールが付いている。— This snack comes with a bonus sticker.
2. おまけに雨まで降ってきた。— On top of that, it even started raining.
3. 店員さんがおまけしてくれた。— The shop clerk gave me a discount/extra as a bonus.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Functions as a noun (a free extra item), a verb おまけする (to give a discount/extra), and a conjunction-like adverb おまけに (often introducing a negative addition) — three distinct uses to keep straight.

## おまたせしました
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** Sorry to keep you waiting

**Similar words:**
- お待たせいたしました — a more formal/humble version.
- ごめん、待った？ — casual, "sorry, did you wait?"

**Opposite words:**
None notable.

**Example sentences:**
1. お待たせしました、こちらへどうぞ。— Sorry to keep you waiting, this way please.
2. 「お待たせしました」と店員が料理を運んできた。— Saying "sorry for the wait," the server brought the food.
3. 大変お待たせしました、今から始めます。— Sorry for the long wait, we'll begin now.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** A fixed set phrase said when returning to someone who has been waiting (serving food, answering a call) — not used for apologizing about future/ongoing waits.

## おまちください
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** Please wait

**Similar words:**
- 少々お待ちください — fuller polite phrase, "please wait a moment."
- 待ってください — plain polite form; less formal/businesslike.

**Opposite words:**
None notable.

**Example sentences:**
1. 少々お待ちください、すぐに確認いたします。— Please wait a moment, I'll check right away.
2. こちらでおまちください。— Please wait here.
3. ただいま担当者が参りますので、お待ちください。— The person in charge will be with you shortly, please wait.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** A polite imperative form (お＋verb stem＋ください) commonly used in service/business settings, more formal than the plain 待ってください used in casual speech.

## おまちどおさま
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** Sorry to have kept you waiting

**Similar words:**
- お待たせしました — the more standard/common equivalent phrase.
- お待ちどおさまでした — fuller, slightly more formal version.

**Opposite words:**
None notable.

**Example sentences:**
1. 「おまちどおさま」と言ってラーメンを出した。— Saying "sorry for the wait," they served the ramen.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** A slightly old-fashioned/casual variant of お待たせしました, often heard from shopkeepers or at traditional eateries — less common in modern formal business contexts.

## お宮 (おみや)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a Shinto shrine

**Similar words:**
- 神社（じんじゃ）— Shinto shrine; the standard/formal term.
- 神宮（じんぐう）— a grand shrine (e.g. Ise Jingu); reserved for especially prestigious shrines.

**Opposite words:**
- お寺（おてら）— Buddhist temple

**Example sentences:**
1. 近くのお宮にお参りした。— I visited the nearby shrine to pray.
2. お宮参りは赤ちゃんの健やかな成長を祈る行事だ。— The shrine visit (omiyamairi) is an event praying for a baby's healthy growth.
3. 村の鎮守のお宮に集まった。— Villagers gathered at the local guardian shrine.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** A more colloquial/affectionate term for 神社, frequently seen in the set phrase お宮参り (a baby's first shrine visit) — don't confuse with お寺 (temple), which is Buddhist, not Shinto.

## 目出度い (おめでたい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** happy, auspicious; (ironic) naive

**Similar words:**
- 喜ばしい（よろこばしい）— joyous/delightful; a more neutral formal register.
- [めでたい](vocab-34.md#めでたい-めでたい) — the same word, commonly written without kanji.

**Opposite words:**
- 不吉な（ふきつな）— ominous, inauspicious

**Example sentences:**
1. ご結婚、おめでたいことですね。— Your marriage is such a happy occasion.
2. おめでたい話が続いている。— Good news (happy occasions) keeps happening.
3. そんなに人を信じるなんて、君もおめでたいね。— Trusting people that much, you're pretty naive, aren't you.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Besides its literal "auspicious/happy" meaning, it's commonly used sarcastically to mean "naively optimistic" or "gullible" — context determines sincere vs. mocking tone.

## おめでとう(ございます)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** Congratulations!

**Similar words:**
- おめでとうございます — the full polite form.
- お祝い申し上げます（おいわいもうしあげます）— very formal written congratulations.

**Opposite words:**
- お悔やみ申し上げます（おくやみもうしあげます）— condolences (opposite occasion)

**Example sentences:**
1. ご卒業おめでとうございます。— Congratulations on your graduation.

3. 新年あけましておめでとうございます。— Happy New Year!

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ございます makes it formal/polite (for teachers, acquaintances); dropping it (just おめでとう) is for close friends and family — the appropriate register depends on the relationship.

## お目に掛かる (おめにかかる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to meet (humble)

**Similar words:**
- お会いする（おあいする）— humble "to meet"; slightly less formal than お目にかかる.
- 会う（あう）— plain form, "to meet."

**Opposite words:**
None notable.

**Example sentences:**
1. 初めてお目にかかります。— It's a pleasure to meet you for the first time (very polite).
2. またお目にかかれる日を楽しみにしております。— I look forward to the day we can meet again.
3. 先生にお目にかかれて光栄です。— It's an honor to meet you, teacher.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Humble (謙譲語) language used to lower oneself when describing meeting someone of higher status — never use it to describe someone else meeting a third party; it refers to the speaker's own action.

## 思い掛けない (おもいがけない)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** unexpected

**Similar words:**
- 予想外（よそうがい）— unexpected, outside prediction; more neutral/formal.
- 意外な（いがいな）— surprising/unexpected; a common everyday synonym.

**Opposite words:**
- 予想通り（よそうどおり）— as expected

**Example sentences:**
1. 思い掛けないプレゼントをもらった。— I received an unexpected present.
2. 思い掛けない事故に遭った。— I had an unexpected accident.
3. 彼から思い掛けない返事が来た。— I got an unexpected reply from him.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Often directly modifies nouns (思いがけない出来事, "an unexpected event") and is slightly more literary than 意外な, which is more common in casual speech.

## 思い込む (おもいこむ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to be convinced of, to assume (often wrongly)

**Similar words:**
- 勘違いする（かんちがいする）— to misunderstand/mistake; implies the assumption turns out wrong.
- 信じ込む（しんじこむ）— to believe wholeheartedly; similar strong-conviction nuance.

**Opposite words:**
- [疑う](vocab-08.md#疑う-うたがう)（うたがう）— to doubt

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は自分が正しいと思い込んでいる。— He's convinced that he's right.
2. 勝手に思い込んで行動しないでください。— Please don't act on your own unfounded assumptions.
3. 子供の頃からそう思い込んでいた。— I had been convinced of that since childhood.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Implies a firm, often mistaken or one-sided belief/assumption (not just a casual thought), frequently carrying a slightly negative nuance of being wrong or stubborn.

## 思い付き (おもいつき)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** an idea, a notion (often spur-of-the-moment)

**Similar words:**
- アイデア — idea (loanword); more neutral/positive.
- ひらめき — a flash of inspiration; more positive/creative connotation.

**Opposite words:**
- 熟考（じゅっこう）— careful deliberation

**Example sentences:**
1. それはただの思い付きにすぎない。— That's nothing more than a passing notion.
2. 思い付きで行動するのは危険だ。— Acting on a whim is dangerous.
3. 彼の思い付きが意外とうまくいった。— His spur-of-the-moment idea unexpectedly worked out.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Often carries a nuance of being a sudden, unconsidered idea (sometimes dismissively) — contrast with ひらめき, which has a more positive "flash of genius" connotation.

**Similar words:**

## 思い付く (おもいつく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to think of, to hit upon (an idea)

**Similar words:**
- ひらめく — to flash upon one, a sudden insight; more sudden/inspired.
- 考えつく（かんがえつく）— to come up with an idea; near-synonym, slightly more deliberate.

**Opposite words:**
- 忘れる（わすれる）— to forget

**Example sentences:**
1. いいアイデアを思い付いた。— I came up with a good idea.
2. 名前が思い付かない。— I can't think of a name.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The verb form corresponding to the noun 思い付き — describes the moment an idea comes to mind, distinct from 思い出す (to recall/remember something already known).

#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** heavy

**Similar words:**
- 重い（おもい）— heavy; the more standard/common form.
- ヘビー — heavy (loanword); used more figuratively, e.g. a "heavy" topic.

**Opposite words:**
- 軽い（かるい）— light

## 主(な/に) (おも(な/に))
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** main, principal; mainly

**Similar words:**
- 主要な（しゅような）— main/key; more formal (often for topics, cities, factors).
- 中心的な（ちゅうしんてきな）— central, core; emphasizes being the core element.

**Opposite words:**
- 副次的な（ふくじてきな）— secondary

**Example sentences:**
1. この地域の主な産業は農業だ。— The main industry of this region is agriculture.
2. 主に魚を食べて生活している。— I mainly live on a diet of fish.
3. 原因は主に二つ考えられる。— Two main causes can be considered.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 主な (na-adjective, modifying nouns) and 主に (adverb, modifying verbs/clauses) share kanji but differ grammatically depending on whether it precedes a noun or not.

## 趣 (おもむき)
#vocabulary

- 風情（ふぜい）— tasteful atmosphere/charm; very similar nuance, often for traditional/aesthetic scenes.
- 雰囲気（ふんいき）— atmosphere/mood; more general and neutral.

## 思わず (おもわず)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** unintentionally, instinctively, before one realizes

**Similar words:**
- つい — unintentionally, before one realizes; more casual, often for habitual slips.
- 反射的に（はんしゃてきに）— reflexively; more about instinctive physical reaction.

**Opposite words:**
- 意図的に（いとてきに）— intentionally

## 親父 (おやじ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** dad, old man; (one's own) father in rough/familiar speech

**Similar words:**
- [父](vocab-27.md#父-ちち)（ちち）— the neutral, formal word for "father," used when speaking to outsiders.
- お父さん（おとうさん）— the standard polite term, used both to address and refer to one's father.
- おやじさん — can refer respectfully to someone else's father or an older male shopkeeper, softer than 親父.

**Opposite words:**
- [お袋](vocab-10.md#お袋-おふくろ)（おふくろ）— the male-speech counterpart for "mom"

**Example sentences:**
1. 親父は毎朝六時に起きる。— My old man gets up at six every morning.
2. あの店の親父さん、いつも元気だね。— That shop's owner (old man) is always energetic, isn't he?
3. 親父に相談してから決めるよ。— I'll decide after talking it over with my dad.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 親父 is casual, male-coded speech — mainly used by men referring to their own father, or to mean "old man" generically (including strangers). Using it to refer to someone else's father directly to their face can sound rude unless softened with さん; in polite contexts use お父さん or 父 instead.

## おやすみ(なさい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** good night (said when going to bed or parting at night)

**Similar words:**
- お休みになる — the honorific/polite form used for someone else "resting" or "sleeping."
- さようなら — general "goodbye," not time-specific, used for departures rather than bedtime.

**Opposite words:**
- [おはよう](vocab-10.md#おはようございます)（ございます）— good morning

**Example sentences:**
1. もう遅いから、おやすみなさい。— It's late already, so good night.
2. 子供に「おやすみ」と言って電気を消した。— I said "good night" to the child and turned off the light.
3. おやすみなさい、また明日。— Good night, see you tomorrow.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** おやすみ (without なさい) is casual, used with close friends or family; おやすみなさい is the polite form appropriate for most situations. Unlike English "good night," it's rarely used as a general farewell during the day.

## おやつ
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a snack (typically an afternoon snack)

**Similar words:**
- 間食（かんしょく）— a more formal/clinical term for "snacking between meals," often used in dietary contexts.
- スナック — refers more specifically to packaged/salty snack foods (chips, etc.), a narrower loanword sense.

**Example sentences:**
1. 三時のおやつにクッキーを食べた。— I ate cookies as my 3 o'clock snack.
2. 子供たちはおやつの時間を楽しみにしている。— The children are looking forward to snack time.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ガイド can refer to both a person (tour guide) and a publication/app (guide/manual) — context determines which; it's distinct from 案内 which more often refers to the act/service of guiding rather than the guide itself.
