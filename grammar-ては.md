# ～ては (repeated action) {#ては}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ては

**Furigana form:** ～ては

**Meaning:** Expresses a repeated or habitual action-reaction cycle — "does X, and then (repeatedly) Y happens" — often used in doubled form (Aては B、Bては A) to depict an endless back-and-forth or iterative process.

**Conjugation:**
- Verb-て form + は (contraction often pronounced/written じゃ in casual speech: 食べては → 食べちゃ)
- Frequently doubled: 降っては止み、止んでは降る (raining, then stopping, then raining again).

**Typical context:** Used in both spoken and written Japanese; register-neutral. Very natural in casual conversation describing repetitive/habitual behavior (a child's routine, weather patterns, trial-and-error processes), and also appears in more literary descriptions of cyclical phenomena.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**たびに**](grammar-たびに.md) — "every time X happens, Y happens"; describes a reliable correlation between two events, but without the cyclical "doing and undoing" nuance of doubled ては.
- **ごとに** — "each time/every," similar to たびに but used more for regular intervals (三日ごとに); lacks the vivid repeated-reaction nuance.
- **ては + 消える / 繰り返す type patterns** — simple repetition without the back-and-forth doubling.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; conceptually contrasts with ～きり ("and that was the only time," a single non-repeated event).

**Example sentences:**
1. 波が寄せては返す。
   (The waves come in and then recede, over and over.)
2. 彼は書いては消し、書いては消ししながら、原稿を仕上げた。
   (He wrote and erased, wrote and erased, finishing the manuscript.)
3. 子どもはおもちゃを並べては崩し、並べては崩ししている。
   (The child keeps lining up the toys and then knocking them down, again and again.)
4. 空は晴れては曇り、曇っては晴れるを繰り返していた。
   (The sky kept alternating between clearing up and clouding over.)
5. 彼女は転んでは立ち上がり、転んでは立ち上がりしながら、スキーを練習した。
   (She fell and got back up, fell and got back up, as she practiced skiing.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Students sometimes treat ては as a simple sequential "and then" (like てから), missing the iterative/cyclical nuance that is strongest when the pattern is doubled (Aては B、Bては A). Watch for contexts describing an ongoing, repeated, often futile or exhausting cycle — that is the signal this construction is intended, not a one-time sequence of events.
