# ～めく {#めく}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～めく

**Furigana form:** ～めく

**Meaning:** "Has a touch of X / looks/feels like X" — a verb-forming suffix attached to nouns to indicate that something takes on the character or appearance of that noun, without fully being it.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + めく → 五段動詞化 (becomes a godan verb; conjugates like 咲く)
- Example base: 春 → 春めく／春めいてきた

**Typical context:** Formal, written/literary register; evokes subtle, poetic transformation, common in descriptive nature writing (seasons, atmosphere) and literary prose. Rare in casual speech.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**っぽい**](grammar-っぽい.md) — "seems/looks like X"; the everyday, casual equivalent conveying a similar "has a quality of X" sense, but far more colloquial and usable with a wider range of nouns/adjectives, while めく is restricted to a small literary set (春めく, 皮肉めく, 謎めく).
- **がかる** — "tinged with X" (e.g., 赤みがかった, "reddish"); similarly a suffix indicating a partial quality, often used for colors, with a similarly subtle, descriptive nuance.
- **らしい** — "seems like X / typical of X"; broader and more common, expressing an inference or typical characteristic rather than めく's poetic sense of visibly "taking on" a quality.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; まさに～そのものだ ("is precisely/entirely X") would contrast with めく's partial, suggestive transformation.

**Example sentences:**
1. 少しずつ春めいてきましたね。 (It's gradually starting to feel like spring, isn't it.)
2. 彼の言葉には皮肉めいた響きがあった。 (His words carried a tone tinged with irony.)
3. 森の奥から謎めいた物音が聞こえてきた。 (A mysterious-sounding noise came from deep in the forest.)
4. その城は古びて、どこか神秘めいた雰囲気を漂わせていた。 (That castle, aged with time, gave off a somewhat mystical atmosphere.)
5. 彼女の口調には説教めいたところがあった。 (There was something sermon-like in her tone.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** めく is a closed, literary class of words (春めく, 皮肉めく, 謎めく, 色めく) — it is not productively attachable to any noun the way っぽい is, so learners should treat it as a small set of fixed vocabulary items rather than a freely generative grammar pattern. It conjugates as an ordinary godan verb (めく、めいて、めいた), which sometimes surprises learners expecting an adjectival suffix instead.
