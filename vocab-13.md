## 下降 (かこう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** descent, decline, falling (altitude, numbers, trend)

**Similar words:**
- 低下（ていか）— "decline/deterioration," often used for a drop in quality, level, or ability, overlapping with one sense of 下降.
- [減少](vocab-02.md#減少-げんしょう)（げんしょう）— "decrease," specifically about a reduction in quantity/number, whereas 下降 is broader and also used for physical descent (altitude, direction).
- [落下](vocab-33.md#落下-らっか)（らっか）— "falling, dropping," implying a more sudden or physical fall than the gradual trend implied by 下降.

**Opposite words:**
- 上昇（じょうしょう）— rise, ascent

**Example sentences:**
1. 飛行機は徐々に高度を下降させた。— The airplane gradually descended in altitude.
2. 株価が下降傾向にある。— Stock prices are on a downward trend.
3. 気温が下降して肌寒くなってきた。— The temperature dropped and it's gotten chilly.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 下降 is used for both literal physical descent (an airplane descending) and figurative decline (a downward trend in prices or statistics) — its direct antonym 上昇 (rise) pairs with it in nearly all these contexts, making the pair useful to learn together.

## 化合 (かごう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** chemical combination, chemical bonding

**Similar words:**
- [結合](vocab-17.md#結合する-けつごう)（けつごう）— "bonding/combination" in a broader sense (including chemical bonds but also other kinds of joining), while 化合 specifically denotes chemical reaction producing a new compound.
- 混合（こんごう）— "mixing," which combines substances without a chemical reaction forming a new compound, unlike 化合.
- 化学反応（かがくはんのう）— "chemical reaction," the broader process that includes 化合 as one possible type of outcome.

**Opposite words:**
- 分解（ぶんかい）— decomposition, breaking down

**Example sentences:**
1. 水素と酸素が化合すると水になる。— When hydrogen and oxygen chemically combine, water is formed.
2. この物質は他の元素と化合しやすい。— This substance readily combines chemically with other elements.
3. 化合物の性質について学んだ。— I learned about the properties of chemical compounds.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 化合 specifically implies a chemical reaction creating a new substance (化合物, compound) — don't confuse it with 混合 (physical mixing without a new substance forming) or general 結合 (bonding/joining in a non-chemical sense).

## 囲む (かこむ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to surround, to encircle

**Similar words:**
- [包む](vocab-28.md#包む-つつむ)（つつむ）— "to wrap," enclosing something fully (like wrapping paper), different from 囲む which implies forming a perimeter around something that may remain open on top.
- 取り囲む（とりかこむ）— an intensified form of 囲む, often used for a crowd densely surrounding someone/something.
- 巡る（めぐる）— "to go around, circle," implying movement around a perimeter rather than a static enclosure.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 庭は高い塀で囲まれている。— The garden is surrounded by a tall wall.
2. 記者たちが有名人を囲んで質問した。— Reporters surrounded the celebrity and asked questions.
3. 家族で食卓を囲んで食事をした。— The family gathered around the table to eat together.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 囲む has a warm, communal figurative use in 食卓を囲む ("to gather around the table," implying family togetherness) — this idiomatic usage goes beyond the literal "surround" and is frequently seen in descriptions of shared meals.

## 貸し (かし)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** lending, a loan; a favor owed to you

**Similar words:**
- [借り](vocab-14.md#借り-かり)（かり）— the exact opposite, "a debt/favor owed by you to someone else" — these two words form a crucial pair.
- [融資](vocab-35.md#融資-ゆうし)（ゆうし）— "financing/loan," a formal financial/banking term, more specific than the general 貸し.
- [恩](vocab-11.md#恩-おん)（おん）— "a debt of gratitude/favor," overlapping with the figurative sense of 貸し when it means "I did you a favor" (貸しがある, to be owed a favor).

**Opposite words:**
- [借り](vocab-14.md#借り-かり)（かり）— a debt, something borrowed

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼には以前から貸しがある。— I've had a favor owed to me by him for a while.
2. 銀行からの貸し出し業務を行っている。— The bank conducts lending operations.
3. 「これで貸し一つだからね」と冗談で言った。— I joked, "Now you owe me one for this."

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 貸し and 借り are a mirror pair (lending vs. borrowing) both literally (money) and figuratively (favors) — 貸しがある ("to be owed a favor by someone") is a common idiomatic expression worth memorizing as a set phrase.

## 火事 (かじ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a fire (destructive, e.g. building fire)

**Similar words:**
- [火災](vocab-06.md#火災-かさい)（かさい）— a more formal/official synonym for "fire," often used in news reports, insurance, and legal contexts, whereas 火事 is the everyday spoken word.
- 炎上（えんじょう）— "going up in flames," can be literal but is now very commonly used figuratively for something "blowing up" controversially online.
- 山火事（やまかじ）— a wildfire, a specific compound combining 山 (mountain) with 火事.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 近所で火事があった。— There was a fire in the neighborhood.
2. 火事だ！早く逃げて！— Fire! Get out quickly!
3. 火の不始末が火事の原因になることが多い。— Careless handling of fire is often the cause of fires.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 火事 is the common spoken/everyday word for a fire, while 火災 is its more formal written counterpart used in official reports and statistics — both mean the same destructive fire, differing mainly in register.

## 賢い (かしこい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** wise, clever, smart

**Similar words:**
- 頭がいい（あたまがいい）— "smart/intelligent," a very common casual phrase overlapping heavily with 賢い but slightly more about raw intellect/academic smarts.
- [利口](vocab-33.md#利口-りこう)（りこう）— "clever, shrewd," often used for animals or children being well-behaved and quick-witted, sometimes with a slightly more superficial nuance than 賢い.
- 聡明（そうめい）— "bright, astute," a more literary/formal word praising someone's sharp intellect and good judgment.

**Opposite words:**
- [愚か](vocab-11.md#愚か-おろか)（おろか）— foolish, stupid
- 馬鹿（ばか）— stupid, foolish

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女はとても賢い子供だった。— She was a very clever child.
2. 賢い消費者は価格だけで判断しない。— A wise consumer doesn't judge based on price alone.
3. この犬は賢くてすぐに芸を覚える。— This dog is smart and quickly learns tricks.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 賢い implies good judgment and practical wisdom, not just raw intelligence (頭がいい can mean "smart" in a purely academic/IQ sense) — 賢い choices or 賢い消費者 emphasizes sound decision-making ability.

## 貸し出し (かしだし)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** lending, checking out (e.g. library books), an advance

**Similar words:**
- [貸す](#貸す-かす)（かす）— the basic verb "to lend"; 貸し出し is the noun form, often used formally for institutional lending (library books, bank loans).
- レンタル— "rental," a loanword typically implying a paid lending service (DVD rental, car rental), distinct from 貸し出し, which can be free (e.g. a library).
- [融資](vocab-35.md#融資-ゆうし)（ゆうし）— "financing/loan" specifically in a banking context, a narrower financial subtype of 貸し出し.

**Opposite words:**
- 返却（へんきゃく）— returning (something borrowed)

**Example sentences:**
1. この図書館は本の貸し出しを行っている。— This library lends out books.
2. 貸し出し期間は二週間です。— The lending period is two weeks.
3. 銀行からの貸し出しが増加している。— Bank lending has been increasing.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 貸し出し is the standard term seen on library signs (貸出中, currently checked out) and in banking contexts (融資/貸出) — it's more institutional/formal than casually saying 貸す, "to lend."

## 過失 (かしつ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** an error, a mistake, negligence (often in a legal sense)

**Similar words:**
- [間違い](#間違い-かんちがい)（まちがい）— "mistake" in general everyday use, much more casual than the formal/legal term 過失.
- 不注意（ふちゅうい）— "carelessness," describing the cause behind a 過失, rather than the legal/formal term for the fault itself.
- 故意（こい）— "intent," the direct legal opposite of 過失 — acting deliberately rather than carelessly.

**Opposite words:**
- 故意（こい）— intentional act

**Example sentences:**
1. 事故の原因は運転手の過失だった。— The cause of the accident was the driver's negligence.
2. 業務上過失致死罪に問われた。— He was charged with professional negligence resulting in death.
3. 過失を認めて謝罪した。— He admitted his fault and apologized.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 過失 is a key legal term contrasted with 故意 (intentional wrongdoing) — in law, whether an act was 過失 (negligent/unintentional) or 故意 (deliberate) significantly changes the nature of liability, so this pairing is important for formal/legal contexts.

## 果実 (かじつ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** fruit; a profit, the fruits (of one's labor)

**Similar words:**
- 果物（くだもの）— the everyday word for "fruit" (as food); 果実 is more formal/botanical and also extends figuratively to "the fruits of effort" (努力の果実).
- 成果（せいか）— "results/achievements," the figurative sense that overlaps with 果実's metaphorical use (成果を得る vs. 努力の果実を得る).
- [実](vocab-21.md#実-じつ)（み）— "a fruit/berry" in a simple, concrete sense, the base word from which 果実 is formed.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. この木は秋になると果実をつける。— This tree bears fruit in autumn.
2. 長年の努力の果実をようやく手にした。— I finally reaped the fruits of years of effort.
3. 果実酒を手作りするのが趣味だ。— Making homemade fruit liqueur is my hobby.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 果実 is more formal/written than 果物 and is the word used in figurative expressions (努力の果実, the fruit of one's efforts) — in a grocery store or casual conversation, 果物 is the natural choice, not 果実.

## 歌手 (かしゅ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a singer

**Similar words:**
- シンガー— a loanword equivalent for "singer," often used in more casual/pop-music contexts (シンガーソングライター, singer-songwriter).
- 声楽家（せいがくか）— "vocalist" in the classical/opera sense, a more formal/specialized term than general 歌手.
- アーティスト— "artist," a broader term covering musicians generally, not specifically singers.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女は国民的な人気歌手だ。— She is a nationally popular singer.
2. 彼は歌手としてデビューした。— He debuted as a singer.
3. この歌手の新曲が話題になっている。— This singer's new song is creating buzz.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 歌手 specifically refers to someone whose profession is singing — it's not used for someone who merely enjoys singing as a hobby, where 歌うのが好きな人 would be more appropriate.

## 貸[し]間 (かしま)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a room for rent, a rented room

**Similar words:**
- 貸家（かしや）— a whole house for rent, as opposed to 貸間 which specifically refers to a single rented room (often within someone's home).
- アパート— "apartment," a self-contained rental unit, distinct from 貸間 which can mean just a room within a larger household.
- 下宿（げしゅく）— a boarding house / lodging arrangement, often including meals, a related but more specific living arrangement than simply 貸間.

**Opposite words:**
- 持ち家（もちいえ）— one's own owned house

**Example sentences:**
1. 大学の近くに貸間を見つけた。— I found a room for rent near the university.
2. この家では二階の部屋を貸間にしている。— In this house, the upstairs room is rented out.
3. 貸間探しに不動産屋を回った。— I went around real estate agencies looking for a room to rent.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 貸間 is a somewhat old-fashioned term for a single room rented out (often within a private home), distinct from 貸家 (a whole rental house) or アパート (a self-contained rental apartment) — in modern usage, more specific terms like アパート or 賃貸 are far more common.

## 箇所 (かしょ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a place, a spot, a passage (in text)

**Similar words:**
- 場所（ばしょ）— the general, everyday word for "place/location"; 箇所 is more formal/technical and often used to count specific points or sections (三箇所, three locations/spots) within a larger whole.
- 部分（ぶぶん）— "a part/section," more about a portion of a whole, which can overlap with 箇所 when referring to a part of a text or document.
- [地点](vocab-27.md#地点-ちてん)（ちてん）— "a point (on a map/route)," a precise geographic location, narrower than the general 箇所.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. この文章には間違いが三箇所ある。— There are three places with mistakes in this text.
2. 修理が必要な箇所を確認した。— I checked the spots that need repair.
3. 気になる箇所に線を引いておいた。— I underlined the passages that caught my attention.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 箇所 doubles as both a standalone noun ("a place/spot") and a counter suffix (三箇所, three places) — it's especially common when referring to specific points within a document, building, or road that need attention or repair.

## 過剰 (かじょう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** surplus, excess, overabundance

**Similar words:**
- [余分](vocab-33.md#余分-よぶん)（よぶん）— "extra, surplus," a more everyday/casual word than the formal 過剰, often used for a small, simple excess amount.
- 過多（かた）— "excess/too much," a formal synonym very close to 過剰, sometimes interchangeable (人口過多 = population excess).
- 過度（かど）— "excessive, to an extreme degree," emphasizing the degree/intensity of something being too much, often paired with abstract actions (過度の運動, excessive exercise).

**Opposite words:**
- 不足（ふそく）— shortage, insufficiency

**Example sentences:**
1. 在庫が過剰になっている。— Inventory has become excessive.
2. 過剰な期待はしない方がいい。— It's better not to have excessive expectations.
3. 彼は過剰反応してしまった。— He overreacted.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 過剰 commonly appears in the compound 過剰反応 (overreaction) and in business contexts (在庫過剰, excess inventory) — as a na-adjective it modifies nouns with な (過剰な期待) just like other na-adjectives, a point learners sometimes get wrong by using の or の instead.

## 頭 (かしら)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** the head; a leader, chief

**Similar words:**
- [頭](#頭-かしら)（あたま）— the much more common reading of this same kanji, meaning "head" in the everyday physical sense; かしら is an older/more specialized reading, often meaning "leader, boss" (especially of a group like firefighters or craftsmen).
- リーダー— the loanword equivalent for "leader," more modern/casual than かしら.
- 親分（おやぶん）— "boss, chief" especially in the context of gangs or close-knit groups, a related but more colloquial/underworld-flavored term.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 大工のかしらが現場を指揮していた。— The master carpenter was directing the work site.
2. 彼は一座のかしらとして皆をまとめている。— He leads the troupe as its head.
3. 「〜かしら」という表現は女性らしい言い回しとしても使われる。— The expression "~kashira" is also used as a feminine-sounding way of wondering aloud.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Beyond meaning "leader/chief" (as in 頭/かしら of a group of workers), かしら is also a common sentence-ending particle expressing wondering/uncertainty, especially in feminine speech ("どうしようかしら", "I wonder what to do") — don't confuse the noun with this grammatically unrelated particle usage.

## かじる (かじる)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to gnaw, to nibble, to bite off (a piece)

**Similar words:**
- 噛む（かむ）— the general verb "to bite/chew," broader than かじる, which specifically implies biting off small pieces (as a rodent gnaws, or biting into an apple).
- 齧る（かじる）— the kanji form of this same word, rarely used in writing since it's an uncommon kanji.
- 食いつく（くいつく）— "to bite into/sink teeth into," implying more aggressive or eager biting than the more delicate かじる.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. ネズミが壁をかじっていた。— A mouse was gnawing at the wall.
2. りんごをそのままかじって食べた。— I bit into and ate the apple as it was.
3. 彼は英語を少しかじった程度だ。— He's only dabbled a little in English (figuratively "nibbled at" it).

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** かじる has a common figurative extension meaning "to dabble in, to have only superficial knowledge of" (少しかじった程度, only scratched the surface) — this metaphorical sense (like nibbling rather than fully consuming a subject) is frequently tested at N2.

## 貸す (かす)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to lend, to loan, to hire out

**Similar words:**
- [借りる](vocab-14.md#借りる-かりる)（かりる）— the direct opposite, "to borrow"; 貸す/借りる is a classic verb pair learners must keep straight (who gives vs. who receives).
- 貸し出す（かしだす）— a slightly more formal/institutional version of 貸す, often used for libraries or businesses.
- レンタルする— "to rent out," a loanword equivalent typically implying a paid commercial transaction.

**Opposite words:**
- [借りる](vocab-14.md#借りる-かりる)（かりる）— to borrow

**Example sentences:**
1. 友達に本を貸した。— I lent a book to my friend.
2. 彼は自分の部屋を学生に貸している。— He rents out his room to students.
3. ちょっと傘を貸してもらえますか。— Could you lend me an umbrella for a bit?

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 貸す (to lend, the giver's perspective) and 借りる (to borrow, the receiver's perspective) are frequently confused by learners because English "lend/borrow" can sometimes blur in casual speech — in Japanese they are never interchangeable; the direction of giving/receiving determines which verb to use.

## 微か (かすか)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** faint, dim, slight (barely perceptible)

**Similar words:**
- かすかに（かすかに）— the adverbial form, "faintly," used to modify verbs (かすかに聞こえる, to be faintly audible).
- [わずか](vocab-36.md#わずか)（わずか）— "slight, a mere small amount," often used quantitatively (a small number/amount) rather than describing perceptual faintness.
- ほのか（ほのか）— "faint, subtle," very close in meaning to 微か, often used for soft light or delicate scent, with a slightly more poetic/gentle connotation.

**Opposite words:**
- 明確（めいかく）— clear, distinct
- [はっきり](vocab-31.md#はっきり-はっきり)（はっきり）— clearly, distinctly

**Example sentences:**
1. 遠くからかすかな音が聞こえた。— A faint sound could be heard from afar.
2. 彼の顔にかすかな笑みが浮かんだ。— A faint smile appeared on his face.
3. 微かな記憶しか残っていない。— Only a faint memory remains.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 微か describes something at the very threshold of perception (barely visible, audible, or felt) — it's used for sensory or emotional subtlety, not for describing a small quantity in a countable sense, where わずか would be more appropriate.

## 霞む (かすむ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to be hazy, to become dim, to blur

**Similar words:**
- ぼやける（ぼやける）— "to become blurry/out of focus," often used for vision or images, with a similar hazy nuance to 霞む.
- 曇る（くもる）— "to become cloudy," specifically about weather or a surface (like glass) fogging up, a narrower physical phenomenon.
- かすかになる— "to become faint," related conceptually (both involve reduced clarity) but かすむ specifically refers to visual haziness, often of landscape or eyesight.

**Opposite words:**
- 澄む（すむ）— to become clear

**Example sentences:**
1. 春になると山が霞んで見える。— In spring, the mountains look hazy.
2. 涙で目が霞んでよく見えなかった。— My eyes blurred with tears and I couldn't see well.
3. 遠くの景色が霞んでいた。— The distant scenery was hazy.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 霞む is strongly associated with 春霞 (spring haze), a classic seasonal image in Japanese poetry and weather reports — it also extends to eyesight blurring (目が霞む), so don't assume it's only about outdoor atmospheric haze.

## 活用 (かつよう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** conjugation (grammar); practical application, making good use of

**Similar words:**
- [利用](vocab-33.md#利用-りよう)（りよう）— "use/utilization," a more neutral, general term for using something; 活用 implies making the most effective or practical use of a resource, often skillfully.
- [応用](vocab-09.md#応用-おうよう)（おうよう）— "application," specifically applying a principle or knowledge to a new situation, a more intellectual/technical sense than general 活用.
- 動詞の活用（どうしのかつよう）— the specific grammatical meaning, "verb conjugation," a distinct technical sense from the general "utilization" meaning.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. この制度をうまく活用すれば便利だ。— If you make good use of this system, it's convenient.
2. 日本語の動詞の活用を覚えるのは大変だ。— Memorizing Japanese verb conjugations is tough.
3. 彼女は自分の経験を仕事に活用している。— She makes practical use of her experience in her work.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 活用 has two quite distinct meanings that must be disambiguated by context: the grammar term "conjugation" (動詞の活用) versus the general sense of "putting something to good practical use" (資源を活用する) — don't confuse the two.

## 活力 (かつりょく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** vitality, energy, vigor

**Similar words:**
- [元気](vocab-18.md#元気-げんき)（げんき）— "energy/good health," the everyday casual word; 活力 is more formal/abstract, often describing vigor in a broader sense (社会に活力を与える, to give vitality to society).
- [エネルギー](vocab-09.md#エネルギー-エネルギー)— "energy," a loanword used both physically (physics) and figuratively, overlapping with 活力 in the figurative sense.
- 生気（せいき）— "life force, liveliness," a more literary synonym close to 活力.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 若者たちの活力が町を元気にしている。— The vitality of young people is energizing the town.
2. 運動は心身に活力を与える。— Exercise gives vitality to mind and body.
3. 経済に活力を取り戻す必要がある。— It's necessary to restore vitality to the economy.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 活力 is often used abstractly to describe vigor in a system, economy, or society (経済の活力, 地域の活力), not just an individual person's physical energy — this broader, often metaphorical scope distinguishes it from the more personal 元気.

## 仮定 (かてい)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a supposition, a hypothesis, an assumption

**Similar words:**
- [推測](vocab-22.md#推測-すいそく)（すいそく）— "speculation/guess," a less rigorous and more casual inference than the more formal/logical 仮定.
- [前提](vocab-23.md#前提-ぜんてい)（ぜんてい）— "premise," a foundational assumption on which an argument is built, closely related to 仮定 but emphasizing its role as a starting point for reasoning.
- 想定（そうてい）— "presupposition/expected scenario," often used in planning contexts (想定内, within expectations), a practical variant of 仮定.

**Opposite words:**
- 事実（じじつ）— fact

**Example sentences:**

**Similar words:**
- 努力する（どりょくする）— "to make an effort," more formal/written, describing sustained effort over time rather than the immediate encouragement tone of 頑張る.
- [励む](vocab-01.md#励む-はげむ)（はげむ）— "to work diligently at," more literary, often used for devoted practice or study.

**Example sentences:**
1. 明日の試験、頑張ってね。— Good luck on tomorrow's exam (do your best).
2. 彼は毎日仕事を頑張っている。— He works hard every day.
3. 最後まで頑張り抜いた。— I persevered all the way to the end.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 頑張って(ください) is an extremely common encouragement phrase ("good luck," "hang in there") — but offering it to someone already severely struggling or depressed can feel like pressuring them, so native speakers are cautious about overusing it in sensitive situations.
