# じゃ {#じゃ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** じゃ（漢字なし／平仮名のみ）

**Furigana form:** じゃ

**Meaning:** "Well then, okay then" — the casual spoken contraction of では, used to transition from the preceding topic/statement to a new action, decision, or next step.

**Conjugation:** Sentence-initial conjunction, no conjugation needed — attaches at the sentence boundary, connecting the previous sentence/situation to the next action or topic. Like では, it is also used as a short stand-alone transitional phrase before a command/invitation (じゃ、行こう "okay then, let's go") rather than linking two clauses within a single sentence.

**Typical context:** Casual, spoken register — the everyday contraction used constantly among friends, family, and in relaxed conversation, including as a casual way to say goodbye (じゃ、またね "see you then"). It is not used in formal writing or professional/polite speech, where the fuller では or それでは is expected; じゃ in a business meeting would sound too familiar/informal.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**では**](grammar-では.md) — the neutral/written form this is contracted from; usable in both writing and more neutral/polite speech, less casual than じゃ.
- [**それでは**](grammar-それでは.md) — more formal/polite spoken version, often used by hosts/announcers/teachers to transition at the start of a formal segment; a step up in formality from both では and especially じゃ.
- [**それから**](grammar-それから.md) — purely sequential "after that, next"; lacks じゃ's function of signaling a shift to a new decision/action.
- [**そこで**](grammar-そこで.md) — narrates a deliberate decision/action in response to a situation in more formal narrative register, unlike じゃ's casual, immediate conversational transition.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; like では, it functions as a casual topic/action transition marker rather than a logical or causal connector.

**Example sentences:**
1. じゃ、そろそろ行こうか。
   (Well then, shall we get going?)
2. A: 準備できたよ。 B: じゃ、始めよう。
   (A: I'm ready. B: Okay then, let's start.)
3. じゃ、また明日ね。
   (Okay then, see you tomorrow.)
4. お腹すいた? じゃ、何か食べに行く?
   (Are you hungry? Okay then, want to go grab something to eat?)
5. わかった、じゃ、それでいいよ。
   (Got it, well then, that's fine.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The most common mistake is using じゃ in formal or polite contexts — emails, presentations, meetings with superiors, or customer service — where it sounds too casual/familiar; では or それでは should be used instead. Learners sometimes also undercorrect in the opposite direction, using the fuller では in very casual texting/chatting with close friends, which can come across as slightly stiff or overly formal for the context, though this is a much milder mistake than the reverse. It also helps to remember that じゃ is purely the casual-register twin of では — the same formality gradient (casual じゃ < neutral では < formal それでは) applies to the whole "well then" function.
