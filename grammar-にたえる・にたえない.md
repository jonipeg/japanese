# ～に堪える／～に堪えない {#にたえる・にたえない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～に堪える／～に堪えない

**Furigana form:** ～にたえる／～にたえない

**Meaning:** 堪える = "worth/able to bear/endure (watching, listening, reading, etc.)" — of sufficient quality to be worth experiencing. 堪えない = "cannot bear to (watch/listen)" — either because the quality is too poor, or because the content is too emotionally moving to endure.

**Conjugation:**
- V辞書形/N + に堪える (鑑賞に堪える作品 — "a work worth viewing")
- V辞書形/N + に堪えない (見るに堪えない — "unbearable to watch")

**Typical context:** Formal, written/literary register, common in critique, reviews, and formal description of quality or emotional impact.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**ようがない**](grammar-ようがない.md) — expresses that there's no method/way to do X (直しようがない), a practical impossibility, distinct from にたえない's emotional/aesthetic inability to endure something.
- [**きわまりない**](grammar-きわまる・きわまりない.md) — directly intensifies a quality as extreme (失礼極まりない), rather than describing the speaker's inability to endure exposure to it.
- [**を禁じえない**](grammar-を禁じえない.md) — both involve an inability to control a reaction, but を禁じえない is about a feeling welling up and overflowing, while にたえない is about a refusal/inability to continue enduring exposure to something.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No single fixed opposite pattern, but 堪える itself (affirmative, "worth enduring/watching") functions as the positive counterpart to 堪えない within the same construction.

**Example sentences:**
1. あの人の話は下品で聞くにたえない。
   (That person's talk is so vulgar it's unbearable to listen to.)
2. この映画は何度見るに堪える名作だ。
   (This movie is a masterpiece worth watching again and again.)
3. 彼の作文は誤字が多すぎて読むに堪えない。
   (His composition has so many typos it's unbearable to read.)
4. その写真集は鑑賞に堪える芸術作品ばかりだ。
   (That photo collection is full of artworks worth appreciating.)
5. あまりに悲惨な映像で、正視するに堪えなかった。
   (The footage was so tragic that I couldn't bear to look at it directly.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** にたえる/にたえない is part of the N1 extreme-degree confusable cluster and is often mixed up with ようがない. The key distinction: ようがない is about the practical impossibility of carrying out an action due to lacking means (部品がないので直しようがない, "there's no way to fix it because there are no parts"), while にたえない is about an emotional or aesthetic refusal/inability to continue enduring something you ARE physically able to do (you can look, but it's too awful to bear).
