## 脚色 (きゃくしょく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** dramatization, adaptation (for stage/screen); embellishment

**Similar words:**
- 翻案（ほんあん）— "adaptation" of a story into a different medium or setting, close in meaning but 脚色 specifically emphasizes scripting for drama/film, while 翻案 is broader in scope.
- 誇張（こちょう）— "exaggeration," which overlaps with 脚色's figurative sense of embellishing a story beyond the facts.

**Opposite words:**
- 事実（じじつ）— fact, truth (as the unembellished original)

**Example sentences:**
1. その小説はドラマ用に脚色された。— The novel was adapted/dramatized for TV.
2. 彼の話はかなり脚色されているようだ。— His story seems to be considerably embellished.
3. 脚色家が原作に手を加えた。— The scriptwriter made changes to the original work.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 脚色 has two senses: the literal "dramatization for stage/screen" and the figurative "embellishing/exaggerating a true story" — context determines which meaning applies, so don't assume it only relates to theater/film.

## 客席 (きゃくせき)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a seat (for the audience), the audience area

**Similar words:**
- 観客席（かんきゃくせき）— essentially synonymous, slightly more explicit about being for "spectators," often used interchangeably with 客席.
- 座席（ざせき）— "a seat" in general (train, theater, any venue), broader and not specific to performance/audience venues like 客席.

**Opposite words:**
- 舞台（ぶたい）— the stage (as the contrasting space)

**Example sentences:**
1. 客席からの拍手が鳴り止まなかった。— The applause from the audience didn't stop.
2. 公演当日、客席はほぼ満席だった。— On the day of the performance, the audience seats were nearly full.
3. 客席の照明が落とされ、舞台が明るくなった。— The house lights dimmed and the stage brightened.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 客席 specifically refers to the audience-side seating in a theater, concert hall, or stadium — it contrasts with 舞台 (the stage) and shouldn't be used for generic seating like on a train or in a restaurant, where 座席 is correct.

## 逆転 (ぎゃくてん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a reversal, turnaround, comeback

**Similar words:**
- 反転（はんてん）— "inversion, flipping," more literal/physical (flipping an image or object), whereas 逆転 is often used figuratively for situations, scores, or fortunes reversing.
- どんでん返し（どんでんがえし）— "a dramatic plot twist/reversal," a more vivid, narrative-focused term often used in stories/dramas, compared to the more general 逆転.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 九回裏に逆転ホームランが飛び出した。— A game-changing home run came out in the bottom of the ninth.
2. 形勢が逆転した。— The situation has turned around completely.
3. 最後の最後で逆転勝ちを収めた。— We pulled off a come-from-behind victory at the very last moment.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 逆転 is extremely common in sports contexts (逆転ホームラン, 逆転勝利 — comeback win) — it implies going from behind/losing to ahead/winning, not just any change, so don't use it for a simple neutral change of state.

## 客間 (きゃくま)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a drawing room, guest room (in a traditional Japanese house)

**Similar words:**
- 応接間（おうせつま）— "reception room," a similar formal room for receiving guests, often found in more Western-style or business settings, whereas 客間 has a more traditional Japanese-house connotation.
- ゲストルーム — the modern loanword for "guest room," often used for hotels or Western-style homes, distinct from the traditional nuance of 客間.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. お客様を客間にお通しした。— I showed the guest into the drawing room.
2. 客間には立派な掛け軸が飾られている。— A splendid hanging scroll is displayed in the guest room.
3. 古い日本家屋には必ず客間がある。— Old Japanese houses always have a guest reception room.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 客間 evokes a traditional Japanese-style house layout with a dedicated room for receiving visitors — it's a somewhat old-fashioned term, less common in modern apartments, where ゲストルーム or simply リビング might be used instead.

## 客観 (きゃっかん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** objectivity

**Similar words:**
- [主観](vocab-20.md#主観-しゅかん)（しゅかん）— "subjectivity," the direct conceptual opposite, often discussed together (主観と客観).
- [中立](vocab-06.md#中立-ちゅうりつ)（ちゅうりつ）— "neutrality," related but focuses on not taking sides, whereas 客観 focuses on being based on facts/evidence rather than personal feeling.

**Opposite words:**
- [主観](vocab-20.md#主観-しゅかん)（しゅかん）— subjectivity

**Example sentences:**
1. もっと客観的な視点から考えてみよう。— Let's try thinking from a more objective viewpoint.
2. 彼の意見には客観性が欠けている。— His opinion lacks objectivity.
3. 自分の行動を客観視することは難しい。— It's difficult to view one's own actions objectively.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Note the irregular reading: 客観 is read きゃっかん (with a small っ), not きゃくかん — a common misreading since many learners expect the straightforward on'yomi combination.

## キャッチ
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a catch (grabbing/receiving something, also a catchy phrase)

**Similar words:**
- [捕まえる](vocab-28.md#捕まえる-つかまえる)（つかまえる）— the native Japanese verb "to catch/grab," used more broadly and naturally in everyday speech than the loanword キャッチ, which is often used in sports or in the sense of "catching" information/attention.
- キャッチコピー — "catchphrase/tagline," a related compound using キャッチ in the advertising sense.

**Opposite words:**
- 落とす（おとす）— to drop, miss (a catch)

**Example sentences:**
1. ボールをうまくキャッチした。— He caught the ball nicely.
2. そのニュースをいち早くキャッチした。— I caught wind of that news early.
3. キャッチコピーが印象に残る広告だった。— It was an advertisement with a memorable catchphrase.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** キャッチ as a standalone verb is usually キャッチする (to catch) — in compounds it broadens to mean "catching" information or attention (キャッチコピー, キャッチフレーズ), not just physical catching like in baseball.

## キャプテン
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a captain (of a team, ship)

**Similar words:**
- 主将（しゅしょう）— the native Japanese word for "team captain," more formal/traditional, often used in school sports contexts alongside or instead of キャプテン.
- リーダー — "leader," broader and not restricted to sports/ship contexts, referring to leadership in any group setting.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼はサッカー部のキャプテンに選ばれた。— He was chosen as the captain of the soccer team.
2. キャプテンの指示に従ってプレーする。— The players follow the captain's instructions.
3. 船のキャプテンが安全確認を行った。— The ship's captain carried out a safety check.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** キャプテン and 主将 are largely interchangeable for sports team captains, but キャプテン is also used for ship/aircraft captains, a context where 主将 would sound wrong — 主将 is reserved for team leadership.

#vocabulary

**Meaning:** a career

**Similar words:**
- [経歴](vocab-17.md#経歴-けいれき)（けいれき）— "one's personal history/background (professional)," more about the documented record of past positions, whereas キャリア often refers to the ongoing trajectory or development of one's professional life.
- 職歴（しょくれき）— "work history," a narrower, more formal/bureaucratic term listing past jobs, often used on resumes.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** キャリア in Japanese often emphasizes career advancement/development (キャリアアップ, キャリア組) rather than just "a job" — it's also used in the specific sense of "career bureaucrats" (キャリア官僚), a nuance tied to Japanese civil service culture.

## ギャング
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a gangster, a gang

**Similar words:**
- やくざ — the specifically Japanese organized crime syndicate, a culturally distinct concept from the more generic, often Western-associated ギャング.
- 暴力団（ぼうりょくだん）— the formal/legal term for organized crime groups in Japan, used in news and official contexts rather than casual speech.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. その映画はシカゴのギャングを描いている。— That movie depicts a Chicago gang.
2. 若者たちがギャング団を結成した。— The young men formed a gang.
3. ギャング映画が好きでよく見る。— I like gangster movies and watch them often.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ギャング typically evokes Western (especially American) organized crime imagery in Japanese media — for Japanese organized crime specifically, やくざ or 暴力団 are the words actually used, not ギャング.

## キャンパス
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a campus

**Similar words:**
- 学校（がっこう）— the general word for "school," broader and not specific to the physical grounds the way キャンパス is.
- 構内（こうない）— "the premises/grounds" (of a school, company), a more formal/written term for the physical area, overlapping with キャンパス but usable for non-educational institutions too.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 大学のキャンパスは緑豊かだ。— The university campus is rich with greenery.
2. キャンパス内に新しい図書館が建てられた。— A new library was built on campus.
3. キャンパスライフを満喫している。— I'm fully enjoying campus life.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** キャンパス is almost exclusively used for university/college grounds in Japanese (not typically for elementary/high schools, which just use 学校) — don't confuse it with キャンプ (camp), a near-homophone with a completely different meaning.

## キャンプ
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a camp, camping

**Similar words:**
- 野営（やえい）— "bivouac, outdoor camping," a more formal/old-fashioned native term, less commonly used in casual conversation than キャンプ.
- 合宿（がっしゅく）— "a training camp/retreat" (for sports, study), implies a group staying together for intensive training rather than recreational camping.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 夏休みに家族でキャンプに行った。— My family went camping during summer vacation.
2. 野球部は夏にキャンプを行う。— The baseball team holds a training camp in summer.
3. 難民キャンプへの支援が呼びかけられている。— Support is being called for toward the refugee camps.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Don't confuse キャンプ (camp/camping) with キャンパス (campus) — these near-homophones are a classic katakana mix-up for learners; also note キャンプ extends beyond recreational camping to training camps (野球キャンプ) and refugee camps (難民キャンプ).

## 球 (きゅう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a sphere, a ball

**Similar words:**
- ボール — the everyday loanword for "ball" (sports equipment), much more commonly used in casual speech than the native/technical 球.
- 球体（きゅうたい）— "a spherical object/shape," more technical/geometric, emphasizing the shape itself rather than a ball used in play.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 速い球を投げるピッチャーだ。— He's a pitcher who throws a fast ball.
2. 地球は完全な球ではない。— The Earth is not a perfect sphere.
3. 好球を見逃してしまった。— He let a good pitch (ball) go by.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 球 (きゅう) is mostly used in formal/technical contexts (地球 "Earth," 球体 "sphere") or baseball jargon (速球 "fastball," 好球 "good pitch") — for everyday "ball" as in sports equipment, ボール is the natural everyday word.

## 救援 (きゅうえん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** relief, rescue, aid

**Similar words:**
- [救助](#救助-きゅうじょ)（きゅうじょ）— "rescue," more specifically the physical act of saving someone from immediate danger, whereas 救援 is broader and can include ongoing aid/relief efforts (救援物資, 救援活動).

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 救援 typically refers to organized relief efforts after disasters (救援物資, 救援活動), distinct from 救助, which is the more immediate, hands-on act of physically rescuing someone from danger.

## 給油 (きゅうゆ)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** refueling (adding fuel/gas)

**Similar words:**
- 補給（ほきゅう）— "resupply, replenishment," broader and can apply to any resource (food, ammunition, fuel), whereas 給油 specifically refers to fuel/oil.
- 燃料補給（ねんりょうほきゅう）— essentially synonymous but more explicit/formal, spelling out "fuel resupply."

- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. ガソリンスタンドで給油した。— I refueled at the gas station.

## 休学 (きゅうがく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** leave of absence from school

**Similar words:**
- 休職（きゅうしょく）— the workplace equivalent, "leave of absence from one's job," used for employees rather than students.
- [退学](vocab-25.md#退学-たいがく)（たいがく）— "withdrawal/expulsion from school," a permanent departure, in contrast to the temporary nature of 休学.

**Opposite words:**
- 復学（ふくがく）— returning to school after a leave of absence

**Example sentences:**
1. 病気療養のため一年間休学した。— I took a year's leave of absence from school for medical treatment.
2. 休学中は学費が免除される場合がある。— Tuition may be waived during a leave of absence.
3. 彼は留学のために休学を申請した。— He applied for a leave of absence from school to study abroad.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 休学 is specifically temporary and reversible — the student intends to return (復学) — unlike 退学, which means leaving school permanently; mixing these up changes the meaning significantly.

## 休業 (きゅうぎょう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** suspension of business, temporary closure

**Similar words:**
- 休店（きゅうてん）— specifically "store closure," a narrower term used for retail shops, whereas 休業 applies more broadly to any business or operation.
- 閉店（へいてん）— "closing (for the day or permanently)," ambiguous between daily closing time and permanent shutdown, whereas 休業 clearly implies a temporary suspension.

**Opposite words:**
- [営業](vocab-09.md#営業-えいぎょう)（えいぎょう）— operating, open for business

**Example sentences:**
1. 本日は臨時休業いたします。— We are temporarily closed today.
2. 工場は点検のため一週間休業する。— The factory will suspend operations for a week for inspection.
3. コロナ禍で多くの店が休業を余儀なくされた。— Many shops were forced to suspend business during the pandemic.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 休業 implies a temporary, often announced suspension (臨時休業, 定休日とは別に) — it's distinct from 定休日 (regular day off), which is a scheduled recurring closure rather than a special, one-off suspension.

## 究極 (きゅうきょく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** ultimate, the final extreme

**Similar words:**
- 最終（さいしゅう）— "final, last," more neutral and simply indicates sequence/order, whereas 究極 implies reaching the highest or most extreme possible degree.
- 極限（きょくげん）— "the limit, extremity," close in meaning, often used for physical/physiological limits (極限状態), while 究極 is broader and often used more abstractly/rhetorically (究極の選択).

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. これは究極の選択だと言える。— This can be called the ultimate choice/dilemma.
2. 彼は究極の美を追求している。— He pursues the ultimate beauty.
3. 究極的には自分自身の問題だ。— Ultimately, it's a matter of oneself.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 究極 often appears as 究極の〜 (the ultimate ~) or 究極的に(は) (ultimately) in both serious and playful/hyperbolic contexts (究極のラーメン, "the ultimate ramen") — it's commonly used in casual advertising/pop-culture hyperbole, not just formal writing.

## 急激 (きゅうげき)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** sudden, rapid, sharp (change)

**Similar words:**
- [急速](#急速-きゅうそく)（きゅうそく）— "rapid," very close synonym, often used for speed of progress/development, whereas 急激 emphasizes the abruptness/sharpness of change more than sustained speed.
- [突然](vocab-30.md#突然-とつぜん)（とつぜん）— "suddenly," an adverb describing the unexpectedness of an event's onset, whereas 急激 (na-adjective) describes the steepness/intensity of change itself.

**Opposite words:**
- [緩やか](vocab-35.md#緩やか-ゆるやか)（ゆるやか）— gradual, gentle

**Example sentences:**
1. 気温が急激に下がった。— The temperature dropped sharply.
2. 急激な人口増加が問題になっている。— Rapid population growth has become a problem.
3. 株価が急激に変動している。— Stock prices are fluctuating drastically.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 急激 is a na-adjective describing the steep/abrupt degree of change (急激な変化, 急激に増える) — it emphasizes intensity of change over time, distinct from 突然, which is about something happening without warning at a single moment.

## 急行 (きゅうこう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** an express train; to rush/hurry to a place

**Similar words:**
- [特急](vocab-30.md#特急-とっきゅう)（とっきゅう）— "limited express," a faster train category than 急行, stopping at even fewer stations.
- 快速（かいそく）— "rapid service," another train speed category, generally slower than 急行 but faster than local trains.

**Opposite words:**
- 各駅停車（かくえきていしゃ）— local train (stopping at every station)

**Example sentences:**
1. 急行電車に乗れば30分早く着く。— If you take the express train, you'll arrive 30 minutes earlier.

3. この駅には急行が停まらない。— The express doesn't stop at this station.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 急行 has two distinct uses: as a noun for a train category (slower than 特急, faster than 快速/普通) and as a verb 急行する meaning "to rush/hasten to a location" — context determines which sense applies.

## 吸収 (きゅうしゅう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** absorption, assimilation

**Similar words:**
- 摂取（せっしゅ）— "intake" (of nutrients, food), more specific to consuming substances into the body, whereas 吸収 describes the subsequent process of the body/material actually absorbing it.
- [統合](vocab-04.md#統合-とうごう)（とうごう）— "integration, consolidation," used more for merging organizations/systems, a figurative extension sometimes shared with 吸収 (e.g., company acquisitions, 吸収合併).

**Opposite words:**
- 放出（ほうしゅつ）— release, discharge

**Example sentences:**
1. ビタミンCは体に吸収されやすい。— Vitamin C is easily absorbed by the body.
2. スポンジは水をよく吸収する。— A sponge absorbs water well.
3. その会社は競合他社に吸収合併された。— That company was absorbed through merger by a competitor.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 吸収 applies both literally (liquids, nutrients, light, sound) and figuratively (absorbing information, or one company absorbing another via 吸収合併) — be aware of this extended business-merger sense, which has no direct physical absorption involved.

#vocabulary

3. このクイズに答えられますか。— Can you answer this quiz question?

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** クイズ is a loanword used for lighthearted trivia/games (quiz shows, classroom quizzes) and doesn't carry the formal, evaluative weight of 試験 or テスト — don't use it for serious academic exams.

## 食い違う (くいちがう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to conflict, diverge, be at odds (especially of opinions, accounts, or facts)

**Similar words:**
- 矛盾する（むじゅんする）— "to contradict," more logical/formal, often about internal inconsistency within a single statement or system.
- 異なる（ことなる）— "to differ," neutral and general, without implying a problematic clash.
- 対立する（たいりつする）— "to be opposed/in conflict," stronger, often about interests or positions clashing directly.

**Opposite words:**
- 一致する（いっちする）— to agree, coincide, match

**Example sentences:**
1. 二人の証言が食い違っている。— The two people's testimonies conflict.
2. 意見が食い違って議論になった。— Our opinions diverged and it turned into an argument.
3. 記録と実際の数が食い違っていた。— The records and the actual numbers didn't match.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 食い違う is most often used for discrepancies between accounts, testimonies, or records (証言が食い違う) rather than a simple difference of taste — it implies a problematic mismatch that needs to be resolved, unlike neutral 異なる.

## 食う (くう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** to eat, devour; to earn a living; (figuratively) to consume/use up

**Similar words:**
- 食べる（たべる）— the standard, neutral word for "to eat," used in polite/everyday speech; 食う is rougher, masculine/casual, sometimes rude depending on context.
- 食らう（くらう）— even rougher/more vulgar synonym, often for "to take a hit" figuratively as well as eating.
- 生活する（せいかつする）— "to live/make a living," the neutral equivalent of the figurative "food を食う" sense.

**Opposite words:**
- None notable

**Example sentences:**
1. 腹が減った、何か食おう。— I'm hungry, let's eat something.
2. 彼はフリーランスで食っている。— He makes a living as a freelancer.
3. この作業は時間を食う。— This task eats up a lot of time.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 食う is blunt/casual and typically used by men or in rough speech — in polite conversation or when speaking to someone you don't know well, 食べる is the safer choice; also note the idiom 時間を食う ("to eat up time, be time-consuming") as a common figurative extension.

## 偶然 (ぐうぜん)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** a chance occurrence, coincidence; (as adverb/adjective) by chance, accidental

**Similar words:**
- 偶然に（ぐうぜんに）— adverbial form, "by coincidence," used to describe how something happened.
- たまたま（たまたま）— more casual/colloquial equivalent of "by chance," very common in spoken Japanese.

## 空想 (くうそう)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** imagination, a daydream, fancy (often unrealistic or fanciful thinking)

**Similar words:**
- 妄想（もうそう）— "delusion/wild fantasy," stronger and can carry a negative, even pathological connotation.
- [想像](vocab-24.md#想像-そうぞう)（そうぞう）— "imagination," neutral and broader, without the "unrealistic/dreamy" connotation of 空想.

## 空腹 (くうふく)
#vocabulary
#to-review

**Meaning:** hunger, an empty stomach

**Similar words:**
- 腹ぺこ（はらぺこ）— very casual/colloquial "starving, famished," used in everyday speech especially by or about children.

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** 空腹 is a neutral/formal word for "hunger," often seen in health and medical contexts (空腹時 "on an empty stomach," as in medicine instructions) — in casual speech, 腹ぺこ or simply お腹が空いた is more natural.
