# ～といったところだ {#といったところだ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～といったところだ

**Furigana form:** ～といったところだ

**Meaning:** "That's about the extent of it" — gives a modest, approximate assessment of a quantity, degree, or situation; implies "no more, no less than roughly X."

**Conjugation:**
- N/数量表現 + といったところだ

**Typical context:** Semi-formal; usable in both spoken and written Japanese when giving a modest rough estimate or assessment, often downplaying an achievement or figure.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**しまつだ**](grammar-しまつだ.md) — describes the final, usually bad, result of an accumulation of events, a conclusory judgment with negative valence, in contrast to といったところだ's neutral, modest approximation of degree.
- [**かぎりだ**](grammar-かぎりだ.md) — expresses an intense personal feeling at its peak (うらやましい限りだ), the opposite in tone from といったところだ's deliberately modest, understated estimate.
- [**それまでだ**](grammar-それまでだ.md) — expresses finality/futility ("if X happens, it's all over"), a very different function from といったところだ's neutral approximation.
- **程度だ** — a plainer, more literal "to the extent of X," closely related in meaning but without といったところだ's conversational hedge-like softness.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 留学生のアルバイトだったら、時給は800円から1000円といったところだ。
   (For a foreign student's part-time job, the hourly wage is about 800 to 1000 yen, give or take.)
2. 今回のテストの出来は、まあまあといったところだ。
   (My performance on this test was about so-so, I'd say.)
3. このプロジェクトの進捗は、半分終わったといったところだ。
   (The progress on this project is about half done, roughly speaking.)
4. 彼の実力は、中級レベルといったところだろう。
   (His skill level is probably about intermediate, I'd guess.)
5. 休日の過ごし方は、家でゆっくり読書するといったところだ。
   (As for how I spend my days off, it's mostly just relaxing at home reading, more or less.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** While といったところだ isn't itself an "extreme degree" expression, it appears in the same N1 MC drill cluster and gets confused with しまつだ because both can end a sentence summing up a situation. The key difference: しまつだ always concludes a negative chain of events with a sense of regret ("...and it ended up like this"), while といったところだ is tonally neutral or mildly modest, simply estimating a degree without implying anything went wrong.
