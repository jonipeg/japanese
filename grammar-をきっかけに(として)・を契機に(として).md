# ～をきっかけに（として）／を契機に（として） {#をきっかけに-を契機に}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～をきっかけに（として）／～を契機に（として）

**Furigana form:** ～をきっかけに（として）／～をけいきに（として）

**Meaning:** "Taking X as a chance/trigger/opportunity" — marks an event as the catalyst that led to a subsequent change or action. をきっかけに is the more common/colloquial form; を契機に is a more formal, written synonym with the same meaning.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + をきっかけに(して/として): 留学をきっかけに英語が得意になった (studying abroad became the trigger for me getting good at English)
- 名詞 + を契機に(して/として): 事故を契機に安全対策が強化された (the accident became the trigger for stronger safety measures)
- Can also follow a clause nominalized with こと: 友人に誘われたことをきっかけに (triggered by being invited by a friend)

**Typical context:** をきっかけに — neutral, usable in both spoken and written Japanese. を契機に — formal, almost exclusively written (news articles, official reports, biographies).

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**を機に**](grammar-を機に.md) — near-synonymous with both, slightly more literary than きっかけに but less stiff than 契機に (see separate を機に entry for detailed comparison).
- **のをきっかけにして** — same meaning, emphasizing the "doing" nominalized clause.
- **に伴って** — "along with/as X occurs," lacks the specific "turning point/catalyst" nuance; more about simultaneous change.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 友人に誘われたことをきっかけに、ヨガを始めた。
   (Being invited by a friend was the trigger for me starting yoga.)
2. 今回の事故を契機に、工場の安全基準が見直された。
   (This accident triggered a review of the factory's safety standards.)
3. コロナ禍をきっかけとして、在宅勤務が一般的になった。
   (The pandemic was the trigger for remote work becoming common.)
4. 彼の一言を契機に、二人の関係は大きく変わった。
   (His one remark was the trigger that greatly changed their relationship.)
5. 旅行先での出会いをきっかけに、二人は結婚することになった。
   (An encounter while traveling was the trigger for the two of them getting married.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** をきっかけに and を契機に are near-synonyms differing mainly in register (きっかけに casual/neutral, 契機に formal/written) — learners should match formality to context rather than mixing registers oddly (e.g., using 契機に in casual conversation sounds stilted). Also compare with the related を機に entry: all three (きっかけに, 契機に, 機に) share the "catalyst/trigger" meaning but differ in formality, from きっかけに (casual) < 機に (neutral-formal) < 契機に (most formal/written).
