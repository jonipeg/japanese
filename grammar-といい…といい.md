# ～といい…といい {#といい…といい}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～といい…といい

**Furigana form:** ～といい…といい

**Meaning:** "Looking at X, and also at Y, the same impression holds" — gives two examples of aspects of something and states an overall judgment or evaluation about it based on them.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + といい
- 味といい、見た目といい、このケーキは最高だ — "both the taste and the appearance of this cake are outstanding"

**Typical context:** Written and spoken, evaluative/descriptive register — used when praising (or occasionally criticizing) something by pointing to multiple notable aspects of it.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**～といわず…といわず**](grammar-といわず…といわず.md) — about things being uniformly present or true everywhere (often messy or negative), rather than a judgment the speaker draws from two evaluative aspects.
- **～も…も** — a simpler listing, lacking the evaluative "judging by X and Y" nuance.
- **～にしても…にしても** — a broader concessive listing, less specifically tied to appearance or evaluation.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a phrase expressing inconsistency across aspects, like "Xはいいが、Yはよくない" ("X is good but Y isn't"), contrasts with this pattern's claim of a uniform impression.

**Example sentences:**
1. この映画は映像の美しさといい音楽の素晴らしさといい、最高の作品だ。
   (Between the beauty of the visuals and the wonderful music, this film is a masterpiece.)
2. 中島さんといい松本さんといい、うちの課の人はみんな話が面白い。
   (Whether it's Nakajima-san or Matsumoto-san, everyone in our section is a fun talker.)
3. この虫は色といい形といい、木の葉にそっくりだ。
   (In both its color and its shape, this insect looks just like a leaf.)
4. この旅館は料理といい温泉といい、申し分ない。
   (Both the food and the hot spring at this inn are beyond reproach.)
5. 彼女は性格といい才能といい、理想的な人材だ。
   (Both in personality and in talent, she's an ideal person for the job.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** What follows must be a statement describing a state or the speaker's evaluation (often adjective-based) — it cannot be followed by the speaker's wishes, intentions, or a request/suggestion to someone else, since this pattern is purely descriptive/evaluative, not action-oriented.
