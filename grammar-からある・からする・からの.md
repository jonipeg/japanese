# ～からある・～からする・～からの {#からある・からする・からの}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～からある・～からする・～からの

**Furigana form:** ～からある・～からする・～からの

**Meaning:** Indicates a quantity, weight, size, price, or number of people that is "at least that much, or more" — used to emphasize how surprisingly large the figure is.

**Conjugation:**
- 数詞 + からある／からする／からの + 名詞
- 2トンからあるこの岩 — "this rock, which weighs a full two tons or more"
- 10万円からする服 — "clothes costing a full ¥100,000 or more"
- 10万人からの人々 — "a crowd of a full 100,000 people or more"

**Typical context:** Written and spoken Japanese, often in slightly dramatic or impressed narration — news reports, storytelling, and conversation about remarkable sizes, prices, or crowds.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～以上** — also means "X or more," but is neutral and factual, lacking the nuance of surprise or emphasis that ～からある／する／の carries.
- [**～ともなると**](grammar-ともなると・ともなれば.md) — shares the sense of "once you reach the level/scale of X," though it's used more broadly for stages or conditions rather than specifically for large quantities.
- [**～も**](grammar-にかけては・も.md) — a simpler way to emphasize a surprisingly large number (e.g., 100人も来た), with a similar emphatic nuance but no restriction on which noun type follows.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct grammatical opposite; a phrase like ～にも満たない ("doesn't even reach X") would instead emphasize that a quantity is surprisingly small.

**Example sentences:**
1. 2トンからあるこの岩を、昔の人はどうやって運んだんだろう。
   (I wonder how people in the past managed to move this rock, which weighs a full two tons or more.)
2. 彼女は10万円からする服を、値段も見ないで何着も買った。
   (She bought several outfits that each cost a full ¥100,000 or more, without even checking the price.)
3. そのデモには10万人からの人々が参加したそうだ。
   (I heard that a full 100,000 people or more took part in that demonstration.)
4. この会場には5000人からある客が集まったらしい。
   (It seems a crowd of 5,000 or more gathered at this venue.)
5. 彼の持っている車は1000万円からする高級車だ。
   (The car he owns is a luxury model costing a full ten million yen or more.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern must be followed by a noun that matches the type of quantity involved — use ～からある／～からの for weight, distance, size, or quantity, ～からする for prices, and ～からの for numbers of people. It is not used with small or trivial quantities (it wouldn't sound natural to say "3人からの人" for a tiny number), nor typically with expressions of time — the whole point is to emphasize that the figure is impressively large.
