# ～をおして {#をおして}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～を押して

**Furigana form:** ～をおして

**Meaning:** "Doing X despite circumstances that would normally prevent it" — describes pushing forward with an action in spite of a hindering condition (illness, bad weather, objections).

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + を押して
- Example base: 高熱 → 高熱を押して

**Typical context:** Formal-to-neutral, usable in both speech and writing; often conveys admiration for perseverance or dedication despite adversity. Common in sports reporting, biographical narration, and formal praise.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**にもかかわらず**](grammar-にかかわらず・にもかかわらず.md) — "despite X"; broader and more general-purpose concessive, usable with any noun/clause, while を押して is more specifically about physically or personally overcoming a hindrance (illness, pain, objection) to carry out an action.
- **をものともせず（に）** — "undaunted by X / not letting X be a hindrance"; very close in meaning and often interchangeable, though をものともせず carries a slightly more dramatic, bold connotation of defiance.
- **を顧みず** — "without regard for X" (e.g., 危険を顧みず, "without regard for danger"); similarly describes disregarding a concern to act, with a slightly more reckless/self-sacrificing nuance than を押して.

**Opposite grammar structures:** ～のために／～を言い訳に休む ("rest because of X / use X as an excuse to stop") expresses the reverse — letting the hindrance prevent the action.

**Example sentences:**
1. キャプテンは高熱を押してサッカーの決勝戦に出場した。 (The captain played in the soccer finals despite having a high fever.)
2. 彼は周囲の反対を押して、結婚を決意した。 (He resolved to marry despite opposition from those around him.)
3. 医者は体の痛みを押して手術を続けた。 (The doctor continued the operation despite the pain in his body.)
4. 彼女は悪天候を押して登山を敢行した。 (She went ahead with the mountain climb despite the bad weather.)
5. 祖母は足の不自由を押して、孫の卒業式に出席した。 (My grandmother attended her grandchild's graduation ceremony despite her difficulty walking.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** を押して always implies genuine personal hardship or a real obstacle being physically/emotionally overcome (illness, pain, opposition, bad weather) — it is not used for trivial inconveniences. Learners should note the admiring, almost heroic tone this pattern carries; it's rarely neutral, usually praising the subject's determination or sense of duty in pushing through adversity.
