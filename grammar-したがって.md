# したがって {#したがって}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** 従って

**Furigana form:** したがって

**Meaning:** "Therefore, accordingly, consequently" — a formal conjunction presenting the following statement as a logical conclusion drawn from the preceding one.

**Conjugation:** Sentence-initial conjunction, no conjugation needed — attaches at the sentence boundary, connecting the previous sentence/clause to the next. It is essentially always used sentence-initially in formal writing rather than mid-sentence; a mid-sentence equivalent would normally be rephrased with ため or ので instead.

**Typical context:** Formal, written register — the standard "therefore" of business documents, academic papers, legal writing, news reports, and formal presentations/speeches. It is rarely used in casual conversation; using it among friends sounds stiff or overly bureaucratic, almost as if reading from a report.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**だから**](grammar-だから.md) — casual, spoken; the everyday equivalent used in conversation.
- [**それで**](grammar-それで.md) — casual-neutral, spoken; narrates a reason/result in a softer, more conversational way.
- [**そのため**](grammar-そのため.md) — neutral-formal, written; slightly less stiff than したがって, common in explanatory/informational writing.
- [**それゆえ**](grammar-それゆえ.md) — very formal, literary/written; even more elevated and archaic-feeling than したがって, often found in philosophical or legal texts.
- [**というのは**](grammar-というのは.md) — written/spoken explanatory; introduces the reason for the preceding statement rather than stating a conclusion drawn from it.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite, but its contrastive-register counterpart in formal writing would be a concessive conjunction like にもかかわらず ("despite this") or それにもかかわらず, which blocks the expected conclusion rather than affirming it.

**Example sentences:**
1. 今年度の売上は前年を大きく下回った。したがって、来期は経費削減が急務である。
   (This year's sales fell significantly below last year's. Therefore, cutting costs is an urgent priority for next term.)
2. 本製品は輸入部品を使用している。したがって、価格は為替の影響を受けやすい。
   (This product uses imported parts. Accordingly, its price is easily affected by exchange rates.)
3. 彼は会議に一度も出席していない。したがって、議決権を持たない。
   (He has not attended a single meeting. Therefore, he does not hold voting rights.)
4. 当社の方針は環境負荷の低減にある。したがって、全工場で省エネ設備を導入する。
   (Our company's policy is to reduce environmental impact. Accordingly, we will install energy-saving equipment at all factories.)
5. このデータには明らかな誤差が含まれている。したがって、結論を出すのは時期尚早である。
   (This data clearly contains errors. Therefore, it is premature to draw a conclusion.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** A frequent error for learners is using したがって in casual spoken conversation (e.g. with friends or family) where it sounds unnaturally stiff — だから or それで should be used instead. The reverse mistake also occurs: using だから in formal reports, cover letters, or academic writing, which undercuts the intended professional tone. As a rule of thumb, if the sentence could be read aloud at a business meeting or printed in a newspaper, したがって fits; if it's something you'd say to a friend over coffee, だから is correct.
