# ～ものを {#ものを}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ものを

**Furigana form:** ～ものを

**Meaning:** "Although X (would have been better/true)" — expresses regret or mild reproach that a better alternative existed but was not taken, often implying "if only..." or "you should have..." with an undertone of frustration.

**Conjugation:**
- [動・い形・な形] の名詞修飾形 + ものを
- Often follows a conditional: ～ば/～と＋ものを (言ってくれればいいものを "if only you'd just told me")

**Typical context:** Used in both spoken and written Japanese; emotionally expressive register. Carries a reproachful, regretful, sometimes slightly accusatory tone — common when someone is lamenting a missed opportunity or criticizing another's poor choice.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **のに** — "although X," a more common and casual expression of similar regret/frustration, usable in nearly identical contexts but without the slightly more literary flavor of ものを.
- **ばよかったものを** — an extended fixed phrase built on this same pattern, reinforcing the "should have done X" regret nuance.
- [**くせに**](grammar-くせに.md) — "even though X" — more directly critical of the subject's character/attitude, rather than simply lamenting a missed better option.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. え？これ、食べたかったの？それなら食べたいと言ってくれればいいものを。
   (What? You wanted to eat this? If only you'd just said so.)
2. もっと早く病院に行けばよかったものを、我慢して悪化させてしまった。
   (If only he'd gone to the hospital sooner — instead he endured it and made things worse.)
3. 素直に謝ればいいものを、彼はいつも意地を張ってしまう。
   (If only he'd just apologize honestly — but he always stubbornly refuses.)
4. もう少し注意していればミスは防げたものを。
   (If only he'd been a bit more careful, the mistake could have been avoided.)
5. 黙っていればよかったものを、余計な一言を言ってしまった。
   (If only she'd kept quiet — but she went and said one unnecessary thing.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Students often substitute the more familiar のに, missing the slightly stronger, more literary regret/reproach nuance that ものを carries, and the way it frequently pairs with a conditional clause (ば/と) just before it. The sentence almost always implies "there was a better option, and it's a shame/frustrating that it wasn't taken" — if no such missed alternative is implied, のに or simple が is likely more appropriate.
