# しかし {#しかし}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** しかし (no common kanji form; conventionally written in kana)

**Furigana form:** しかし

**Meaning:** "However, but" — a standard conjunction introducing a contrast or counterpoint to the preceding statement.

**Conjugation:** Sentence-initial conjunction, no conjugation needed — attaches at the sentence boundary, connecting the previous sentence to the next. Occasionally appears mid-sentence after a comma for emphasis, but its default position is at the start of a new sentence.

**Typical context:** Standard written/formal register. This is the default "however" used in essays, news articles, business writing, and formal speeches. It is perfectly usable in neutral spoken Japanese too (e.g. news anchors, lectures), but it reads as noticeably more formal/composed than でも or だけど in casual conversation.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**だけど**](grammar-だけど.md) — very casual spoken "but"; far more informal than しかし.
- [**でも**](grammar-でも.md) — casual spoken "but," the most common everyday equivalent; less formal than しかし.
- [**けれども**](grammar-けれども.md) — polite-neutral, spoken/written, softer than でも but still less formal/terse than しかし.
- [**だが**](grammar-だが.md) — literary/written, terse, often used by narrators or in male speech; similar register to しかし but more clipped and less "neutral-polite."
- [**それなのに**](grammar-それなのに.md) — adds emotional nuance of surprise/frustration/unfairness; stronger and more emotional than plain しかし.
- [**一方**](grammar-一方.md) — "on the other hand"; contrasts two parallel facts/perspectives rather than negating the first, more structural than しかし.

**Opposite grammar structures:** そして/また (additive, continuation conjunctions) are the structural opposite in function — they continue/build on the preceding statement rather than contrasting with it.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼は一生懸命勉強した。しかし、試験に落ちてしまった。
   (He studied hard. However, he failed the exam.)
2. この商品は性能がいい。しかし、値段がかなり高い。
   (This product performs well. However, it's quite expensive.)
3. 天気予報は晴れだった。しかし、実際は一日中雨だった。
   (The forecast said sunny. However, it actually rained all day.)
4. 彼女は謝った。しかし、彼はまだ怒っていた。
   (She apologized. However, he was still angry.)
5. 計画は順調に進んでいた。しかし、最後の最後で問題が起きた。
   (The plan was going smoothly. However, a problem arose at the very last moment.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often use しかし in casual conversation where でも or だけど would sound far more natural — しかし can come across as stiff or overly formal among friends. Conversely, using でも or だけど in a formal essay sounds too casual. Also distinguish しかし from それなのに: しかし is a neutral logical contrast, while それなのに adds a layer of emotional surprise or complaint ("and yet, despite that") that しかし does not carry on its own.
