# ～たとたん(に) {#たとたんに}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～たとたん(に)

**Furigana form:** ～たとたん(に) (no additional kanji readings needed)

**Meaning:** "The instant that..., no sooner had X happened than Y" — emphasizes immediacy and often surprise between two closely sequential events.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞た形 (verb, past plain form) + とたん(に)

**Typical context:** Spoken and written narrative Japanese, used to describe sudden or surprising sequences of events.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **たら** — general conditional, broader usage without the "instant/surprise" nuance.
- [**なり**](grammar-なり.md) — "as soon as," more literary/formal, similar meaning.
- **か～ないかのうちに** — "almost simultaneously," describes two nearly overlapping events.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. ドアを開けたとたんに、猫が飛び出してきた。
   (The instant I opened the door, the cat jumped out.)
2. 立ち上がったとたん、めまいがした。
   (The moment I stood up, I felt dizzy.)
3. 彼の顔を見たとたんに、泣き出してしまった。
   (The instant I saw his face, I burst into tears.)
4. 家に着いたとたん、雨が降り始めた。
   (No sooner had I arrived home than it started raining.)
5. 電気を消したとたんに、電話が鳴った。
   (The instant I turned off the light, the phone rang.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** とたん always follows a past-tense verb, and the following event must be unplanned or sudden — it cannot describe intentional, controlled sequences. Learners often confuse it with the more literary なり, which carries a similar meaning but a stiffer register.
