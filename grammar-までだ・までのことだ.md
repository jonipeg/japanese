# ～までだ・～までのことだ {#までだ・までのことだ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～までだ・～までのことだ

**Furigana form:** ～までだ・～までのことだ

**Meaning:** (A) Expresses the speaker's resolve that, if there's no other option, they will simply go ahead and do ～ — "if all else fails, I'll just do X." (B) Expresses that an action the speaker took was simply ～ and nothing more, downplaying its significance — "I just did X, that's all, no deeper meaning to it."

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞辞書形 + までだ／までのことだ (meaning A — resolve)
- 動詞た形 + までだ／までのことだ (meaning B — downplaying a past action)
- 一人でやってみるまでだ — "I'll just try doing it alone"
- 自分のするべきことをしたまでです — "I merely did what I was supposed to do"

**Typical context:** Spoken and written Japanese, neutral-to-formal register — often appears in statements of quiet determination (meaning A) or modest, humble responses to praise (meaning B).

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**～しかない**](grammar-しかない.md) — overlaps with meaning (A), both expressing "there's no choice but to do X," though ～しかない is more neutral and lacks the resolute, almost defiant tone of ～までだ.
- **～だけだ** — overlaps with meaning (B), downplaying an action as "just X, nothing more," but without the modest, self-effacing nuance ～までだ often carries in response to praise.
- [**～ばそれまでだ**](grammar-ばそれまでだ.md) — shares the ～まで root but means something quite different: that if a certain condition is met, everything becomes futile.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a statement expressing hesitation or lack of resolve (e.g., ～かどうか迷っている, "I'm unsure whether to...") would contrast with meaning (A)'s firm decisiveness.

**Example sentences:**
1. その日に全部作業が終わらなければ、次の日に続きをやるまでだ。
   (If I can't finish all the work that day, I'll simply continue it the next day.)
2. だれも協力してくれないのなら、一人でやってみるまでだ。
   (If no one is willing to help, I'll just go ahead and try it alone.)
3. こちら側の言い分が通らなかった場合は、この計画を取りやめるまでのことだ。
   (If our side of the argument doesn't get through, we'll simply cancel this plan.)
4. お褒めの言葉をいただきましたが、わたしはただ自分のするべきことをしたまでです。
   (Thank you for the kind words, but I merely did what I was supposed to do.)
5. 先日のメールは、あなたの発言が気になったから一言書き添えたまでで、他意はありません。
   (That email the other day was just a brief note I added because your remark had been on my mind — there was no hidden meaning behind it.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Meaning (A) expresses the speaker's resolve that doing ～ isn't a big deal and is often used with a conditional clause ("if X, then I'll just do Y") — it is used only for present/future actions, never for the past. Meaning (B), by contrast, always attaches to the past (た形) and is used when the speaker themselves performed an action they consider unremarkable, often as a modest response to being praised or misunderstood. Keep these two uses — and their differing verb forms — clearly distinct.
