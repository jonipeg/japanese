# ～うではないか {#うではないか}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～うではないか

**Furigana form:** ～うではないか

**Meaning:** "Let's ~, shall we? / why don't we ~" — a formal, rhetorical volitional invitation, often used to rally or persuade a group toward collective action, with a somewhat dramatic or speech-like tone.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞意向形 (volitional form) + ではないか — 例: 頑張ろうではないか (let's do our best, shall we!)
- Casual/contracted spoken form: ～うじゃないか

**Typical context:** Formal spoken — speeches, rallying calls, political or motivational addresses, and dramatic narration; sounds overly theatrical in normal daily conversation. The casual variant ～うじゃないか appears in more informal but still assertive speech (e.g., among close friends making a bold suggestion).

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **ましょう** — the plain, everyday equivalent "let's ~," far more common in daily conversation and lacking the rhetorical/persuasive weight.
- **う/よう** (plain volitional alone) — simple "let's," without the rhetorical "isn't that so?" framing that ではないか adds.
- [**べきだ**](grammar-べきだ.md) — "should," a related persuasive tool but framed as obligation rather than a shared invitation to act.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. さあ、みんなで力を合わせて頑張ろうではないか。
   (Come now, let's all join forces and do our best together!)
2. この difficult な時代を共に乗り越えようではないか。
   (Let us overcome these difficult times together!)
3. 未来のために、今こそ行動を起こそうではないか。
   (For the sake of the future, let's take action right now, shall we!)
4. お互いを信じて、最後まで戦おうではないか。
   (Let's trust each other and fight until the end!)
5. この機会に、新しい挑戦を始めようではないか。
   (Let's take this opportunity to begin a new challenge!)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** ～うではないか is far more dramatic and rhetorical than ～ましょう; learners sometimes use it in casual daily requests (e.g., asking a friend to grab lunch), which sounds comically stiff or overly theatrical — it's best reserved for speeches, motivational contexts, or deliberately dramatic/literary effect.
