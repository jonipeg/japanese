# ～にまつわる {#にまつわる}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～に纏わる

**Furigana form:** ～にまつわる

**Meaning:** "Related to/associated with X (legend, rumor, story)" — attaches to a noun to describe a story, legend, rumor, or memory connected to it.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + にまつわる + 名詞
- Example base: 白蛇池 → 白蛇池にまつわる伝説

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal, usable in both speech and writing; commonly used when introducing legends, anecdotes, or background stories tied to a place, object, or event.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **に関する** — "concerning/regarding X"; a more general, neutral topic-marking noun modifier, lacking にまつわる's specific narrative/anecdotal connotation (stories, legends, rumors).
- **をめぐる** — "surrounding X / revolving around X" (e.g., 事件をめぐる議論, "debate surrounding the incident"); similar in connecting a noun to related discourse, but をめぐる more often implies controversy or debate, while にまつわる more often implies lore, anecdotes, or sentimental associations.
- **ゆかりの** — "connected to X / associated with X" (e.g., 歴史ゆかりの地, "a place with historical connections"); close in the sense of meaningful association, though ゆかり leans toward historical/personal connection rather than specifically stories/legends.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; 無関係の ("unrelated to") expresses the reverse — the absence of any such associated story or connection.

**Example sentences:**
1. 村の人々は白蛇池にまつわる伝説を信じていて、誰も近づかないそうだ。 (The villagers believe the legend associated with White Snake Pond, and apparently no one goes near it.)
2. このお城にまつわる怖い話を聞いたことがありますか。 (Have you heard the scary story associated with this castle?)
3. 彼の家族にまつわる複雑な事情があるらしい。 (It seems there are complicated circumstances connected to his family.)
4. この祭りにまつわる由来を調べてみた。 (I looked into the origins associated with this festival.)
5. その指輪にまつわる悲しい思い出を、彼女は誰にも話さなかった。 (She never told anyone the sad memory associated with that ring.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** にまつわる is best reserved for narrative, legendary, or anecdotal associations (stories, rumors, memories, origins) — using it for a plain factual/topical connection (e.g., a report "concerning" a subject) sounds slightly off; に関する would be more appropriate there. The verb 纏わる (まつわる) literally means "to wind around/cling to," which evokes the image of a story wrapping around its subject over time.
