# ～がたい {#がたい}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～難い

**Furigana form:** ～がたい

**Meaning:** "Hard to do ~ / difficult to ~ (emotionally or morally, not physically)" — expresses that something is very difficult to do due to psychological, moral, or emotional reasons, often used with verbs like 信じる, 許す, 理解する.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ます形 (verb stem) + がたい — 例: 信じがたい (hard to believe), 許しがたい (hard to forgive)

**Typical context:** Written and formal spoken — common in literary writing, formal speeches, and serious discussions; sounds stiff/bookish in casual chat.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **にくい** — "hard to do," refers to physical/practical difficulty (e.g., 読みにくい字 "hard-to-read handwriting"); がたい is reserved for emotional/moral difficulty.
- **づらい** — "hard to do due to personal discomfort/pain," e.g. 言いづらい (hard to say out of awkwardness); overlaps a bit but づらい is more personal/casual.
- [**かねる**](grammar-かねる.md) — "cannot do ~ (due to one's position)," more about inability in a social/professional role than emotional difficulty.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No single direct opposite, though ～やすい ("easy to do") is the structural opposite in form, though not always natural in meaning (e.g., 信じやすい means "easy to believe/gullible," a different nuance).

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼が嘘をついたなんて、信じがたい。
   (It's hard to believe that he told a lie.)
2. 彼の裏切り行為は許しがたい。
   (His act of betrayal is hard to forgive.)
3. この結果は到底理解しがたい。
   (This result is extremely hard to understand.)
4. 言いがたいことですが、プロジェクトは失敗に終わりました。
   (It's hard to say this, but the project ended in failure.)
5. その光景は言葉で表しがたいほど美しかった。
   (The scene was so beautiful it was hard to put into words.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often use にくい and がたい interchangeably, but がたい is limited to abstract/emotional verbs (信じる, 許す, 理解する, 近づく in metaphorical sense) — physical difficulty verbs like 読む or 書く should use にくい, not がたい (読みがたい sounds unnatural for literal reading difficulty).
