# ～ものと思う {#ものと思う}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～ものと思う

**Furigana form:** ～ものとおもう

**Meaning:** "I take it as given/natural that X is the case" — expresses the speaker's confident personal assumption or firm belief, often one that later turns out to be confirmed or overturned.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞／形容詞普通形 + ものと思う
- 当然来るものと思っていた — "I had simply assumed/taken for granted that he would come"

**Typical context:** Spoken and written, neutral-to-formal register; often used in retrospective narration to describe an assumption the speaker held, especially in the past tense (～ものと思っていた).

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **～と思い込む** — "to be under a (possibly mistaken) impression," carries a stronger implication that the belief was simply wrong.
- [**～に違いない**](grammar-に違いない.md) — "must be," expresses pure confidence/certainty without the "taken for granted" framing of ものと思う.

**Opposite grammar structures:** ～かどうかわからない ("not sure whether X is the case") expresses the lack of certainty that ものと思う conveys.

**Example sentences:**
1. 落としたものと思っていた指輪が出てきた。
   (The ring I had assumed was lost turned up.)
2. バスは頻繁にあるものと思うが、確認してみよう。
   (I assume the buses run frequently, but let's check to be sure.)
3. 彼は当然同意するものと思っていたが、意外にも反対された。
   (I took for granted that he'd agree, but surprisingly he objected.)
4. てっきり今日は休みだものと思っていた。
   (I was completely convinced that today was a day off.)
5. 両親はわたしが医者になるものと思っていたらしい。
   (It seems my parents had simply assumed I would become a doctor.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This expresses the speaker's confident judgment, but that judgment can later turn out to be mistaken — this is the key difference from plain ～と思う, which doesn't carry the same "taken for granted" implication. It frequently appears in the past tense to narrate an assumption that was later confirmed or disproven, rather than a present-moment opinion.
