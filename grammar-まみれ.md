# ～まみれ {#まみれ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～まみれ

**Furigana form:** ～まみれ

**Meaning:** "Covered in/with X" — describes something or someone thoroughly covered by a messy, unpleasant, or negative substance (dirt, blood, sweat, debt), with a strongly negative connotation.

**Conjugation:**
- [Noun] + まみれ
- Example: 血まみれ (covered in blood), 借金まみれ (buried in debt), 泥まみれ (covered in mud).

**Typical context:** Used in both spoken and written Japanese; register-neutral to slightly informal. Always carries a negative, unpleasant connotation — never used for positive or neutral coverings.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**ずくめ**](grammar-ずくめ.md) — also means "covered/full of," but skews positive or neutral (いいことずくめ "nothing but good things," 黒ずくめ "dressed all in black") — the opposite emotional polarity from まみれ.
- [**だらけ**](grammar-だらけ.md) — very similar to まみれ, meaning "covered with/full of," and can describe both negative and more neutral abundances (間違いだらけ "full of mistakes," ほこりだらけ "covered in dust"); まみれ is more specifically used for sticky, liquid, or grime-like substances clinging to a surface (血, 泥, 汗, 借金), while だらけ has broader application to abstract or countable things.
- **づくし** — "a full set/series of X" (ごちそうづくし "a feast of delicacies"), emphasizing completeness or variety rather than an unpleasant covering.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct grammatical opposite; conceptually contrasts with 清潔な／きれいな ("clean," a simple adjective rather than a grammatical suffix).

**Example sentences:**
1. 交通事故で全身血まみれの怪我人が病院に運び込まれた。
   (An injured person covered in blood from head to toe was brought to the hospital after the traffic accident.)
2. 子どもたちは泥まみれになって公園で遊んでいた。
   (The children were playing in the park, completely covered in mud.)
3. 彼は若い頃、借金まみれの生活を送っていたそうだ。
   (I hear he lived a life buried in debt when he was young.)
4. 工事現場から戻った作業員は汗まみれだった。
   (The worker who came back from the construction site was drenched in sweat.)
5. 古い倉庫の棚はほこりまみれになっていた。
   (The shelves in the old warehouse were completely covered in dust.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern is often grouped with ずくめ as a near-synonym pair, but the two have opposite emotional polarity: ずくめ skews positive/neutral, while まみれ is almost always negative. The source notes an ambiguous, flagged-but-unconfirmed student wrong-answer area around まみれ vs ずくめ confusion — reinforcing that this is a commonly tested distinction. When deciding between まみれ and だらけ, check whether the covering substance is sticky/liquid/grime-like (favoring まみれ) versus a more abstract or countable "full of X" situation (favoring だらけ).
