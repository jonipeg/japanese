# ～のもむりはない {#のもむりはない}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～のも無理はない

**Furigana form:** ～のもむりはない

**Meaning:** "It's reasonable/no wonder that X" — expresses understanding and justification for someone's reaction or state, given the circumstances.

**Conjugation:**
- 普通形 + の + も無理はない
- Example base: 怒る → 怒るのも無理はない

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal, usable in both speech and writing; conveys an empathetic, understanding tone, often used to defend or explain someone's emotional reaction as justified.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **のも当然だ** — "it's only natural that X"; nearly synonymous, perhaps slightly more matter-of-fact, while のも無理はない carries a touch more empathy/justification nuance.
- **はずだ** — "should be the case / it's expected that"; expresses a logical expectation generally, not specifically defending a reaction as reasonable given context.
- **も仕方がない** — "it can't be helped that X"; close in the sense of excusing something, but 仕方がない focuses on resignation/acceptance of an outcome, while 無理はない focuses on validating the reasonableness of a reaction or belief.

**Opposite grammar structures:** ～のはおかしい／～のは理解できない ("it's strange/incomprehensible that X") expresses the reverse — that a reaction seems unreasonable.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼の同じミスは3回目だったので、課長が怒るのも無理はありません。 (Since it was his third time making the same mistake, it's no wonder the section chief got angry.)
2. 何日も徹夜が続いたのだから、疲れているのも無理はない。 (Since he's been up all night for days, it's no wonder he's exhausted.)
3. 初めての海外旅行なのだから、緊張するのも無理はない。 (Since it's her first trip abroad, it's no wonder she's nervous.)
4. あんなに努力したのだから、合格して喜ぶのも無理はない。 (Since she worked so hard, it's no wonder she's overjoyed to have passed.)
5. 長年苦労してきたのだから、彼が弱気になるのも無理はない。 (Since he's struggled for so many years, it's no wonder he's losing his nerve.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** This pattern requires an explicit or implicit reason clause (usually with から／ので) establishing why the reaction is justified — using it without any stated cause leaves the "no wonder" feeling unanchored. It is empathetic in tone, siding with the person reacting, so don't use it to criticize an unreasonable reaction; for that, のはおかしい or理解できない would be more appropriate.
