# ～ない限り {#ない限り}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ない限り

**Furigana form:** ～ないかぎり

**Meaning:** "Unless ~ / as long as ~ not" — states that a result will not occur unless a particular negative condition changes; the negative mirror of ～限り(は).

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ない形 + 限り — 例: 謝らない限り (unless you apologize)
- い形容詞（くない） + 限り
- な形容詞/名詞 + で(は)ない + 限り

**Typical context:** Both written and spoken, neutral-to-formal — common in warnings, conditions for success/failure, and serious statements of resolve.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**なければ**](grammar-てからでないと・なければ.md) — plain conditional "if not," more neutral and less emphatic than ない限り.
- **ない以上は** — "given that ~ not," similar but emphasizes a firm stance/decision rather than a pure condition.
- **さえ～なければ** — "if only ~ not," emphasizes the single deciding factor more strongly.

**Opposite grammar structures:** [**限り(は)**](grammar-限り(は).md) ("as long as ~ is the case") is the direct positive counterpart.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼が謝らない限り、私は許すつもりはない。
   (Unless he apologizes, I don't intend to forgive him.)
2. 努力しない限り、成功はありえない。
   (Unless you make an effort, success is impossible.)
3. 雨が止まない限り、試合は中止になるだろう。
   (Unless the rain stops, the game will likely be cancelled.)
4. 特別な事情がない限り、この規則は適用される。
   (Unless there are special circumstances, this rule applies.)
5. 本人が同意しない限り、契約は成立しない。
   (Unless the person concerned agrees, the contract won't be valid.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners sometimes use なければ and ない限り interchangeably, but ない限り carries a stronger "no way around it" nuance — it implies that absolutely nothing else will change the outcome except that one negative condition being resolved.
