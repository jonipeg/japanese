# ～とはいえ {#とはいえ}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～とはいえ

**Furigana form:** ～とはいえ

**Meaning:** "Having said that / even so" — acknowledges a preceding fact or statement, then introduces a qualification or contrast showing the situation isn't as simple/complete as that statement implies.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞・い形容詞・な形容詞・名詞 の普通形 + とはいえ(ども)
- Example from source: 春(だ)とはいえ (even though it's spring)

**Typical context:** Formal-to-neutral written and spoken Japanese — common in both essays/news commentary and measured spoken remarks. Slightly literary/bookish in feel (especially the archaic ども variant, とはいえども), but also natural in everyday formal speech. Not rude; conveys a thoughtful, balanced, "to be fair" tone.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**けれども**](grammar-けれども.md) — the common gloss/rough equivalent ("but, however"); とはいえ is more formal and specifically emphasizes "granting that the preceding statement is true, nonetheless..." whereas けれども is a general-purpose, more casual conjunction for any kind of contrast.
- [**にもかかわらず**](grammar-にかかわらず・にもかかわらず.md) — "despite X"; similarly contrastive, but にもかかわらず emphasizes an unexpected or illogical gap between X and the result, while とはいえ is milder, simply qualifying/tempering the initial statement rather than expressing surprise.
- **もっとも** — "although, mind you..."; very close in function, used to add a qualifying afterthought, but もっとも feels slightly more casual/conversational than とはいえ.
- **ながらも** — "while/even though X, nonetheless Y"; attaches directly to a single clause's verb/adjective rather than following a complete statement, and emphasizes simultaneity/concession within one subject's state, unlike とはいえ which more broadly qualifies a whole preceding idea.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a conceptual contrast would be だからこそ ("precisely because of X"), which reinforces rather than qualifies a preceding statement.

**Example sentences:**
1. 春(だ)とはいえ、まだ寒い日もある。
   (Even though it's spring, there are still some cold days.)
2. 彼は新人とはいえ、仕事ぶりはベテラン並みだ。
   (Even though he's a newcomer, his work is on par with a veteran's.)
3. 休みだとはいえ、やるべきことは山ほどある。
   (Even though it's a day off, there's still a mountain of things to do.)
4. 値段が安いとはいえ、品質まで悪いわけではない。
   (Even though the price is cheap, it doesn't mean the quality is bad too.)
5. 完治したとはいえ、しばらくは無理をしない方がいい。
   (Even though he's fully recovered, it's still better not to overexert himself for a while.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** A margin note (scan1) records the recurring gloss "とはいえ ≈ けれども." The key nuance for learners is that とはいえ specifically first concedes that a preceding statement is true before immediately tempering or qualifying it — it is not simply a generic "but," and tends to introduce a reality check or caveat rather than a strong reversal, which is a subtler and more formal usage than plain けれども.
