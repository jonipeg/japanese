# ～ところを {#ところを}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～ところを

**Furigana form:** ～ところを (no special kanji readings needed)

**Meaning:** (1) "At a moment when X" — describing being seen/caught in a particular (often awkward or vulnerable) state. (2) An apologetic/polite concessive use: "even though it's X (busy, inconvenient, etc.)," often in set phrases of gratitude or apology.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞ている形／名詞修飾 + ところを + 見られる／捕まる等 (caught/seen doing): 居眠りしているところを先生に見つかった。(I was caught by the teacher dozing off.)
- お忙しいところを／お忙しいところ恐縮ですが (polite apologetic set phrase): お忙しいところを、お越しいただきありがとうございます。(Thank you for coming despite your busy schedule.)

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal; the "caught in the act" sense appears in narrative/spoken Japanese, while the お忙しいところを apologetic pattern is very common formal/polite business language (emails, speeches).

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **ところに／へ** — similar for describing an interrupting coincidence, but without the specific "caught/witnessed" nuance.
- **のに** — "even though," a more general concessive, lacking the polite set-phrase register of お忙しいところを.
- [**にもかかわらず**](grammar-にかかわらず・にもかかわらず.md) — "despite X," more formal equivalent usable in similar apologetic contexts (お忙しいにもかかわらず).

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite.

**Example sentences:**
1. 彼女は泣いているところを友達に見られてしまった。
   (She was seen crying by her friend.)
2. 犯人は逃げているところを警察に捕まった。
   (The criminal was caught by the police while trying to flee.)
3. お忙しいところを恐縮ですが、ご確認いただけますでしょうか。
   (I'm sorry to trouble you despite how busy you are, but could you please confirm this?)
4. 授業中、居眠りしているところを先生に注意された。
   (During class, I was scolded by the teacher for dozing off.)
5. お休みのところを申し訳ございませんが、ご連絡させていただきました。
   (I apologize for contacting you during your time off.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** Learners often confuse ところを with ところに／へ; ところを specifically emphasizes being witnessed/caught in an action, or (in polite set phrases) acknowledges an inconvenient circumstance apologetically. The お忙しいところを pattern is a fixed polite expression worth memorizing as a chunk for business Japanese.
