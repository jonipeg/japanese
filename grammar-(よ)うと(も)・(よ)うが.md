# ～(よ)うと(も)／～(よ)うが {#（よ）うと（も）・（よ）うが}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar
#to-review

**Kanji form:** ～(よ)うと(も)／～(よ)うが

**Furigana form:** ～(よ)うと(も)／～(よ)うが

**Meaning:** "No matter what/how ~" / "Even if ~" — states that whatever the preceding condition turns out to be, it has no bearing on the speaker's judgment, decision, or determination expressed afterward.

**Conjugation:**
- 動詞う・よう形／イ形-かろう／ナ形-だろう・であろう／名詞-だろう・であろう ＋ と(も)／が
- たとえ大地震が起ころうと、このビルは安全なはずだ — "even if a huge earthquake were to strike, this building should be safe"

**Typical context:** Written and formal spoken register, often emphatic or rhetorical — frequently paired with words like たとえ, いかに, どんなに for added force; common in speeches, declarations of resolve, and dramatic narration.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- [**～ても**](grammar-たとえ~ても.md) — "even if ~," the basic, all-purpose concessive conditional; (よ)うと(も)／(よ)うが is a more formal, literary, and emphatic variant built on the volitional form.
- **～にせよ／～にしろ** — "whether it be ~," also expresses that a range of possibilities doesn't change the conclusion, often used with paired or listed alternatives (例：賛成にせよ反対にせよ).
- **～う(よう)が～まいが** — a paired variant meaning "whether ~ or not," explicitly presenting both the affirmative and negative possibility.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; a structure like ～なら ("if ~") stating that the outcome does depend on the condition would express the reverse relationship.

**Example sentences:**
1. たとえ大地震が起ころうと、このビルは安全なはずだ。
   (Even if a huge earthquake were to strike, this building should be safe.)
2. 社長は何を言われようが、自分のやり方を押し通した。
   (No matter what he was told, the company president pushed through with his own way of doing things.)
3. 目標までどんなに遠かろうと、僕は夢を捨てないぞ。
   (No matter how far away the goal is, I won't give up on my dream.)
4. いかに困難だろうと、戦争のない世界を目指して闘いたい。
   (No matter how difficult it is, I want to fight for a world without war.)
5. たとえ有名な政治家であろうとも、家庭では普通の親でしかない。
   (Even a famous politician is just an ordinary parent at home.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** The clause that follows typically expresses the speaker's own firm judgment, resolve, or determination, which remains unaffected by whatever is stated in the preceding condition — it isn't a neutral, factual conditional. This pattern pairs naturally with emphatic adverbs like たとえ, いかに, and どんなに, and learners should not forget that it attaches to the volitional (意向) form of verbs/adjectives/copula rather than the plain form.
