# ～をかねて {#をかねて}
#japanese-grammar
#grammar

**Kanji form:** ～を兼ねて

**Furigana form:** ～をかねて

**Meaning:** "Combining X with (another purpose)" — describes an action or thing that serves two purposes simultaneously.

**Conjugation:**
- 名詞 + を兼ねて
- Example base: 雑誌の取材 → 雑誌の取材を兼ねて

**Typical context:** Neutral-to-formal, usable in both speech and writing; common in describing trips, events, or objects that efficiently serve a dual purpose.

**Similar grammar structures:**
- **も兼ねて** — the same construction with the emphatic も inserted, emphasizing "also serving as X" among possibly other purposes.
- [**ながら**](grammar-ながら.md) — "while doing X"; describes simultaneous actions by a single subject, but without the specific "serving double duty/purpose" nuance of を兼ねて, which is about combined functions rather than simultaneous separate actions.
- **を目的として** — "for the purpose of X"; states a single stated purpose, whereas を兼ねて specifically signals that two purposes are being combined into one action.

**Opposite grammar structures:** No direct opposite; 一つの目的だけで ("for one purpose only") would contrast with を兼ねて's dual-purpose framing.

**Example sentences:**
1. 雑誌の取材を兼ねて一ヶ月ほどヨーロッパを旅行した。 (Combining it with magazine reporting work, I traveled around Europe for about a month.)
2. 気分転換を兼ねて、散歩に出かけた。 (Combining it with a change of pace, I went out for a walk.)
3. 健康診断を兼ねて、久しぶりに病院へ行った。 (Combining it with a health checkup, I went to the hospital for the first time in a while.)
4. お礼を兼ねて、先生に手紙を書いた。 (Combining it with an expression of thanks, I wrote a letter to my teacher.)
5. 新居のお披露目を兼ねて、友人たちを招いてパーティーを開いた。 (Combining it with showing off our new home, we invited friends and held a party.)

**Common mistakes / key nuances:** を兼ねて always implies the main action described in the sentence is doing double duty for an additional stated purpose — it should name both purposes explicitly (the one after 兼ねて, and the main verb's own purpose), otherwise it can seem like a dangling reference. Learners should distinguish it from を目的として, which commits to a single, undivided purpose rather than combining two.
